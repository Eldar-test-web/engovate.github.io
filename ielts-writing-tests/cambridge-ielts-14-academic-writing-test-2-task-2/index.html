    It is argued by some that the most serious environmental concern today is the loss of specific species of plants and animals, while others believe that there are wider environmental issues that require attention. I contend that resolving urgent environmental challenges is a better strategy for both humanities and other species, and this essay will discuss both views and my position.
    On the one hand, the extinction of plant and animal species is a critical issue. Biodiversity plays an essential role in maintaining balanced ecological systems, lacking this will lead to a disruption of the food chain or food insecurity. For instance, a significant decline in bee populations will result in a dramatic reduction in crop yields that rely on bee pollination. Moreover, a loss of biodiversity can lead to a higher risk of disease due to a surge in populations of disease-carrying animals, enabling viruses or bacteria to spread to humans more rapidly.
    On the other hand, many people argue that broader environmental issues are more important because they affect both nature and human life on a larger scale. Climate change can be a good example. It affects various global dimensions, such as rising temperature and sea-level rise, which increase the likelihood of natural disasters impacting all life within the ecosystems. Another major environmental concern is air contamination. There has been a noticeable increase in the number of toxic gases, due to the rapid expansion of environmentally unfriendly industries. Higher levels of air contamination, particularly PM 2.5, significantly impact humans and increase the risk of lung cancer, leading to loss of life.
    In conclusion, while species extinction is a severe concern, from my point of view, tackling broader environmental problems is more essential. If societies do not take effective action on these issues, they may lead to negative consequences for humanity and nature.

    While some argue that losing specific plant and animal species is the main environmental issue, other people, myself included, believe that other aspects of the environmental discussion are bigger causes for concern.

However, while new means of conserving endangered species and avoiding additional losses are paramount, the increasing amount of gas and water pollution is an emblematic example of a significant number of more urgent environmental concerns. Moreover, the growing global human population poses a real threat to the environment, since most partake in the current throwaway, high-consumption culture, which clearly adversely affects it.

In fact, this is directly related to the greenhouse effect, a change in the now highly polluted oceans’ water properties, the alarmingly rising amount of waste produced each year, and a progressively greater scarcity of food and other essential resources.

    In conclusion, although losing diversity through the extinction of a growing number of animals and plants should never be considered trivial, this concern is outweighed by some even scarier issues, such as global warming, sea pollution, air quality, and the increasing number of human births each year.
