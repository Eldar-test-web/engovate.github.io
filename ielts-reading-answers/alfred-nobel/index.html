From IELTS Recent Actual Test 2 Academic Reading Test 5 · Part 1 · Questions 1–13

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 1-13 which are based on Reading Passage 1 below.

Alfred Nobel

The man behind the Nobel Prize

Since 1901, the Nobel Prize has been honoring men and women from all corners of the globe for outstanding achievements in physics, chemistry, medicine, literature, and for work in peace. The foundations for the prize were laid in 1895 when Alfred Nobel wrote his last will, leaving much of his wealth to the establishment of the Nobel Prize.

Alfred Nobel was born in Stockholm on October 21, 1833. His father Immanuel Nobel was an engineer and inventor who built bridges and buildings in Stockholm. In connection with his construction work Immanuel Nobel also experimented with different techniques for blasting rocks. Successful in his industrial and business ventures, Immanuel Nobel was able, in 1842, to bring his family to St. Petersburg. There, his sons were given a first class education by private teachers. The training included natural sciences, languages and literature. By the age of 17 Alfred Nobel was fluent in Swedish, Russian, French, English and German. His primary interests were in English literature and poetry as well as in chemistry and physics. Alfred's father, who wanted his sons to join his enterprise as engineers, disliked Alfred's interest in poetry and found his son rather introverted.

In order to widen Alfred's horizons his father sent him abroad for further training in chemical engineering. During a two year period Alfred Nobel visited Sweden, Germany, France and the United States. In Paris, the city he came to like best, he worked in the private laboratory of Professor T. J. Pelouze, a famous chemist. There he met the young Italian chemist Ascanio Sobrero who, three years earlier, had invented nitroglycerine, a highly explosive liquid. But it was considered too dangerous to be of any practical use. Although its explosive power greatly exceeded that of gunpowder, the liquid would explode in a very unpredictable manner if subjected to heat and pressure. Alfred Nobel became very interested in nitroglycerine and how it could be put to practical use in construction work. He also realized that the safety problems had to be solved and a method had to be developed for the controlled detonation of nitroglycerine.

After his return to Sweden in 1863, Alfred Nobel concentrated on developing nitroglycerine as an explosive. Several explosions, including one (1864) in which his brother Emil and several other persons were killed, convinced the authorities that nitroglycerine production was exceedingly dangerous. They forbade further experimentation with nitroglycerine within the Stockholm city limits and Alfred Nobel had to move his experimentation to a barge anchored on Lake Mälaren. Alfred was not discouraged and in 1864 he was able to start mass production of nitroglycerine. To make the handling of nitroglycerine safer Alfred Nobel experimented with different additives. He soon found that mixing nitroglycerine with kieselguhr would turn the liquid into a paste which could be shaped into rods of a size and form suitable for insertion into drilling holes. In 1867 he patented this material under the name of dynamite. To be able to detonate the dynamite rods he also invented a detonator (blasting cap) which could be ignited by lighting a fuse. These inventions were made at the same time as the pneumatic drill came into general use. Together these inventions drastically reduced the cost of blasting rock, drilling tunnels, building canals and many other forms of construction work.

The market for dynamite and detonating caps grew very rapidly and Alfred Nobel also proved himself to be a very skillful entrepreneur and businessman. Over the years he founded factories and laboratories in some 90 different places in more than 20 countries. Although he lived in Paris much of his life he was constantly traveling. When he was not traveling or engaging in business activities Nobel himself worked intensively in his various laboratories, first in Stockholm and later in other places. He focused on the development of explosives technology as well as other chemical inventions, including such materials as synthetic rubber and leather, artificial silk, etc. By the time of his death in 1896 he had 355 patents.

Intensive work and travel did not leave much time for a private life. At the age of 43 he was feeling like an old man. At this time he advertised in a newspaper "Wealthy, highly-educated elderly gentleman seeks lady of mature age, versed in languages, as secretary and supervisor of household." The most qualified applicant turned out to be an Austrian woman, Countess Bertha Kinsky. After working a very short time for Nobel she decided to return to Austria to marry Count Arthur von Suttner. In spite of this Alfred Nobel and Bertha von Suttner remained friends and kept writing letters to each other for decades. Over the years Bertha von Suttner became increasingly critical of the arms race. She wrote a famous book, Lay Down Your Arms and became a prominent figure in the peace movement. No doubt this influenced Alfred Nobel when he wrote his final will which was to include a Prize for persons or organizations who promoted peace. Several years after the death of Alfred Nobel, the Norwegian Storting (Parliament) decided to award the 1905 Nobel Peace Prize to Bertha von Suttner.

Alfred Nobel died in San Remo, Italy, on December 10, 1896. When his will was opened it came as a surprise that his fortune was to be used for Prizes in Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature and Peace. The executors of his will were two young engineers, Ragnar Sohlman and Rudolf Lilljequist. They set about forming the Nobel Foundation as an organization to take care of the financial assets left by Nobel for this purpose and to coordinate the work of the Prize-Awarding Institutions. This was not without its difficulties since the will was contested by relatives and questioned by authorities in various countries.

Alfred Nobel's greatness lay in his ability to combine the penetrating mind of the scientist and inventor with the forward-looking dynamism of the industrialist. Nobel was very interested in social and peace-related issues and held what were considered radical views in his era. He had a great interest in literature and wrote his own poetry and dramatic works. The Nobel Prizes became an extension and a fulfillment of his lifetime interests.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 FALSE Since 1901, the Nobel Prize has been honoring men and women from all corners of the globe for outstanding achievements in physics, chemistry, medicine, literature, and for work in peace. The foundations for the prize were laid in 1895 when Alfred Nobel wrote his last will, leaving much of his wealth to the establishment of the Nobel Prize Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that the Nobel Prize has been given to people since 1901. In 1895, Alfred Nobel only wrote the plan (his will) for the prize. The first prize was not given in that year.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE. This means the statement, 'The first Nobel Prize was awarded in 1895,' is incorrect.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage clearly states that the Nobel Prize has been awarded 'since 1901'. The year 1895 is mentioned, but that is when Alfred Nobel wrote his will to create the prize, not when the first prize was given.
Q2 NOT GIVEN Successful in his industrial and business ventures, Immanuel Nobel was able, in 1842, to bring his family to St. Petersburg. There, his sons were given a first class education by private teachers Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Alfred Nobel's father, Immanuel, was successful in his business. Because of this success, he moved his family to St. Petersburg. There, he made sure his sons got a top-quality ('first class') education from private teachers.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is NOT GIVEN. This means the text does not say if this statement is true or false.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage does not tell us about the education of Alfred Nobel's father, Immanuel. The passage states that Alfred and his brothers received a 'first class education'. However, it does not compare this education to the one their father had. We do not know if Immanuel's education was good or bad, so we cannot confirm that he wanted his sons to have a 'better' education than his own.
Q3 FALSE The market for dynamite and detonating caps grew very rapidly and Alfred Nobel also proved himself to be a very skillful entrepreneur and businessman Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that many people quickly started buying Nobel's dynamite. It also states that Nobel showed he was a 'skillful entrepreneur and businessman', which means he was very good at creating and managing businesses.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE. This means the statement 'Nobel was an unsuccessful businessman' is incorrect. In fact, Alfred Nobel was a very successful businessman.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage provides clear evidence of Alfred Nobel's business success. The text states that the market for his inventions grew quickly. It directly describes him as a 'skillful entrepreneur and businessman' and mentions that he founded factories and labs in many different countries. This proves he was successful, not unsuccessful.
Q4 FALSE Several years after the death of Alfred Nobel, the Norwegian Storting (Parliament) decided to award the 1905 Nobel Peace Prize to Bertha von Suttner Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that the Norwegian Parliament gave the Nobel Peace Prize to Bertha von Suttner in 1905. This happened many years after Alfred Nobel had already died, so he could not have chosen her himself.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE. This means the statement that Bertha von Suttner was chosen by Alfred Nobel for the first peace prize is not correct.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage states that Alfred Nobel died in 1896. Bertha von Suttner received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1905, which was several years after his death. Therefore, he could not have selected her. The passage also says that the Norwegian Parliament, not Nobel himself, decided to give her the prize.
Q5 TRUE Alfred Nobel died in San Remo, Italy, on December 10, 1896. When his will was opened it came as a surprise that his fortune was to be used for Prizes in Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature and Peace. The executors of his will were two young engineers, Ragnar Sohlman and Rudolf Lilljequist. They set about forming the Nobel Foundation as an organization to take care of the financial assets left by Nobel for this purpose and to coordinate the work of the Prize-Awarding Institutions Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Alfred Nobel passed away on December 10, 1896. After his death, his will (a document with his last wishes) was read. His 'executors' (the people he chose to handle his money and property) then started to create the Nobel Foundation. This shows that the foundation was made after he died.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is TRUE. This means the statement is correct; the Nobel Foundation was created after Alfred Nobel passed away.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage states that Alfred Nobel died in 1896. After he died, his will was opened. The text then says that the people in charge of his will, the executors, started to create, or 'set about forming,' the Nobel Foundation. This shows a clear order of events: first Nobel's death, then the creation of the foundation.
Q6 TRUE Nobel was very interested in social and peace-related issues and held what were considered radical views in his era Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Alfred Nobel was very interested in topics related to society and peace. It also says that his opinions on these topics were thought to be "radical" for the time he lived in. "Radical views" means ideas that are very different and unusual compared to what most people believed back then.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is "TRUE". This means the statement is correct. Alfred Nobel's ideas about society were not common for the time he lived in.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the final paragraph of the passage states that Nobel had strong interests in "social and peace-related issues". It then says his views on these subjects were considered "radical" during his lifetime. The word "radical" means very new and different from what is normal or traditional, which has the same meaning as "uncommon". His "era" was the 1800s, as he lived from 1833 to 1896.
Q7 chemical engineering In order to widen Alfred's horizons his father sent him abroad for further training in chemical engineering Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Alfred's father wanted him to learn more, so he 'sent him abroad' (to other countries) for more 'training' (study) in 'chemical engineering'.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'chemical engineering'. This is a type of study about how to use chemicals to make things.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'chemical engineering' because the passage clearly says that Alfred Nobel's father sent him to other countries so he could get more training in this subject. The question asks what Nobel's father sent him to be trained in.
Q8 Ascanio Sobrero There he met the young Italian chemist Ascanio Sobrero who, three years earlier, had invented nitroglycerine, a highly explosive liquid Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that in the laboratory, Alfred Nobel met a young chemist from Italy. This chemist's name was Ascanio Sobrero. The passage also tells us that Ascanio Sobrero had created nitroglycerine, which is a liquid that explodes easily.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is Ascanio Sobrero. He was the young chemist from Italy that Alfred Nobel met in Paris.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'Ascanio Sobrero' because the passage says that when Alfred Nobel was studying in a laboratory in Paris, he met a young Italian chemist. The passage gives this chemist's name as Ascanio Sobrero and says he was the person who 'had invented nitroglycerine'. The question asks for the name of this person.
Q9 gunpowder Although its explosive power greatly exceeded that of gunpowder, the liquid would explode in a very unpredictable manner if subjected to heat and pressure Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that the power of nitroglycerine to explode ('explosive power') was much, much bigger than the power of gunpowder. The words 'greatly exceeded' mean it was much more powerful.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'gunpowder' is a type of old explosive material.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'gunpowder' because the passage says that nitroglycerine's power was much greater than the power of gunpowder. The notes say that nitroglycerine was a 'more powerful' explosive. The passage uses the keyword 'exceeded', which means 'was more than' or 'was greater than', to show that nitroglycerine was stronger than gunpowder.
Q10 Stockholm They forbade further experimentation with nitroglycerine within the Stockholm city limits and Alfred Nobel had to move his experimentation to a barge anchored on Lake Mälaren Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that the leaders or government ('authorities') stopped ('forbade') Alfred Nobel from doing more tests ('experimentation') with nitroglycerine inside the city of Stockholm.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is Stockholm. Stockholm is the name of a city.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'Stockholm' because the passage states that after some very dangerous explosions happened while Nobel was working on nitroglycerine, the authorities decided it was too unsafe. They then stopped him from doing more experiments inside the city of Stockholm. The word 'forbade' in the passage means the same as 'banned' in the question.
Q11 detonator To be able to detonate the dynamite rods he also invented a detonator (blasting cap) which could be ignited by lighting a fuse Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that to make the dynamite explode, Alfred Nobel also created a device called a 'detonator'. This device would start working if you lit a fuse connected to it.
Answer Explanation:
The answer, "detonator," is a small device used to make an explosive, like dynamite, blow up safely.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'detonator' because the question asks what Nobel invented along with dynamite. The passage states that after inventing dynamite, Nobel needed a way to make it explode. For this purpose, he 'also invented a detonator'.
Q12 pneumatic drill These inventions were made at the same time as the pneumatic drill came into general use. Together these inventions drastically reduced the cost of blasting rock, drilling tunnels, building canals and many other forms of construction work Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Nobel's new inventions were made when a tool called the 'pneumatic drill' started to be used everywhere. These inventions, along with the drill, made construction work much cheaper.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is a type of tool called a 'pneumatic drill'. A pneumatic drill is a powerful drill that uses air pressure.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'pneumatic drill' because the passage says that Nobel's inventions were made 'at the same time as the pneumatic drill came into general use'. The phrase 'came into general use' means that something became popular or common. The notes ask what became popular at the same time as Nobel's inventions.
Q13 cost Together these inventions drastically reduced the cost of blasting rock, drilling tunnels, building canals and many other forms of construction work Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Nobel's inventions, used together, made the price for construction work much lower. This included jobs like breaking up rocks, making tunnels, and building canals.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'cost'. 'Cost' is the amount of money you have to pay for something. The inventions made construction work cheaper.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'cost' because the passage says that Nobel's inventions of dynamite and the detonator, along with the pneumatic drill, 'drastically reduced the cost' of construction. The question asks what was 'dramatically lowered', which has the same meaning as 'drastically reduced'. Therefore, the 'cost' of construction work was lowered.
