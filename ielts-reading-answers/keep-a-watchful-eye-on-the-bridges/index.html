Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 1-13 which are based on Reading Passage 1 below.

Keep a Watchful Eye on the Bridges

A

Most road and rail bridges are only inspected visually, if at all. Every few months, engineers have to clamber over the structure in an attempt to find problems before the bridge shows obvious signs of damage. Technologies developed at Los Alamos National Laboratory, New Mexico, and Texas A&M University may replace these surveys with microwave sensors that constantly monitor the condition of bridges.

B

“The device uses microwaves to measure the distance between the sensor and the bridge, much like radar does,” says Albert Migliori, a Los Alamos physicist. “Any load on the bridge – such as traffic – induces displacements, which change that distance as the bridge moves up and down.” By monitoring these movements over several minutes, the researchers can find out how the bridge resonates. Changes in its behaviour can give an early warning of damage.

C

The Interstate 40 bridge over the Rio Grande river in Albuquerque provided the researchers with a rare opportunity to test their ideas. Chuck Farrar, an engineer at Los Alamos, explains: “The New Mexico authorities decided to raze this bridge and replace it. We were able to mount instruments on it, test it under various load conditions and even inflict damage just before it was demolished.” In the 1960s and 1970s, 2500 similar bridges were built in the US. They have two steel girders supporting the load in each section. Highway experts know that this design is “fracture critical” because a failure in either girder would cause the bridge to fail.

D

After setting up the microwave dish on the ground below the bridge, the Los Alamos team installed conventional accelerometers at several points along the span to measure its motion. They then tested the bridge while traffic roared across it and while subjecting it to pounding from a “shaker”, which delivered precise punches to a specific point on the road.

E

“We then created damage that we hoped would simulate fatigue cracks that can occur in steel girders,” says Farrar. They first cut a slot about 60 centimetres long in the middle of one girder. They then extended the cut until it reached the bottom of the girder and finally they cut across the flange – the bottom of the girder’s “I” shape.

F

The initial, crude analysis of the bridge’s behaviour, based on the frequency at which the bridge resonates, did not indicate that anything was wrong until the flange was damaged. But later the data were reanalysed with algorithms that took into account changes in the mode shapes of the structure – shapes that the structure takes on when excited at a particular frequency. These more sophisticated algorithms, which were developed by Norris Stubbs at Texas A&M University, successfully identified and located the damage caused by the initial cut.

G

“When any structure vibrates, the energy is distributed throughout with some points not moving, while others vibrate strongly at various frequencies,” says Stubbs. “My algorithms use pattern recognition to detect changes in the distribution of this energy.” NASA already uses Stubbs’ method to check the behaviour of the body flap that slows space shuttles down after they land.

H

A commercial system based on the Los Alamos hardware is now available, complete with the Stubbs algorithms, from the Quatro Corporation in Albuquerque for about $100,000. Tim Darling, another Los Alamos physicist working on the microwave interferometer with Migliori, says that as the electronics become cheaper, a microwave inspection system will eventually be applied to most large bridges in the US. “In a decade I would like to see a battery or solar-powered package mounted under each bridge, scanning it every day to detect changes,” he says.

Questions

Questions 1–4 One Choice

Choose the correct answers A, B, C or D.

1 How did the traditional way to prevent damage to the bridges work before the invention of the new monitoring system?
  1. Bridges have to be tested in every movement on two points.
  2. Bridges have to be closely monitored by microwave devices.
  3. Bridges have already been monitored by sensors.
  4. Bridges have to be frequently inspected by professional workers with naked eyes.
2 How do the new microwave monitors find out the problems of bridges?
  1. by changing the distance between the positions of devices
  2. by controlling the traffic flow on the bridges
  3. by monitoring the distance caused by traffic between two points
  4. by displacement of the several critical parts in the bridges
3 Why did the expert believe there is a problem for the design called “fracture critical”?
  1. Engineers failed to apply the newly developed construction materials.
  2. There was not enough finance to repair the bridges.
  3. The supporting parts of the bridges may crack and cause the bridge to fail.
  4. There were bigger traffic load conditions than the designers had anticipated.
4 The defect was not recognized by a basic method in the beginning
  1. until the mid of faces of bridges has fractured.
  2. until the damage appears along and down to the flanges.
  3. until the points on the road have been punched.
  4. until the frequency of resonates appears disordered.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 D Most road and rail bridges are only inspected visually, if at all. Every few months, engineers have to clamber over the structure in an attempt to find problems before the bridge shows obvious signs of damage Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that bridges are usually checked only by looking at them. Every few months, engineers have to climb on the bridge structure to try to find problems before clear damage appears.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that in the past, bridge experts regularly climbed on bridges and checked them using only their eyes.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because paragraph A explains what was done before new sensor technologies were introduced. The text states that bridges were checked "visually" (which means looking with naked eyes) and that "every few months" (frequently), "engineers" (professional workers) had to climb across the bridge to search for damage.
Q2 C “The device uses microwaves to measure the distance between the sensor and the bridge, much like radar does,” says Albert Migliori, a Los Alamos physicist. “Any load on the bridge – such as traffic – induces displacements, which change that distance as the bridge moves up and down.” Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the tool measures the space between itself and the bridge. When cars or trucks pass over, the bridge moves up and down, altering this measurement.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the device detects bridge issues by tracking how the space between the sensor and the bridge changes when vehicles drive over it.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because paragraph B states that the device measures the distance between two points: the sensor and the bridge. When traffic crosses the bridge, the bridge moves up and down, which changes this distance. By watching and tracking these movements, the system can discover bridge problems early. Keywords like "traffic" match "traffic" in the passage, and "distance... between two points" corresponds to the passage's "distance between the sensor and the bridge".
Q3 C They have two steel girders supporting the load in each section. Highway experts know that this design is “fracture critical” because a failure in either girder would cause the bridge to fail Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that each section of the bridge is supported by two steel beams. Experts call this structure dangerous because if just one beam breaks, the entire bridge will fall down.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that if the main parts holding up the bridge break or fail, the whole bridge could collapse.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because paragraph C explains the reason why highway experts consider this structure to be "fracture critical". The bridge has two steel girders that hold up the weight (the "supporting parts"). If either girder breaks or fails, the whole bridge will fail. Therefore, the danger is that damage or failure in these support beams can make the entire bridge fall down.
Q4 B The initial, crude analysis of the bridge’s behaviour, based on the frequency at which the bridge resonates, did not indicate that anything was wrong until the flange was damaged Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that the first simple check of how the bridge moved did not show that anything was broken until the bottom part, called the flange, was damaged.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the simple initial test could not detect any problem until the crack reached and harmed the flange (the bottom part of the beam).
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because paragraph F clearly states that the early, simple analysis did not show any problem until the flange received damage. In the passage, "initial, crude analysis" matches "basic method in the beginning," and "did not indicate that anything was wrong" matches "The defect was not recognized." This lasted "until the flange was damaged."
Q5 microwave dish After setting up the microwave dish on the ground below the bridge, the Los Alamos team installed conventional accelerometers at several points along the span to measure its motion Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the researchers placed the microwave dish on the ground underneath the bridge before putting other measuring tools in place.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to a round, bowl-shaped tool that sends and receives microwave signals to check the bridge.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is identified in paragraph D, which describes the equipment set up on the ground beneath the bridge. The text states that the team set up the "microwave dish on the ground below the bridge" to monitor its movement.
Q6 accelerometers After setting up the microwave dish on the ground below the bridge, the Los Alamos team installed conventional accelerometers at several points along the span to measure its motion Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the researchers placed standard measuring tools called accelerometers at multiple spots on the bridge structure to record how the bridge moved.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "accelerometers" refers to standard sensors or instruments placed at different points on the bridge to detect and measure its movement.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "accelerometers" because Paragraph D describes the equipment the researchers placed directly on the bridge structure. Specifically, after putting the microwave dish on the ground, the team placed "conventional accelerometers" along the span of the bridge to monitor its motion.
Q7 steel girders They have two steel girders supporting the load in each section Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that each section of these bridges uses two strong steel beams to hold up the heavy weight.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means large, strong beams made of steel that hold up a bridge.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "steel girders" because the text explains that bridges of this design rely on two large steel beams to hold up the weight in every section.
Q8 flange finally they cut across the flange – the bottom of the girder’s “I” shape Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the "flange" is the specific term used for the bottom section of the "I"-shaped steel beam.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "flange" means the flat bottom edge of a steel beam shaped like the letter "I".
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "flange" because paragraph E explains the different parts of the steel girder that researchers cut to test the bridge. The passage directly identifies the bottom part of the girder's "I" shape as the "flange".
Q9 C The Interstate 40 bridge over the Rio Grande river in Albuquerque provided the researchers with a rare opportunity to test their ideas. Chuck Farrar, an engineer at Los Alamos, explains: “The New Mexico authorities decided to raze this bridge and replace it. We were able to mount instruments on it, test it under various load conditions and even inflict damage just before it was demolished.” Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the scientists got an uncommon and great chance to experiment on a bridge because it was scheduled to be torn down. This allowed them to place equipment on it, put different weights and forces on it, and intentionally damage it.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "C" means that Paragraph C is the section of the text that talks about the special chance the scientists had to test a bridge under different types of heavy weight and pressure.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Paragraph C discusses how the Interstate 40 bridge gave researchers a "rare opportunity to test their ideas." It explains that because the bridge was going to be knocked down, the engineers were allowed to test it under "various load conditions" (different levels of weight and pressure) and even cause intentional damage before it was destroyed.
Q10 H “In a decade I would like to see a battery or solar-powered package mounted under each bridge, scanning it every day to detect changes,” he says Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that in ten years (a decade), the scientist hopes to have small, self-powered microwave sensors placed under every bridge to check them daily.
Answer Explanation:
The answer, paragraph H, is the section that talks about what good changes could happen with the microwave tool over the next ten years.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is paragraph H because it mentions future hopes for the microwave technology over a period of ten years. In this paragraph, a scientist looks ahead to the future and says, "In a decade I would like to see a battery or solar-powered package mounted under each bridge, scanning it every day to detect changes." Here, the word "decade" means a period of ten years, which matches the question's phrase "ten-year positive change".
Q11 G NASA already uses Stubbs’ method to check the behaviour of the body flap that slows space shuttles down after they land Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the famous space agency NASA is already using the scientist's system to inspect a part of their space shuttles.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "G" means that paragraph G mentions the researchers' method being chosen and used by a prestigious, highly respected organization.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is G because the paragraph explains that NASA, a world-famous and prestigious space agency, already employs Norris Stubbs' method to inspect space shuttle parts. Having an important client like NASA represents receiving an "honourable contract" or high-status working agreement.
Q12 B “The device uses microwaves to measure the distance between the sensor and the bridge, much like radar does,” says Albert Migliori, a Los Alamos physicist. “Any load on the bridge – such as traffic – induces displacements, which change that distance as the bridge moves up and down.” By monitoring these movements over several minutes, the researchers can find out how the bridge resonates Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains how the tool functions: it uses microwave waves similar to radar to measure distance. As traffic makes the bridge move up and down, the sensor watches these movements to see how the bridge shakes.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means that paragraph B has the description of how the new microwave tool works.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because paragraph B explains the working mechanism (how the system functions). Physicist Albert Migliori states that the tool sends microwaves to track the distance between the sensor and the bridge. When cars move across the bridge, the structure shifts up and down, and tracking these movements helps researchers find early signs of damage.
Q13 E “We then created damage that we hoped would simulate fatigue cracks that can occur in steel girders,” says Farrar. They first cut a slot about 60 centimetres long in the middle of one girder. They then extended the cut until it reached the bottom of the girder and finally they cut across the flange – the bottom of the girder’s “I” shape Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage clearly tells us how the researchers made damage on purpose by cutting into a steel beam step by step.
Answer Explanation:
The answer E means that Paragraph E describes how the scientists intentionally harmed the bridge.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because this paragraph details the exact process of how the scientists intentionally harmed the structure. The text uses the words "created damage" (which matches "deliberately created") and explains the steps: first cutting a 60-centimetre slot into a girder, then lengthening that cut to the bottom, and finally cutting across the flange.
