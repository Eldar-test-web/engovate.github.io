From IELTS Simulation Tests Academic Reading Test 03 · Part 2 · Questions 14–26

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 14–26, which are based on Reading Passage 2 below.

CHANGES IN MALE BODY IMAGE

A The pressures on women to look slender, youthful and attractive have been extensively documented, but changing expectations for women's bodies have varied widely. From voluptuous and curvy in the days of Marilyn Monroe, to slender and androgynous when Twiggy hit the London scene in the mid-1960s, and then on to the towering Amazonian models of the 1980s and the "heroin chic" and size-zero obsession of today, it is not just clothes that go in and out of fashion for women. The prevailing notion of the perfect body for men, however, has remained remarkably static: broad shoulders, a big chest and arms, and rippling, visible abdominal muscles and powerful legs have long been the staple ingredients of a desirable male physique.

B A growing body of evidence suggests this is changing, however. Rootsteins, a mannequin design company in Britain, has released its newest male model—the homme nouveau—with a cinched-in 27-inch waist. "To put that into perspective," says one female fashion reporter, "I had a 27-inch waist when I was thirteen...and I was really skinny." The company suggests that the homme nouveau "redresses the prevailing 'beefcake' figure by carving out a far more streamlined, sinuous silhouette to match the edgier attitude of a new generation".

C Elsewhere in the fashion industry, the label American Apparel is releasing a line of trousers in sizes no larger than a 30-inch waist, which squeezes out most of the younger male market who have an average waistline over five inches larger. Slender young men are naturally starting to dominate the catwalks and magazine pages as well. "No one wanted the big guys," model David Gandy has said, describing how his muscled physique was losing him jobs. "It was all the skinny, androgynous look. People would look at me very, very strangely when I went to castings."

D Achieving such a physique can be unattainable for those without the natural genetic make-up. “I don't know that anyone would consider my body archetypal or as an exemplar to work towards," notes model Davo McConville. "You couldn't aim for this; it's defined by a vacuum of flesh, by what it's not." Nevertheless, statistics suggest it is not just an obsession of models, celebrities and the media—more and more ordinary men are prepared to go to great lengths for a slender body. One indication is the growing number of men who are discovering surgical reconstruction. Male breast-reduction has become especially popular; in 2009 the year-on-year growth rate for this procedure rose to 44 percent in the United Kingdom. Liposuction also remains popular in the market for male body reconstructive surgery, with 35,000 such procedures being performed on men every year.

E Additionally, more men now have eating disorders than ever before. These are characterised by abnormal eating habits, typically either the consumption of insufficient or excessive amounts of food. Eating disorders are detrimental to the physical and mental condition of people who suffer from them, and the desire to achieve unrealistic physiques has been implicated as a cause. In 1990 only 10% of people suffering from anorexia or bulimia were believed to be male, but this figure has climbed steadily to around one quarter today. Around two in five binge eaters are men. Women still make up the majority of those afflicted by eating disorders, but the perception of it being a "girly" problem has contributed to men being less likely to pursue treatment. In 2008, male eating disorders were thrust into the spotlight when former British Deputy Prime Minister, John Prescott, admitted to habitually gorging on junk food and then inducing himself to vomit while in office. "I never admitted to this out of the shame and embarrassment," he said. "I found it difficult as a man like me to admit that I suffered from bulimia."

F In some respects, the slim male silhouette seems to be complementing, rather than displacing, the G. I. Joe physique. Men's Health, one of the only titles to weather the floundering magazine market with sales increasing to a quarter of a million per issue, has a staple diet of bulky men on the cover who entice readers with the promise of big, powerful muscles. Advertising executives and fashion editors suggest that in times of recession and political uncertainty, the more robust male body image once again becomes desirable. Academic research supports this claim, indicating that more "feminine" features are desirable for men in comfortable and secure societies, while "masculine" physical traits are more attractive where survival comes back to the individual. A University of Aberdeen study, conducted using 4,500 women from over 30 countries, found a pronounced correlation between levels of public healthcare and the amount of effeminacy women preferred in their men. In Sweden, the country considered to have the best healthcare, 68 percent of women preferred the men who were shown with feminine facial features. In Brazil, the country with the worst healthcare in the study, only 45 percent of women were so inclined. "The results suggest that as healthcare improves, more masculine men fall out of favour," the researchers concluded.

G Ultimately, columnist Polly Vernon has written, we are left with two polarised ideals of masculine beauty. One is the sleek, slender silhouette that exudes cutting-edge style and a wealthy, comfortable lifestyle. The other is the "strong, muscular, austerity-resistant" form that suggests a man can look after himself with his own bare hands. These ideals co-exist by pulling men in different directions and encouraging them to believe they must always be chasing physical perfection, while simultaneously de-stabilising any firm notions of what physical perfection requires.

H As a result, attaining the ideal body becomes an ever more futile and time-consuming task. Vernon concludes that this means less time for the more important things in life, and both sexes should resist the compulsive obsession with beauty.

Questions

Questions 14–20 Matching Information

Reading Passage 2 has eight paragraphs, A–H.

Which paragraph contains the following information?

Write the correct letter, A–H.

NB You may use any letter more than once.

14 an opinion on whether body image changes have positive or negative effects
15 a historical comparison of gendered body images
16 a humiliating confession of overeating by a public figure
17 a cosmetic operation that has become increasingly popular
18 a health condition afflicting increasing numbers of men
19 the effect of changing body ideals on a male model
20 an explanation of how living standards affect the desirability of male physiques

Questions 21–26 Yes / No / Not Given

Do the following statements agree with the information given in Reading Passage 2?

YES if the statement agrees with the views of the writer

NO if the statement contradicts the views of the writer

NOT GIVEN if it is impossible to say what the writer thinks about this

21 A thin body is achievable for men regardless of their genes.
22 Male liposuction is more popular than male breast-reduction.
23 Eating disorders harm the mind and body.
24 Women seek help for eating disorders more often than men.
25 Men's Health has suffered from a downturn in magazine sales.
26 As public healthcare improves men become more feminine.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q14 G These ideals co-exist by pulling men in different directions and encouraging them to believe they must always be chasing physical perfection, while simultaneously de-stabilising any firm notions of what physical perfection requires Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that these two body types affect men in a bad way by making them feel pressured to constantly pursue a perfect body while confusing them about what that perfect body should look like.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "G" means that Paragraph G includes a viewpoint explaining the effects of changing standards of male body image.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is G because Paragraph G shares the opinion of columnist Polly Vernon. She explains that having two opposing ideals of male beauty affects men negatively by pulling them in conflicting directions, constantly pushing them to chase an impossible standard of perfection, and confusing what true physical perfection actually means.
Q15 A From voluptuous and curvy in the days of Marilyn Monroe, to slender and androgynous when Twiggy hit the London scene in the mid-1960s, and then on to the towering Amazonian models of the 1980s and the "heroin chic" and size-zero obsession of today, it is not just clothes that go in and out of fashion for women. The prevailing notion of the perfect body for men, however, has remained remarkably static: broad shoulders, a big chest and arms, and rippling, visible abdominal muscles and powerful legs have long been the staple ingredients of a desirable male physique Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that female body trends have changed across different historical eras, while the ideal male body shape remained mostly the same over that time.
Answer Explanation:
The answer identifies Paragraph A as the section describing how ideal body shapes for both women and men have changed or stayed the same throughout past decades.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is Paragraph A because it provides a historical comparison between male and female body standards. It details how female body expectations shifted across different eras—from Marilyn Monroe to the 1960s, 1980s, and modern times—and compares this with male body ideals, which historically remained unchanged with broad shoulders, big chests, and strong muscles. Key matching terms include the historical references across decades (such as 'the mid-1960s' and 'the 1980s') and the gender comparison between 'women' and 'men'.
Q16 E In 2008, male eating disorders were thrust into the spotlight when former British Deputy Prime Minister, John Prescott, admitted to habitually gorging on junk food and then inducing himself to vomit while in office. "I never admitted to this out of the shame and embarrassment," he said. "I found it difficult as a man like me to admit that I suffered from bulimia." Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that an important government leader, John Prescott, confessed that he struggled with an eating disorder where he ate lots of unhealthy food and made himself throw up, which he had hidden for years because he felt very ashamed.
Answer Explanation:
The answer E means that Paragraph E talks about a well-known politician who felt embarrassed when admitting that he ate too much food.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because this paragraph describes how the former British Deputy Prime Minister, John Prescott (a public figure), admitted (confession) that he repeatedly ate huge amounts of junk food (overeating) and vomited, and that he kept it a secret for a long time because of feeling ashamed and embarrassed (humiliating).
Q17 D Male breast-reduction has become especially popular; in 2009 the year-on-year growth rate for this procedure rose to 44 percent in the United Kingdom Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that surgery to reduce male breast size has become very popular, showing a large increase of 44 percent in just one year in the UK.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "D" means that paragraph D discusses a type of surgery used to improve physical appearance that is being chosen by more and more people.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because paragraph D directly describes a cosmetic surgery that is rising in popularity among men. The paragraph notes that "surgical reconstruction" is being discovered by more men, specifically pointing out that "Male breast-reduction has become especially popular" with a growth rate rising to 44 percent. Here, "cosmetic operation" matches "surgical reconstruction" / "procedure" / "Male breast-reduction", and "become increasingly popular" matches "become especially popular" and "growth rate for this procedure rose to 44 percent".
Q18 E Additionally, more men now have eating disorders than ever before. These are characterised by abnormal eating habits, typically either the consumption of insufficient or excessive amounts of food. Eating disorders are detrimental to the physical and mental condition of people who suffer from them, and the desire to achieve unrealistic physiques has been implicated as a cause Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that more men than in the past are suffering from eating problems. These problems hurt a person's body and mind, and they often happen because men want to get an unrealistic body shape.
Answer Explanation:
The answer E means that Paragraph E discusses a medical or psychological problem that is affecting a growing number of men.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because Paragraph E focuses on eating disorders, such as anorexia and bulimia, which harm a person's body and mind. The paragraph clearly shows that the number of men suffering from these health issues is rising by stating that "more men now have eating disorders than ever before" and that the male percentage of sufferers "has climbed steadily to around one quarter today." Key matching words include "health condition" (eating disorders that are detrimental to physical and mental condition), "afflicting" (suffering from / afflicted by), and "increasing numbers" (more men than ever before / climbed steadily).
Q19 C "No one wanted the big guys," model David Gandy has said, describing how his muscled physique was losing him jobs. "It was all the skinny, androgynous look. People would look at me very, very strangely when I went to castings." Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that male model David Gandy lost jobs because the fashion industry no longer wanted muscular men, preferring very skinny men instead.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "C" means that Paragraph C contains the information about how new body trends affected a male model's career.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Paragraph C specifically talks about a male model named David Gandy. It describes the effect of changing beauty standards on him: because the fashion world began preferring very skinny, slender models instead of muscular men, his muscular body caused him to lose work and receive strange looks at auditions.
Q20 F Academic research supports this claim, indicating that more "feminine" features are desirable for men in comfortable and secure societies, while "masculine" physical traits are more attractive where survival comes back to the individual Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that when people live in safe and wealthy conditions, softer male features are liked more, whereas strong, muscular traits are preferred in tougher environments where survival is harder.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is F because Paragraph F explains how a country's living conditions and healthcare standards change what people find attractive in a man's body.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is F because it discusses research showing that living standards (such as living in 'comfortable and secure societies' or having better 'public healthcare') directly influence which male physical traits are considered attractive or preferred ('desirable').
Q21 NO Achieving such a physique can be unattainable for those without the natural genetic make-up Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage means that getting this kind of slim body can be impossible for people who were not born with the right natural body type in their genes.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement directly contradicts the ideas written in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because the passage explains that getting a very slim body can be impossible ("unattainable") for men who do not naturally have the right genes ("natural genetic make-up"). Therefore, the statement's claim that a thin body is achievable regardless of genetics is incorrect.
Q22 NOT GIVEN Male breast-reduction has become especially popular; in 2009 the year-on-year growth rate for this procedure rose to 44 percent in the United Kingdom. Liposuction also remains popular in the market for male body reconstructive surgery, with 35,000 such procedures being performed on men every year Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that both male breast-reduction and liposuction are popular surgical procedures for men, but it does not state which of the two surgeries is more popular.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the passage does not provide enough information to know whether male liposuction is more popular than male breast-reduction.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because while the text mentions both procedures, it does not compare them to say which one is more popular. Paragraph D states that male breast-reduction has become "especially popular" (rising by 44 percent in the UK in 2009) and that liposuction "remains popular" (35,000 procedures per year on men). Because there is no direct comparison of total popularity between the two, we cannot determine if the statement is true or false.
Q23 YES Eating disorders are detrimental to the physical and mental condition of people who suffer from them, and the desire to achieve unrealistic physiques has been implicated as a cause Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that eating disorders are harmful to a person's physical health (body) and mental health (mind).
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means that the statement completely matches what the text says.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because Paragraph E clearly states that eating disorders cause harm to both a person's body and mind. The word "detrimental" in the text means causing harm or damage, "physical condition" refers to the body, and "mental condition" refers to the mind.
Q24 YES Women still make up the majority of those afflicted by eating disorders, but the perception of it being a "girly" problem has contributed to men being less likely to pursue treatment Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that most people who have eating disorders are women, and because this illness is seen as something that mainly affects females, men are less willing to ask for and receive medical treatment.
Answer Explanation:
The answer YES means the statement is true and matches what is written in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because Paragraph E explains that eating disorders are often viewed as a "girly" problem, which causes men to be less likely to seek or pursue medical help (treatment). Therefore, women seek help for eating disorders more frequently than men do.
Q25 NO Men's Health, one of the only titles to weather the floundering magazine market with sales increasing to a quarter of a million per issue, has a staple diet of bulky men on the cover who entice readers with the promise of big, powerful muscles Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that even though the magazine industry was having a hard time, Men's Health did well and saw its sales grow to 250,000 copies per issue.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement directly contradicts or disagrees with the facts mentioned in the passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because the passage explains that while the general magazine market was struggling ("floundering"), Men's Health did not suffer from falling sales. Instead, it was able to survive ("weather") the market trouble, and its sales actually went up ("sales increasing to a quarter of a million per issue"). Since the statement claims that Men's Health suffered a downturn (decrease) in sales, it is false.
Q26 NOT GIVEN A University of Aberdeen study, conducted using 4,500 women from over 30 countries, found a pronounced correlation between levels of public healthcare and the amount of effeminacy women preferred in their men Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that researchers found a connection between better healthcare in a country and women liking more feminine traits in men. It does not state that the men themselves change or become more feminine.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether men actually become more feminine as healthcare gets better.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because Paragraph F discusses what women prefer in men, not how men actually physically change. The text mentions a study finding a link between public healthcare and the "amount of effeminacy women preferred in their men," concluding that "more masculine men fall out of favour." However, the passage never claims that men themselves become more feminine as healthcare improves.
