Innovative Types Of Tourism - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From IELTS Mock Tests Academic Reading Test 11 · Part 3 · Questions 28–40

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 28-40 which are based on Reading Passage 3 below.

Innovative types of tourism

A When you get tired of typical sight-seeing, when you have had enough of monuments, statues, and cathedrals, then think outside the box. Read the four paragraphs below about the innovative types of tourism emerging around the globe and discover ways to spice up your itinerary.

B You could eat your way through your travels if you wished. A comparatively new kind of tourism is gaining popularity across the world. In this, food and beverages are the main factors that motivate a person to travel to a particular destination. Combining food, drink and culture, this type of travel provides for an authentic experience, the food and restaurants reflecting the local and unique flavors of a particular region or country. Studies conducted into this travel phenomenon have shown that food plays, consciously or unconsciously, an important part in the vacations of a good number of travelers. Those trying this are looking for a more participatory style of holiday experience. Analysts have noticed a shift from ‘passive observation’ to ‘interaction and involvement’ in tourists, whereby the visitor comes into close contact with locals and their way of life rather than remaining a mere spectator.

C This is a novel approach to tourism in which visitors do not visit the ordinary tourist attractions in traditional fashion. Rather, they let their whims be their guides! Destinations are chosen not on their standard touristic merit but on the basis of an idea or concept often involving elements of humor, serendipity, and chance. One example is known as Monopoly-travel. Participants armed with the local version of a Monopoly game board explore a city at the whim of a dice roll, shuttling between elegant shopping areas and the local water plant – with the occasional visit to jail.

Another example is Counter-travel, which requires you to take snapshots with your back turned to landmarks like the Eiffel Tower or Big Ben. Joël Henry, the French founder of Latourex, has developed dozens of ideas since coming up with the concept in 1990. The traveler must increase his or her receptiveness, in this way, no trip is ever planned or predictable. Henry’s most unusual invention is known as “Erotravel”, where a couple heads to the same town but travels there separately. The challenge is to find one another abroad. He and his wife have engaged in the pursuit in five cities and have managed to meet up every time.

D This involves any crop-based or animal based operation or activity that brings visitors to a farm or ranch. It has recently become widespread in America, and participants can choose from a wide range of activities that include picking fruits and vegetables, riding horses, tasting honey, learning about wine and cheese making, or shopping in farm gift shops for local and regional products or handicrafts. For rural economies struggling to stay afloat in this age of industrial farming, it has become an important and marketable opportunity for improving the incomes and potential economic viability of small farms and rural communities. In western North Carolina, the organization ‘HandMade in America’ is using this method to develop their local economy and craft trades, and to educate visitors about farming practices. On their website, it is described as a niche market. As people are becoming more interested in the ecological importance of local food production, related projects reinforce the need to support local growers and allow visitors to experience the relationship between food and our natural environment.

E This is the trend of traveling to destinations that are first seen in movies, for instance, touring London in a high-speed boat like James Bond or visiting the stately homes that are seen in Jane Austen films. The term was first coined in the US press in the New York Post by journalist Gretchen Kelly, who wrote a 2007 article entitled “The sexiest film locations from 2007 to visit now.”

Currently, summer blockbuster movies are being used as themed marketing tools by companies like Expedia and Fandango, who are promoting trips to where the Steven Spielberg film, Indiana Jones and the Kingdom of the Crystal Skull was made. Corporations as well as convention and tourism boards are exploiting the trend, creating their own location based travel maps, like the Elizabeth: The Golden Age movie map published by VisitBritain, Britain’s official travel and tourism guide. Other travel itineraries have been created by tourism boards for movies including The Da Vinci Code (France), In Bruges (Belgium), and P.S. I Love You (Ireland). Although a new concept, it’s fast becoming a major factor in the choices travelers make in an increasingly tight economic climate. If a traveler has seen a site in a major motion picture, its media exposure makes it a compelling choice for a family vacation or honeymoon.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q28 ii In this, food and beverages are the main factors that motivate a person to travel to a particular destination Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that for this kind of travel, food and drinks are the primary reasons why someone decides to go on a trip.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "ii" refers to "Cuisine Tourism", which means traveling to places mainly to enjoy food and drinks.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is ii because Section B is entirely about traveling focused on food, eating, and drinking. The passage states that "food and beverages" motivate people to travel and highlights the connection between "food, drink and culture". In English, the word "cuisine" refers to a style of cooking or food, directly matching the theme of this paragraph.
Q29 i This is a novel approach to tourism in which visitors do not visit the ordinary tourist attractions in traditional fashion. Rather, they let their whims be their guides! Destinations are chosen not on their standard touristic merit but on the basis of an idea or concept often involving elements of humor, serendipity, and chance Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that this kind of travel is not traditional sightseeing. Tourists decide where to go based on playful concepts, luck, or sudden ideas instead of typical tourist attractions.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "i" means "Experimental Tourism," which refers to a new and playful way of traveling that tests unusual ideas, games, and chance rather than following normal travel plans.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "i" because Section C describes a completely new travel concept where people do not follow traditional sightseeing routes. Instead, trips are based on fun experiments, games, or sudden ideas—such as exploring a city by rolling dice like in the board game Monopoly or taking photos facing away from famous monuments. Keywords like "novel approach", "idea or concept", and "chance" show that these trips are unusual experiments.
Q30 vii This involves any crop-based or animal based operation or activity that brings visitors to a farm or ranch Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that this style of tourism includes any activity involving plants or animals that takes guests to a farm or ranch.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "vii" means heading vii, which is "Agritourism."
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading vii because Paragraph D focuses entirely on travel related to agriculture and farms. It describes activities like visiting a "farm or ranch", "picking fruits and vegetables", and learning about "farming practices". The term "Agritourism" directly refers to this type of tourism based on agriculture and rural farm life.
Q31 vi This is the trend of traveling to destinations that are first seen in movies, for instance, touring London in a high-speed boat like James Bond or visiting the stately homes that are seen in Jane Austen films Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that this style of travel means visiting places people first watched in movies, giving examples like doing things seen in James Bond or Jane Austen films.
Answer Explanation:
The answer, heading vi, means "Set-jetting," which refers to visiting real-world places that were featured as filming locations in movies.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading vi because Section E focuses entirely on tourists visiting places they have seen on screen. The paragraph highlights travelers visiting destinations from famous movies (such as James Bond or Indiana Jones), discusses "film locations," and mentions travel agencies creating movie-themed maps and itineraries. The term "set-jetting" combines "movie set" and "jet-setting" (traveling).
Q32 authentic Combining food, drink and culture, this type of travel provides for an authentic experience, the food and restaurants reflecting the local and unique flavors of a particular region or country Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that bringing together food, drinks, and local culture gives travelers a real and genuine experience that reflects the area's true tastes.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means real, genuine, or true to the local culture rather than fake or made just for tourists.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by Paragraph B, which discusses food-based tourism. The text explains that mixing food, beverages, and culture gives travelers an "authentic experience." In the question sentence, "putting together and enjoying culinary delights" matches "combining food, drink and culture", and making the trip more genuine corresponds directly to providing an "authentic" experience.
Q33 elegant Participants armed with the local version of a Monopoly game board explore a city at the whim of a dice roll, shuttling between elegant shopping areas and the local water plant – with the occasional visit to jail Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that people playing this travel game travel back and forth between fancy, stylish shopping districts and ordinary places like the local water plant.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "elegant" means stylish, graceful, fancy, and high-class.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "elegant" because the sentence refers to moving between regular public places and upscale shopping places. In paragraph C, the text describes Monopoly-travel participants "shuttling between elegant shopping areas and the local water plant". Here, "shuttling between" matches "moving quickly between", "the local water plant" represents the "mundane public service facilities", and "shopping areas" corresponds to "malls". Therefore, the word describing these shopping areas is "elegant".
Q34 honeymoon If a traveler has seen a site in a major motion picture, its media exposure makes it a compelling choice for a family vacation or honeymoon Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when a location appears in a big movie, people find it very appealing to visit that place for a family holiday or for their honeymoon.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "honeymoon" means a special holiday taken by a newly married couple to celebrate their wedding.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "honeymoon" because the passage explains that seeing a place in a major film makes people want to visit it for a family holiday or a post-wedding trip. In the sentence, "couples" going somewhere after their wedding matches the word "honeymoon", and "blockbuster movies" corresponds to "major motion picture".
Q35 widespread It has recently become widespread in America, and participants can choose from a wide range of activities that include picking fruits and vegetables, riding horses, tasting honey, learning about wine and cheese making, or shopping in farm gift shops for local and regional products or handicrafts Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that visiting farms for activities like picking fruit and learning how wine is made has recently become common across America.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "widespread" means happening in many places, affecting many people, or becoming very common.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "widespread" because Paragraph D discusses farm-based activities such as "picking fruits and vegetables" (matching "visiting a strawberry picking field") and "learning about wine and cheese making" (matching "listening to lectures on producing good wine"). The passage explicitly states that this type of tourism "has recently become widespread in America", which corresponds directly to "in the USA... is becoming increasingly widespread".
Q36 NOT GIVEN Studies conducted into this travel phenomenon have shown that food plays, consciously or unconsciously, an important part in the vacations of a good number of travelers Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that research shows food is an important factor in the holidays of many travelers, but it does not claim that it is the single most important part or that this is true for most travelers.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not contain enough information to say if the statement is true or false.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the text only mentions that food plays "an important part" in trips for "a good number of travelers." It never states that enjoying food is "the most critical" (the most important) part of a holiday, nor does it say this applies to the "majority" (more than half) of all travelers. Since this specific claim is never confirmed or denied in the text, the information is not given.
Q37 TRUE Another example is Counter-travel, which requires you to take snapshots with your back turned to landmarks like the Eiffel Tower or Big Ben Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that doing Counter-travel means you must take pictures with your back facing famous sights such as the Eiffel Tower or Big Ben.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is TRUE because the statement correctly matches the details given in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because Paragraph C explains that Counter-travel demands that you photograph yourself while turning away from famous places. The passage uses the phrase "requires you to take snapshots with your back turned to landmarks", where "requires" means it is a "need", "snapshots" means "photos", and "with your back turned to landmarks" means "facing away from a popular tourist site".
Q38 TRUE As people are becoming more interested in the ecological importance of local food production, related projects reinforce the need to support local growers and allow visitors to experience the relationship between food and our natural environment Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that people care more and more about growing food locally for environmental reasons, and these activities emphasize why it is important to help and support local farmers.
Answer Explanation:
The answer TRUE means that the statement matches the information stated in the reading text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because Paragraph D explains that people are paying more attention to the value of food grown locally, which highlights the importance of helping area farmers. In the text, "becoming more interested in the ecological importance of local food production" and "reinforce the need to support local growers" directly match the idea of "gaining appreciation for the need to back those producing local grown vegetables and other crops." Therefore, the statement agrees with the passage.
Q39 FALSE The term was first coined in the US press in the New York Post by journalist Gretchen Kelly, who wrote a 2007 article entitled “The sexiest film locations from 2007 to visit now.” Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that the name for this kind of travel was first created and published by a journalist in an American newspaper (the US press), not in the British media.
Answer Explanation:
The answer FALSE means that the statement directly contradicts the facts given in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the statement claims that the term was first used in the British media. However, the passage clearly states that the term was first coined in the US press (in the New York Post). Since "US press" contradicts "British media", the statement is false. Key phrases to notice include "first coined" (which means first created or used) and "US press" (American newspapers/media).
Q40 C When you get tired of typical sight-seeing, when you have had enough of monuments, statues, and cathedrals, then think outside the box Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage tells travelers that if they are bored of seeing the same normal monuments and sights, they should think differently and come up with creative ideas.
Answer Explanation:
The answer choice C means having original, imaginative ideas rather than ordinary ones.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the phrase 'think outside the box' is an English idiom that means to think in a creative, unconventional, and imaginative way. In paragraph A, the author suggests thinking outside the box when one gets bored of standard sightseeing, meaning one should approach travel with creative and unusual ideas.
