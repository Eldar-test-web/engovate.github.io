Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 1-13 which are based on Reading Passage 1 below.

THE PLIGHT OF RICE

Rice is a tall grass with a drooping panicle that contains numerous edible grains and has been cultivated in China for more than 6,000 years. A staple throughout Asia and large parts of Africa, it is now grown in flooded paddy fields from sea level to high mountains and harvested three times a year. According to the Food and Health Organisation of the United Nations, around four billion people currently receive a fifth of their calories from rice.

Recently, Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan have slightly reduced rice consumption due to the adoption of more western diets, but almost all other countries have raised their consumption due to population increase. Yet, since 1984, there have been diminishing rice yields around the world.

From the 1950s to the early 1960s, rice production was also suffering: India was on the brink of famine, and China was already experiencing one. In the late 1950s, Norman Borlaug, an American plant pathologist, began advising Punjab State in northwestern India to grow a new semi-dwarf variety of wheat. This was so successful that, in 1962, a semi-dwarf variety of rice, called IR8, developed by the Philippine International Rice Research Institute (IRRI), was planted throughout Southeast Asia and India. This semi-dwarf variety heralded the Green Revolution, which saved the lives of millions of people by almost doubling rice yields: from 1.9 metric tons per hectare in 1950-64, to 3.5 metric tons in 1985-98.

IR8 survived because, as a semi-dwarf, it only grows to a moderate height, and it does not thin out, keel over, and drown like traditional varieties. Furthermore, its short thick stem is able to absorb chemical fertilisers, but, as stem growth is limited, the plant expends energy on producing a large panicle of heavy seeds, ensuring a greater crop.

However, even with a massive increase in rice production, semi-dwarf varieties managed to keep up with population growth for only ten years. In Africa, where rice consumption is rising by 20% annually, and where one-third of the population now depends on the cereal, this is disturbing. At the current rate, within the next 20 years, rice will surpass maize as the major source of calories on that continent. Meantime, even in ideal circumstances, paddies worldwide are not producing what they once did, for reasons largely unknown to science. An average 0.8% fall in yields has been noted in rich rice-growing regions; in less ideal ones, flood, drought and salinity have meant yields have fallen drastically, sometimes up to 40%.

The sequencing of the rice genome took place in 2005, after which the IRRI developed genetically modified flood-resistant varieties of rice, called Sub 1, which produce up to four times more edible grain than non-modified strains. In 2010, a handful of farmers worldwide were planting IRRI Sub 1 rice; now, over five million are doing so. Currently, drought- and salt-resistant varieties are being trialled since most rice is grown in the great river basins of the Brahmaputra, the Irrawaddy, and the Mekong that are all drying up or becoming far saltier.

With global warming, many rice-growing regions are hotter than 20 years ago. Nearly all varieties of rice, including IR8, flower in the afternoon, but the anthers – little sacs that contain male pollen - wither and die in soaring temperatures. IRRI scientists have identified one variety of rice, known as Odisha, that flowers in the early morning and they are in the process of genetically modifying IR8 so it contains Odisha-flowering genes, although it may be some time before this is released.

While there is a clear need for more rice, many states and countries seem less keen to influence agricultural policy directly than they were in the past. Some believe rice demand will dip in wealthier places, as occurred in Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan; others consider it more prudent to devote resources to tackling obesity or to limiting intensive farming that is environmentally destructive.

Some experts say where there is state intervention it should take the form of reducing subsidies to rice farmers to stimulate production; others propose that small landholdings should be consolidated into more economically viable ones. There is no denying that land reform is pressing, but many governments shy away from it, fearing losses at the ballot box, all the while knowing that rural populations are heading for the city in droves anyway. And, as they do so, cities expand, eating up fertile land for food production.

One can only hope that the IRRI and other research institutions will spearhead half a dozen mini green revolutions, independently of uncommitted states.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 FALSE it is now grown in flooded paddy fields from sea level to high mountains and harvested three times a year Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that rice is planted in areas ranging from sea level (low elevation) up to high mountains (high elevation).
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE because the statement claims rice is grown only at low heights, which is incorrect.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the text clearly states that rice is grown "from sea level to high mountains." The keyword "elevation" means height above the ground or sea. Since rice is grown all the way up into "high mountains," it is not "only" grown at a low elevation.
Q2 NOT GIVEN Rice is a tall grass with a drooping panicle that contains numerous edible grains and has been cultivated in China for more than 6,000 years. A staple throughout Asia and large parts of Africa, it is now grown in flooded paddy fields from sea level to high mountains and harvested three times a year Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that rice has been grown in China for more than 6,000 years and is eaten a lot in Asia and Africa, but it does not mention how long rice has been grown in Africa.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say how long rice has been grown in Africa.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage tells us that rice has been grown in China for over 6,000 years and is an important basic food in Africa, but it never mentions how many years people in Africa have been farming it. Because the text does not give a time period like "3,000 years" for Africa, we cannot know if the statement is true or false.
Q3 NOT GIVEN Yet, since 1984, there have been diminishing rice yields around the world Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that the amount of rice harvested worldwide has been dropping since 1984, but it never talks about bugs or pests causing this problem.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the passage does not say whether pest attacks caused rice production to go down after 1984.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage tells us that rice crops have been decreasing since 1984 ("diminishing rice yields"), but it does not mention "infestations of pests" anywhere as the cause. In fact, it later states that yields are dropping "for reasons largely unknown to science". Since the text gives no information about pests being responsible for this decrease, the statement cannot be confirmed as true or false.
Q4 FALSE In the late 1950s, Norman Borlaug, an American plant pathologist, began advising Punjab State in northwestern India to grow a new semi-dwarf variety of wheat Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that Norman Borlaug told people in Punjab to plant a special kind of wheat, rather than rice.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE because the statement gives information that is the opposite of what the text says.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage clearly states that Norman Borlaug advised the farmers in Punjab State to plant a new semi-dwarf variety of wheat, not rice. The semi-dwarf variety of rice was developed later by a research institute (IRRI) after the wheat project was successful.
Q5 TRUE This semi-dwarf variety heralded the Green Revolution, which saved the lives of millions of people by almost doubling rice yields: from 1.9 metric tons per hectare in 1950-64, to 3.5 metric tons in 1985-98 Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the Green Revolution helped save many people because it nearly doubled the amount of rice that fields produced.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means that the text confirms that the Green Revolution raised the amount of rice harvested by about 100%.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage states that the Green Revolution saved millions of lives by "almost doubling rice yields" (going from 1.9 to 3.5 metric tons per hectare). In mathematics and everyday language, to double an amount is to increase it by 100%, so "almost doubling" directly matches the phrase "increased rice yields by around 100%".
Q6 thin IR8 survived because, as a semi-dwarf, it only grows to a moderate height, and it does not thin out, keel over, and drown like traditional varieties Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that older, traditional kinds of rice grow too tall, become weak and thin, fall over, and drown in water, whereas the newer IR8 plant does not.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "thin" means slender, narrow, or not thick.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "thin" because the passage contrasts the IR8 variety with traditional varieties of rice. The text states that IR8 only reaches a medium height and does not "thin out, keel over, and drown like traditional varieties." This shows that older, traditional plants grow tall, become thin ("thin out"), and then fall down or collapse ("keel over").
Q7 stem its short thick stem is able to absorb chemical fertilisers Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the IR8 plant has a stalk (stem) that is both short and thick, and this part can take in chemical plant food (fertilisers).
Answer Explanation:
The answer "stem" refers to the main stalk or body of a plant that holds it up and carries nutrients.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "stem" because the notes ask which part of the IR8 plant absorbs fertilizer. The passage specifically mentions that IR8 has a "short thick stem" that "is able to absorb chemical fertilisers". Therefore, "stem" directly completes the phrase "short [stem]".
Q8 crop Furthermore, its short thick stem is able to absorb chemical fertilisers, but, as stem growth is limited, the plant expends energy on producing a large panicle of heavy seeds, ensuring a greater crop Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that because the IR8 plant's stem stays short, it uses its energy to create a big group of heavy seeds, which guarantees a larger harvest (a greater crop).
Answer Explanation:
The answer "crop" means the plants or grains that are grown and gathered by farmers during a harvest.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "crop" because the text explains that the IR8 rice plant focuses its energy on making a large panicle filled with heavy seeds. This results in "ensuring a greater crop." In the notes, the phrase "produces a bigger" directly matches "ensuring a greater" in the passage, making "crop" the correct word to fill in the blank.
Q9 4 / four The sequencing of the rice genome took place in 2005, after which the IRRI developed genetically modified flood-resistant varieties of rice, called Sub 1, which produce up to four times more edible grain than non-modified strains Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that researchers created a modified rice type named Sub 1 that can survive floods and make up to four times as much edible grain as varieties that have not been modified.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "4/four" refers to the number of times more grain that the Sub 1 rice variety can produce compared to regular, unmodified types of rice.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by paragraph 6, which discusses the genetically altered "Sub 1" rice strains. The text explains that this flood-resistant variety can yield "up to four times more edible grain than non-modified strains." Therefore, the word "four" (or the number "4") correctly fills in the blank before "times the amount of grain than non-modified varieties."
Q10 5 million / five million / 5 000 000 / 5,000,000 In 2010, a handful of farmers worldwide were planting IRRI Sub 1 rice; now, over five million are doing so Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that in 2010 only a few farmers grew Sub 1 rice. Now, more than five million farmers grow it.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the number of farmers who now grow Sub 1 rice is over five million.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "5 million" because the notes say Sub 1 is "Now grown by over ___ farmers." The passage says "now, over five million are doing so." Key words: "now" and "over" match the notes, and "planting" means the same as "grown."
Q11 morning IRRI scientists have identified one variety of rice, known as Odisha, that flowers in the early morning and they are in the process of genetically modifying IR8 so it contains Odisha-flowering genes, although it may be some time before this is released Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that researchers found a rice type called Odisha which opens its flowers early in the morning, rather than during the hotter afternoon.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "morning" means the first part of the day, between sunrise and the afternoon.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "morning" because the notes ask about the specific time of day when the Odisha variety blooms. The text explicitly mentions that scientists found a type of rice named Odisha that "flowers in the early morning". Matching the phrase "early" in the prompt leads directly to the word "morning".
Q12 C others consider it more prudent to devote resources to tackling obesity or to limiting intensive farming that is environmentally destructive Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that some governments believe it is better and more sensible to spend resources on solving the health problem of obesity instead of pushing for more agricultural food production.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that governments prefer spending their time and money on dealing with the problem of being extremely overweight (obesity).
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the passage discusses why many governments are not actively encouraging rice farming. It states that some governments think it is wiser to use their resources for "tackling obesity" rather than directly managing agriculture. The word "confronting" matches "tackling", and "devote resources to" indicates that they are more interested in focusing on this problem.
Q13 B And, as they do so, cities expand, eating up fertile land for food production Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when cities become bigger, they take over and destroy the rich land used to grow food.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that good farming soil is being lost as cities grow larger.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage states that as people move from rural areas to cities, the cities expand and eat up "fertile land for food production." Here, "urbanisation speeds up" matches cities expanding, and "disappearing" matches "eating up."
