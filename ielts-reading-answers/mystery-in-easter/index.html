Mystery in Easter

A One of the world's most famous yet least visited archaeological sites, Easter Island is a small, hilly, now treeless island of volcanic origin. Located in the Pacific Ocean at 27 degrees south of the equator and some 2200 miles (3600 kilometers) off the coast of Chile, it is considered to be the world's most remote inhabited island. The island is, technically speaking, a single massive volcano rising over ten thousand feet from the Pacific Ocean floor. The island received its most well-known current name, Easter Island, from the Dutch sea captain Jacob Roggeveen who became the first European to visit the island on Easter Sunday, April 5, 1722.

B In the early 1950s, the Norwegian explorer Thor Heyerdahl popularized the idea that the island had been originally settled by advanced societies of Indians from the coast of South America. Extensive archaeological, ethnographic and linguistic research has conclusively shown this hypothesis to be inaccurate. It is now recognized that the original inhabitants of Easter Island are of Polynesian stock (DNA extracts from skeletons have confirmed this), that they most probably came from the Marquesas or Society islands, and that they arrived as early as 318 AD (carbon dating of reeds from a grave confirms this). At the time of their arrival, much of the island was forested, was teeming with land birds, and was perhaps the most productive breeding site for seabirds in the Polynesia region. Because of the plentiful bird, fish and plant food sources, the human population grew and gave rise to a rich religious and artistic culture.

C That culture's most famous features are its enormous stone statues called moai, at least 288 of which once stood upon massive stone platforms called ahu. There are some 250 of these ahu platforms spaced approximately one half mile apart and creating an almost unbroken line around the perimeter of the island. Another 600 moai statues, in various stages of completion, are scattered around the island, either in quarries or along ancient roads between the quarries and the coastal areas where the statues were most often erected. Nearly all the moai are carved from the tough stone of the Rano Raraku volcano. The average statue is 14 feet and 6 inches tall and weighs 14 tons. Some moai were as large as 33 feet and weighed more than 80 tons. Depending upon the size of the statues, it has been estimated that between 50 and 150 people were needed to drag them across the countryside on sleds and rollers made from the island's trees.

D Scholars are unable to definitively explain the function and use of the moai statues. It is assumed that their carving and erection derived from an idea rooted in similar practices found elsewhere in Polynesia but which evolved in a unique way on Easter Island. Archaeological and iconographic analysis indicates that the statue cult was based on an ideology of male, lineage-based authority incorporating anthropomorphic symbolism. The statues were thus symbols of authority and power, both religious and political. But they were not only symbols. To the people who erected and used them, they were actual repositories of sacred spirit. Carved stone and wooden objects in ancient Polynesian religions, when properly fashioned and ritually prepared, were believed to be charged by a magical spiritual essence called mana. The ahu platforms of Easter Island were the sanctuaries of the people, and the moai statues were the ritually charged sacred objects of those sanctuaries.

E Besides its more well-known name, Easter Island is also known as Te-Pito-O-Te-Henua, meaning 'The Navel of the World', and as Mata-Ki-Te-Rani, meaning ' Eyes Looking at Heaven '. These ancient names and a host of mythological details ignored by mainstream archaeologists, point to the possibility that the remote island may once have been a geodetic marker and the site of an astronomical observatory of a long forgotten civilization. In his book, Heaven's Mirror, Graham Hancock suggests that Easter Island may once have been a significant scientific outpost of this antediluvian civilization and that its location had extreme importance in a planet-spanning, mathematically precise grid of sacred sites. Two other alternative scholars, Christopher Knight and Robert Lomas, have extensively studied the location and possible function of these geodetic markers. In their fascinating book, Uriel's Machine, they suggest that one purpose of the geodetic markers was as part of a global network of sophisticated astronomical observatories dedicated to predicting and preparing for future cometary impacts and crustal displacement cataclysms.

F In the latter years of the 20th century and the first years of the 21st century various writers and scientists have advanced theories regarding the rapid decline of Easter Island's magnificent civilization around the time of the first European contact. Principal among these theories, and now shown to be inaccurate, is that postulated by Jared Diamond in his book Collapse: How Societies Choose to Fail or Survive. Basically these theories state that a few centuries after Easter Island's initial colonization the resource needs of the growing population had begun to outpace the island's capacity to renew itself ecologically. By the 1400s the forests had been entirely cut, the rich ground cover had eroded away, the springs had dried up, and the vast flocks of birds coming to roost on the island had disappeared. With no logs to build canoes for offshore fishing, with depleted bird and wildlife food sources, and with declining crop yields because of the erosion of good soil, the nutritional intake of the people plummeted. First famine, then cannibalism, set in. Because the island could no longer feed the chiefs, bureaucrats and priests who kept the complex society running, the resulting chaos triggered a social and cultural collapse. By 1700 the population dropped to between one-quarter and one-tenth of its former number, and many of the statues were toppled during supposed "clan wars " of the 1600s and 1700s.

G The faulty notions presented in these theories began with the racist assumptions of Thor Heyerdahl and have been perpetuated by writers, such as Jared Diamond, who do not have sufficient archaeological and historical understanding of the actual events which occurred on Easter Island. The real truth regarding the tremendous social devastation which occurred on Easter Island is that it was a direct consequence of the inhumane behavior of many of the first European visitors, particularly the slavers who raped and murdered the islanders, introduced small pox and other diseases, and brutally removed the natives to mainland South America.

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 v It is now recognized that the original inhabitants of Easter Island are of Polynesian stock (DNA extracts from skeletons have confirmed this), that they most probably came from the Marquesas or Society islands, and that they arrived as early as 318 AD (carbon dating of reeds from a grave confirms this). At the time of their arrival, much of the island was forested, was teeming with land birds, and was perhaps the most productive breeding site for seabirds in the Polynesia region. Because of the plentiful bird, fish and plant food sources, the human population grew and gave rise to a rich religious and artistic culture Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Polynesian settlers arrived on Easter Island very early in history, and because the island had lots of food and trees, the population expanded and developed a rich culture.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "v" means that the paragraph is about the first people who moved to the island, how their numbers grew, and how their society became successful.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is v because Paragraph B discusses who the first settlers were (original inhabitants of Polynesian stock arriving around 318 AD) and describes how the abundance of natural resources allowed the community to expand and develop a flourishing culture. The words "original inhabitants" and "arrival" correspond to "early immigrants," while "human population grew and gave rise to a rich religious and artistic culture" corresponds directly to "rise and prosperity."
Q28 ii The statues were thus symbols of authority and power, both religious and political. But they were not only symbols. To the people who erected and used them, they were actual repositories of sacred spirit Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the moai statues were not made for just one simple reason. Instead, they stood for political and religious power, while also serving as real holy objects that held sacred spiritual energy.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that heading ii is the best title for Paragraph D because the paragraph explains that the statues had several different meanings and purposes combined together.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is ii because Paragraph D discusses the meaning and roles of the moai statues. The text highlights that the statues served multiple functions: they were "symbols of authority and power, both religious and political," as well as "actual repositories of sacred spirit" containing a magical spiritual energy called "mana." Therefore, the statues represented a combination of political, social, and religious purposes.
Q29 iii These ancient names and a host of mythological details ignored by mainstream archaeologists, point to the possibility that the remote island may once have been a geodetic marker and the site of an astronomical observatory of a long forgotten civilization Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the island's old names and myths suggest it could have served as a location for measuring the Earth and observing the stars in ancient times.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'iii' means that Paragraph E discusses ancient locations or markers that were used for scientific purposes, such as studying the stars.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading iii because Paragraph E focuses on theories that Easter Island was used for scientific work. The text mentions that ancient names and stories suggest the island was a "geodetic marker" and an "astronomical observatory." It also mentions that researchers view the island as a "scientific outpost" within a global network of observatories used for astronomical calculations and predictions.
Q30 viii The faulty notions presented in these theories began with the racist assumptions of Thor Heyerdahl and have been perpetuated by writers, such as Jared Diamond, who do not have sufficient archaeological and historical understanding of the actual events which occurred on Easter Island. The real truth regarding the tremendous social devastation which occurred on Easter Island is that it was a direct consequence of the inhumane behavior of many of the first European visitors, particularly the slavers who raped and murdered the islanders, introduced small pox and other diseases, and brutally removed the natives to mainland South America Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that earlier popular theories were based on wrong and biased assumptions. It provides the real explanation: the destruction of the population was actually caused by the cruel actions and diseases brought by European visitors.
Answer Explanation:
The answer heading 'viii' means giving an opposing, true explanation against false beliefs and racially biased ideas.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading viii because Paragraph G directly refutes the incorrect ideas ('faulty notions') and 'racist assumptions' promoted by earlier writers. The author counters these misconceptions by revealing the 'real truth' behind the island's devastation, explaining that it was caused by the brutal and inhumane actions of European visitors rather than the islanders destroying their own society.
Q31 TRUE It is now recognized that the original inhabitants of Easter Island are of Polynesian stock (DNA extracts from skeletons have confirmed this), that they most probably came from the Marquesas or Society islands, and that they arrived as early as 318 AD (carbon dating of reeds from a grave confirms this) Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that tests on ancient skeletons prove that the earliest settlers of Easter Island were Polynesian people who most likely came from the Marquesas or Society islands.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is TRUE because the text clearly states that the first people living on Easter Island were Polynesian and likely originated from the Marquesas or Society islands.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because Paragraph B directly confirms this information. It states that the original inhabitants (the first people living there) were of Polynesian origin and most likely traveled from the Marquesas or Society islands. Key terms in the passage like 'original inhabitants' match 'first inhabitants' in the question.
Q32 TRUE Another 600 moai statues, in various stages of completion, are scattered around the island, either in quarries or along ancient roads between the quarries and the coastal areas where the statues were most often erected Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that there are 600 moai statues across the island that are in different steps of being made, which confirms that they were not all completed.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means that the passage confirms some of the moai statues were left unfinished.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because Paragraph C mentions that around 600 moai statues were found "in various stages of completion." The phrase "in various stages of completion" means that the statues were at different levels of being made, showing that some of them were not yet finished.
Q33 FALSE It is assumed that their carving and erection derived from an idea rooted in similar practices found elsewhere in Polynesia but which evolved in a unique way on Easter Island Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that while the basic idea came from common Polynesian customs, the moai statues developed in a special and unique style only found on Easter Island.
Answer Explanation:
The answer FALSE means the statement contradicts the information in the text, so moai statues themselves are not found across other parts of Polynesia.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage explains that making and raising the moai statues came from traditions found in other Polynesian areas, but this practice "evolved in a unique way on Easter Island". This means the moai statues themselves are unique to Easter Island and are not found elsewhere in Polynesia.
Q34 FALSE These ancient names and a host of mythological details ignored by mainstream archaeologists, point to the possibility that the remote island may once have been a geodetic marker and the site of an astronomical observatory of a long forgotten civilization Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that regular or mainstream archaeologists have ignored the clues and myths suggesting Easter Island was used as an astronomical observatory.
Answer Explanation:
The answer FALSE means that the statement directly contradicts the facts given in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the text shows that mainstream archaeologists do not support the idea of the island having an astronomical function. Instead, mainstream archaeologists "ignored" these details, and the astronomical theory was only suggested by "alternative scholars" like Graham Hancock, Christopher Knight, and Robert Lomas. Therefore, most archaeologists do not recognize these astronomical functions.
Q35 NOT GIVEN In his book, Heaven's Mirror, Graham Hancock suggests that Easter Island may once have been a significant scientific outpost of this antediluvian civilization and that its location had extreme importance in a planet-spanning, mathematically precise grid of sacred sites Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that one author believes the island was an important scientific base for a very old, forgotten human civilization, but it never mentions aliens or extraterrestrial beings.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the passage does not provide enough information to know whether the structures were used as an astronomical base for visitors from outer space.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because while Paragraph E discusses theories that Easter Island might have been a "scientific outpost" or an "astronomical observatory" created by an "antediluvian civilization" (a long-lost ancient human civilization), there is no mention anywhere in the text about "extraterrestrial visitors" (aliens from space). Since this claim is neither confirmed nor denied by the text, the correct answer is NOT GIVEN.
Q36 TRUE The faulty notions presented in these theories began with the racist assumptions of Thor Heyerdahl and have been perpetuated by writers, such as Jared Diamond, who do not have sufficient archaeological and historical understanding of the actual events which occurred on Easter Island Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the ideas in these collapse theories are wrong and based on biased assumptions. It also mentions that the authors promoting these theories did not truly understand the real history and events of Easter Island.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means that the passage confirms that the theory blaming the collapse of Easter Island on running out of natural resources is based on a flawed or misleading point of view.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage clearly states that the resource-depletion theories (such as the one promoted by Jared Diamond) are based on "faulty notions" and "racist assumptions." The author explains that these writers lacked a proper archaeological and historical understanding of what actually happened, showing that this theory does indeed have a distorted perspective.
Q37 growing population Basically these theories state that a few centuries after Easter Island's initial colonization the resource needs of the growing population had begun to outpace the island's capacity to renew itself ecologically Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that some theories believe the island's resources were used up because there were more and more people needing food and materials than nature could replace.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to an increasing number of people living in an area.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by Paragraph F, which discusses theories about the collapse of Easter Island's civilization. According to these theories, as the islanders' numbers increased over time, the "resource needs of the growing population" exceeded what the island's environment could naturally replenish, leading to depleted resources.
Q38 racist assumptions The faulty notions presented in these theories began with the racist assumptions of Thor Heyerdahl and have been perpetuated by writers, such as Jared Diamond, who do not have sufficient archaeological and historical understanding of the actual events which occurred on Easter Island Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the incorrect ideas about the island started from the racially biased beliefs of Thor Heyerdahl and were continued by other authors who lacked proper knowledge of the island's history.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to the prejudiced and unfair beliefs about racial groups held by an early explorer.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by Paragraph G, which explains where the incorrect ideas about Easter Island's history originated. In the passage, "faulty notions" corresponds to "erroneous theories," and "began with" matches "began with a root of." The text states that these flawed ideas started with the "racist assumptions" introduced by Thor Heyerdahl.
Q39 archaeological and historical / archeological and historical The faulty notions presented in these theories began with the racist assumptions of Thor Heyerdahl and have been perpetuated by writers, such as Jared Diamond, who do not have sufficient archaeological and historical understanding of the actual events which occurred on Easter Island Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says the wrong ideas started with Thor Heyerdahl's racist beliefs. Writers like Jared Diamond continued them because they did not know enough archaeological and historical facts about Easter Island.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the early writers lacked enough 'archaeological and historical' understanding.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'archaeological and historical' because the summary says writers did not have adequate ___ understandings. This matches 'do not have sufficient archaeological and historical understanding' in paragraph G. Keywords: 'adequate' = 'sufficient', 'understandings' = 'understanding'. It is only three words, so it fits the limit.
Q40 inhumane behavior / inhumane behaviour The real truth regarding the tremendous social devastation which occurred on Easter Island is that it was a direct consequence of the inhumane behavior of many of the first European visitors, particularly the slavers who raped and murdered the islanders, introduced small pox and other diseases, and brutally removed the natives to mainland South America Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the real cause of Easter Island's destruction was the terrible, cruel treatment by the first European visitors who attacked, infected, and enslaved the local people.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to the cruel and evil actions done by people to others.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by Paragraph G, which explains the true reason behind the island's social collapse. The text states that the huge social damage ("tremendous social devastation") was caused directly by the "inhumane behavior" (cruel actions) of the early European visitors, rather than internal environmental failure.
