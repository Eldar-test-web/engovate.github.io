COMMISSION FOR SOCIAL DEVELOPMENT, TO ADDRESS EMPLOYMENT, AGEING, DISABILITY - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 27-40, which are based on Reading Passage 3 below.

COMMISSION FOR SOCIAL DEVELOPMENT, TO ADDRESS EMPLOYMENT, AGEING, DISABILITY

• Employment, ageing and disability will be the main issues addressed by the Commission for Social Development when it convenes from 7 to 16 February at United Nations Headquarters for its forty-fifth session.

• The Commission will discuss shifting trends in employment, such as the rise of unemployment even when the economy is going strong, the growing conditions of job insecurity and instability experienced by workers worldwide, the expansion in the number of the self-employed and the fast growth of the service sector, which is poised to overtake agriculture as the world's largest source of employment.

• The Commission will review the Madrid International Plan of Action on Ageing, five years after it was adopted at the Second World Assembly on Ageing. The Plan is the first international agreement that specifically recognizes the potential of older people to contribute to the development of their societies, and commits Governments to include ageing in all social and economic development policies.

Employment

• Before the Commission is a report of the Secretary-General on Promoting full employment and decent work for all. Total employment has risen since the mid-1990s, the report says, but problems of unemployment and underemployment have worsened. A large number of people, despite working, remain below the poverty line. An increasing share of the labour force works under conditions of job insecurity, instability and discrimination.

• The global labour force (people who were either working or looking for work) grew between 1995 and 2005 by some 438 million, or 16.5 per cent, to over 3 billion, says the report. In 2005, about 84 per cent of the global labour force was in developing countries, with Asia and the Pacific accounting for about 60 per cent of world employment. The number of people in work rose by 400 million (16.3 per cent) to 2.85 billion. But at the same time that global economic output grew by 3.8 per cent per year, the number of unemployed worldwide rose by 21.9 per cent, to 192 million people, and the unemployment rate rose from about 6 to 6.3 per cent.

• Women's earnings are substantially lower than men's throughout the world. Women are also overrepresented in the informal economy, where jobs are less stable and workers' rights are less protected. The increasing number of women working outside the home poses a problem for social integration, since work outside the home often means less time spent in traditional roles.

• Older persons in poorer regions continue to participate, to a great extent, in the labour force because of the limited coverage of social security systems and the relatively low guaranteed incomes.

• Persons with disabilities are disproportionately unemployed: in some countries, as many as 80 per cent of them are without work. They are often underemployed, paid below minimum wage and doing work below their skill levels. Many work in the informal economy. According to estimates by the International Labour Organization (ILO), 368 million of the world's working-age people have a disability.

• Poverty is pervasive in most indigenous communities because of lack of employment opportunities. Migration, which is becoming the coping mechanism for indigenous populations, increases their vulnerability, because migrants often lack the skills required to operate in the formal economy and are often discriminated against.

• However, the report notes, Governments have sought to improve the labour market opportunities of women and youth. The inclusion of persons with disabilities has also gained impetus and Governments are beginning to enact policies and programmes to address the needs of indigenous peoples.

• Looking at trends by sector, the service sector could soon overtake agriculture as the largest global source of employment. The fastest-growing sector, it employed 1.1 billion people in 2005, almost equal to the number employed in agriculture. Its share of total employment rose from 34.5 to 38.9 per cent over the last 10 years. Industry in 2005 employed about 600 million people, or 21 per cent of world employment.

• Self-employment has grown in recent years. In developing countries, self-employment outside of agriculture accounts for 60-70 per cent of informal work. The growth has also been significant in developed countries. In the United States, the number of businesses with no paid employees was 18.6 million in 2003, the highest figure since the Census Bureau began releasing such statistics.

• Various trends are affecting the workplace, the report says. Stiff competition under increased globalization has led to reduced job security and job-related benefits, as well as to a diminished role for organized labour. The "standard" employment relationship has been declining in favour of more precarious forms of employment that typically offer lower wages, poorer working conditions and less social protection, such as on-call or temporary work, self-employment or home-based work. Where State-supported social protection systems exist, they have not been modified to accommodate the growth of precarious employment.

• To address this situation, says the report, the goals of full and productive employment and decent work for all should become central in national and international policies and national development strategies. A key challenge is creating an international and national enabling environment that supports full employment and decent work.

• Offshoring, which can have a considerable impact on the labour market of developing countries, should be better regulated to arrest a possible "race to the bottom" in labour standards. At the same time, for many countries participation in these systems is an important way to attract investment and increase technological capacity.

• The employment intensity of growth should be increased through measures to remove policy discriminations against the agricultural sector. Programmes should provide small agricultural producers with credit, extension services and marketing assistance. Likewise, policy changes should remove biases against small enterprises, provide incentives for their subcontracting and provide them with more information and marketing assistance.

• Active labour market policies (ALMPs) should facilitate adjustment to changes brought about by trade liberalization and globalization. These include retraining for displaced workers, job search assistance and other measures to facilitate labour mobility. Social dialogue should be strengthened on economic reform programmes and on worker-management cooperation about enterprise restructuring. Such dialogue is essential to reach consensus on reforms that improve the functioning of labour markets while preserving essential protection for workers.

• Policymakers' tendency to equate progress in reducing unemployment or poverty with progress in social integration is inadequate. In particular, employment and poverty reduction strategies still do not adequately target marginalized and vulnerable groups. Various social, political and economic forces have opposed improving social integration through employment, and have led to the marginalization of various segments of the labour force, especially the most vulnerable. Specific efforts should address this increasing marginalization to make the objectives of full employment and decent work attainable for all.

Ageing

• A report of the Secretary-General addresses Major Developments in the Area of Ageing since the Second World Assembly on Ageing (document E/ CN.5/2007/7). Progress in achieving "a society for all ages" has been uneven, the report says. Growing awareness of ageing is a clear sign of advances, but in many countries progress since 2002 in achieving the objectives of the Madrid Plan of Action appears rather slow.

• The report suggests that the Commission recommend to Governments to redouble their efforts to integrate older persons into the mainstream of development policies. Coordinated actions should seek to improve working conditions for older workers, dismantle employer barriers to hiring and retaining older workers, promote employability and provide better care services.

• The Commission should reiterate the importance of independent and impartial monitoring of progress in implementing the Madrid Plan of Action, and invite academia and civil society organizations to conduct studies, share their findings during the review and appraisal and formulate proposals for policy action.

• The first cycle of the review and appraisal of the Plan of Action will be concluded at the Commission's next session in 2008.

Disability

• A note of the Secretary-General on Monitoring the Implementation of the Standard Rules on the Equalization of Opportunities for Persons with Disabilities (document E/CN.5/2007/4) includes the annual report of the Special Rapporteur on Disabilities on that subject.

• A global survey on implementation of the 1993 Standard Rules, carried out in 1995-1996 by the office of the Special Rapporteur and the South-North Centre for Dialogue and Development, yielded the most comprehensive data yet on the actions taken by Member States. On the basis of the information provided by 114 Member States, the Special Rapporteur submits a number of recommendations to Governments.

• These include adopting policies and passing legislation to implement the measures identified by the Standard Rules; allocating financial resources to such programmes; intensifying awareness-raising efforts; enrolling the media in a campaign to change attitudes and behaviours; and ensuring that medical care, rehabilitation and assistive services are available in a way that enables full participation and equality to live independent lives.

• The Rapporteur also recommends treating all forms of accessibility – whether the built environment, transportation, information or communication – with the same importance; ensuring integrated education and employment; supporting organizations of persons with disabilities financially and technically; increasing regional and international cooperation, sharing of information and expertise, and signing and ratifying the 2006 Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 unemployment Total employment has risen since the mid-1990s, the report says, but problems of unemployment and underemployment have worsened Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that even though the total number of people working increased after the mid-1990s, the problems of people having no jobs (unemployment) or not having enough work (underemployment) became worse.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "unemployment" means the state of not having a job even though a person wants to work.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is found in the section titled "Employment." The passage states that total employment has increased since the middle of the 1990s, but issues related to "unemployment and underemployment" have gotten worse. In the summary sentence, "has grown" corresponds to "has risen," and "have become worse" corresponds to "have worsened." Therefore, "unemployment" correctly fills the blank.
Q28 underemployment Total employment has risen since the mid-1990s, the report says, but problems of unemployment and underemployment have worsened Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that even though the overall number of working people increased after the middle of the 1990s, the issues of people having no jobs (unemployment) and people having inadequate jobs (underemployment) became worse.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "underemployment" refers to a situation where people have jobs, but they do not work enough hours or their jobs do not fully use their skills and abilities.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "underemployment" because the passage directly connects it to unemployment as an issue that has become worse since the mid-1990s. The summary sentence states that "problems of [blank] and [blank] have become worse," which paraphrases the passage's statement that "problems of unemployment and underemployment have worsened." Here, "risen" matches "grown," and "worsened" matches "become worse."
Q29 needs However, the report notes, Governments have sought to improve the labour market opportunities of women and youth. The inclusion of persons with disabilities has also gained impetus and Governments are beginning to enact policies and programmes to address the needs of indigenous peoples Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that governments are trying to help women and young people find work, while also starting new plans and rules to help meet the essential requirements (needs) of native or indigenous populations.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "needs" refers to the essential requirements, problems, or things that indigenous groups of people require help with.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "needs" because the sentence in the summary discusses government efforts concerning different groups of people: women, youth, and indigenous peoples. In the text, it states that governments want to improve work opportunities for women and youth, and they are starting to create policies and programmes to "address the needs of indigenous peoples." The summary matches this directly with the phrase "to address the of indigenous peoples."
Q30 precarious employment Where State-supported social protection systems exist, they have not been modified to accommodate the growth of precarious employment Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that insecure work (precarious employment) is growing, but government protection systems have not changed to support workers in these kinds of jobs.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to unstable and insecure types of work where workers often have lower pay, fewer protections, and little job safety.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the paragraph discussing how various trends affect the workplace. The text mentions that traditional standard jobs are decreasing, while insecure or unstable forms of work—referred to as "precarious forms of employment" and "precarious employment"—are increasing and taking over.
Q31 decent work To address this situation, says the report, the goals of full and productive employment and decent work for all should become central in national and international policies and national development strategies Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that to fix the current job problems, policies worldwide must focus mainly on two goals: helping everyone find productive jobs and ensuring good, fair work for all.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to good, safe jobs that treat workers fairly and respect their rights.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is confirmed by the section discussing solutions to current employment problems. The passage states that to address the situation, the goals of 'full and productive employment' and 'decent work for all' should be central to national and international policies. The summary replaces 'address' with 'alleviate' and 'become central' with 'be the main concern', meaning 'decent work' directly fills the blank.
Q32 TRUE Women's earnings are substantially lower than men's throughout the world Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that all across the globe, the money women earn from work is much less than the money men earn.
Answer Explanation:
The answer TRUE means the statement matches what is stated in the reading text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage states that women earn much less than men globally. Saying that "Women's earnings are substantially lower than men's throughout the world" directly means that men earn considerably more than women across the globe. Key matching words include "substantially" (which means "considerably"), "throughout the world" (which means "around the world"), and "earnings" (money earned from working).
Q33 NOT GIVEN Persons with disabilities are disproportionately unemployed: in some countries, as many as 80 per cent of them are without work Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that in certain countries up to 80 percent of disabled people have no jobs, but it never mentions whether any of those places are in Asia.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether the statement is true or false because the specific information is missing.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage mentions that "in some countries, as many as 80 per cent of them are without work", but it does not specify which countries these are. It never states whether these places are Asian countries. Because the passage does not provide this specific geographical detail, we cannot confirm or deny the statement.
Q34 FALSE Migration, which is becoming the coping mechanism for indigenous populations, increases their vulnerability, because migrants often lack the skills required to operate in the formal economy and are often discriminated against Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that moving to other places (migration) makes indigenous people more vulnerable, partly because these migrants frequently face unfair treatment (discrimination).
Answer Explanation:
The answer "FALSE" means the statement directly contradicts or goes against what is written in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage explicitly says that migrants are "often discriminated against." The statement claims that migrants are "always treated equally," which contradicts the text because being discriminated against is the direct opposite of being treated equally.
Q35 TRUE Active labour market policies (ALMPs) should facilitate adjustment to changes brought about by trade liberalization and globalization. These include retraining for displaced workers, job search assistance and other measures to facilitate labour mobility Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that active labour market policies (ALMPs) help workers adjust to changes, and these policies include training again for workers who lost their jobs, helping them look for new jobs, and other actions to help them move to new work.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is TRUE because the passage confirms that ALMPs provide training for people who lost their jobs as well as help with looking for work.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage states that active labour market policies (ALMPs) include "retraining for displaced workers" and "job search assistance." The phrase "displaced workers" refers to people who have lost their jobs, which means the exact same thing as "laid off workers." Therefore, the statement completely matches the information in the text.
Q36 B On the basis of the information provided by 114 Member States, the Special Rapporteur submits a number of recommendations to Governments
The Rapporteur also recommends treating all forms of accessibility – whether the built environment, transportation, information or communication – with the same importance; ensuring integrated education and employment; supporting organizations of persons with disabilities financially and technically; increasing regional and international cooperation, sharing of information and expertise, and signing and ratifying the 2006 Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities		 Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that the Special Rapporteur presents advice (recommendations) to Governments. Among these recommendations is making sure that education and jobs are integrated for people with disabilities.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means that the statement describes recommendations found in the annual report written by the Special Rapporteur on Disabilities.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage explains in the "Disability" section that the Special Rapporteur gives several recommendations to Governments based on a global survey. One of the specific recommendations listed is "ensuring integrated education and employment". This connects directly to the annual report of the Special Rapporteur on Disabilities.
Q37 A The Plan is the first international agreement that specifically recognizes the potential of older people to contribute to the development of their societies, and commits Governments to include ageing in all social and economic development policies Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that this specific plan is the first worldwide agreement that officially sees and accepts that elderly people have the ability to help their communities grow and succeed.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "A" means that the Madrid International Plan of Action on Ageing is the document that clearly acknowledges how older individuals can help improve society.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the text clearly states that the Madrid International Plan of Action on Ageing is the agreement that "specifically recognizes the potential of older people to contribute to the development of their societies." In the question, "particularly identifies" matches "specifically recognizes," and "older people's contribution to the social development" corresponds directly to "older people to contribute to the development of their societies."
Q38 C Offshoring, which can have a considerable impact on the labour market of developing countries, should be better regulated to arrest a possible "race to the bottom" in labour standards Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that offshoring should have better rules to prevent ("arrest") worker conditions and rules from falling to very low levels. This applies to offshoring regulation, not to the ageing plan or the disability report.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "C" means that this statement is not connected to either the Madrid International Plan of Action on Ageing (A) or the annual report on Disabilities (B).
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the passage discusses preventing a "race to the bottom" in labour standards within the "Employment" section, specifically regarding the regulation of "offshoring". The keyword "arrest" in the text matches the word "stop" in the statement. Since this recommendation is about regulating offshoring rather than the Madrid International Plan of Action on Ageing or the report of the Special Rapporteur on Disabilities, the correct choice is "neither A nor B".
Q39 A The first cycle of the review and appraisal of the Plan of Action will be concluded at the Commission's next session in 2008 Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the first stage of checking and assessing the progress of the Plan of Action will finish at the meeting in 2008.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'A' means that the statement describes the Madrid International Plan of Action on Ageing.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the section on 'Ageing' discusses the Madrid Plan of Action and explicitly states that the first round of reviewing and evaluating it would finish in 2008. In the text, the phrase 'first cycle of the review and appraisal of the Plan of Action' matches 'first cycle of review and appraisal', and 'in 2008' directly matches the year in the question.
Q40 B On the basis of the information provided by 114 Member States, the Special Rapporteur submits a number of recommendations to Governments Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage clearly states that the Special Rapporteur used the details and facts given by 114 Member States to create recommendations for governments.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means that the statement describes the annual report created by the Special Rapporteur on Disabilities.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the text under the heading 'Disability' explains that the Special Rapporteur made recommendations based on data received from 114 countries (Member States). This work is included in the annual report of the Special Rapporteur on Disabilities.
