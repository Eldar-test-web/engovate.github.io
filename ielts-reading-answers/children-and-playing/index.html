Children And Playing - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From IELTS Mock Tests Academic Reading Test 78 · Part 3 · Questions 27–40

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 27-40 which are based on Reading Passage 3 below.

Children and Playing

Paragraph A

Childhood is in every sense a preparation for adulthood, both in terms of physical and mental maturation. It is an essential stepping stone that allows all young people to adjust optimally to the requirements that collective expectations and rules set for any adult individual, to both integrate, exploit and enjoy living within society. By definition, individuals who fail to meet these criteria are classified as anti-social or in some way incapable of coping with societal expectations and therefore unlikely to experience a positive quality of life. Aspects of human brain maturation continue from birth until into the late teens. During this long developmental period, a child will learn from interactions with parents, siblings, relations, peers and teachers, and will have both positive and negative emotional experiences due to these and other life events. Not only is this a phase of intense adaptation and information gathering, but the process of maturation is organised so as to structure it progressively in the most effective way. Of course, a child is strongly influenced by being taught by other individuals, but each child also has at their disposal a highly effective, self-help tuition kit that is present for all children. This is play.

Paragraph B

Play is a fundamental behaviour of juveniles in all species of birds and mammals. While adults can also show it, although at a much-reduced frequency, they often give the impression that it is an attractive, but wasteful expression of leisure that only the young with minimal responsibilities can indulge in. In truth, it is this play of youth that prepares an individual in an important variety of ways for adulthood. While the building blocks for brain development are created before birth, the brain is only about 25 per cent complete at birth. The brain maturation process continues during the first two decades of life, although of course, brain networks can continue to adapt in the light of experience throughout the whole of an individual's life. The types of play that humans exhibit change during the various maturational stages of the brain.

Paragraph C

Between birth and year one, play during this period involves practice of the movement and sensory systems with interest in patterns, colours and sounds, exploring textures, grasping objects and performing simple actions with them. Play is highly repetitive, like a form of practice. Over the next two years or so, examples of pretend play are seen, indicating that individuals are capable of symbolic thought. The brain's specialised language production and comprehension regions develop, as language communication expands rapidly. Language is also played with in rhymes and songs and 'silly' sounds, and pretend characters can talk, express emotions and perform actions. Between ages three and eight, play becomes more elaborate and extensive. Children engage in complex theme play involving roles, scripts and costumes. By the age of six, games with rules become a pervasive play activity, as well as rule negotiations and discussion of "fairness". Both co-operation and competition occur in these games, as well as in spontaneously invented ones. Through this kind of play, children demonstrate extensive symbolic thought capacities and the ability to self-regulate and explain their own behaviour. From ages eight to fourteen, play becomes increasingly sophisticated and symbolic, with pretend themes often carrying on for months. Symbolic board and computer games are also popular. At this stage, many children will have been introduced to adult-controlled more organised games, such as sport, which require greater control of motor, social and cognitive processes.

Paragraph D

While young animals of most species show mainly relatively simple forms of play up to the age of puberty, it is notable that the species that tend to have the most complex life styles engage in play the most and with the most sophisticated repertoire. This naturally includes non-human primates and particularly the great apes, where quite complex forms of social and object play are seen. Dolphins are also notable in the range and complexity of the games they play. It has been claimed that captive bottle nosed dolphins can show 317 distinct forms of play behaviour. One of these is blowing bubbles and catching them in the mouth before they reach the surface. To add interest and competition, they can apparently make the task more and more difficult by releasing the bubbles closer and closer to the surface or modifying swimming style while the bubbles are released.

Paragraph E

Amongst mammals other than humans, it is the species that rely on their group hunting skills that show the greatest amount of play behaviour as juveniles. Hunting species also seem happy to play with objects, presumably because they represent practice for catching and manipulating prey. Apart from developing sensorimotor dexterity, these play interactions also help to sort out dominance relationships and promote social alliances and cohesion in the group, though not with outsiders to the group. Play for all species is a key adaptive tool for survival and therefore, like all-important behaviour, its expression is linked to brain dopamine reward systems.

Paragraph F

Play deprivation can have a significant effect in humans. Children who have been deprived of play are likely to have been neglected in many other ways. The effects can include an inability to make friends and socialise, leading to an isolated childhood and adolescence, which in turn can lead to a lonely and isolated adulthood, with individuals unable to understand their own or others' emotions. A childhood without adequate play experiences can lead to a lack of physical skills and abilities, which often leads to obesity and poor health, and a lack of confidence in one's own abilities.

Questions

Questions 27–34 Matching Information

The text has 6 paragraphs A - F.

Which paragraph contains the following information?

27 The earliest forms of human play are often very repetitive.
28 Some animals consciously like making their play games more challenging.
29 A newborn baby's brain is only about a quarter developed.
30 Children will often start to engage in role-playing after the age of three.
31 Children who miss out on adequate play can experience emotional problems.
32 People who fail to adapt to social expectations of behavior can be seen as being anti-social.
33 The animals that have the most complex lives also have the more complex play routines.
34 Animals that hunt like to play with objects.

Questions 35–39 One Choice

Choose the correct letter A, B, C or D.

35 Children's interaction with parents is
  1. initially better experienced with both parents present.
  2. not always a positive experience.
  3. never to be avoided.
  4. always a positive experience.
36 Adults often display that
  1. play is a waste of time for them.
  2. play is still an everyday part of their lives.
  3. play is something to do with their children.
  4. play is something that should be done with increased frequency.
37 When children first start to play games with rules,
  1. they develop the ability to cheat.
  2. they develop the ability to plan ahead.
  3. they develop the ability to justify their actions.
  4. they develop the ability to choose teams.
38 Play routines for hunting animals do not help them
  1. develop the ability to chase prey.
  2. develop social relationships with animals outside their group.
  3. develop social relationships within their group.
  4. develop coordination.
39 Children deprived of play are liable to
  1. be entrepreneurs.
  2. have older siblings.
  3. be smaller.
  4. have often suffered some form of neglect.

Questions 40–40 One Choice

Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D.

40 What is the writer's purpose in Reading Passage 3?
  1. To explain the problems faced by children who do not experience enough play.
  2. To analyse the different types of play games experienced by humans and animals.
  3. To assess the role and importance of play in humans and animals.
  4. To discuss the role of the child psychiatrist in recommending different play games for children.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 C Between birth and year one, play during this period involves practice of the movement and sensory systems with interest in patterns, colours and sounds, exploring textures, grasping objects and performing simple actions with them. Play is highly repetitive, like a form of practice Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that from birth to one year of age, babies play by testing their senses and movements, and this early kind of play is repeated many times over.
Answer Explanation:
The answer C means that Paragraph C is the paragraph that mentions that the first types of play in a human's life repeat the same actions many times.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is Paragraph C because it explains how babies play during their very first year of life (from birth to age one). The author states that during this earliest stage, play involves repeating simple actions again and again, describing it directly with the words "highly repetitive".
Q28 D To add interest and competition, they can apparently make the task more and more difficult by releasing the bubbles closer and closer to the surface or modifying swimming style while the bubbles are released Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that dolphins purposefully make their bubble-catching game harder by letting bubbles out closer to the top of the water or changing how they swim, just to make the game more exciting and challenging.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "D" means that Paragraph D is the section of the reading passage that mentions animals intentionally making their games harder.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is Paragraph D because it describes how dolphins play games with bubbles. To make the game more interesting and competitive, dolphins purposefully make the activity harder by blowing bubbles closer to the water's surface or changing how they swim. This directly matches the statement that some animals intentionally make their games more challenging ("make the task more and more difficult").
Q29 B the brain is only about 25 per cent complete at birth Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that when an infant is born, its brain is only about one-fourth (25%) finished growing.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means that Paragraph B contains the information about how much of a baby's brain is formed when it is born.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because Paragraph B clearly mentions that when a baby is born, its brain is only twenty-five percent finished. In this context, "at birth" refers to a "newborn baby", "25 per cent" means "a quarter", and "complete" means "developed".
Q30 C Between ages three and eight, play becomes more elaborate and extensive. Children engage in complex theme play involving roles, scripts and costumes Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when children are between three and eight years old, their games become more detailed, and they begin playing pretend games where they act out different characters and parts.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Paragraph C includes the information that children usually begin taking part in pretend play with characters and roles after they turn three years old.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Paragraph C outlines how children's play changes as they grow older. It specifically points out that from age three to eight, kids start doing pretend activities that involve acting out parts. In the text, the phrase "Between ages three and eight" matches "after the age of three", and "theme play involving roles, scripts and costumes" corresponds directly to "role-playing".
Q31 F Children who have been deprived of play are likely to have been neglected in many other ways. The effects can include an inability to make friends and socialise, leading to an isolated childhood and adolescence, which in turn can lead to a lonely and isolated adulthood, with individuals unable to understand their own or others' emotions Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when children do not get the chance to play, they can struggle to make friends and end up feeling lonely and unable to understand feelings, both during childhood and later as adults.
Answer Explanation:
The answer F means that Paragraph F contains information about how not playing enough can cause emotional difficulties for children.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is Paragraph F because it discusses what happens when children do not get enough play. The text mentions that "play deprivation" (missing out on play) can lead to children being lonely, isolated, and "unable to understand their own or other's emotions," as well as having a "lack of confidence." These are clear examples of emotional problems.
Q32 A By definition, individuals who fail to meet these criteria are classified as anti-social or in some way incapable of coping with societal expectations and therefore unlikely to experience a positive quality of life Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that people who do not fit into society's rules are called anti-social and struggle to deal with what society expects from them.
Answer Explanation:
The answer choice "A" means that Paragraph A mentions that people who cannot follow society's rules and expectations are described as anti-social.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is Paragraph A because it directly talks about people who cannot meet the shared rules and expectations of society. In Paragraph A, the text states that people who fail to follow these standards are considered "anti-social" and unable to handle what society expects from them. Key words like "expectations" and "anti-social" directly match the question.
Q33 D it is notable that the species that tend to have the most complex life styles engage in play the most and with the most sophisticated repertoire Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that animals with complicated lifestyles play the most and perform the most advanced and varied play activities.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "D" means that Paragraph D has the information mentioned in the question.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the paragraph directly explains that animal species with the most complex ways of living play the most and have the most advanced and varied types of play. The question's words "animals that have the most complex lives" match "the species that tend to have the most complex life styles", and "more complex play routines" matches "most sophisticated repertoire".
Q34 E Hunting species also seem happy to play with objects, presumably because they represent practice for catching and manipulating prey Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that animals that hunt for food enjoy playing with items, likely because this play helps them practice how to catch and handle their food.
Answer Explanation:
The answer E means that Paragraph E is the section of the text that mentions that hunting animals enjoy playing with things.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because Paragraph E clearly states that animals that hunt like to play with objects because it helps them practice catching prey. In this paragraph, the text uses the phrase "Hunting species" to mean "Animals that hunt", and "seem happy to" to mean they "like to".
Q35 B During this long developmental period, a child will learn from interactions with parents, siblings, relations, peers and teachers, and will have both positive and negative emotional experiences due to these and other life events Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that while growing up, a child interacts with parents and other people, which brings both happy (positive) and unhappy (negative) feelings.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that spending time or communicating with parents can bring good feelings, but it can also bring bad feelings sometimes.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage says that a child experiences both "positive and negative" emotions when interacting with parents and others. Since the experiences can be negative, it means they are not always positive. The other choices are incorrect because the passage never mentions that both parents must be present, or that these interactions should never be avoided, and option D directly contradicts the fact that there are negative experiences.
Q36 A While adults can also show it, although at a much-reduced frequency, they often give the impression that it is an attractive, but wasteful expression of leisure that only the young with minimal responsibilities can indulge in Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that although grown-ups can play, they do it much less often and usually act like playing is a useless waste of free time that only young people without adult duties should do.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that grown-up people often show or think that playing wastes their time.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because Paragraph B explains what adults think about play. The text states that adults "often give the impression that it is an attractive, but wasteful expression of leisure that only the young with minimal responsibilities can indulge in." In this context, "give the impression that" corresponds to "display that", and "wasteful expression of leisure" matches "a waste of time for them" since adults have responsibilities and do not feel they should spend time playing.
Q37 C By the age of six, games with rules become a pervasive play activity, as well as rule negotiations and discussion of "fairness". Both co-operation and competition occur in these games, as well as in spontaneously invented ones. Through this kind of play, children demonstrate extensive symbolic thought capacities and the ability to self-regulate and explain their own behaviour Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that around age six, children start playing games that follow rules. By taking part in these games, children learn to control themselves and explain why they act the way they do.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that when children begin playing games that have set rules, they gain the ability to give good reasons for what they do.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Paragraph C explains what happens when children reach about six years of age and play "games with rules". The passage states that playing these games helps children develop the ability to "explain their own behaviour". In the question, "justify their actions" has the exact same meaning as "explain their own behaviour". The text does not mention cheating, planning ahead, or choosing teams in this context.
Q38 B Apart from developing sensorimotor dexterity, these play interactions also help to sort out dominance relationships and promote social alliances and cohesion in the group, though not with outsiders to the group Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that play helps young animals practice movement, decide who is in charge, and become friendly and close with members inside their group, but it does not do this with animals outside their group.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that playing does not help hunting animals build friendly relationships with animals that belong to other groups.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because Paragraph E discusses the benefits of play for hunting animals. The text states that playing helps them practice catching prey, develop physical movement and coordination ("sensorimotor dexterity"), and build strong bonds ("social alliances and cohesion") within their own group. However, the text specifically adds "though not with outsiders to the group," meaning play does not help them build relationships with animals outside their group.
Q39 D Children who have been deprived of play are likely to have been neglected in many other ways Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when children are prevented from playing, it is very common that people have also failed to take proper care of them in other aspects.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that children who do not get the chance to play are likely to have experienced a lack of care or attention.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the text directly states that children who do not have enough play are likely to experience neglect in other areas of life. In the text, the phrase "likely to have been neglected in many other ways" matches the phrase "are liable to have often suffered some form of neglect" in the question and answer choices.
Q40 C Play for all species is a key adaptive tool for survival and therefore, like all-important behaviour, its expression is linked to brain dopamine reward systems Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that playing is not just for fun; it is an essential tool that helps all living creatures learn how to survive and thrive.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the author wrote the article to examine why playing is necessary and how it helps both people and animals.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because throughout the passage, the author examines what play does for development and survival in both human children (Paragraphs A, B, C, and F) and various animal species (Paragraphs B, D, and E). While other choices only mention a single aspect—such as the negative effects of not playing (Paragraph F) or lists of games—the overarching purpose of the entire text is to evaluate the vital functions ("role") and necessity ("importance") of play across species.
