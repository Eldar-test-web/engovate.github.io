Antibiotic Resistance - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From IELTS Mock Tests Academic Reading Test 77 · Part 3 · Questions 27–40

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 27-40 which are based on Reading Passage 3 below.

Antibiotic Resistance

An antibiotic is a drug that kills or prevents the growth of bacteria. The term 'antibiotic', coined by Selman Waksman, originally described only those antibiotics derived from living bodies, in contrast to 'chemotherapeutic agents', which are purely synthetic. Currently, the term antibiotic is also applied to synthetic antimicrobials.

Overuse or misuse of antibiotics may result in the development of antibiotic resistance in the infecting organisms, similar to the development of pesticide resistance in insects. Doctor Iain Nicholson explains. "The concept of genetic selection requires that as close as possible to 100 per cent of the infecting organisms be killed off to avoid selection of resistance. If a small subset of the population survives the treatment and is allowed to multiply, the average susceptibility of this new population to the compound will be much less than that of the original population, since they have descended from those few organisms that survived the original treatment."

Antibiotic resistance has become a serious problem in both developed and underdeveloped nations. In certain settings, such as hospitals, the rate of antibiotic resistance is so high today that the normal, low-cost antibiotics are virtually useless for the treatment of frequently seen infections. This leads to more common use of newer and more expensive compounds, which in turn leads to the rise of resistance to those drugs. Drug company spokesperson, Emma Thompson, explains the challenge facing the drug industry. "A continuous race to discover new and different antibiotics results in an attempt to keep humanity from losing ground in the battle against infection. The fear is that we will eventually fail to keep up in this race, and that people may again face life-threatening bacterial infections."

An example of antibiotic resistance is Staphylococcus aureus, which used to be treated successfully with penicillin in the 1940's and 1950's. At present, nearly all strains are resistant to penicillin, and many are resistant to other antibiotics, leaving only a narrow selection of drugs useful for treatment. The situation is worsened by the fact that gene coding for antibiotic resistance can be transferred between bacteria, making it possible for bacteria never exposed to an antibiotic to acquire resistance from those which have.

Microbial resistance to antibiotics is a natural consequence of selective pressures placed on bacteria. However, humans have greatly accelerated the evolution of resistant bacteria by the overuse and misuse of antibiotics in the community. Government health official, Georgina Haynes, explains. “One of the major problems is when antibiotics are used to treat disorders for which they have no efficacy, such as the common cold or other viral complaints, and when they are used widely as prophylaxis rather than treatment, because this exposes more bacteria to selection for resistance." Similarly, in order to kill all the bacteria of an infection, antibiotics must be used for a whole course and not stopped just when symptoms improve. The discoverer of penicillin himself, Alexander Fleming, warned the scientific community: "The administration of too small doses leads to the production of resistant strains of bacteria." This can happen due to patient ignorance and to improper pharmacy actions, usually in the developing world. In developing countries, antibiotic prescriptions are often broken up. For example, in some Asian countries, it is common for pharmacies to sell as many tablets as the patient can afford, which may only be two or three. This is an insufficient dose to cure infection and will only further contribute to the evolution of resistant strains of bacteria. Recent reports have also shown that containers of medicine from some African countries sometimes contain only half the drug content that was indicated on the label. This may be due to 'counterfeit' medicines or improper storage, but also tropical conditions, such as those often found in Africa, readily cause degradation of medical compounds. Chenbo Okonkwa, a pharmaceutical wholesaler, describes the problem. “Although local regulations may require pharmacies to store drugs in air-conditioned premises, most undergo frequent power outages and warehouses are rarely kept cool. Furthermore, unauthorised dealers rarely bother to follow official storage guidelines.”

Another problem contributing to antibiotic resistance is that antibiotics are widely used in food-producing animals, which contributes to the emergence of antibiotic-resistant bacteria in them. These resistant bacteria can contaminate the foods that come from these animals, and persons who consume these foods can develop antibiotic-resistant infections. This can be seen, for instance, in the fish farm industry. It is common to put upwards of 100,000 fish into each pen, which creates cramped and stressful conditions. This increases the susceptibility of fish to certain bacterial diseases, such as furunculosis. Furunculosis, an infection of the kidneys, is easily transmitted through water and so is devastating to fish farms. To combat the disease, antibiotics were added to fish food pellets, but these were unpalatable to the fish. They were already suffering from loss of appetite due to their infections and so most of the antibiotics ended up at the bottom of the pens, allowing residual drugs to spread throughout the marine environment. Fish farmer, Jo Hardwick, explains that this is different now. “Today, vaccines are replacing the antibiotic pellets as a treatment for the disease, but unfortunately, most of the damage has already been done."

In conclusion, antibiotics must be used judiciously in humans and animals, because both uses contribute to the emergence, persistence, and spread of resistant bacteria. Resistant bacteria in food-producing animals are of particular concern. Food animals serve as a reservoir of resistant pathogens and resistance mechanisms that can directly or indirectly result in antibiotic resistant infections in humans. If people do not wish to be faced with an attack of all the diseases they thought they had conquered in the twentieth century, society has to change its approach to antibiotic use.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 AF The discoverer of penicillin himself, Alexander Fleming, warned the scientific community: "The administration of too small doses leads to the production of resistant strains of bacteria." Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Alexander Fleming warned scientists that giving doses of medicine that are too small will create bacteria that medicines cannot kill.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "AF" stands for Alexander Fleming.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is AF because the text directly quotes Alexander Fleming stating that giving medicine in amounts that are too small causes resistant bacteria to develop. In the text, the phrase "administration of too small doses" means giving or prescribing not enough medicine, and "leads to the production of resistant strains of bacteria" matches the idea that resistant bacteria can develop.
Q28 ET / aet Drug company spokesperson, Emma Thompson, explains the challenge facing the drug industry. "A continuous race to discover new and different antibiotics results in an attempt to keep humanity from losing ground in the battle against infection. The fear is that we will eventually fail to keep up in this race, and that people may again face life-threatening bacterial infections." Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Emma Thompson is afraid medicine might not keep up with resistant bacteria, which could cause people to suffer from dangerous bacterial illnesses all over again.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "ET" refers to Emma Thompson.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is ET because Emma Thompson expresses the worry that if new antibiotics cannot be developed quickly enough, people might once again suffer from dangerous bacterial infections. In her words, she talks about the risk that "people may again face life-threatening bacterial infections," which directly matches the statement about society having to deal with infectious diseases that were once treatable.
Q29 GH Government health official, Georgina Haynes, explains. “One of the major problems is when antibiotics are used to treat disorders for which they have no efficacy, such as the common cold or other viral complaints, and when they are used widely as prophylaxis rather than treatment, because this exposes more bacteria to selection for resistance." Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Georgina Haynes identifies taking antibiotics to prevent illness (prophylaxis) rather than treat an active bacterial sickness as a major cause of antibiotic resistance.
Answer Explanation:
The answer GH refers to Georgina Haynes, the person who made this statement.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is GH because Georgina Haynes points out that using antibiotics widely as "prophylaxis" (which means as a preventative treatment) instead of as an actual cure is one of the main reasons bacteria become resistant. In the text, "used widely as prophylaxis" matches the question's phrase "preventative measure", and "major problems" matches "a key factor".
Q30 JH Fish farmer, Jo Hardwick, explains that this is different now. “Today, vaccines are replacing the antibiotic pellets as a treatment for the disease, but unfortunately, most of the damage has already been done." Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that fish farmer Jo Hardwick says farms now use vaccines instead of antibiotic food pellets, but the bad effects and damage to the environment have already happened.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "JH" stands for Jo Hardwick, a fish farmer mentioned in the passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is JH because Jo Hardwick points out that although fish farms now use a better method (vaccines instead of antibiotic pellets) to fight disease, it is too late because "most of the damage has already been done." This matches the idea that better practices came too late to stop the harm caused by antibiotics spreading into the surrounding environment.
Q31 ET / aet Drug company spokesperson, Emma Thompson, explains the challenge facing the drug industry. "A continuous race to discover new and different antibiotics results in an attempt to keep humanity from losing ground in the battle against infection. The fear is that we will eventually fail to keep up in this race, and that people may again face life-threatening bacterial infections." Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Emma Thompson fears medicine companies will one day fall behind in finding new antibiotics, which could leave people in danger from deadly bacterial illnesses.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "ET" refers to Emma Thompson, the person who made the statement.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is ET because in paragraph 3, Emma Thompson, a representative for a drug company, discusses the difficulty the pharmaceutical industry faces. She worries that medicine makers will not be able to continually find new drugs in this ongoing race, warning that "we will eventually fail to keep up" and that people will suffer from "life-threatening" (lethal) bacterial infections again.
Q32 IN Doctor Iain Nicholson explains. "The concept of genetic selection requires that as close as possible to 100 per cent of the infecting organisms be killed off to avoid selection of resistance Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that, according to Doctor Iain Nicholson, almost 100 percent of the bacteria causing the illness must be eliminated so that resistant bacteria do not develop.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "IN" refers to Doctor Iain Nicholson, who stated that almost all the bacteria causing an infection must be killed to prevent them from becoming resistant.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is IN because Doctor Iain Nicholson explains that nearly "100 per cent of the infecting organisms" must be killed to avoid the selection of resistance. The statement's phrase "All a patient's infection must be destroyed" directly matches Nicholson's explanation that "close as possible to 100 per cent of the infecting organisms be killed off", and "in order to stop resistance growing" matches "to avoid selection of resistance".
Q33 CO Furthermore, unauthorised dealers rarely bother to follow official storage guidelines Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that sellers who do not have legal permission to sell drugs almost never care to obey the formal rules on how medicines should be stored.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "CO" refers to Chenbo Okonkwa, who stated that illegal sellers of medicines almost never follow the proper rules for storing antibiotics.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is CO because in paragraph 5, Chenbo Okonkwa specifically mentions the behavior of illegal medicine sellers regarding storage rules. The statement matches his words through clear synonyms: "Illicit antibiotic salesmen" corresponds to "unauthorised dealers," "hardly ever follow" corresponds to "rarely bother to follow," and "instructions for keeping antibiotics in the right way" corresponds to "official storage guidelines."
Q34 FALSE Currently, the term antibiotic is also applied to synthetic antimicrobials Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage clearly states that today, the word antibiotic is also used for synthetic (artificially made) drugs, which proves that it is not limited only to drugs from living organisms.
Answer Explanation:
The answer FALSE means that the statement is not true because it contradicts the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage explains that while the word 'antibiotic' in the past referred only to medicines made from living organisms, today it is also used for man-made ('synthetic') medicines. Therefore, the current meaning does not only include drugs from living bodies. Key words to note are 'currently' (at present) and 'synthetic' (man-made, not from living organisms).
Q35 TRUE Antibiotic resistance has become a serious problem in both developed and underdeveloped nations Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that bacteria becoming resistant to antibiotics is a major, dangerous challenge in rich (developed) countries as well as in poorer (underdeveloped) countries.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means that the statement accurately reflects the facts described in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage clearly states that antibiotic resistance has grown into a major issue across different types of nations worldwide. In the text, the phrase "serious problem" matches the statement's "significant issue", and "developed and underdeveloped nations" corresponds to countries across the developed and developing world. Because the text confirms that this problem affects both wealthier and less wealthy nations, the statement is true.
Q36 NOT GIVEN In certain settings, such as hospitals, the rate of antibiotic resistance is so high today that the normal, low-cost antibiotics are virtually useless for the treatment of frequently seen infections. This leads to more common use of newer and more expensive compounds, which in turn leads to the rise of resistance to those drugs Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that cheap antibiotics often fail in hospitals, so hospitals use newer, more costly medicines. However, it does not state whether hospitals pay for the research to create these new drugs.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether hospitals have to pay for new antibiotic research on their own.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage mentions hospitals, but it never talks about them paying for or funding research. Paragraph 3 explains that in "hospitals", common antibiotics no longer work well, forcing them to use "newer and more expensive compounds". A drug company spokesperson then mentions the industry's race to find new antibiotics. However, there is no mention of who pays for this research or whether hospitals must "fund" it themselves.
Q37 TRUE Recent reports have also shown that containers of medicine from some African countries sometimes contain only half the drug content that was indicated on the label. This may be due to 'counterfeit' medicines or improper storage, but also tropical conditions, such as those often found in Africa, readily cause degradation of medical compounds Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that medicine bottles or packages in parts of Africa frequently lose their strength because the local tropical weather causes the drugs to break down and become damaged.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means that the statement accurately reflects what is written in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is confirmed by the text when it discusses medicine quality in Africa. The passage mentions that medicine containers in some African countries often have less medicine than stated on the label because tropical weather easily damages or breaks down medical drugs. In this context, "tropical conditions" means the hot climate, and "degradation of medical compounds" means damaging the medicine.
Q38 FALSE These resistant bacteria can contaminate the foods that come from these animals, and persons who consume these foods can develop antibiotic-resistant infections Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that dangerous bacteria can get onto food from animals, and people who eat this food can become sick with these bacteria.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE because the statement says the opposite of what the passage states.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the text directly states that resistant bacteria from animals can be on our food, and people who eat this food can get antibiotic-resistant infections. The statement claims that these bacteria 'cannot' be passed on to humans who eat the food, which contradicts the passage. Important words to notice are 'consume' (which means eat) and 'develop antibiotic-resistant infections' (which means getting the sickness caused by the resistant bacteria).
Q39 Fish food pellets / Fish pellets / food pellets / pellets / fish food / food To combat the disease, antibiotics were added to fish food pellets, but these were unpalatable to the fish Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that to fight the fish disease, people put antibiotics into fish food pellets, but the fish did not like eating them.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the antibiotics were added to fish food pellets, so the label is 'fish food pellets' or 'fish food'.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer comes from the words 'antibiotics were added to' in the passage. The thing the antibiotics were added to is 'fish food pellets'. The words 'unpalatable' and 'ended up at the bottom of the pens' also show that this is the item in the diagram.
Q40 appetite They were already suffering from loss of appetite due to their infections and so most of the antibiotics ended up at the bottom of the pens, allowing residual drugs to spread throughout the marine environment Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that the sick fish did not want to eat because of their illness, which caused the medicated food pellets to drop to the bottom of the fish enclosures unused.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "appetite" means the natural desire or feeling of wanting to eat food.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "appetite" because the passage discusses fish farms where sick fish did not want to eat the antibiotic food pellets. According to the text, the fish were already experiencing a "loss of appetite" because of bacterial infections such as furunculosis.
