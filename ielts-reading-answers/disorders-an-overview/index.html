Reading Passage

DISORDERS: AN OVERVIEW

Autistic Spectrum Disorder

Children with Autistic Spectrum Disorder have difficulty understanding what other people are saying, need help to play with other children, enjoy routines and find unfamiliar situations difficult. People with Autistic Spectrum Disorder can be good at creative activities like art, music and poetry. They can concentrate on one thing for a long time so they can become very good at something that they like doing.

ADHD - Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder

People with ADHD have three types of problems. Overactive behaviour (hyperactivity), impulsive behaviour and difficulty paying attention. Children with ADHD are not just very active but have a wide range of problem behaviours which can make them very difficult to care for and control. Those who have ADHD often find it difficult to fit in at school. They may also have problems getting on with other children. Some children have significant problems with concentration and attention, but are not necessarily overactive or impulsive. These children are sometimes described as having Attention Deficit Disorder (ADD) rather than ADHD. ADD can easily be missed because the child is quiet and dreamy rather than disruptive. ADHD is not related to intelligence. Children with all levels of ability can have ADHD.

Stress

Stress can be defined as the way you feel when you're under abnormal pressure. All sorts of situations can cause stress. The most common, however, involve work, money matters and relationships with partners, children or other family members. Stress may be caused either by major upheavals and life events such as divorce, unemployment, moving house and bereavement, or by a series of minor irritations such as feeling undervalued at work or dealing with difficult children. Some stress can be positive and research has suggested that a moderate level of stress makes us perform better. It also makes us more alert and can help us in challenging situations such as job interviews or public speaking. Stressful situations can also be exhilarating and some people actually thrive on the excitement that comes with dangerous sports or other 'high-risk' activities.

Schizophrenia

Schizophrenia is a diagnosis given to some people who have severely disrupted beliefs and experiences. During an episode of schizophrenia, a person's experience and interpretation of the outside world is disrupted - they may lose touch with reality, see or hear things that are not there and act in unusual ways in response to these 'hallucinations'. An episode of schizophrenia can last for several weeks and can be very frightening. The causes are unknown but episodes of schizophrenia appear to be associated with changes in some brain chemicals. Stressful experiences and some recreational drugs are sometimes thought to trigger an episode.

Depression

Depression describes a range of moods, from the low spirits that we all experience, to a severe problem that interferes with everyday life. The latter type, sometimes referred to as 'clinical depression', is defined as 'a persistent exaggeration of the everyday feelings that accompany sadness'. If you have severe depression you may experience low mood, loss of interest and pleasure as well as feelings of worthlessness and guilt. You may also experience tearfulness, poor concentration, reduced energy, reduced or increased appetite, changes in weight, sleep problems and anxiety. You may even feel that life is not worth living, and plan or attempt suicide.

Obsessive Compulsive Disorder in Adults

Imagine you are getting up in the morning. You know you will need to go to the bathroom, but the thought of accidentally touching the doorknob is frightening. There may be dangerous bacteria on it. Of course you cleaned the entire bathroom yesterday, including the usual series of spraying disinfectant, washing and rinsing. As usual it took a couple of hours to do it the right way. Even then you weren't sure whether you had missed an area, so you had to re-wash the floor. Naturally the doorknob was sprayed and rubbed three times with a bactericidal spray. Now the thought that you could have missed a spot on the doorknob makes you very nervous.

This description might give you some sense of the tormented and anxious world that people with Obsessive Compulsive Disorder (OCD) live in. It is a world filled with dangers from outside and from within. Often elaborate rituals and thoughts are used to ward off feared events, but no amount of mental or physical activity seems adequate, so doubt and anxiety are often present.

People who do not have OCD may perform behaviours in a ritualistic way, repeating, checking, or washing things out of habit or concern. Generally this is done without much, if any, worry. What distinguishes OCD as a psychiatric disorder is that the experience of obsessions, and the performance of rituals, reaches such an intensity or frequency that it causes significant psychological distress and interferes in a significant way with psychosocial functioning. The guideline of at least one hour spent on symptoms per day is often used as a measure of 'significant interference'. However, among patients who try to avoid situations that bring on anxiety and compulsions, the actual symptoms may not consume an hour. Yet the quantity of time lost from having to avoid objects or situations would clearly constitute interfering with functioning. Consider, for instance, a welfare mother who throws out more than $100 of groceries a week because of contamination fears. Although this behaviour has a major effect on her functioning, it might not consume one hour per day.

Patients with OCD describe their experience as having thoughts (obsessions) that they associate with some danger. The sufferer generally recognises that it is his or her own thoughts, rather than something imposed by someone else (as in some paranoid schizophrenic patients). However, the disturbing thoughts cannot be dismissed, and simply nag at the sufferer. Something must then be done to relieve the danger and mitigate the fear. This leads to actions and thoughts that are intended to neutralise the danger. These are the compulsions. Because these behaviours seem to give the otherwise 'helplessly anxious' person something to combat the danger, they are temporarily reassuring. However, since the 'danger' is typically irrational or imaginary, it simply returns, thereby triggering another cycle of the briefly reassuring compulsions. From the standpoint of classic conditioning, this pattern of painful obsession followed by temporarily reassuring compulsion eventually produces an intensely ingrained habit. It is rare to see obsessions without compulsions.

The two most common obsessions are fears of contamination and fear of harming oneself or others, while the two most common compulsions are checking and cleaning.

Questions 10–13 One Choice

Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D.

  1. Autistic Spectrum Disorder
  2. Stress
  3. Schizophrenia
  4. Depression

  1. ADHD
  2. Autistic Spectrum Disorder
  3. schizophrenia
  4. depression

  1. Autistic Spectrum Disorder
  2. ADHD
  3. Stress

  1. the fear of going outside
  2. the performance of rituals
  3. the desire to hurt others
  4. the feeling that they are helpless to ease their distress

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 A Stress can be defined as the way you feel when you're under abnormal pressure
Some stress can be positive and research has suggested that a moderate level of stress makes us perform better		 Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage clearly links stress to being under pressure, and notes that having a moderate amount of it can actually have positive effects on our performance.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is option A, which is Stress.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the text explains that stress occurs when a person is under pressure. The text also states that a small or moderate amount of stress can be good or helpful for us, which means it can be positive in small doses.
Q2 G This description might give you some sense of the tormented and anxious world that people with Obsessive Compulsive Disorder (OCD) live in. It is a world filled with dangers from outside and from within Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that people suffering from OCD live in a stressed and worried state where they constantly feel surrounded by dangers, both around them and inside their own minds.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "G" refers to Obsessive Compulsive Disorder (OCD).
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is G because the text describes the daily experience of people with Obsessive Compulsive Disorder (OCD) as living in a world full of danger from both outside and inside themselves. The passage explains that they constantly fear dangers and bad events, and because their rituals never fully remove these fears, worry and anxiety are regularly felt. Key phrases in the text include "world filled with dangers" and "associate with some danger," which directly match the idea of "feeling that there is danger constantly present."
Q3 D During an episode of schizophrenia, a person's experience and interpretation of the outside world is disrupted - they may lose touch with reality, see or hear things that are not there and act in unusual ways in response to these 'hallucinations' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when someone has an episode of schizophrenia, they can lose contact with real life and see or hear things that do not truly exist.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "D" means that Schizophrenia is the disorder where a person goes through things that might not exist in real life.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the text describes Schizophrenia as an illness where an individual's view of the world is changed. During this time, they "may lose touch with reality" and "see or hear things that are not there" (hallucinations), which directly matches having experiences that may not be part of the real world.
Q4 E People with ADHD have three types of problems. Overactive behaviour (hyperactivity), impulsive behaviour and difficulty paying attention
ADD can easily be missed because the child is quiet and dreamy rather than disruptive		 Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that individuals with ADHD are overly active and struggle to focus or pay attention. It also points out that while children with ADD are calm and quiet, children with ADHD act disruptively.
Answer Explanation:
The answer E refers to Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD).
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because the text explains that people with ADHD experience overactive behavior (which matches "active"), have trouble paying attention (which matches "losing concentration"), and behave in ways that cause trouble. When contrasting it with ADD, the text notes that ADD can be missed because the child is quiet rather than "disruptive," meaning that disruptive behavior is characteristic of ADHD.
Q5 B Children with Autistic Spectrum Disorder have difficulty understanding what other people are saying, need help to play with other children, enjoy routines and find unfamiliar situations difficult. People with Autistic Spectrum Disorder can be good at creative activities like art, music and poetry Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that young people with Autistic Spectrum Disorder find it hard to understand what others say to them, but they can be talented in creative areas such as art, music, and poetry.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B corresponds to Autistic Spectrum Disorder.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage directly links Autistic Spectrum Disorder to both communication difficulties and creative skills in art. The text notes that children with this disorder "have difficulty understanding what other people are saying" (which means having trouble communicating) and that they "can be good at creative activities like art" (which means being good at art). Therefore, statement matches Autistic Spectrum Disorder.
Q6 creative People with Autistic Spectrum Disorder can be good at creative activities like art, music and poetry Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that individuals who have Autistic Spectrum Disorder can perform very well in imaginative tasks such as art, music, and poetry.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "creative" means using imagination to make new and original things, especially in areas like art, music, or writing.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "creative" because the passage discusses people with Autistic Spectrum Disorder and states that they "can be good at creative activities". In the question, the phrase "May excel in" matches "can be good at", and "activities of a creative nature" matches "creative activities". Therefore, the missing word describing the nature of the activities is "creative".
Q7 quiet and dreamy ADD can easily be missed because the child is quiet and dreamy rather than disruptive Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Attention Deficit Disorder (ADD) might not be noticed right away because a child with this condition tends to stay silent and daydream instead of causing trouble or being noisy.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means being calm, not making much noise, and often lost in one's own thoughts or daydreams.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the paragraph on Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) and Attention Deficit Disorder (ADD). The table asks about what personality traits a person with Attention Deficit Disorder might show ("May appear ..."). The text notes that children with ADD can be easily overlooked because they are "quiet and dreamy" instead of disruptive. Therefore, "quiet and dreamy" fits the gap accurately.
Q8 unusual ways they may lose touch with reality, see or hear things that are not there and act in unusual ways in response to these 'hallucinations' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when someone has an episode of schizophrenia, they might see or hear things that do not exist, and as a result, they act or behave strangely.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "unusual ways" means strange, uncommon, or not normal types of behavior.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the paragraph on Schizophrenia. The text explains that during an episode, individuals may experience hallucinations and "act in unusual ways in response". In the question, the phrase "behaving in" corresponds directly to the synonym "act in", and "respond to" matches "in response to". Therefore, "unusual ways" correctly completes the blank describing how sufferers behave during these episodes.
Q9 suicide You may even feel that life is not worth living, and plan or attempt suicide Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that someone with severe depression might feel that life has no purpose or value, which can lead them to plan or try to end their own life.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "suicide" refers to the act of intentionally ending one's own life.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "suicide" because the passage discusses what can happen in cases of severe depression. The table refers to "feelings of futility that lead to thoughts of [suicide]", which directly matches the text where it states that a person may "feel that life is not worth living" (a feeling of futility) and then "plan or attempt suicide".
Q10 D You may also experience tearfulness, poor concentration, reduced energy, reduced or increased appetite, changes in weight, sleep problems and anxiety Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that severe depression can cause a person to gain or lose weight, which is a visible change in their physical appearance.
Answer Explanation:
The answer D means that depression is the illness that can lead to bodily changes that other people can see.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the section on Depression notes that a person with this condition may experience "changes in weight". Losing or gaining weight is a direct physical change that can be seen on a person's body. None of the other disorder descriptions in the passage mention changes in the physical body.
Q11 C An episode of schizophrenia can last for several weeks and can be very frightening Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that an episode of schizophrenia can continue for a few weeks, which shows that it lasts for a limited period.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "C" means that schizophrenia is the disorder where an episode continues for a certain amount of time.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the text specifically mentions that an episode of schizophrenia can go on for "several weeks." This indicates a limited duration of time for an episode. The other disorders mentioned in the choices do not state this specific timeframe for an episode.
Q12 C Stress may be caused either by major upheavals and life events such as divorce, unemployment, moving house and bereavement, or by a series of minor irritations such as feeling undervalued at work or dealing with difficult children Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that stress can happen because of big changes in life, including when someone you care about passes away (bereavement).
Answer Explanation:
The answer "C" means that stress is the condition that can be caused by the death of a loved one.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because under the section titled "Stress", the passage states that stress can be caused by big life events such as "bereavement". The word "bereavement" means the state of losing a loved one to death. Therefore, the death of a loved one can trigger stress.
Q13 B What distinguishes OCD as a psychiatric disorder is that the experience of obsessions, and the performance of rituals, reaches such an intensity or frequency that it causes significant psychological distress and interferes in a significant way with psychosocial functioning Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that OCD is identified by having unwanted thoughts and carrying out repeated actions (rituals) so frequently and strongly that it creates serious emotional pain and disrupts normal life.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that people who have Obsessive Compulsive Disorder (OCD) regularly do special, repeated actions or habits called rituals.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage directly states what defines or "distinguishes" OCD. It explains that doing repeated routines ("the performance of rituals") alongside unwanted thoughts ("obsessions") happens so strongly and often that it causes severe stress and disrupts daily life. Choice A is incorrect because the text does not say OCD sufferers fear going outside. Choice C is incorrect because they have a fear of hurting others, not a "desire" to do so. Choice D is incorrect because patients do things (compulsions) that give temporary relief.
