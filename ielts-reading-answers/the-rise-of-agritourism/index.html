The Rise Of Agritourism - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From IELTS Mock Tests Academic Reading Test 36 · Part 2 · Questions 14–26

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 14-26 which are based on Reading Passage 2 below.

The Rise of Agritourism

In advanced industrialised countries, small farmers have been challenged by changing economic and social conditions, such as increased global competition, falling commodity prices, and capital- and technology-intensive agricultural production. In addition, there has been added public pressure to make expensive changes in farming methods, due to public environmental concerns about industrialised agricultural production in combination with political pressures to reduce agricultural subsidies. These changing economic and social conditions have disproportionately impacted smaller farms in Europe and the US.

Agritourism is becoming an increasingly popular way for rural property owners to earn additional income from agricultural properties. In addition to more traditional farm tours and seasonal activities, such as hay rides, corn mazes and u-pick fruits, farm owners are devising new ways to bring people to their door by offering more entertainment-oriented activities. Some farmers are offering their barns as venues for weddings, parties, dances and other special events. Others are opening their homes to visitors for vacations, so guests can experience life on a working farm by helping out with routine farm chores, such as feeding or herding the livestock, milking the animals, making cheese, collecting eggs, picking vegetables and preparing farm fresh meals. Agritourism works in combination with a growing public desire to engage in rural experiences and outdoor recreational activities. By combining agriculture and tourism, agritourism offers these rural experiences to urban residents and economic diversification to farmers.

Part of the attraction of agritourism is the nostalgia it creates for a simpler time and its authenticity. Tourists are being sold, not only on beautiful sceneries and visual aesthetics, but also experiences that are meant to open up a new world for these customers who are tired of the hustle and bustle of city life. Authenticity has been an abiding theme in tourism studies and it may have a special meaning in this combination of agriculture and tourism. For one thing, the image of the family farm remains imbued with deep authenticity, the surviving representation of an old world ideal. To partake in agritourism is therefore likely to convey the sense of having a deeply authentic experience. Critics have claimed that this desire to reconnect with the life world of one’s ancestors may conflict with the nature of modern agriculture and whether the tourist will want to face its true realities. It seems therefore that often the most distinctive innovative effort involves the reinvention of tradition and rural tourism products. Examples are the recreation of home-produced products long since replaced by manufactured commodities and the provision of hands-on-experiences in crafts often recreated for tourists. As a result, some critics argue that the tourists who are running to the countryside are overcrowding and ruining the pristine beauty that they so desperately want to experience.

Agritourism can benefit the life and economy of local communities, as well as the farms themselves. Agritourism firstly means that some farms can continue in business and employ workers. Employment underscores the genuine importance of agritourism farms to local economies, as rural communities are usually areas that both have high unemployment and few alternatives for the unemployed to find work. Secondly, a significant number of agritourists come from areas reasonably local to the visited farms. This means that tourist spending on agritourism often stays in the region, helping to generate taxable revenues and more disposable incomes. The U.S. Department of Agriculture’s agricultural census, taken every five years, found that last year approximately 23,000 farms took part in agritourism.

These farms each earned $24,300 from agritourism, compared to five years ago, when farms engaged in this brought in only $7,200 per farm. The trend is clearly growing and the money generated will stimulate local economies. Thirdly, agritourism benefits the local community in terms of education. Many farms offer tours for elementary school-age children, who can learn where their food is coming from and how it is produced.

Farms choosing to develop agritourism have had reasonable government support. Over the last 20 years, European Union countries have spent 2 billion euros to subsidise agritourism development in rural farming areas that cannot compete in a global market with declining commodity prices. This, in turn, helps governments by keeping farmers on land, protecting picturesque rural landscapes that attract tourists, and supporting the production of regional agricultural products. As well as finance, local and national governments should create in the areas under their jurisdiction favourable environments for the development of agritourism, by changing regulatory and tax constraints, so that more farms are encouraged to enter the industry.

It is clear that there are strong economic and social benefits that agritourism can provide farmers, customers and the local areas where the farms are situated. Agritourism contributes to and enhances the quality of life in communities by expanding recreational opportunities, differentiating rural economies, and promoting the retention of agricultural lands. Working agricultural landscapes reflect the efforts of generations of farm families and often provide a defining sense of culture, heritage, and rural character. Agritourism provides educational opportunities for school children and adults to learn about this agrarian heritage, the production of food, and resource stewardship. Finally, many agritourism operations provide consumers with direct access to fresh farm goods. Agritourism is an industry with an enormous potential for growth. With it, farming could become more efficient and sustainable, rural areas could become more beautiful and farmers could become better off and more significant employers and contributors to economies.

Questions

Questions 14–19 One Choice

Choose the correct letter A, B, C or D.

14 Farmers today face demands from the public about
  1. reacting to global competition.
  2. improving workers' conditions.
  3. changing to more environmentally friendly production methods.
  4. making production cheaper.
15 Farmers today are experiencing pressure from governments, as the latter wishes to
  1. reduce the amount of money they provide to help farmers.
  2. increase taxes to gain more public income.
  3. force farmers to employ more workers.
  4. reduce the amount of pesticides used in agricultural production.
16 Farmers can attract tourists by
  1. undercutting the competition of traditional holidays on prices.
  2. being close to transport hubs.
  3. letting people get married on the farm.
  4. marketing on a door-by-door basis.
17 Farming authenticity partly depends on
  1. the beauty of the farms' surroundings.
  2. the public's traditional perceptions of a small farm.
  3. how the farm workers dress and behave.
  4. the type of livestock the farms have.
18 Farms can create authenticity by
  1. making tourist workers get up very early in the morning.
  2. having interactive displays of farm workings.
  3. not showing animals being killed.
  4. re-establishing old processes that are not usually used any more.
19 One criticism of agritourism is that
  1. farm workers lose their jobs.
  2. tourists change how farms operate.
  3. the extra numbers of people in the countryside spoil its appeal.
  4. only the farmer receives the extra income.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q14 C In addition, there has been added public pressure to make expensive changes in farming methods, due to public environmental concerns about industrialised agricultural production in combination with political pressures to reduce agricultural subsidies Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that people are putting pressure on farmers to change their farming practices because they care about and worry about the environment.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that people in society are asking farmers to use new ways of farming that do not harm nature and the environment.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the first paragraph explains that farmers face "public pressure" (demands from the public) to alter how they farm due to "public environmental concerns". In other words, people worry about how modern farming affects the earth, so they want farmers to switch to more eco-friendly methods. Choices A, B, and D are incorrect because the passage does not mention public demands regarding workers' conditions, cheaper production, or public pressure regarding global competition.
Q15 A In addition, there has been added public pressure to make expensive changes in farming methods, due to public environmental concerns about industrialised agricultural production in combination with political pressures to reduce agricultural subsidies Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that besides environmental worries from the public, there is political pressure to cut down on financial help (subsidies) given to agriculture.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that governments want to give less financial support to farmers.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because paragraph 1 mentions that farmers face "political pressures to reduce agricultural subsidies." Here, "political pressures" refers to pressure from governments, and "subsidies" are financial aids or money given by governments to support farmers. Therefore, the government wants to cut down or reduce the amount of money provided to help farmers.
Q16 C Some farmers are offering their barns as venues for weddings, parties, dances and other special events Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that some farm owners allow people to use their farm buildings (barns) to hold weddings and other celebrations.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that farm owners can bring visitors to their land by allowing them to hold wedding ceremonies and celebrations there.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the text explains how farmers find new ways to attract people for entertainment activities. It directly mentions that some farmers provide their barns as places for "weddings", which means letting people get married on the farm. None of the other choices (cheaper prices than regular holidays, nearby transport stations, or going door-to-door to advertise) are mentioned in the text.
Q17 B For one thing, the image of the family farm remains imbued with deep authenticity, the surviving representation of an old world ideal Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that people strongly connect realness and authenticity with the mental picture of a family farm because it symbolizes an ideal, traditional way of life from the past.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that people feel a farm is authentic and real partly because of how they imagine a traditional, old-fashioned family farm.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the third paragraph discusses farm authenticity. It explains that the feeling of authenticity comes from "the image of the family farm", which represents an "old world ideal". Here, the passage's phrase "image of the family farm" directly corresponds to the public's perception of a "small farm", and "old world ideal" matches "traditional perceptions". The other options (scenery/surroundings, workers' dress, or livestock types) are either explicitly noted as secondary or not mentioned as the source of authenticity.
Q18 D Examples are the recreation of home-produced products long since replaced by manufactured commodities and the provision of hands-on-experiences in crafts often recreated for tourists Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that to give visitors a real taste of tradition, farms remake homemade goods and old-fashioned crafts that factory-made items replaced a long time ago.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that farms make the experience feel traditional and genuine by bringing back old ways of making goods that people do not typically use today.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the text explains how farms innovate to create an authentic, traditional experience. Paragraph 3 notes that this involves the "reinvention of tradition" through the "recreation of home-produced products long since replaced by manufactured commodities" and recreating old crafts. The phrase "recreation of home-produced products long since replaced" directly matches "re-establishing old processes that are not usually used any more."
Q19 C As a result, some critics argue that the tourists who are running to the countryside are overcrowding and ruining the pristine beauty that they so desperately want to experience Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that some critics complain that so many people are going to rural places that they make them overcrowded and destroy the clean, untouched natural beauty they came to enjoy.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that having too many visitors in rural areas damages the natural charm and beauty that attracts people there.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because paragraph 3 mentions a direct criticism made by critics. In that paragraph, critics say that visitors traveling to rural areas are "overcrowding and ruining the pristine beauty". In other words, too many visitors (extra numbers of people) spoil the natural beauty and attraction (appeal) of the countryside.
Q20 spending This means that tourist spending on agritourism often stays in the region, helping to generate taxable revenues and more disposable incomes Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the money visitors spend on farm tourism stays inside the local area, which helps support the regional economy.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means the money that visitors pay or spend when they take part in farm activities.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by paragraph 4 of the passage. The sentence states that visitors often live relatively close to the farms, so "tourist spending" remains within the local area ("stays in the region"). In the note, "remains in the area" matches "stays in the region", which directly identifies "spending" as the missing word following "Tourist".
Q21 disposable incomes / incomes This means that tourist spending on agritourism often stays in the region, helping to generate taxable revenues and more disposable incomes Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that money spent by visitors stays in the local community, which produces tax money for the area and gives local residents more money to spend.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means the extra money people have left to spend or save after paying taxes.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is confirmed by paragraph 4. The notes state that agritourism 'generates taxes and creates [answer]'. In the text, the author explains that visitor spending stays in the area, 'helping to generate taxable revenues and more disposable incomes'. Here, 'taxable revenues' corresponds to 'taxes', and creating or producing 'more disposable incomes' matches the blank.
Q22 Governments This, in turn, helps governments by keeping farmers on land, protecting picturesque rural landscapes that attract tourists, and supporting the production of regional agricultural products Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that supporting farm tourism aids governments directly because it ensures farmers remain working on their land, keeps the countryside attractive, and preserves local farming.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "Governments" refers to the official leaders and governing bodies of countries or local regions.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the text where it discusses the benefits of subsidising agritourism. The text states that supporting agritourism helps governments because it keeps farmers on their land, protects beautiful countryside scenery, and supports local food production. Therefore, "governments" are the group that benefits from keeping farms operating on the land.
Q23 landscapes This, in turn, helps governments by keeping farmers on land, protecting picturesque rural landscapes that attract tourists, and supporting the production of regional agricultural products Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that supporting farm tourism helps keep farmers working and protects beautiful countryside scenery (rural landscapes) for visitors to see.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "landscapes" refers to the visible features and views of the countryside or countryside scenery.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is confirmed by the passage when discussing the benefits of government support for agritourism. The text states that this assistance helps by "protecting picturesque rural landscapes that attract tourists". In the notes, the phrase "are preserved" means the same as "protecting", and it is directly paired with the adjective "Rural". Therefore, the word that fills the gap is "landscapes".
Q24 Regional agricultural products / Regional products / agricultural products / products This, in turn, helps governments by keeping farmers on land, protecting picturesque rural landscapes that attract tourists, and supporting the production of regional agricultural products Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that helping agritourism allows farmers to stay on their land, preserves beautiful scenery, and helps continue the making of local farm goods.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to farming goods or food items made locally in a specific area.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by paragraph 6, which discusses the advantages of supporting agritourism. The text states that assisting these farms helps governments by "supporting the production of regional agricultural products." In the summary notes, the phrase "continue to be manufactured" matches the ongoing "production" of these local farming goods.
Q25 rural economies / economies Agritourism contributes to and enhances the quality of life in communities by expanding recreational opportunities, differentiating rural economies, and promoting the retention of agricultural lands Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says agritourism makes community life better. It gives more fun activities, makes rural economies different from each other, and helps keep farmland.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'rural economies'. It fills the gap after 'Diversified' in the notes.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'rural economies' because the notes say 'Improved quality of life and more recreational possibilities' and then 'Diversified ___'. The passage says 'expanding recreational opportunities, differentiating rural economies'. 'Diversified' means the same as 'differentiating'. The next note, 'Land remains in use by agriculture', matches 'retention of agricultural lands'.
Q26 fresh farm goods / fresh goods / farm goods / goods Finally, many agritourism operations provide consumers with direct access to fresh farm goods Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that agritourism businesses let buyers get fresh products directly from the farm.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means newly produced items or food that come straight from a farm.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the final paragraph of the text. The note states that 'People can easily buy' something. In the text, 'consumers' refers to 'people', and having 'direct access to' means they are able to buy these items directly from the source. The passage states that agritourism gives consumers direct access to 'fresh farm goods'. Therefore, 'fresh farm goods' (or 'farm goods' / 'goods') completes the blank accurately.
