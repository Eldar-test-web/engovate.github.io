The Psychology Of Wealth - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From IELTS Mock Tests Academic Reading Test 81 · Part 3 · Questions 27–40

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 27-40 which are based on Reading Passage 3 below.

The Psychology of Wealth

What stops people from succeeding financially and having on-going prosperity in their life? The answer is generally focused around the belief that financial success is not a possibility. There are many people who have unconscious barriers that prevent them from having the wealth and abundance that they deserve.

At a conscious level, most people think they are doing everything possible to achieve their goals. However, there still might be some unwitting part of them that does not believe they can obtain success. The more that unconscious part is avoided, the more a person will be blocked in their everyday life. Another problem is that, instead of focusing on all the possible ways to get rich, many people have an obsession about what they do not have. An interesting pattern develops in which they can become angry or resentful over their situation and this in turn can limit these people in their lives more and more. Individuals would find it so much easier to get ahead in life with a peaceful state of mind, rather than an angry or resentful one.

A first step in understanding the unconscious patterning of a person’s financial situation is to explore the deeper nature of how they represent money. For example, a person with money issues may have had parents who lived in poverty, and they subsequently formed a ‘Depression Era’ mentality. An unconscious belief can develop that he or she will always have to struggle financially, because that is what their parents did. Alternatively, the person might have had a parent tell them over and over again that they will never be successful, and eventually they begin to believe it.

It is very common for children to unconsciously form limiting beliefs around money at an early age. In the field of Neuro Linguistic Programming (NLP), these types of limiting beliefs are referred to as ‘imprints.’ An imprint is basically a memory that is formed at an early age, and can serve as a root for both the limiting and empowering beliefs that people form as children. Some of the beliefs that people may develop at early ages are not always healthy, and are created as a result of a traumatic or confusing experience that they have forgotten. How we unconsciously and consciously view the world in terms of money is often based on such beliefs.

A primary and fundamental psychological difference between those who do well financially and those who do not revolves around beliefs. For example, many people do not even view financial success as an option. They do not have the capability to open themselves up to all of the possibilities that are available for achieving prosperity and they will nearly always get stuck in a monthly routine, so that they are unwilling to take risks or try something different, because they are afraid that they will end up being even worse off than before.

Another issue can be that people become over-absorbed with the idea of making money and this can be extremely unhealthy. Money does not determine who you are; it’s simply a resource. There is a term called ‘affluenza,’ which has been defined as "a painful, contagious, socially transmitted condition of overload, debt, anxiety and waste resulting from the dogged pursuit of more.” Affluenza is an unsustainable and seriously unhealthy addiction to personal and societal economic growth. It is most acute in those who inherit wealth and seem to have no purpose or direction. For those with wealth or for those who desire it more than anything, abandoning the urge for more can often be the key to being more successful, and certainly happier. Once people stop equating their self-worth with money, then the doors of possibility can swing open for them, because they are willing to try more things. Once they start feeling better about themselves, they become less fearful and can be open to trying something completely different.

So, can money make people happy? Research shows that it does up to a point, after which there are diminishing returns, so that the extremely wealthy are no happier than the comfortably well off. Rich nations are generally happier than poor ones, but the relationship is far from consistent; other factors like political stability, freedom and security also play a part. Research likewise shows that the money-happiness connection seems to be stronger for people paid hourly than those on a salary. This is presumably because salaried people can more easily compensate with career satisfaction. Money can also impair the ability to enjoy the simple things in life, which rather offsets the happiness that wealth brings.

Money can also impair people’s satisfaction in their play and humanitarian works. When someone has done something out of the goodness of their heart, they can be insulted by offers of payment. Cognitive dissonance experiments show that paying people derisory amounts of money for their work results in them enjoying it less and doing it less well than if they had no pay at all. The capacity for monetary reward to undermine a person’s intrinsic pleasure in work performance has been demonstrated neurologically.

In conclusion, people need to realise that their own attitudes to wealth can affect their chances of acquiring both money and happiness. As a person begins to embrace self worth and open himself or herself up to the idea of what is possible, he or she will attract wealth and prosperity into their life. The outer world is truly a reflection of people’s inner worlds. If someone feels good inside, generally it will show on the outside and they will draw positive experiences into their life.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 unconscious barriers / barriers There are many people who have unconscious barriers that prevent them from having the wealth and abundance that they deserve Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that many individuals have hidden inner blocks that keep them from obtaining the wealth they could otherwise have.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means hidden mental blocks that people are not fully aware of, which stop them from getting rich.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "(unconscious) barriers" because the passage states that people have mental blocks without even realizing it that keep them from gaining wealth. In the text, the phrase "prevent them from having the wealth and abundance" matches the note's phrase "stop them from financial success." Therefore, the word "barriers" (or "unconscious barriers") correctly fills in the blank.
Q28 obsession Another problem is that, instead of focusing on all the possible ways to get rich, many people have an obsession about what they do not have Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that another issue people face is spending all their mental energy thinking about the things they do not possess, rather than thinking about ways to become wealthy.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "obsession" means thinking constantly or too much about something in an unhealthy way.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the second paragraph. The note mentions that people can have a certain feeling or mental focus concerning "their lack of possessions" (meaning things they do not own). In the passage, the author states that instead of looking for ways to become wealthy, "many people have an obsession about what they do not have." Therefore, the word that accurately fills the blank is "obsession."
Q29 parent Alternatively, the person might have had a parent tell them over and over again that they will never be successful, and eventually they begin to believe it Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that if a mother or father repeatedly tells their child that they will fail, the child will in the end start to believe those negative words.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "parent" means a mother or a father.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "parent" because the text explains that someone might hear negative words about their future from a father or mother repeatedly until they finally believe it. In the text, having a "parent tell them over and over again that they will never be successful" matches the idea of a parent who is always negative about a child's future.
Q30 YES It is very common for children to unconsciously form limiting beliefs around money at an early age. In the field of Neuro Linguistic Programming (NLP), these types of limiting beliefs are referred to as ‘imprints.’ Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that young children frequently create negative, restrictive thoughts about money, and psychology terms these early thoughts "imprints."
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means that the text directly agrees with the statement that people can form harmful or limiting early memories (called imprints) about money during childhood.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because the passage clearly states that children often develop limiting beliefs about money when they are young, and it explicitly explains that these kinds of limiting beliefs are called "imprints." Here, "limiting beliefs" corresponds directly to the synonym phrase "unhelpful imprints," and "children" or "at an early age" matches "when a child."
Q31 NO A primary and fundamental psychological difference between those who do well financially and those who do not revolves around beliefs Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that beliefs are the most important and central reason why some people achieve financial success while others do not.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement contradicts what the writer says in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because the statement claims that belief is not a key factor in achieving wealth, which directly contradicts the passage. The text explicitly states that a "primary and fundamental" difference between people who succeed financially and those who do not centers directly on their beliefs. Keywords to notice include "primary and fundamental" (meaning basic, essential, or key) and "beliefs" (what a person thinks or accepts as true).
Q32 NO they will nearly always get stuck in a monthly routine, so that they are unwilling to take risks or try something different, because they are afraid that they will end up being even worse off than before Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that individuals caught in a regular monthly routine do not want to try new things or take risks because they fear becoming even poorer.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement directly disagrees with the information and views given in the passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because the statement says that people stuck in a routine are the "most likely" to try something new, whereas the passage states the exact opposite. According to the text, people trapped in a monthly routine are "unwilling" (do not want) to take risks or try something different because they are scared of ending up in a worse financial position.
Q33 NOT GIVEN There is a term called ‘affluenza,’ which has been defined as "a painful, contagious, socially transmitted condition of overload, debt, anxiety and waste resulting from the dogged pursuit of more.” Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage introduces the definition of the word 'affluenza', but it gives no information about whether this issue has been reported in the media recently.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the text does not say whether the issue of 'affluenza' has appeared often in news, TV, or other media lately.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage explains what the term 'affluenza' means and who suffers from it, but it never mentions the media, news reports, or how frequently this topic has been discussed recently.
Q34 YES It is most acute in those who inherit wealth and seem to have no purpose or direction Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that affluenza is at its worst in people who receive inherited riches and lack a clear goal in life.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means that the writer agrees that "affluenza" affects people who received money without working for it more strongly.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because the text directly states that "affluenza" is "most acute" in individuals who "inherit wealth." To inherit wealth means to receive money from family rather than having to work to earn it. The word "acute" means severe or sharp, showing that this problem is strongest and most common among those who did not work for their money.
Q35 Diminishing returns after which there are diminishing returns, so that the extremely wealthy are no happier than the comfortably well off Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that once a person reaches a certain level of wealth, getting even more money brings diminishing returns, meaning super rich people do not feel happier than those who are simply living comfortably.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "Diminishing returns" refers to a situation where getting more of something (in this case, money) gives less and less extra benefit or happiness.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "Diminishing returns" because the text explains that money only adds happiness up to a certain level. After that specific point, "diminishing returns" happen, which means that super rich individuals are not any happier than people who just have enough comfortable wealth. In the summary sentence, "beyond a certain amount" corresponds directly to "up to a point, after which" in the text.
Q36 factors Rich nations are generally happier than poor ones, but the relationship is far from consistent; other factors like political stability, freedom and security also play a part Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that wealthier countries are usually happier than poorer ones, but not always, because other things like political peace, personal freedom, and safety also influence happiness.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "factors" means other things, elements, or conditions that affect a situation.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "factors" because the text explains that richer countries are usually happier than poorer countries, but this relationship is not always straightforward. This is because additional elements—specifically "other factors" such as political stability, freedom, and safety—also affect how happy people are. In the summary sentence, "other relevant " corresponds directly to "other factors" in the reading passage.
Q37 career satisfaction This is presumably because salaried people can more easily compensate with career satisfaction Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that people who earn a fixed salary might find it easier to balance their feelings of happiness because they enjoy and feel satisfied with their careers.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "career satisfaction" means feeling happy, pleased, and content with your job or professional work.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by paragraph 7, which discusses the link between money and happiness. The text states that salaried people might balance out their happiness "presumably because" (meaning "maybe due to") they have "career satisfaction". Therefore, "career satisfaction" perfectly completes the statement explaining why salaried workers can be happier.
Q38 simple things Money can also impair the ability to enjoy the simple things in life, which rather offsets the happiness that wealth brings Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that having money can hurt a person's ability to appreciate and enjoy basic, everyday joys in life.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "simple things" refers to ordinary, easy, everyday pleasures that do not cost a lot of money.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the seventh paragraph of the text. The summary states that wealthy people sometimes do not enjoy life's "simple things." In the text, the author writes that having a lot of money can "impair the ability to enjoy the simple things in life." Here, the word "impair" means to damage or weaken the ability to enjoy something, matching the summary statement that rich individuals sometimes do not enjoy them.
Q39 cognitive dissonance experiments / cognitive experiments / dissonance experiments / experiments Cognitive dissonance experiments show that paying people derisory amounts of money for their work results in them enjoying it less and doing it less well than if they had no pay at all Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that psychological tests known as cognitive dissonance experiments demonstrate that offering very little money for a task makes people enjoy the task less and perform worse than if they were not paid anything.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means scientific tests or studies about conflicting thoughts and feelings, known as "cognitive dissonance experiments".
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the eighth paragraph of the text. The summary states that money can affect how people approach activities, and that certain tests have shown or proved this effect. In the passage, the author points out that "Cognitive dissonance experiments show that paying people derisory amounts of money for their work results in them enjoying it less and doing it less well than if they had no pay at all." Therefore, "cognitive dissonance experiments" (or simply "experiments") correctly fills the blank.
Q40 monetary reward / reward The capacity for monetary reward to undermine a person’s intrinsic pleasure in work performance has been demonstrated neurologically Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that brain studies have shown that getting paid money can actually reduce how much a person naturally enjoys doing their work.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means money given to a person for doing a job or task.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the eighth paragraph of the text. The summary talks about the connection between getting paid and enjoying your job. The passage states that brain research has shown the effect of a "monetary reward" on reducing a person's natural pleasure or enjoyment in doing work. In this context, "monetary reward" directly fits after the article "a", and "demonstrated" matches the summary's word "proved".
