Food Advertising On Children - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 1-13 which are based on Reading Passage 1 below.

Food advertising on children

This review was commissioned by the Food Standards Agency to examine the current research evidence on:

• the extent and nature of food promotion to children

• the effect, if any, that this promotion has on their food knowledge, preferences and behaviour.

A

Children's food promotion is dominated by television advertising, and the great majority of this promotes the so-called 'Big Four' of pre-sugared breakfast cereals, soft drinks, confectionery and savoury snacks. In the last ten years advertising for fast food outlets has rapidly increased. There is some evidence that the dominance of television has recently begun to wane. The importance of strong, global branding reinforces a need for multi-faceted communications combining television with merchandising, 'tie-ins' and point of sale activity. The advertised diet contrasts sharply with that recommended by public health advisors, and themes of fun and fantasy or taste, rather than health and nutrition, are used to promote it to children. Meanwhile, the recommended diet gets little promotional support.

B

There is plenty of evidence that children notice and enjoy food promotion. However, establishing whether this actually influences them is a complex problem. The review tackled it by looking at studies that had examined possible effects on what children know about food, their food preferences, their actual food behaviour (both buying and eating), and their health outcomes (eg. obesity or cholesterol levels). The majority of studies examined food advertising, but a few examined other forms of food promotion. In terms of nutritional knowledge, food advertising seems to have little influence on children's general perceptions of what constitutes a healthy diet, but, in certain contexts, it does have an effect on more specific types of nutritional knowledge. For example, seeing soft drink and cereal adverts reduced primary aged children's ability to determine correctly whether or not certain products contained real fruit.

C

The review also found evidence that food promotion influences children's food preferences and purchase behaviour. A study of primary school children, for instance, found that exposure to advertising influenced which foods they claimed to like; and another showed that labelling and signage on a vending machine had an effect on what was bought by secondary school pupils. A number of studies have also shown that food advertising can influence what children eat. One, for example, showed that advertising influenced a primary class's choice of daily snack at playtime.

D

The next step, of trying to establish whether or not a link exists between food promotion and diet or obesity, is extremely difficult as it requires research to be done in real-world settings. A number of studies have attempted this by using the amount of television viewing as a proxy for exposure to television advertising. They have established a clear link between television viewing and diet, obesity, and cholesterol levels. It is impossible to say, however, whether this effect is caused by the advertising, the sedentary nature of television viewing or snacking that might take place whilst viewing. One study resolved this problem by taking a detailed diary of children's viewing habits. This showed that the more food adverts they saw, the more snacks and calories they consumed.

E

Thus the literature does suggest food promotion is influencing children's diet in a number of ways. This does not amount to proof; as noted above with this kind of research, incontrovertible proof simply isn't attainable. Nor do all studies point to this conclusion; several have not found an effect. In addition, very few studies have attempted to measure how strong these effects are relative to other factors influencing children's food choices. Nonetheless, many studies have found clear effects and they have used sophisticated methodologies that make it possible to determine that i) these effects are not just due to chance; ii) they are independent of other factors that influence diets, such as parents' eating habits or attitudes; and iii) they occur at a brand and category level.

F

Furthermore, two factors suggest that these findings actually downplay the effect that food promotion has on children. First, the literature focuses principally on television advertising; the cumulative effect of this combined with other forms of promotion and marketing is likely to be significantly greater. Second, the studies have looked at the direct effects on individual children, and understate indirect influences. For example, promotion for fast food outlets may not only influence the child but also encourage parents to take them for meals and reinforce the idea that this is normal and desirable behaviour.

G

This does not amount to proof of an effect, but in our view does provide sufficient evidence to conclude that an effect exists. The debate should now shift to what action is needed, and specifically to how the power of commercial marketing can be used to bring about improvements in young people's eating.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 viii Children's food promotion is dominated by television advertising, and the great majority of this promotes the so-called 'Big Four' of pre-sugared breakfast cereals, soft drinks, confectionery and savoury snacks. In the last ten years advertising for fast food outlets has rapidly increased. There is some evidence that the dominance of television has recently begun to wane. The importance of strong, global branding reinforces a need for multi-faceted communications combining television with merchandising, 'tie-ins' and point of sale activity. The advertised diet contrasts sharply with that recommended by public health advisors, and themes of fun and fantasy or taste, rather than health and nutrition, are used to promote it to children. Meanwhile, the recommended diet gets little promotional support Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that most food adverts aimed at children show unhealthy foods like sweets, sugary cereals, and sodas, which go against the healthy food choices recommended by doctors and health experts.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that heading viii, "Advertising's focus on unhealthy options", is the best title for Paragraph A.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is viii because Paragraph A explains that most food advertisements for children promote unhealthy foods, known as the 'Big Four' (sugary cereals, soft drinks, sweets, and snacks), along with fast food. The author notes that what is advertised is the opposite of the healthy diet recommended by health experts ("contrasts sharply with that recommended by public health advisors"), meaning advertisements focus mainly on unhealthy choices.
Q2 ii In terms of nutritional knowledge, food advertising seems to have little influence on children's general perceptions of what constitutes a healthy diet, but, in certain contexts, it does have an effect on more specific types of nutritional knowledge Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that advertising does not change what children generally think a healthy diet is, but it can change what they know about specific nutritional details.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'ii' means that Paragraph B discusses what children understand or do not understand about food and nutrition.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'ii' because Paragraph B focuses on research regarding what children know about food. The paragraph discusses children's "nutritional knowledge" and "general perceptions of what constitutes a healthy diet." It also gives an example showing how ads affect their "ability to determine correctly whether or not certain products contained real fruit." Therefore, this section directly explores what and how much children really know about food.
Q3 vi The review also found evidence that food promotion influences children's food preferences and purchase behaviour Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage clearly states that researchers discovered proof that food advertising changes the foods children like and the things they decide to purchase.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'vi' means that Paragraph C is about proof showing that food ads change the foods children choose to buy and eat.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is vi because Paragraph C focuses entirely on research showing that food marketing affects what children like, buy, and consume. Key phrases like 'purchase behaviour' match 'buy', while 'influence what children eat' directly matches 'what children buy to eat'. The paragraph gives specific examples, such as students buying items from vending machines and picking daily snacks.
Q4 v The next step, of trying to establish whether or not a link exists between food promotion and diet or obesity, is extremely difficult as it requires research to be done in real-world settings Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that researchers tried to see if there is a relationship between food advertising and what children eat or whether they become obese (severely overweight).
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Paragraph D is about the link between food advertisements and children being overweight or having health issues related to weight.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading v because Paragraph D discusses research trying to find a link between food promotion and health issues like being overweight. In this paragraph, the word "link" means connection, "food promotion" and "television advertising" mean advertising, and "obesity" refers to weight problems. The paragraph also explains how a study found that the more food ads children watched, the more snacks and calories they ate.
Q5 i Nor do all studies point to this conclusion; several have not found an effect. In addition, very few studies have attempted to measure how strong these effects are relative to other factors influencing children's food choices. Nonetheless, many studies have found clear effects Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that not every study finds that food ads affect children, meaning researchers do not fully agree. However, many studies do agree and clearly show that these ads change what children eat.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "i" means the main ideas that researchers agree on and the things they disagree on.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "i" because Paragraph E summarizes the overall findings of the research. It points out disagreements among studies by stating that "Nor do all studies point to this conclusion; several have not found an effect." It also points out areas of agreement, noting that "many studies have found clear effects" using advanced methods. Therefore, this paragraph focuses on the general agreements and disagreements among researchers.
Q6 x Furthermore, two factors suggest that these findings actually downplay the effect that food promotion has on children Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that two reasons show that the research results actually make the influence of food advertising on children look smaller than it really is.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "x" means that research studies might make the true influence of food advertising on children seem smaller than it really is.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading x because Paragraph F explains why the research studies likely make the power of food promotion look weaker than reality. The text uses the word "downplay" and "understate", which have the exact same meaning as "underestimating". The paragraph gives two main reasons why the real impact is actually bigger: studies mainly look at TV instead of all promotions combined, and they miss indirect effects like parents also being influenced.
Q7 iii The debate should now shift to what action is needed, and specifically to how the power of commercial marketing can be used to bring about improvements in young people's eating Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that people should now talk about what actions must be taken to help improve children's diets using marketing.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that heading iii, "Need to take action", is the best title for Paragraph G because the paragraph talks about deciding what steps we should take next.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "iii" because Paragraph G states that people should stop arguing about whether food advertising affects children and instead focus on what steps to take. The phrase "what action is needed" directly matches the heading "Need to take action".
Q8 NO The advertised diet contrasts sharply with that recommended by public health advisors, and themes of fun and fantasy or taste, rather than health and nutrition, are used to promote it to children Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the food shown in advertisements is completely different from the healthy food that health experts recommend.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement directly disagrees with what the author says in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because the statement claims there is "little difference" between the healthy diet suggested by health experts and the food shown in adverts. However, the passage clearly states that the advertised diet "contrasts sharply" (has a very large, noticeable difference) with the diet recommended by health advisors. Because the statement contradicts the text, the answer is NO.
Q9 NO In terms of nutritional knowledge, food advertising seems to have little influence on children's general perceptions of what constitutes a healthy diet, but, in certain contexts, it does have an effect on more specific types of nutritional knowledge. For example, seeing soft drink and cereal adverts reduced primary aged children's ability to determine correctly whether or not certain products contained real fruit Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that food adverts have almost no impact on helping children understand what a healthy diet is. Instead of teaching them good nutritional facts, adverts actually made it harder for children to tell if foods contained real fruit.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement directly contradicts what the author states in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because the author explains that food advertising does not successfully teach children about nutrition or healthy eating. In fact, food advertising has "little influence" on what children think is healthy, and seeing ads for soft drinks and cereals actually "reduced" children's ability to know whether food products had real fruit. Additionally, ads focus on fun and taste rather than health and nutrition. Therefore, television advertising does not successfully teach children nutritional knowledge.
Q10 YES It is impossible to say, however, whether this effect is caused by the advertising, the sedentary nature of television viewing or snacking that might take place whilst viewing Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that no one can know for sure which specific part of watching television—the commercials, sitting still without moving, or snacking while watching—is the real cause of these health effects.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means that the statement accurately reflects what the writer says in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because the author clearly states that researchers cannot easily tell which specific factor related to watching TV leads to poor health outcomes like obesity. Paragraph D explains that there is a connection between watching television and problems such as bad diet, high cholesterol, and obesity. However, the author notes that "it is impossible to say" whether this is because of the adverts themselves, sitting still for too long (sedentary nature), or eating snacks while watching. Therefore, the statement matches the passage.
Q11 NOT GIVEN The review also found evidence that food promotion influences children's food preferences and purchase behaviour Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that food advertising changes what foods children like and buy. However, it does not mention whether a child's age or gender also influences their food preferences.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether a child's age and gender change what kinds of food they like.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage discusses how advertising and promotion affect children's food choices, but it never talks about "gender" (boy or girl) or compares how "age" changes their food "preferences". Even though it mentions studies done with primary and secondary school children, it never states whether age and gender affect what food children prefer.
Q12 YES Furthermore, two factors suggest that these findings actually downplay the effect that food promotion has on children. First, the literature focuses principally on television advertising; the cumulative effect of this combined with other forms of promotion and marketing is likely to be significantly greater Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that existing research mostly looks at television ads and therefore underestimates the total impact, making the current findings incomplete because they do not include other forms of marketing.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means the statement matches the author's view that research focusing mostly on television advertisements does not give the whole picture.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because Paragraph F states that research "focuses principally on television advertising" while missing the bigger combined effect of other marketing types. Because of this, the research findings "downplay" (minimize or present only a limited view of) the true impact of food advertising, which means the research is partial and incomplete.
Q13 NOT GIVEN they are independent of other factors that influence diets, such as parents' eating habits or attitudes Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage mentions that children's food choices can be affected by their parents' habits or feelings about food, but it says nothing about rich or wealthy parents buying more sensible food.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not mention whether rich parents usually buy healthier food for their children.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage discusses food advertising, children's eating habits, and briefly mentions parents' habits and attitudes, but it never talks about "wealthy parents" or how a family's wealth affects buying "sensible food". Therefore, we cannot know the author's view on this statement.
