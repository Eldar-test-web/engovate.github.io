The Grand Banks - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From IELTS Mock Tests Academic Reading Test 79 · Part 2 · Questions 14–26

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 14-26 which are based on Reading Passage 2 below.

The Grand Banks

Paragraph A

The Grand Banks is a large area of submerged highlands southeast of Newfoundland and east of the Laurentian Channel on the North American continental shelf. Covering 93,200 square kilometres, the Grand Banks are relatively shallow, ranging from 25 to 100 meters in depth. It is in this area that the cold Labrador Current mixes with the warm waters of the Gulf Stream. The mixing of these waters and the shape of the ocean bottom lifts nutrients to the surface and these conditions created one of the richest fishing grounds in the world. Extensive marine life flourishes in the Grand Banks, whose range extends beyond the Canadian 200-mile exclusive economic zone (EEZ) and into international waters. This has made it an important part of both the Canadian and the high seas fisheries, with fishermen risking their lives in the extremely inhospitable environment consisting of rogue waves, fog, icebergs, sea ice, hurricanes, winter storms and earthquakes.

Paragraph B

While the area's 'official' discovery is credited to John Cabot in 1497, English and Portuguese vessels are known to have first sought out these waters prior to that, based upon reports they received from earlier Viking voyages to Newfoundland. Several navigators, including Basque fishermen, are known to have fished these waters in the fifteenth century. Some texts from that era refer to a land called Bacalao, 'the land of the codfish', which is possibly Newfoundland. However, it was not until John Cabot noted the waters' abundance of sea life that the existence of these fishing grounds became widely known in Europe. Soon, fishermen and merchants from France, Spain, Portugal and England developed seasonal inshore fisheries producing for European markets. Known as 'dry' fishery, cod were split, salted, and dried on shore over the summer before crews returned to Europe. The French pioneered 'wet' or 'green' fishery on the Grand Banks proper around 1550, heavily salting the cod on board and immediately returning home.

Paragraph C

The Grand Banks were possibly the world's most important international fishing area in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. Technological advances in fishing, such as sonar and large factory ships, including the massive factory freezer trawlers introduced in the 1950's, led to overfishing and a serious decline in the fish stocks. Based upon the many foreign policy agreements Newfoundland had entered into prior to its admittance into the Canadian Confederation, foreign fleets, some from as far away as Russia, came to the Grand Banks in force, catching unprecedented quantities of fish.

Paragraph D

Between 1973 and 1982, the United Nations and its member states negotiated the Third Convention of the Law of the Sea, one component of which was the concept of nations being allowed to declare an EEZ. Many nations worldwide declared 200-nautical mile EEZ's, including Canada and the United States. On the whole, the EEZ was very well received by fishermen in eastern Canada, because it meant they could fish unhindered out to the limit without fear of competing with the foreign fleets. During the late 1970's and early 1980s, Canada's domestic offshore fleet grew as fishermen and fish processing companies rushed to take advantage. It was during this time that it was noticed that the foreign fleets now pushed out to areas of the Grand Banks off Newfoundland outside the Canadian EEZ. By the late 1980's, dwindling catches of Atlantic cod were being reported throughout Newfoundland and eastern Canada, and the federal government and citizens of coastal regions in the area began to face the reality that the domestic and foreign overfishing had taken its toll. The Canadian government was finally forced to take drastic action in 1992, when a total moratorium was declared indefinitely for the northern cod.

Paragraph E

Over the last ten years, it has been noted that cod appear to be returning to the Grand Banks in small numbers. The reasons for this fragile recovery are still unknown. Perhaps, the damage done by trawlers is not permanent and the marine fauna and ecosystems can rebuild themselves if given a prolonged period of time without any commercial activity. Either way, the early stage recovery of the Grand Banks is encouraging news, but caution is needed, as, after nearly twenty years of severe limitations, cod stocks are still only at approximately ten per cent of 1960's levels. It is hoped that in another ten to twenty years, stocks may be close to a full recovery, although this would require political pressure to maintain strict limitations on commercial fishing. If cod do come back to the Grand Banks in meaningful numbers, it is to be hoped that the Canadians will not make the same mistakes again.

Paragraph F

Further riches have now been found in the Grand Banks. Petroleum reserves have been discovered and a number of oil fields are under development in the region. The vast Hibernia oil field was discovered in 1979, and, following several years of aborted start-up attempts, the Hibernia megaproject began construction of the production platform and gravity base structures in the early 1990's. Production commenced on November 17, 1997, with initial production rates in excess of 50,000 barrels of crude oil per day from a single well. Hibernia has proven to be the most prolific oil well in Canada. However, earthquake and iceberg activity in the Grand Banks pose a potential ecological disaster that could devastate the fishing grounds that are only now starting to recover.

Questions

Questions 14–20 Matching Information

The text has 6 paragraphs A - F.

Which paragraph contains the following information?

14 Many countries could legally fish Newfoundland waters because of treaties Newfoundland had made before becoming part of Canada.
15 The establishment of the EEZ did not stop over-fishing in the Grand Banks.
16 Natural disasters could cause oil to destroy what is left of the Grand Banks ecosystem.
17 The original amount of fish in the Grand Banks was due to different temperature waters mixing.
18 East Canadian fishermen were generally happy with the establishment of the Canadian EEZ.
19 Grand Banks' cod stocks are still 90 per cent lower than what they were in the 1960's.
20 The French were the first to prepare the cod on board their ships before going back to France.

Questions 21–23 One Choice

Choose the correct letter A, B, C or D.

21 The first English fishermen to come to the Grand Banks to fish
  1. were told about the fishery by Basque fishermen.
  2. were sent word about the fishery from the first American colonists.
  3. acted on information from previous Viking expeditions.
  4. discovered the fishery themselves while exploring.
22 John Cabot's reports of the Grand Banks
  1. led to the establishment of the Canadian EEZ.
  2. meant the fishery was well known in Europe.
  3. led to fighting between rival fishing fleets.
  4. were not immediately publicised, so that English fishermen could benefit.
23 The establishment of the Canadian EEZ
  1. did not stop foreign fishermen from fishing the Grand Banks.
  2. was not ratified by the United Nations.
  3. temporarily stopped the over-fishing of cod in the Grand Banks.
  4. meant Canadian fishermen were excluded from fishing the Grand Banks.

Questions 24–26 True / False / Not Given

Do the following statements agree with the information given in the text?

write:

TRUE if the statement agrees with the information

FALSE if the statement contradicts the information

NOT GIVEN if there is no information on this

24 Even now, cod stocks have shown no signs of recovery in the Grand Banks.
25 Initial efforts to extract oil from the Grand Banks' Hibernia oil field were unsuccessful.
26 Oil exploration companies have to follow strict safety controls imposed by the Canadian government.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q14 C Based upon the many foreign policy agreements Newfoundland had entered into prior to its admittance into the Canadian Confederation, foreign fleets, some from as far away as Russia, came to the Grand Banks in force, catching unprecedented quantities of fish Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that before Newfoundland joined Canada, it made agreements with other nations that allowed foreign fishing ships to come in large numbers and catch fish.
Answer Explanation:
The answer identifies Paragraph C as the section of the text that mentions international agreements made before Newfoundland joined Canada allowing foreign fleets to fish there.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Paragraph C specifically discusses agreements made by Newfoundland. The text states that because of 'foreign policy agreements Newfoundland had entered into prior to its admittance into the Canadian Confederation', foreign ships ('foreign fleets') were able to come to the Grand Banks and catch huge amounts of fish. In this context, 'foreign policy agreements' means treaties, 'prior to its admittance into the Canadian Confederation' means before becoming part of Canada, and 'foreign fleets' refers to many countries fishing there.
Q15 D By the late 1980's, dwindling catches of Atlantic cod were being reported throughout Newfoundland and eastern Canada, and the federal government and citizens of coastal regions in the area began to face the reality that the domestic and foreign overfishing had taken its toll Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that by the late 1980s, the amount of cod being caught was dropping, and people realized that both local and foreign overfishing had caused serious harm even after the EEZ was in place.
Answer Explanation:
The answer D means that Paragraph D has the information showing that creating the EEZ (exclusive economic zone) did not prevent people from catching too many fish in the Grand Banks.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because Paragraph D discusses the establishment of the 200-nautical mile EEZ by nations like Canada. Despite this new rule, foreign ships continued fishing just outside the border while domestic ships caught fish inside it, leading to a point where "domestic and foreign overfishing had taken its toll." Keywords like "EEZ" and "overfishing" in Paragraph D directly match the statement in the question.
Q16 F However, earthquake and iceberg activity in the Grand Banks pose a potential ecological disaster that could devastate the fishing grounds that are only now starting to recover Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that dangerous natural events like earthquakes and moving icebergs could trigger a major environmental disaster, which could completely destroy the sea areas that are just beginning to heal.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "F" means that Paragraph F contains the statement about how natural events could lead to serious environmental damage to the recovering sea life around the Grand Banks.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is F because this paragraph discusses oil development in the area and ends with a warning about natural threats. It explains that natural risks such as "earthquake and iceberg activity" could cause a serious "ecological disaster" that would ruin ("devastate") the sea environment and fishing grounds that are just beginning to recover.
Q17 A It is in this area that the cold Labrador Current mixes with the warm waters of the Gulf Stream. The mixing of these waters and the shape of the ocean bottom lifts nutrients to the surface and these conditions created one of the richest fishing grounds in the world Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that cold ocean water and warm ocean water mix together in this location, bringing food up to the surface and making it one of the best places on Earth with huge amounts of fish.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is Paragraph A, which explains that cold and warm ocean waters came together to create an area full of fish.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is Paragraph A because it mentions that the cold Labrador Current meets and mixes with the warm Gulf Stream. The text explains that this blending of cold and warm water brings up food (nutrients) from the ocean floor, which made the Grand Banks "one of the richest fishing grounds in the world." Key matching ideas include "cold" and "warm waters" for "different temperature waters mixing," and "richest fishing grounds" for the "amount of fish."
Q18 D On the whole, the EEZ was very well received by fishermen in eastern Canada, because it meant they could fish unhindered out to the limit without fear of competing with the foreign fleets Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that fishermen in eastern Canada generally liked the EEZ rule because it allowed them to fish freely without worrying about boats from other countries.
Answer Explanation:
The answer identifies Paragraph D as the section of the text discussing how fishermen from eastern Canada were pleased when Canada created its exclusive economic zone (EEZ).
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is Paragraph D because it directly states how fishermen in eastern Canada reacted to the creation of the EEZ. The paragraph uses the phrase "On the whole" to mean 'generally', and "very well received by fishermen in eastern Canada" to show that these fishermen were happy with the new rule, since they could fish without competition from foreign boats.
Q19 E cod stocks are still only at approximately ten per cent of 1960's levels Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the amount of cod fish is currently only around ten percent of the amount that existed during the 1960s, meaning ninety percent has been lost.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Paragraph E mentions that the number of cod fish today is still 90% less than it was in the 1960s.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is Paragraph E because it discusses the current recovery of cod in the Grand Banks and states that the fish population is currently at only around 10% of what it used to be in the 1960s. Since the stock is at 10% of its past level, it means it is 90% lower. Key phrases to notice are 'cod stocks', 'ten per cent of 1960's levels' (which is the opposite of 90 per cent lower), and 'Grand Banks'.
Q20 B The French pioneered 'wet' or 'green' fishery on the Grand Banks proper around 1550, heavily salting the cod on board and immediately returning home Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that around the year 1550, the French were the first to put lots of salt on cod right on their boats and then travel straight back home.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means that Paragraph B includes the detail about French fishermen being the first people to treat or preserve cod directly on their boats before sailing back home.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because Paragraph B explains that French fishermen started the practice of salting cod directly on their ships instead of drying them on land. In the text, the keyword "pioneered" matches "were the first", "salting the cod on board" matches "prepare the cod on board their ships", and "returning home" refers to going back to France.
Q21 C While the area's 'official' discovery is credited to John Cabot in 1497, English and Portuguese vessels are known to have first sought out these waters prior to that, based upon reports they received from earlier Viking voyages to Newfoundland Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that before John Cabot arrived in 1497, English and Portuguese ships had already searched for these waters because they heard about them from earlier trips made by Vikings to Newfoundland.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the earliest English fishermen went to the Grand Banks because they learned about the area from old Viking trips.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Paragraph B states that English ships looked for these waters before 1497 after getting reports from older Viking journeys. In the text, the phrase 'reports they received' matches 'information', and 'earlier Viking voyages' matches 'previous Viking expeditions'.
Q22 B However, it was not until John Cabot noted the waters' abundance of sea life that the existence of these fishing grounds became widely known in Europe Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that people across Europe only learned about these rich fishing waters after John Cabot saw and reported how many fish lived there.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that John Cabot's observations made people across Europe become widely aware of these fishing areas.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the text explicitly states that after John Cabot noted how much sea life was in the waters, the fishing grounds became widely known across Europe. The key phrase in the passage is "became widely known in Europe", which directly matches the idea in option B that "the fishery was well known in Europe."
Q23 A It was during this time that it was noticed that the foreign fleets now pushed out to areas of the Grand Banks off Newfoundland outside the Canadian EEZ Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that ships from other countries moved just outside the boundary line of Canada's economic zone, but they were still fishing in areas of the Grand Banks.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that creating Canada's special fishing zone (EEZ) did not completely prevent fishing boats from other countries from catching fish in the Grand Banks.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because Paragraph D explains that after Canada established its 200-nautical mile Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ), foreign fishing ships simply moved to parts of the Grand Banks that were outside this Canadian zone and continued fishing. The words "foreign fleets" refer to foreign fishermen, and "pushed out to areas of the Grand Banks... outside the Canadian EEZ" shows that they still fished there rather than stopping completely.
Q24 FALSE Over the last ten years, it has been noted that cod appear to be returning to the Grand Banks in small numbers. The reasons for this fragile recovery are still unknown Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that in the past ten years, cod fish have started coming back to the area in small numbers, which shows that a weak or early recovery is taking place.
Answer Explanation:
The answer FALSE means the statement is wrong because the text says cod numbers are actually starting to recover.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the statement claims that cod stocks have shown "no signs of recovery." In contrast, Paragraph E states that cod are coming back in small numbers and calls this a "fragile recovery" and an "early stage recovery." Paragraph F also mentions that the fishing grounds are "only now starting to recover." Because the fish are indeed showing early signs of returning, the statement directly contradicts the facts in the text.
Q25 TRUE The vast Hibernia oil field was discovered in 1979, and, following several years of aborted start-up attempts, the Hibernia megaproject began construction of the production platform and gravity base structures in the early 1990's Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that after finding the huge Hibernia oil field in 1979, people tried to start the project for several years, but those early attempts failed or were stopped before construction finally started in the 1990s.
Answer Explanation:
The answer TRUE means the statement is correct according to the passage: the first tries to produce oil at the Hibernia oil field failed.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because Paragraph F directly states that there were "several years of aborted start-up attempts" before the Hibernia project actually began construction. Here, the phrase "aborted start-up attempts" means the same thing as "unsuccessful initial efforts" to get the project going and produce oil.
Q26 NOT GIVEN Petroleum reserves have been discovered and a number of oil fields are under development in the region Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that oil has been found and projects are being developed in the area, but it does not mention any safety rules set by the government.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the text does not say whether oil companies must follow strict safety rules from the Canadian government.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because Paragraph F discusses oil discovery, the development of oil fields, and environmental risks like icebergs and earthquakes, but it never mentions safety rules or government regulations for oil companies. Since there is no information about strict safety controls imposed by the Canadian government, the statement cannot be confirmed as true or false.
