Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 14–26, which are based on Reading Passage 2 below.

The attraction of video games The world's love of video games has much to do with people's desires and motives

A

Video games, it is often claimed, are about wasting time. It is a misunderstanding that players and game makers have been trying to correct for many years. While movies and television are endlessly analysed in the media, games are often dismissed as irresponsible, unimportant by-products of the broader digital revolution.

But a growing number of experts disagree. This is, after all, an entertainment medium that worldwide makes $50 billion a year. Using ideas from psychology and sociology, theorists and designers suggest that our love of video games may actually have important things to tell us about our most basic desires and motivations.

Game design has become a big business and has led to the creation of a multitude of companies. 'The industry attitude toward training has changed radically,' says prominent game researcher Jesper Juul. 'I recall hearing professionals claim that game design was a strange and unteachable art, but now this attitude has mostly faded.' Designing video games is increasingly recognised as a valid field of university study throughout the world.

B

Central to an understanding of games is the theory that games are fun because they teach us in a way that our brains prefer, that is, through systems and puzzles. Raph Koster, designer of multiplayer fantasy games, points out that an effective learning environment is one in which failure is acceptable, even welcomed. Accordingly, Koster says that in games, the player enters into a situation where the rules of the real world don't apply - and typically being judged on success and failure is part of the real world. When gaming, people feel free to try things and to learn, and not worry about what might happen.

Consistently, Koster says, the best games are the ones that provide us with interesting tools such as weapons or magic, and allow us to experiment with them. For example, in one early game, players are given the ability to jump, and can practise this for as long as they like, but to get to the next stage they need to master this ability so they can leap over an enemy and onto a platform.

'Games allow us to create these little systems where learning is controlled really brilliantly,' says Margaret Robertson, director at a London-based game design company. 'Something we don't talk about is that, actually, one of the strengths of games is the vague sense of disapproval that still surrounds them - they feel like something that's forbidden!' And that can, of course, be very exciting.

C

Another important element in the popularity of games is the player's ability to determine what happens. Games tap into our need to have direction; this is very obvious in games where we shape the lives of virtual humans, but it's becoming a vital element of action adventures too.

'Games are increasingly complex systems,' says Dan Pinchbeck, an experimental game designer. 'There's an emphasis on the pleasure of choosing and planning. We've moved quite dramatically away from the very first video games. These games mostly involved the player merely reacting to events. But games, then, became more about approaching a situation and making a plan depending on your preferred play style.'

D

Many studios design their games around reward systems. 'A good game will have progression at the end of each level, but it will also provide surprise rewards halfway through,' says Ben Weedon, a games studio consultant. 'In a game, you're essentially pressing the same buttons and doing the same things over and over again, so you need those occasional surprises to stay motivated.'

E

Games have constantly evolved over the years and continue to do so right up to the present. Now, incorporating a narrative structure into a game is becoming increasingly important. Many games have adopted Hollywood's three-act structure, which is designed to maintain our loyalty to a particular game. As in many films, a short final act is often used to give a sense of acceleration towards a preferably startling climax. Opening levels of games are also short, because this flatters us into thinking we're making good progress, whereas the middle levels are more extensive.

F

Games even tap into our friendships. The rise of multiplayer gaming means that gaming increasingly involves social interaction. And other businesses are taking notice and using this as an element in advertising their brands. Then there's the new concept of 'gamification', in which websites and smartphone apps are being designed like games, with high scores and achievement points to keep customers entertained. Research estimates that businesses spent more than $100 million worldwide on gamification projects last year, a figure predicted to rise to $1.6 billion in the next four years.

So, in fact, games aren't just an insignificant fad, as some people might suggest. They fulfill intrinsic human needs, whether we are conscious of it or not. The loop of learning control and rewards is at the heart of something very important, and very attractive.

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q14 vi Game design has become a big business and has led to the creation of a multitude of companies Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that creating video games has turned into a major industry and has caused many new companies to start up.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'vi' means how the business of making video games has grown and changed over time.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is vi because Section A discusses how making video games has grown from being seen as a waste of time into a huge global business. The paragraph highlights that game design is now a massive commercial field with many companies, professional training programs, and university degrees. Key phrases like 'Game design has become a big business' and 'industry attitude toward training has changed radically' directly match the keywords 'development' and 'game design industry'.
Q15 i When gaming, people feel free to try things and to learn, and not worry about what might happen. Consistently, Koster says, the best games are the ones that provide us with interesting tools such as weapons or magic, and allow us to experiment with them Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that while playing games, people feel safe to try new actions, test tools, and learn because they do not have to worry about bad results or real-world consequences.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'i' means that heading i ('Being able to experiment without consequences') best summarizes Section B, because players can try out actions and learn inside a game without worrying about real-life failure or negative results.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading i because paragraph B explains that video games create a safe environment where people can freely try things. The text notes that 'failure is acceptable, even welcomed' and that 'rules of the real world don't apply.' Players 'feel free to try things and to learn, and not worry about what might happen,' and the best games 'allow us to experiment with them.' Here, 'try things' and 'experiment' match 'experiment' in heading i, while 'failure is acceptable' and 'not worry about what might happen' correspond to 'without consequences.'
Q16 vii Another important element in the popularity of games is the player's ability to determine what happens Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that a big reason people love video games is that the player has the power to decide what takes place.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "vii" means that Section C is mainly about how players can decide and control what takes place inside video games.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is vii because Section C focuses on how players enjoy having the power to make decisions, shape events, and plan their actions in games. The text specifically highlights that games satisfy our desire to direct outcomes, using keywords like "player's ability to determine what happens" and "pleasure of choosing and planning", which match heading vii's words "ability to control what occurs in games" ("determine" means "control", and "what happens" means "what occurs").
Q17 iii 'In a game, you're essentially pressing the same buttons and doing the same things over and over again, so you need those occasional surprises to stay motivated.' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that players keep doing the same actions repeatedly while playing, which is why they need unexpected surprises from time to time to remain interested and excited.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that surprise or unexpected things are very important to keep players interested in a game.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading iii because Section D discusses how important surprise elements are for keeping players motivated. Ben Weedon explains that players repeat the same actions constantly, so "surprise rewards" and "occasional surprises" are needed to keep them playing. The word "unexpected" in the heading directly matches "surprise" and "surprises" in the text, and "key role" matches the idea that these surprises are necessary to "stay motivated".
Q18 ii Now, incorporating a narrative structure into a game is becoming increasingly important. Many games have adopted Hollywood's three-act structure, which is designed to maintain our loyalty to a particular game Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that adding a story structure to a game is very important because it helps keep players engaged and loyal to that game.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'ii' means that Section E explains the reasons why stories or storytelling elements are added to video games.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading ii because Section E discusses adding a 'narrative structure' (story) to games and explains the reasons for doing so. Specifically, it notes that borrowing a three-act structure from movies helps 'maintain our loyalty to a particular game' and creates exciting pacing from the beginning to the climax. Keywords in the text like 'narrative structure' directly relate to 'stories', and 'incorporating' means 'including'.
Q19 iv Then there's the new concept of 'gamification', in which websites and smartphone apps are being designed like games, with high scores and achievement points to keep customers entertained Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that other digital products, like apps and websites, are now being made with game-like features such as points and scores to make them more engaging for people.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means taking elements or ideas from games and using them in different things, like regular apps and websites.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading iv because Section F explains how other industries use game elements. It introduces "gamification," where non-game products such as "websites and smartphone apps" use game features like "high scores and achievement points" to engage users and customers.
Q20 D 'There's an emphasis on the pleasure of choosing and planning. We've moved quite dramatically away from the very first video games. These games mostly involved the player merely reacting to events. But games, then, became more about approaching a situation and making a plan depending on your preferred play style.' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows Dan Pinchbeck saying that earlier games just required fast reactions, while newer games focus on letting players think ahead, make choices, and form a plan.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "D" refers to Dan Pinchbeck, who said that players enjoy making plans and deciding what to do beforehand.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because in paragraph C, game designer Dan Pinchbeck explains that modern games focus on "choosing and planning" and "making a plan depending on your preferred play style." Here, the action of "thinking ahead" directly matches the concepts of "planning" and "making a plan."
Q21 E 'A good game will have progression at the end of each level, but it will also provide surprise rewards halfway through,' says Ben Weedon, a games studio consultant. 'In a game, you're essentially pressing the same buttons and doing the same things over and over again, so you need those occasional surprises to stay motivated.' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states Ben Weedon's belief that because video games involve doing the same actions repeatedly, players require unexpected surprises to remain motivated and interested in the game.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'E' means that Ben Weedon is the expert who said that adding unexpected things keeps gamers wanting to play.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because in Paragraph D, game studio consultant Ben Weedon explains that players need unexpected events to remain engaged. In his statement, 'occasional surprises' directly matches 'unanticipated elements', and 'stay motivated' corresponds directly to 'keeps players interested'. None of the other listed experts make this point about unexpected rewards or surprises keeping players motivated.
Q22 A 'The industry attitude toward training has changed radically,' says prominent game researcher Jesper Juul. 'I recall hearing professionals claim that game design was a strange and unteachable art, but now this attitude has mostly faded.' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains through Jesper Juul that opinions about learning how to design games have changed completely. People used to believe that game design could not be taught, but hardly anyone thinks that way anymore.
Answer Explanation:
The answer A refers to Jesper Juul, the expert who stated that people now accept that making video games is something a person can be taught to do.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because in Paragraph A, researcher Jesper Juul discusses how people's views on training for game design have changed. He mentions that professionals used to think game design was an "unteachable art," but this view has "mostly faded." This directly matches the idea in the statement that creating games is now recognized as a skill that can be learned and taught.
Q23 D 'Games are increasingly complex systems,' says Dan Pinchbeck, an experimental game designer. 'There's an emphasis on the pleasure of choosing and planning. We've moved quite dramatically away from the very first video games. These games mostly involved the player merely reacting to events. But games, then, became more about approaching a situation and making a plan depending on your preferred play style.' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows Dan Pinchbeck saying that modern games are more complicated because players plan their moves, while the very first video games were much simpler because players only reacted to events.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "D" means Dan Pinchbeck is the expert who said that the first video games were much simpler than the games made today.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because Dan Pinchbeck explains how video games have changed over time. He states that modern games are "increasingly complex systems" where players must plan and make choices. In contrast, the very first video games ("early games") were much simpler, because players only had to react to what happened on the screen.
Q24 failure Raph Koster, designer of multiplayer fantasy games, points out that an effective learning environment is one in which failure is acceptable, even welcomed. Accordingly, Koster says that in games, the player enters into a situation where the rules of the real world don't apply - and typically being judged on success and failure is part of the real world. When gaming, people feel free to try things and to learn, and not worry about what might happen Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Koster sees games as a special place where failing is totally okay. In games, players are not judged like they are in real life, so they can try things without worrying about failing.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "failure" means not being successful or making a mistake.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "failure" because Raph Koster explains that games create a space where the usual pressure of the real world does not apply. In everyday life, people are evaluated on whether they succeed or not, but in games, "failure" is welcomed and accepted. Because of this, players do not worry about making mistakes and feel free to try new things. Therefore, games take away people's fear of failure.
Q25 disapproval 'Something we don't talk about is that, actually, one of the strengths of games is the vague sense of disapproval that still surrounds them - they feel like something that's forbidden!' And that can, of course, be very exciting Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains Margaret Robertson's thought: games are exciting because some people still dislike or criticize them, making playing games feel like doing something forbidden.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "disapproval" means the feeling or belief that something is bad, wrong, or not accepted by other people.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "disapproval" because Margaret Robertson states in paragraph B that games have an appealing strength due to the "vague sense of disapproval" that surrounds them, making them feel forbidden and therefore very exciting. The phrase "associated with them" matches the passage's description of disapproval that "still surrounds them", and both describe this feeling as something that makes games "exciting".
Q26 short As in many films, a short final act is often used to give a sense of acceleration towards a preferably startling climax. Opening levels of games are also short, because this flatters us into thinking we're making good progress, whereas the middle levels are more extensive Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that both the ending part (the final act) and the beginning part (the opening levels) of games are made short in duration, while the middle part is longer.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "short" means not lasting for a long time or small in length.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "short" because paragraph E discusses how narrative games are built using a three-part structure. The passage notes that games use a "short final act" (the last part) and that the "opening levels" (the first part) are "also short". Therefore, both the first and last parts of these games are described with the word "short".
