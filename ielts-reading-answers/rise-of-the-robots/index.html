Rise Of The Robots - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From Expert on Cambridge IELTS 08 Academic Reading Test 1 · Part 3 · Questions 27–40

Skyrocket your IELTS band score by 1-2 points in under a month with our premium plan!

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 27-40, which are based on Reading Passage 3 below.

Rise of the Robots

If you're into technology, you're living in wonderful times. Things are developing in leaps and bounds, especially gadgets. Let's look at the technology that's set to break through.

CELESTRON SKYSCOUT

Backyard star-gazing goes seriously hi-tech with the Celestron SkyScout, which was judged to be the Best of Innovations at the New York Consumer Electronics Show press preview event in November. It's not difficult to see why. The SkyScout is a hand-held viewing device that's capable of finding and identifying more than 6000 celestial objects visible to the naked eye, thus transforming the night sky into your own personal planetarium. Using GPS technology and a substantial celestial database, the camcorder-sized SkyScout enables stargazers to point the device at any visible object in the sky, press a button and then listen to a commentary. For the truly celestially challenged, if you want to view a star or planet but haven't a clue which bit of the heavens to look in, don't despair; the SkyScout's "locate" feature will guide you to it using illuminated arrows in the viewfinder.

NOKIA N91

This amazing mobile jukebox is due out early in 2006. Nokia's N91 looks set to be in a class of its own as a multimedia mobile phone. It will play music, take photos, surf the web and download videos, store contact details and generally organise your life. The robust little phone, resplendent in its stainless steel case, is the first Nokia to be equipped with a hard drive (4Gb), which means that it can store up to 3000 songs. The N91, which has a hi-fi quality headset and remote control, supports a wide range of digital music formats, including MP3, Real, WAV and WMA. It uses wireless technology to allow users to find and buy music from the operator's music-store. You can also drag and drop music from your PC to the N91 and manage and share play-lists. If you can find the time, you can get on the blower, too.

SEIKO SPECTRUM E-PAPER WATCH

The Seiko Spectrum is no ordinary wristwatch. At first glance, it's an attractive and futuristic bracelet-style watch. Look closer, however, and you'll notice that its display is unlike any you've seen before. Rather than the usual LCD screen, the display is made of "e-paper" – from the electronic paper pioneers E Ink Corp – and shows a constantly changing mosaic pattern along with the time. Because e-paper is so flexible and thin, it allows the display to curve round the wrist along with the watch band – something conventional liquid-crystal displays cannot do, as they have to be flat. Seiko says the e-paper display not only produces far better contrast than an LCD screen, but requires no power to retain an image, so the batteries last longer. Seiko is releasing only 500 of the watches next month, priced at about £1,250 – so you'd better lose no time.

HIGH-DEFINITION TV

HDTV, already available in the United States, Japan and Australia, will hit the UK in 2006. When you watch a programme filmed in the HD format, you'll see a much sharper, clearer and more vibrant image. This is due partly to the way a programme is filmed, but also to the high-definition TV set itself, which uses either 720 or 1080 visible rows of pixels (depending on which format the individual HDTV uses) to display images, compared to the 576 rows of pixels used in current sets.

ELECTROLUX TRILOBITE 2.0 ROBOT VACUUM CLEANER

Next time you're expecting visitors, don't bother to vacuum first-wait until they arrive, and then entertain them with this little gadget. The Electrolux Trilobite 2.0 is a robotic vacuum cleaner that navigates its way around your floors using ultrasound, just like a bat. It pings out ultrasound vibrations at surfaces to create a map of the room, which it remembers for future cleaning assignments. The Trilobite has no problem avoiding collision with things placed on the floor. Special magnetic strips are placed in doorways, near stairs and other openings. These act as a wall, keeping the Trilobite in the room. You can also programme it to glide round when you're at work or after you've gone to bed. When Electrolux introduced the original Trilobite in 2001, it was voted among the 100 most-innovative designs (though whether the judges were dedicated couch-potatoes, and thus biased, we were not able to discover). The name comes from the hard-shelled sea creature from the Paleozoic era (between 250 million and 560 million years ago) that roamed the ocean floor feeding on particles and small animals.

HONDA ASIMO ROBOT

Need an extra pair of hands around the office? Look no further; this month, the Honda Motor Company showcased its second-generation humanoid robot, Asimo. The machine has come a long way since its first incarnation five years ago. The 1.3 metre-tall droid is now capable of performing a variety of office tasks, including reception duties, serving drinks and acting as an information guide, as well as making deliveries. Using multiple sensors, Asimo has the ability to recognise the surrounding environment and interact with people using integrated circuit tags. It can walk and run at a fair pace, and push a cart. Honda plans to start using Asimo's receptionist functions at its Wako Building in Japan early in 2006, and it's hoped it will become available for leasing afterwards. It could soon be pushing a cart at an office near you.

Questions

Questions 27–30 One Choice

For each question, only ONE of the choices is correct. Write the corresponding letter.

27 The Celestron SkyScout can
  1. tell you information about the stars.
  2. tell you where in the world you are.
  3. find objects in the sky that are not normally visible.
28 The Seiko Spectrum e-paper watch
  1. cannot be bent.
  2. can be used for surfing the internet.
  3. is being produced as a limited edition.
29 The Electrolux Trilobite 2.0 robot vacuum cleaner
  1. asks permission before moving from room to room.
  2. uses lasers to help it avoid objects.
  3. is programmable.
30 The Honda Asimo robot
  1. has two pairs of hands.
  2. uses lasers to help it recognize its surroundings.
  3. can run.

Questions 31–35 Sentence Completion

Complete the following sentences using NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS from the text.

  • The SkyScout uses GPS and 31 to help you find a star.
  • The Seiko Spectrum doesn't need batteries to power the 32.
  • HDTV uses more 33 than conventional TV.
  • The Trilobite 2.0 could be used to 34 guests.
  • Asimo first appeared 35.

Questions 36–40 True / False / Not Given

Do the following statements agree with the information given in the text?

TRUE if the information in the text agrees with the statement

FALSE if the information in the text contradicts the statement

NOT GIVEN if there is no information on this

36 The Nokia N91 is strong.
37 E-paper can be torn easily.
38 HDTV is filmed differently to conventional TV.
39 The Trilobite 2.0 looks just like the original design.
40 Asimo is available for export.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 A Using GPS technology and a substantial celestial database, the camcorder-sized SkyScout enables stargazers to point the device at any visible object in the sky, press a button and then listen to a commentary Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that people who look at the stars can aim this small device at any visible sky object, push a button, and hear an audio description giving information about it.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the Celestron SkyScout device gives users facts and details about the stars.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is choice A because the passage describes the Celestron SkyScout as a tool that identifies celestial objects and lets stargazers "listen to a commentary" after pointing it at an object and pressing a button. Here, listening to a "commentary" directly matches the idea of getting information about stars. Choice B is incorrect because GPS is only used as a tool to identify objects, not to tell users where they are on Earth. Choice C is incorrect because the passage specifies that the objects are "visible to the naked eye," meaning they are normally visible.
Q28 C Seiko is releasing only 500 of the watches next month, priced at about £1,250 – so you'd better lose no time Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage means that the company Seiko is going to sell just 500 of these watches next month, so very few people will be able to buy one.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the company is only making a small number of the Seiko Spectrum e-paper watch.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the passage states that Seiko will release "only 500 of the watches". When a product is made in a small, fixed quantity like this, it is called a "limited edition". Choice A is incorrect because the watch can curve around the wrist. Choice B is incorrect because surfing the internet describes the Nokia phone, not the watch.
Q29 C You can also programme it to glide round when you're at work or after you've gone to bed Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that you can set up the vacuum cleaner in advance to clean your home while you are at your job or sleeping.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that users can set a schedule or give instructions to the robot vacuum cleaner so that it cleans automatically at a specific time.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the text directly states that users can set the machine to clean automatically when they are asleep or away at work. In the text, the verb "programme" directly matches the adjective "programmable" in the question. In contrast, choice A is incorrect because magnetic strips block it from leaving rather than it asking for permission, and choice B is incorrect because it uses "ultrasound", not lasers.
Q30 C It can walk and run at a fair pace, and push a cart Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage clearly states that the robot is able to walk, run, and push a cart.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the Honda Asimo robot has the ability to run.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the text clearly says that Asimo is able to both walk and run at a decent speed. Choice A is incorrect because "an extra pair of hands" is just an expression meaning extra help. Choice B is incorrect because the passage mentions it uses multiple sensors and integrated circuit tags to sense surroundings, not lasers.
Q31 arrows / illuminated arrows the SkyScout's "locate" feature will guide you to it using illuminated arrows in the viewfinder Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when you are looking for a star or planet, the device will lead you to the right spot by showing glowing arrows through its viewing screen.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means glowing pointers or light-up pointer symbols that direct you where to look.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "arrows" or "illuminated arrows" because the text describes how the SkyScout directs a person trying to find an object in space. The passage mentions that the device uses GPS technology, and if you want to find a star or planet but do not know where to look, its "locate" tool will guide you by using "illuminated arrows in the viewfinder". Therefore, "arrows" or "illuminated arrows" completes the description of what the device uses to help find a star.
Q32 e-paper display Seiko says the e-paper display not only produces far better contrast than an LCD screen, but requires no power to retain an image, so the batteries last longer Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the watch's screen, called an e-paper display, uses no electricity to keep showing an image, allowing the batteries to last much longer.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to the special screen of the watch, known as an "e-paper display", which does not need electrical energy to hold an image.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "e-paper display" because the text explains that this specific screen "requires no power to retain an image, so the batteries last longer." In the sentence, "doesn't need batteries to power" matches the passage's keywords "requires no power" and "batteries last longer", pointing directly to the "e-paper display".
Q33 rows of pixels uses either 720 or 1080 visible rows of pixels (depending on which format the individual HDTV uses) to display images, compared to the 576 rows of pixels used in current sets Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that an HDTV screen displays images with 720 or 1080 lines of screen dots, which is more than the 576 lines used by standard television screens today.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to lines of tiny colored dots on a screen that come together to show a picture.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the section on High-Definition TV. The text explains that an HDTV displays images using either 720 or 1080 visible "rows of pixels", whereas conventional or current televisions only use 576 "rows of pixels". Therefore, HDTV uses more rows of pixels than standard TVs.
Q34 entertain Next time you're expecting visitors, don't bother to vacuum first-wait until they arrive, and then entertain them with this little gadget Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage tells the reader that when people come to visit, you can use the robot vacuum to entertain them.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "entertain" means to provide fun, amusement, or interest to people so they have an enjoyable time.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "entertain" because the passage suggests waiting until guests come over and using the robot vacuum to amuse them. In the text, the word "visitors" means the same thing as "guests," and the action you can do with the gadget is "entertain" them.
Q35 five years ago The machine has come a long way since its first incarnation five years ago Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the robot has improved a lot since its very first version was created five years ago.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "five years ago" means a time period of five years in the past.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is found in the section about the Honda Asimo robot. The sentence in the task asks when Asimo first appeared. In the passage, the word "incarnation" refers to a version or form of something. The text says that the robot has progressed greatly since its "first incarnation five years ago", meaning it first appeared five years ago.
Q36 TRUE The robust little phone, resplendent in its stainless steel case, is the first Nokia to be equipped with a hard drive (4Gb), which means that it can store up to 3000 songs Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the Nokia N91 phone is robust (meaning strong and sturdy) and protected by a metal case made of stainless steel.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means that the text confirms that the Nokia N91 phone is sturdy and strong.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage explicitly describes the Nokia N91 as a "robust" phone in a "stainless steel case." The word "robust" means strong, tough, and not easily broken, which directly matches the statement that the phone is strong.
Q37 NOT GIVEN Because e-paper is so flexible and thin, it allows the display to curve round the wrist along with the watch band – something conventional liquid-crystal displays cannot do, as they have to be flat Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that e-paper can bend and is very thin, but it does not give any information about tearing it.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether e-paper can be easily ripped or torn.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage discusses e-paper in the section about the Seiko Spectrum watch, describing it as "flexible and thin" and able to bend around the wrist. However, there is no mention anywhere in the text about how easily e-paper can be torn. Therefore, this information is not provided.
Q38 TRUE When you watch a programme filmed in the HD format, you'll see a much sharper, clearer and more vibrant image. This is due partly to the way a programme is filmed, but also to the high-definition TV set itself Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that an HD programme looks much clearer partly because of how it is recorded and filmed.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means the statement matches the facts given in the text: programmes for high-definition TV are recorded in a different way compared to normal, standard TV programmes.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage explains that the clearer picture of high-definition TV comes "partly to the way a programme is filmed". This shows that making an HD show involves a different method of filming than traditional TV programmes.
Q39 NOT GIVEN When Electrolux introduced the original Trilobite in 2001, it was voted among the 100 most-innovative designs Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage tells us that the first Trilobite came out in 2001 and won an award for design, but it does not tell us what it looked like or if the new version looks identical to it.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether the Trilobite 2.0 looks the same as or different from the original version.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage mentions the "original Trilobite in 2001" and states that it was chosen as one of the most innovative designs, but it never describes or compares the physical appearance or look of the Trilobite 2.0 with the first model.
Q40 NOT GIVEN Honda plans to start using Asimo's receptionist functions at its Wako Building in Japan early in 2006, and it's hoped it will become available for leasing afterwards Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains where Honda will first use the robot and mentions future plans for renting it out, but it never says if the robot can be exported.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether the Asimo robot can be sent to or sold in other countries.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage does not provide any information about exporting Asimo. The text mentions that Honda plans to use the robot in its building in Japan and hopes to make it available for leasing later, but it never mentions whether Asimo is available for export to other countries.
