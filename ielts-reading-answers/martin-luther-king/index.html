Martin Luther King - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From Succeed in IELTS Academic Reading Test 8 · Part 3 · Questions 27–40

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 27 - 40, which are based on Reading Passage 3 below.

Martin Luther King

A

Martin Luther King was born on January 15, 1929, in Atlanta, Georgia. He was the son of the Reverend Martin Luther King, Sr. and Alberta Williams King. He had an older sister, Willie Christine King, and a younger brother Alfred Daniel Williams King. Growing up in Atlanta, King attended Booker T. Washington High School. He skipped ninth and twelfth grade, and entered Morehouse College at age fifteen without formally graduating from high school. From the time that Martin was born, he knew that black people and white people had different rights in certain parts of America. If a black family wanted to eat at a restaurant, they had to sit in a separate section of the restaurant. They had to sit at the back of the cinema, and even use separate toilets. Worse, and perhaps even more humiliating still, in many southern states, if a black man was on a bus and all the seats were taken, he would have to endure the indignity of relinquishing his own seat to a white man. King could never understand the terrible injustice of this.

In 1948, he graduated with a Bachelor of Arts degree in sociology. Later, King began doctoral studies in systematic theology at Boston University and received his Doctor of Philosophy on June 5, 1955. King married Coretta Scott, on June 18, 1953 and they had four children.

B

Returning to the South to become pastor of a Baptist Church in Montgomery, Alabama, King first achieved national renown when he helped mobilise the black boycott of the Montgomery bus system in 1955. This was organised after Rosa Parks, a black woman, refused to give up her seat on the bus to a white man - in the segregated south, black people could only sit at the back of the bus. The 382-day boycott led the bus company to change its regulations, and the Supreme Court declared such segregation unconstitutional.

C

In 1957 King was active in the organisation of the Southern Christian Leadership Conference (SCLC), formed to co-ordinate protests against discrimination. He advocated non-violent direct action based on the methods of Gandhi, who led protests against British rule in India culminating in India's independence in 1947. In 1963, King led mass protests against discriminatory practices in Birmingham, Alabama, where the white population were violently resisting desegregation. The city was dubbed 'Bombingham' as attacks against civil rights protesters increased, and King was arrested and jailed for his part in the protests.

D

After his release, King participated in the enormous civil rights march, in Washington, in August 1963, and delivered his famous 'I have a dream' speech, predicting a day when the promise of freedom and equality for all would become a reality in America. In 1964 he was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize. In 1965, he led a campaign to register blacks to vote. The same year the US Congress passed the Voting Rights Act outlawing the discriminatory practices that had barred blacks from voting in the south.

E

As the civil rights movement became increasingly radicalised, King found that his message of peaceful protest was not shared by many in the younger generation. King began to protest against the Vietnam War and poverty levels in the US. On March 29, 1968, King went to Memphis, Tennessee, in support of the black sanitary public works employees who had been on strike since March 12 for higher wages and better treatment. In one incident, black street repairmen had received pay for two hours when they were sent home because of bad weather, but white employees had been paid for the full day. King could not bear to stand by and let such patent acts of racism go unnoticed. He moved to unite his people, and all the peoples of America on the receiving end of discriminatory practices, to protest for their rights, peacefully but steadfastly.

F

On his trip to Memphis, King was booked into room 306 at the Lorraine Motel, owned by Walter Bailey. King was shot at 6:01 p.m. April 4, 1968 while he was standing on the motel's second-floor balcony. King was rushed to St. Joseph's Hospital, where doctors opened his chest and performed manual heart massage. He was pronounced dead at 7:05 p.m. King's autopsy revealed that although he was only 39 years old, he had the heart of a 60-year-old man.

Questions 27–31 One Choice

Choose the correct letter A, B, C or D.

27 From a young age Martin Luther King
  1. wanted to protest for the rights of black people.
  2. could not understand why black people were treated differently.
  3. was not allowed to go to the cinema or to restaurants.
  4. was aware that black people were being humiliated in many northern states.
28 What initially made Martin Luther King famous?
  1. the black boycott of the Montgomery bus system
  2. becoming a pastor at a Baptist Church
  3. when Rosa Parks refused to give up her seat on a bus
  4. when he persuaded Rosa Parks not to give up her bus seat to a white man
29 What influenced Martin Luther King regarding non-violence?
  1. India's independence in 1947
  2. Christianity
  3. the Southern Christian Leadership Conference
  4. the methods of Gandhi
30 What did Martin Luther King fight for in 1965?
  1. the right of black people to vote
  2. the actions of the US Congress
  3. the right to win the Nobel Peace Prize
  4. the right of black people to travel abroad

  1. It was helping the war in Vietnam.
  2. It brought the younger generation together.
  3. It had been exploited by politicians who wanted to get more votes.
  4. The protesters sometimes behaved too violently.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 B From the time that Martin was born, he knew that black people and white people had different rights in certain parts of America. If a black family wanted to eat at a restaurant, they had to sit in a separate section of the restaurant. They had to sit at the back of the cinema, and even use separate toilets. Worse, and perhaps even more humiliating still, in many southern states, if a black man was on a bus and all the seats were taken, he would have to endure the indignity of relinquishing his own seat to a white man. King could never understand the terrible injustice of this Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that from birth, King saw that black and white people were treated unequally, such as being separated in public places or being forced to surrender seats on buses, and he could never understand why this unfair treatment existed.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that ever since he was a child, Martin Luther King could not make sense of why black individuals did not receive the same fair treatment as white individuals.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because Paragraph A describes Martin Luther King's early life and states that "From the time that Martin was born, he knew that black people and white people had different rights in certain parts of America." After mentioning several examples of unfair and unequal rules (such as separate restaurant sections, separate toilets, and giving up bus seats), the text concludes: "King could never understand the terrible injustice of this." Here, "never understand the terrible injustice" corresponds directly to "could not understand why black people were treated differently."
Q28 A King first achieved national renown when he helped mobilise the black boycott of the Montgomery bus system in 1955 Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Martin Luther King became famous throughout the country for the first time by helping organize the bus boycott in Montgomery in 1955.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Martin Luther King first became well known across the country because of the Montgomery bus protest.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because Paragraph B states that King first became famous nationwide when he helped organize the bus protest in Montgomery. In the text, the phrase 'first achieved national renown' matches the question's phrase 'initially made Martin Luther King famous.'
Q29 D He advocated non-violent direct action based on the methods of Gandhi, who led protests against British rule in India culminating in India's independence in 1947 Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that Martin Luther King encouraged peaceful protest without violence, and he based this strategy on the protest ways of Gandhi.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Martin Luther King learned and copied his ideas about peaceful protest from the ways Mahatma Gandhi protested.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because paragraph C explicitly states that King supported peaceful protests ("non-violent direct action") using the techniques and ideas ("methods") of Gandhi. Therefore, Gandhi's peaceful approach directly influenced King.
Q30 A In 1965, he led a campaign to register blacks to vote Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that in 1965, Martin Luther King organized and guided an effort so that black people could sign up and cast their votes.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Martin Luther King worked to make sure black people were allowed to vote.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because paragraph D clearly says that in the year 1965, King led a major effort or movement to help black citizens sign up to vote. Key words to notice are "In 1965" and "campaign to register blacks to vote", where leading a "campaign to register" means fighting for their "right to vote".
Q31 D As the civil rights movement became increasingly radicalised, King found that his message of peaceful protest was not shared by many in the younger generation Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that as the movement grew more extreme, many young people did not agree with Martin Luther King's belief in peaceful protesting.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Martin Luther King believed that some people in the civil rights movement were acting with too much violence instead of staying peaceful.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the text states in Paragraph E that the movement became more extreme and that many younger people did not follow his idea of peaceful action. In this context, "radicalised" and not sharing his "message of peaceful protest" match the idea that protesters were acting too aggressively or violently.
Q32 YES The 382-day boycott led the bus company to change its regulations, and the Supreme Court declared such segregation unconstitutional Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that after the protest lasted 382 days, the bus company changed its rules, and the highest court in the country decided that separating black and white passengers was illegal.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means that the statement is true and matches the information given in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because the boycott achieved its goals. In paragraph B, the text explains that the protest caused the bus company to "change its regulations" and led the Supreme Court to rule that separating people by race on buses was against the law ("unconstitutional"). These positive outcomes show that the protest was indeed a success.
Q33 NO In 1963, King led mass protests against discriminatory practices in Birmingham, Alabama, where the white population were violently resisting desegregation Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that during 1963 in Birmingham, Alabama, the white community fought hard and used violence to stop desegregation from happening.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement is false because the passage says the opposite.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because the statement says that white people in Alabama wanted desegregation in 1963, but the text clearly states that they were "violently resisting desegregation." To "resist" something means to fight against or oppose it, which is the opposite of wanting it.
Q34 NOT GIVEN King began to protest against the Vietnam War and poverty levels in the US Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage simply says that King started speaking out against the Vietnam War and poverty in America, but it does not say what happened as a result of his protests.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the text does not say whether Martin Luther King was successful or achieved a lot in his protests against the war in Vietnam.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage only mentions that King "began to protest against the Vietnam War". It never talks about the results of these protests or whether he "achieved a lot" through them. Since this specific information is missing from the text, the statement cannot be confirmed as true or false.
Q35 v Martin Luther King was born on January 15, 1929, in Atlanta, Georgia. He was the son of the Reverend Martin Luther King, Sr. and Alberta Williams King. He had an older sister, Willie Christine King, and a younger brother Alfred Daniel Williams King. Growing up in Atlanta, King attended Booker T. Washington High School Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage tells us when and where Martin Luther King was born, who his parents and siblings were, and where he went to school as a child. This information introduces his life history and background.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'v' means that the best heading for Paragraph A is 'The background of an iconic man'.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is v because Paragraph A describes the early life, education, and family of Martin Luther King, who is a famous historical figure (an 'iconic man'). The paragraph details where he was born, his parents and siblings, his schooling, his marriage, and how he grew up experiencing racial segregation in the American South. All of these details present his personal and early 'background'.
Q36 iv King first achieved national renown when he helped mobilise the black boycott of the Montgomery bus system in 1955 Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Martin Luther King became famous across the country because he helped bring people together to refuse to ride the buses in Montgomery as a peaceful protest.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "iv" means that the best title for Paragraph B is "Protests and action".
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "iv" because Paragraph B focuses on how Martin Luther King took action by helping to organize the Montgomery bus boycott. A boycott is a form of public protest where people refuse to use a service to demand change. In this paragraph, the author describes this major protest action and how it succeeded in changing unfair rules. Key words to look for include "boycott" and "mobilise", which both connect directly to taking action and protesting.
Q37 ii He advocated non-violent direct action based on the methods of Gandhi, who led protests against British rule in India culminating in India's independence in 1947. In 1963, King led mass protests against discriminatory practices in Birmingham, Alabama, where the white population were violently resisting desegregation. The city was dubbed 'Bombingham' as attacks against civil rights protesters increased, and King was arrested and jailed for his part in the protests Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Martin Luther King believed in peaceful, non-violent protests, even while facing severe violence and bomb attacks from people who resisted equal rights.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "ii" refers to the heading "Unhappy about violence," which means being troubled by violent behavior and choosing peaceful methods instead.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is ii because Paragraph C focuses on Martin Luther King's commitment to peace and the violent reaction he faced. Specifically, King supported "non-violent direct action" rather than physical fighting, showing his opposition to violence. The paragraph also describes how opponents were "violently resisting" civil rights with dangerous attacks that earned Birmingham the nickname 'Bombingham.' Keywords and related ideas include "non-violent" (not using violence) and "violently resisting" / "attacks" (acts of violence).
Q38 i After his release, King participated in the enormous civil rights march, in Washington, in August 1963, and delivered his famous 'I have a dream' speech, predicting a day when the promise of freedom and equality for all would become a reality in America Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Martin Luther King joined a big march in Washington in 1963 and gave his very famous speech called "I have a dream" about freedom and equal rights for everyone in America.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Paragraph D matches the title "The memorable speech".
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is i because Paragraph D talks about Martin Luther King delivering his famous "I have a dream" speech in Washington in August 1963. The word "memorable" in the heading directly connects with the word "famous" used in the text to describe his well-known speech.
Q39 vi King began to protest against the Vietnam War and poverty levels in the US Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that King started speaking out and protesting against the war in Vietnam, showing his involvement in global and international matters outside America.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "vi" means heading number 6, which is "Making his mark internationally" (becoming influential and known on global issues beyond his own country).
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading vi because Paragraph E describes how Martin Luther King expanded his activism beyond local civil rights issues to global affairs. Specifically, the passage mentions that he started speaking out against the "Vietnam War", an international conflict, which showed him taking a stand on worldwide foreign policy matters.
Q40 iii King was shot at 6:01 p.m. April 4, 1968 while he was standing on the motel's second-floor balcony. King was rushed to St. Joseph's Hospital, where doctors opened his chest and performed manual heart massage. He was pronounced dead at 7:05 p.m Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that someone shot King while he was on a motel balcony, and despite doctors trying to save his life at the hospital, he died shortly after.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Paragraph F describes a very sad, terrible, and painful event.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading iii because Paragraph F focuses entirely on the shooting and death of Martin Luther King Jr. In English, a "tragic incident" means a deeply sad and terrible event, which directly matches words in the text like "shot" and "pronounced dead".
