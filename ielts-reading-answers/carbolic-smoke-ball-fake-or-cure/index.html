Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 14-26, which are based on Reading Passage 2 below.

CARBOLIC SMOKE BALL: FAKE OR CURE?

The curious case of the carbolic smoke ball forced companies to treat customers honestly and openly and still has impact today.

The 1892 case of Carlill and the Carbolic Smoke Ball Company is an odd tale set against the backdrop of the swirling mists and fog of Victorian London, a terrifying Russian flu pandemic, and a forest of unregulated quack medicines offering cures for just about everything.

It is a case known to legal students the world over. It is also seen by some as the birth of modern consumer protection as it helped to define in law the trading relationship between a company and its customers.

The carbolic smoke ball was a peculiar device marketed as a cure for various ailments including influenza. It consisted of a rubber ball, filled with powdered carbolic acid. You squeezed the ball sending a puff of acidic smoke right up a tube inserted into your nose. The idea was that your nose would run and the cold would be flushed out.

The company making the ball advertised it in the Pall Mall Gazette offering a £100 reward to anyone using it correctly who then contracted influenza. They deposited £1,000 in the Alliance Bank in Regent Street to show the money was there. The advertisement also contained testimonials from a raft of aristocrats and clergy - the Victorian equivalent of today's celebrity endorsement.

It was seen by one Mrs Louisa Elizabeth Carlill. She bought a ball and used it, as directed, three times daily for nearly two months, then promptly caught the flu. Not surprisingly she claimed her reward from the company.

The manufacturers ignored two letters from her husband who had trained as a solicitor. They finally replied saying that if used properly they had total confidence in their product. In order to protect themselves against a fraudulent claim they stated Mrs Carlill would have to attend their offices at 27 Princess Street, Hanover Square, London each day and have her use of the ball checked by their secretary.

Mrs Carlill sued, claiming there was a contract between her and the company. The company denied there was one. She won her case in the High Court. The company and its proprietor, Mr Frederick Rowe, appealed. They raised almost every conceivable reason why there was no contract between themselves and Mrs Carlill. Among their arguments were the following: First, they said the advertisement was "mere puff", like a modern toothpaste which claims to leave your teeth "whiter than white". Claims like these are not meant to have any legal consequences and are understood not to by the public. The company failed largely because they had deposited £1,000 to show that they were serious. Second, they maintained there was no way of knowing whether Mrs Carlill had followed the instructions correctly. That would have been a good argument had the court not simply dismissed it out of hand. Next they said an offer could not be understood to have been made to the entire world.

Again the court disagreed. It said that if the offer was clear and to a group, anyone fulfilling the terms could be deemed to have accepted it. There was no need for those accepting to say who they were when they accepted the offer.

One of the judges, Lord Bowen, put it like this: "If I advertise to the world that my dog is lost, and that anybody who brings the dog to a particular place will be paid some money, are all the police or other persons whose business it is to find lost dogs to be expected to sit down and write me a note saying that they have accepted my proposal?" The company also argued there was no "consideration" or "thing of value" passing between Mrs Carlill and themselves. To be valid all contracts must have consideration. The court decided any use of the smoke ball would be likely to promote sales, and would thus be of value to the company. Mrs Carlill's use of the smoke ball was therefore deemed to be good or "valuable" consideration.

Mrs Carlill won the appeal, and so significantly helped to define the relationship between companies, their products and their customers. However, it was not until the Thalidomide scandal in 1961, which led to the Medicines Act of 1968 and the Trade Descriptions Act of the same year, that there was a clear statutory improvement in consumer rights.

The carbolic smoke ball case helped ordinary people to fight big companies. In 1992, Hoover came unstuck when it offered customers free flights – initially to Europe and then to the United States – if they bought over £100 worth of its appliances. Thousands of people took up the offer.

The company simply could not keep up with demand. Customers claimed Hoover was not keeping its promises. Hoover found itself fighting legal battles up and down the country and ended up losing nearly £50 million. It was one of the biggest marketing disasters of all time.

And what of Mrs Carlill who set the consumer rights ball rolling? She died on 10 March 1942 at the age of 96. Clearly her considerable age contributed to her death. But on the death certificate, there was only one cause stated: influenza.

Questions 20–23 Summary Completion

Complete the summary below.

Choose NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS from the passage for each answer.

The Carbolic Smoke Ball Company made a product called the "smoke ball". It claimed to be a cure for 20 and a number of other diseases. The smoke ball was a 21 ball, which was filled with 22. A 23 was then inserted into the user's nose. It was squeezed at the bottom to release a puff of acidic smoke into the nose of the user. This would cause the nose to run, and hopefully flush out the viral infection.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q14 YES It is also seen by some as the birth of modern consumer protection as it helped to define in law the trading relationship between a company and its customers Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that this legal case is viewed as the start of modern consumer protection laws because it officially set legal rules for the business relationship between companies and buyers.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means that the text confirms that the Carbolic Smoke Ball court case was a model for consumer protection laws, particularly regarding how companies and buyers deal with one another.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because the passage clearly states that this legal case is considered by some to be the beginning of modern laws protecting consumers. It explicitly explains that the case helped establish in law how a business and its buyers relate to each other. Key phrases in the text like "birth of modern consumer protection" and "helped to define in law the trading relationship between a company and its customers" match the question statement directly.
Q15 NO The company making the ball advertised it in the Pall Mall Gazette offering a £100 reward to anyone using it correctly who then contracted influenza. They deposited £1,000 in the Alliance Bank in Regent Street to show the money was there Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the reward for catching the flu after using the device was £100, while the £1,000 placed in the bank was only put there to prove that the company had real funds.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement is false according to the passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because the advertisement did not promise to give the entire deposited amount (£1,000) as the reward. Instead, the company promised a reward of £100 to anyone who still caught influenza after using the ball properly. The £1,000 was simply placed in a bank account to prove that the company had enough money and was serious about paying the £100 rewards.
Q16 NO In order to protect themselves against a fraudulent claim they stated Mrs Carlill would have to attend their offices at 27 Princess Street, Hanover Square, London each day and have her use of the ball checked by their secretary Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that the company gave a clear reason: they wanted to protect themselves against dishonest claims, so they demanded that she visit their office every day to be monitored.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement is false according to the text. The company did give reasons when they refused to pay Mrs Carlill.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because the statement claims the company refused her request "for no reason," but the passage shows they actually provided reasons. Specifically, the company said they needed to protect themselves from a fake claim ("fraudulent claim") and required her to prove she used the product properly under supervision. Later, they gave several legal arguments in court, meaning they did not refuse her without giving any reasons.
Q17 YES She won her case in the High Court Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that Mrs. Carlill was successful and won her legal fight in the High Court.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means that the text confirms that Mrs. Carlill won her legal case when it was first heard in the High Court.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because the passage clearly states that Mrs. Carlill took the company to court and won her lawsuit in the High Court before the company decided to appeal. In legal terms, "suit" is a synonym for a legal "case", which directly matches the fact that she was successful in the High Court.
Q18 NO First, they said the advertisement was "mere puff", like a modern toothpaste which claims to leave your teeth "whiter than white" Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the Carbolic Smoke Ball Company argued their advertisement was just simple marketing exaggeration ("mere puff"), comparing it to a toothpaste advertisement as an example.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is NO because the statement is false according to the passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because it was not a toothpaste company that made this argument. It was the Carbolic Smoke Ball Company defending itself in court. The text only mentions modern toothpaste as an example or comparison (comparing their advertisement to an exaggerated toothpaste claim). Because the statement claims that a "toothpaste company" said this, it contradicts the passage.
Q19 NOT GIVEN However, it was not until the Thalidomide scandal in 1961, which led to the Medicines Act of 1968 and the Trade Descriptions Act of the same year, that there was a clear statutory improvement in consumer rights Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that the Thalidomide problem in 1961 brought about the Medicines Act of 1968 to improve buyer rights, but it does not say whether a customer won any court battle.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the text does not give enough information to confirm if a customer won a legal case during the 1961 Thalidomide event.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage only mentions that the Thalidomide scandal in 1961 resulted in the Medicines Act of 1968. However, the text never says anything about a customer winning a lawsuit in that situation. Because this reason is missing from the passage, the statement cannot be proven true or false.
Q20 influenza The carbolic smoke ball was a peculiar device marketed as a cure for various ailments including influenza Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the smoke ball was sold to heal different kinds of sicknesses, and one of those sicknesses was the flu (influenza).
Answer Explanation:
The answer "influenza" means the flu, which is a common viral infection that causes fever, cough, and body aches.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "influenza" because the text explains what the device was advertised to heal. In the passage, it states that the smoke ball was "marketed as a cure for various ailments including influenza." Here, "marketed as a cure for" matches the summary's phrase "claimed to be a cure for", and "various ailments" corresponds to "a number of other diseases". Therefore, "influenza" correctly fills in the blank.
Q21 rubber It consisted of a rubber ball, filled with powdered carbolic acid Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the device was made of a ball made from rubber, and inside it was a powder made of carbolic acid.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "rubber" refers to a strong, stretchy, waterproof material often used to make balls and tires.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "rubber" because the summary asks what kind of ball the smoke ball was. The passage directly states that the product "consisted of a rubber ball, filled with powdered carbolic acid." Therefore, "rubber" is the exact word that describes the ball.
Q22 powdered carbolic acid / carbolic acid It consisted of a rubber ball, filled with powdered carbolic acid Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says the smoke ball was a rubber ball. Inside the ball was powdered carbolic acid.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the ball was filled with powdered carbolic acid, or just carbolic acid.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "(powdered) carbolic acid" because the summary says the ball "was filled with" something, and the passage says "filled with powdered carbolic acid." Pay attention to the matching words "filled with." Both "powdered carbolic acid" and "carbolic acid" fit the three-word limit.
Q23 tube You squeezed the ball sending a puff of acidic smoke right up a tube inserted into your nose Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when a person pressed the rubber ball, a cloud of smoke went directly through a pipe placed inside their nose.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "tube" refers to a long, hollow, pipe-like piece through which air or smoke can travel.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "tube" because the passage explains how the smoke ball device was used. According to the text, when the ball was squeezed, acidic smoke travelled up a "tube inserted into your nose". The summary statement describes the exact same action: "A [tube] was then inserted into the user's nose."
Q24 B The manufacturers ignored two letters from her husband who had trained as a solicitor Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Mrs Carlill's husband wrote two letters to the company that made the smoke ball, but the company did not pay attention to them at first.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means that the husband of Mrs Louisa Elizabeth Carlill wrote and mailed letters to the makers of the carbolic smoke ball.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage states that the makers of the product did not answer two letters sent by Mrs Carlill's husband. In the text, the phrase "ignored two letters from her husband" matches choice B, which says that her husband "sent letters to the carbolic smoke ball company".
Q25 D The company and its proprietor, Mr Frederick Rowe, appealed. They raised almost every conceivable reason why there was no contract between themselves and Mrs Carlill Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the owner of the company, Mr. Frederick Rowe, challenged the court's first decision by appealing, and he gave many different reasons to show that there was no agreement with Mrs. Carlill.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "D" means that Mr. Frederick Rowe asked a higher court to review the decision (appealed) and gave multiple points to argue his side.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the text clearly mentions that after Mrs. Carlill won in the High Court, the company and its owner, Mr. Frederick Rowe, appealed the case. During this appeal, they presented many different arguments ("almost every conceivable reason") to try to prove that no legal contract existed between them and Mrs. Carlill.
Q26 E One of the judges, Lord Bowen, put it like this: "If I advertise to the world that my dog is lost, and that anybody who brings the dog to a particular place will be paid some money, are all the police or other persons whose business it is to find lost dogs to be expected to sit down and write me a note saying that they have accepted my proposal?" Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that Judge Lord Bowen gave an example of a person offering money to find a lost dog. He asked if people who find the dog need to write a letter accepting the offer first. By asking this, he explained why the company's argument made no sense.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "E" means that Lord Bowen used a situation as an example (about finding a lost dog) to show why the company was wrong and reject their argument.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because the passage describes Lord Bowen as one of the judges who disagreed with the company's claims. To reject the company's argument that people needed to formally notify them of accepting an offer made to the public, Lord Bowen gave an illustrative example about offering a reward to anyone who returns a lost dog. In this context, "quoted an example" refers to his lost dog scenario, and "turn the company down" means rejecting the company's legal argument.
