Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 1-13, which are based on Reading Passage 1 below.

Sleepy Students Perform Worse

A Staying up an hour or two past bedtime makes it far harder for kids to learn, say scientists who deprived youngsters of sleep and tested whether their teachers could tell the difference. They could. If parents want their children to thrive academically, "Getting them to sleep on time is as important as getting them to school on time," said psychologist Gahan Fallone, who conducted the research at Brown Medical School.

B The study, unveiled Thursday at an American Medical Association science writers meeting, was conducted on healthy children who had no evidence of sleep or learning-related disorders. Difficulty paying attention was among the problems the sleepy youngsters faced-raising the question of whether sleep deprivation could prove even worse for people with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, or ADHD. Fallone now is studying that question, and suspects that sleep problems "could hit children with ADHD as a double whammy."

C Sleep experts have long warned that Americans of all ages don't get enough shuteye. Sleep is important for health, bringing a range of benefits that, as Shakespeare put it, "knits up the ravelled sleeve of care." Not getting enough is linked to a host of problems, from car crashes as drivers doze off to crippled memory and inhibited creativity. Exactly how much sleep correlates with school performance is hard to prove. So Brown researchers set out to test whether teachers could detect problems with attention and learning when children stayed up late – even if the teachers had no idea how much sleep their students actually got.

D They recruited seventy-four 6 to 12-year-olds from Rhode Island and southern Massachusetts for the three-week study. For one week, the youngsters went to bed and woke up at their usual times. They already were fairly good sleepers, getting nine to 9.5 hours of sleep a night. Another week, they were assigned to spend no fewer than ten hours in bed a night. The other week, they were kept up later than usual: First- and second-graders were in bed no more than eight hours and the older children no more than 6.5 hours. In addition to parents' reports, the youngsters wore motion-detecting wrist monitors to ensure compliance.

E Teachers weren't told how much the children slept or which week they stayed up late, but rated the students on a variety of performance measures each week. The teachers reported significantly more academic problems during the week of sleep deprivation, the study, which will be published in the journal Sleep in December, concluded. Students who got eight hours of sleep or less a night were more forgetful, had the most trouble learning new lessons, and had the most problems paying attention, reported Fallone, now at the Forest Institute of Professional Psychology.

F Sleep has long been a concern of educators. Potter-burns Elementary School sends notes to parents reminding them to make sure students get enough sleep prior to the school's yearly achievement testing. Another school considers it important enough to include in the school's monthly newsletters. Definitely there is an impact on students' performance if they come to school tired. However, the findings may change physician practice, said Dr. Regina Benjamin, a family physician in Bayou La Batre, who reviewed the data at Thursday's AMA meeting. "I don't ask about sleep" when evaluating academically struggling students, she noted. "I'm going to start."

G So how much sleep do kids need? Recommended amounts range from about ten to eleven hours a night for young elementary students to 8.5 hours for teens. Fallone insists that his own second-grader get ten hours a night, even when it meant dropping soccer the season that practice didn't start until 7:30 – too late for her to fit in dinner and time to wind down before she needed to be snoozing. "It's tough," he acknowledged, but "parents must believe in the importance of sleep."

Questions

Questions 1–4 Matching Information

The text has 7 paragraphs (A-G).

Which paragraph contains each of the following pieces of information?

1 Traffic accidents are sometimes caused by lack of sleep.
2 The number of children included in the study.
3 How two schools are trying to deal with the problem.
4 How the effect of having less sleep was measured.

Questions 5–8 Sentence Completion

Complete the following sentences using NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS from the text for each gap.

  • Fallone is now studying the sleep patterns of children with 5.
  • The researchers used 6 that show movement to check that children went to bed at the right times.
  • Students with less sleep had problems with memory, remembering new material and 7.
  • Fallone admitted that it was 8 for children to get enough sleep.

Questions 9–13 True / False / Not Given

Do the following statements agree with the information given in the text?

TRUE if the information in the text agrees with the statement

FALSE if the information in the text contradicts the statement

NOT GIVEN if there is no information on this

9 The results of the study were first distributed to principals of American schools.
10 Some of the children in the study had previously shown signs of sleeping problems.
11 The study could influence how doctors deal with children's health problems.
12 Fallone doesn't let his daughter play soccer.
13 Staying up later is acceptable if the child is doing homework.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 C Not getting enough is linked to a host of problems, from car crashes as drivers doze off to crippled memory and inhibited creativity Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that not sleeping enough causes many problems, such as car accidents when drivers fall asleep, as well as bad memory and reduced creativity.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Paragraph C is the section of the text that mentions that not getting enough sleep can cause road accidents.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because paragraph C directly connects a lack of sleep to car accidents. It states that not getting enough sleep leads to many problems, including "car crashes as drivers doze off". Here, "car crashes" means "traffic accidents", and "doze off" (falling asleep) is the result of "lack of sleep".
Q2 D They recruited seventy-four 6 to 12-year-olds from Rhode Island and southern Massachusetts for the three-week study Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that the researchers selected 74 children between the ages of 6 and 12 to participate in the three-week experiment.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is paragraph D because it states the exact count of young people who took part in the research.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because paragraph D directly mentions the number of children who participated in the experiment. The text states that the researchers recruited "seventy-four" children aged 6 to 12. Keywords to note are "recruited" (meaning gathered or enlisted participants), "seventy-four" (which answers "The number of children"), and "study" (which matches the task).
Q3 F Potter-burns Elementary School sends notes to parents reminding them to make sure students get enough sleep prior to the school's yearly achievement testing. Another school considers it important enough to include in the school's monthly newsletters Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains what two schools do: one elementary school sends reminders to parents before annual exams, and another school regularly writes about good sleep habits in its monthly letters.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Paragraph F describes the actions that two different schools take to handle the issue of children not sleeping enough.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is Paragraph F because it directly mentions two educational institutions tackling this issue. First, it names "Potter-burns Elementary School," which contacts parents before annual tests to remind them that children need rest. Second, it mentions "Another school" that writes about the importance of sleep in its monthly news updates to parents.
Q4 E Teachers weren't told how much the children slept or which week they stayed up late, but rated the students on a variety of performance measures each week Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that teachers scored and evaluated the students' classroom performance each week without knowing how many hours the students had slept.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "E" means that paragraph E explains the method used to test and check the results of children getting less sleep.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because this paragraph describes how researchers evaluated the impact of sleep loss. It states that teachers were not told about the students' sleep schedules, but they "rated the students on a variety of performance measures each week." Here, the keyword "measured" corresponds to "rated the students on a variety of performance measures," and the "effect of having less sleep" corresponds to finding "more academic problems during the week of sleep deprivation."
Q5 ADHD Difficulty paying attention was among the problems the sleepy youngsters faced-raising the question of whether sleep deprivation could prove even worse for people with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, or ADHD. Fallone now is studying that question, and suspects that sleep problems "could hit children with ADHD as a double whammy." Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that tired children had trouble focusing, which made scientists wonder if missing sleep is even more harmful to children with ADHD. It states that Fallone is currently researching this topic.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to ADHD, which is a condition that makes it hard for a person to pay attention, sit still, or control their actions.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by Paragraph B. The sentence mentions that the researcher, Fallone, is currently investigating how sleep loss affects children who have this specific condition. In the text, the phrase "Fallone now is studying that question" directly refers to researching the effects of sleep deprivation on people with "attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, or ADHD."
Q6 wrist monitors In addition to parents' reports, the youngsters wore motion-detecting wrist monitors to ensure compliance Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that besides reports from parents, children wore devices on their wrists that tracked movement to make sure they followed the study rules.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to special devices worn around the wrist that detect movement.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "wrist monitors" because paragraph D states what equipment the scientists used to check that the children followed the sleep schedule. The text mentions that "the youngsters wore motion-detecting wrist monitors to ensure compliance." Here, "motion-detecting" means "that show movement", and "ensure compliance" matches the idea of checking that the children went to bed at the designated times.
Q7 paying attention Students who got eight hours of sleep or less a night were more forgetful, had the most trouble learning new lessons, and had the most problems paying attention, reported Fallone, now at the Forest Institute of Professional Psychology Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that children who did not sleep enough forgot things more easily, struggled to learn new topics, and had a very hard time focusing.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means focusing, listening carefully, or staying concentrated on a task.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "paying attention" because paragraph E describes the negative effects on students who slept eight hours or less. The text notes that these students became more forgetful (problems with memory), had difficulty learning new lessons (remembering new material), and suffered from the most problems "paying attention". Matching these effects in the sentence shows that "paying attention" completes the list.
Q8 tough "It's tough," he acknowledged, but "parents must believe in the importance of sleep." Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows Fallone admitting that making sure children go to bed early and get enough sleep is difficult ("tough"), but he says parents still need to realize how important sleep is.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "tough" means difficult or hard to do.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "tough" because the passage mentions that Fallone admitted this difficulty when trying to make sure his own daughter got enough sleep. The text uses the word "acknowledged", which is a direct synonym for "admitted", right after his quote: "'It's tough,' he acknowledged".
Q9 FALSE The study, unveiled Thursday at an American Medical Association science writers meeting, was conducted on healthy children who had no evidence of sleep or learning-related disorders Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the research was first presented on a Thursday at a meeting of science writers for the American Medical Association.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE because the statement is incorrect according to the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the statement claims that the study's results were first shared with school principals. However, the text says the study was "unveiled" (shown or presented for the first time) at a meeting for American Medical Association science writers, not given to school principals.
Q10 FALSE The study, unveiled Thursday at an American Medical Association science writers meeting, was conducted on healthy children who had no evidence of sleep or learning-related disorders Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the research involved healthy kids who showed zero signs or proof of any problems with sleeping or learning.
Answer Explanation:
The answer FALSE means that the statement directly contradicts the facts given in the reading passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the text clearly says that the children chosen for the research were healthy and showed no previous signs of sleep issues. The statement claims that some of the children had previously shown signs of sleeping problems, but Paragraph B explicitly states the study was done on children who had 'no evidence of sleep or learning-related disorders.' Therefore, the statement is false.
Q11 TRUE However, the findings may change physician practice, said Dr. Regina Benjamin, a family physician in Bayou La Batre, who reviewed the data at Thursday's AMA meeting Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage means that the study's results might change the way doctors work when treating their patients.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means the passage clearly supports the statement that this research can change the way doctors treat or check children.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because Paragraph F mentions that the results of the research might alter how doctors do their jobs. In the text, Dr. Regina Benjamin, a family doctor, states that "the findings may change physician practice." She explains that she will begin asking struggling students about their sleep habits. Here, the keyword "findings" refers to "the study," and "physician practice" refers to how "doctors deal with" patients.
Q12 FALSE Fallone insists that his own second-grader get ten hours a night, even when it meant dropping soccer the season that practice didn't start until 7:30 – too late for her to fit in dinner and time to wind down before she needed to be snoozing Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Fallone made his daughter stop soccer only during the season when practice started late at 7:30 p.m., not that he bans her from playing the sport completely.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE because the statement claims that Fallone forbids his daughter from playing soccer, but this is not true.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the text shows that Fallone's daughter only stopped playing soccer for one specific season ("dropping soccer the season that practice didn't start until 7:30") because practice was too late for her bedtime. This means he does not forbid her from playing soccer in general; she only had to stop during that particular season so she could get enough sleep.
Q13 NOT GIVEN Staying up an hour or two past bedtime makes it far harder for kids to learn, say scientists who deprived youngsters of sleep and tested whether their teachers could tell the difference Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that going to bed late makes learning harder for children, but it never mentions whether doing homework makes going to bed late acceptable.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means the text does not mention whether it is okay for a child to stay up late to do homework.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage never discusses "homework" or whether doing schoolwork makes staying up late acceptable. While the text explains that "staying up" late harms learning and that sleeping on time is vital, it never provides any information about exceptions for homework.
