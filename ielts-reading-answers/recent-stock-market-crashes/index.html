Recent Stock-market Crashes - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From IELTS Mock Tests Academic Reading Test 58 · Part 2 · Questions 14–27

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 14-27 which are based on Reading Passage 2 below.

Recent stock-market crashes

For as long as there have been financial markets, there have been financial crises. Most economists agree, however, that from 1994 to 2013 crashes were deeper and the resultant troughs longer-lasting than in the 20-year period leading up to 1994. Two notable crashes, the Nifty Fifty in the mid-1970s and Black Monday in 1987, had an average loss of about 40% of the value of global stocks, and recovery took 240 days each, whereas the Dot-com and credit crises, post-1994, had an average loss of about 52%, and endured for 430 days. What economists do not agree upon is why recent crises have been so severe or how to prevent their recurrence.

John Coates, from the University of Cambridge in the UK and a former trader for Goldman Sachs and Deutsche Bank, believes three separate but related phenomena explain the severity. The first is dangerous but predictable risk-taking on the part of traders. The second is a lack of any risk-taking when markets become too volatile. (Coates does not advocate risk-aversion since risk-taking may jumpstart a depressed market.) The last is a new policy of transparency by the US Federal Reserve – known as the Fed – that may have encouraged stock-exchange complacency, compounding the dangerous risk-taking.

Many people imagine a trader to have a great head for maths and a stomach for the rollercoaster ride of the market, but Coates downplays arithmetic skills, and doubts traders are made of such stern stuff. Instead, he draws attention to the physiological nature of their decisions. Admittedly, there are women in the industry, but traders are overwhelmingly male, and testosterone appears to affect their choices.

Another common view is that traders are greedy as well as thrill-seeking. Coates has not researched financial incentive, but blood samples taken from London traders who engaged in simulated risk-taking exercises for him in 2013 confirmed the prevalence of testosterone, cortisol, and dopamine – a neurotransmitter precursor to adrenalin associated with raised blood pressure and sudden pleasure.

Certainly anyone faced with danger has a stress response involving the body's preparation for impending movement – for what is sometimes called 'Fight or flight', but, as Coates notes, any physical act at all produces a stress response: even a reader's eye movement along words in this line requires cortisol and adrenalin. Neuroscientists now see the brain not as a computer that acts neutrally, involved in a process of pure thought, but as a mechanism to plan and carry out a movement, since every single piece of information humans absorb has an attendant pattern of physical arousal.

For muscles to work, fuel is needed, so cortisol and adrenalin employ glucose from other muscles and the liver. To burn the fuel, oxygen is required, so slightly deeper or faster breathing occurs. To deliver fuel and oxygen to the body, the heart pumps a little harder and blood pressure rises. Thus, the stress response is a normal part of life, as well as a resource in fighting or fleeing. Indeed, it is a highly pleasurable experience in watching an action movie, making love or pulling off a multi-million-dollar stock-market deal.

Cortisol production also increases during exposure to uncertainty. For example, people who live next to a train line adjust to the noise of passing trains, but visitors to their home are disturbed. The phenomenon is equally well-known of anticipation being worse than an event itself: sitting in the waiting room thinking about a procedure may be more distressing than occupying the dentist's chair and having one. Interestingly, if a patient does not know approximately when he or she will be called for that procedure, cortisol levels are the most elevated of all. This appeared to happen with the London traders participating in some of Coates' gambling scenarios.

When there is too much volatility in the stock market, Coates suspects adrenaline levels decrease while cortisol levels increase, explaining why traders take fewer risks at that time. In fact, typically traders freeze, becoming almost incapable of buying or selling anything but the safest bonds. In Coates' opinion, the market needs investment as it falls and at rock bottom – at such times, greed is good.

The third matter – the behaviour of the Fed – Coates thinks could be controlled, albeit counterintuitively. Since 1994, the US Federal Reserve has adopted a policy called Forward Guidance. Under this, the public is informed at regular intervals of the Fed's plans for short-term interest rates. Recently, rates have been raised by small but predictable increments. By contrast, in the past, the machinations of the Fed were largely secret, and its interest rates fluctuated apparently randomly. Coates hypothesises these meant traders were on guard and less likely to indulge in wild speculation. In introducing Forward Guidance, the Fed hoped to lower stock and housing prices; instead, before the crash of 2008, the market surged from further risk-taking, like an unleashed pit bull terrier.

There are many economists who disagree with Coates, but he has provided some physiological evidence for both traders' recklessness and immobilisation and made the radical proposal of greater opacity at the Fed. Although, as others have noted, we could just let more women onto the floor.

Questions

Questions 14–19 One Choice

Choose the correct letter A, B, C, or D.

14 What do most economists agree about the financial crashes from 1994 to 2013?
  1. They were the worst global markets had ever experienced.
  2. Global stocks fell around 40% for a period of 240 days.
  3. They were particularly acute in the US.
  4. They were more severe than those between 1974 and 1993.
15 What does John Coates think about risk-taking among stock-market traders?
  1. It is almost invariably dangerous.
  2. It was prevalent at Goldman Sachs and Deutsche Bank.
  3. It should be regulated by the US Federal Reserve.
  4. It can sometimes assist a weak market.
16 What is some popular belief about traders?
  1. They are clever, calm, and acquisitive.
  2. They are usually men who are good at maths.
  3. They love danger and seek it out.
  4. They do not deserve their high salaries.
17 What did Coates find in blood samples from London traders in 2013?
  1. They had high levels of testosterone and dopamine.
  2. They produced excessive glucose and oxygen.
  3. They experienced high blood pressure.
  4. They drank large amounts of alcohol.
18 How do neuroscientists now view the brain?
  1. As an extraordinary computer.
  2. As an organ to control movement.
  3. As the main producer of adrenaline and cortisol.
  4. As a significant enhancer of pleasure.
19 Why might a person waiting to see a dentist have extremely high cortisol levels?
  1. He or she may dislike going to the dentist.
  2. He or she may be worried about the procedure.
  3. He or she may not have a specific appointment.
  4. He or she may not be able to afford the consultation.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q14 D Most economists agree, however, that from 1994 to 2013 crashes were deeper and the resultant troughs longer-lasting than in the 20-year period leading up to 1994 Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that most financial experts share the view that market crashes between 1994 and 2013 dropped further and took longer to recover from than the crashes in the 20 years before 1994.
Answer Explanation:
The answer choice D means that the crashes that happened between 1994 and 2013 were worse and caused more damage than the crashes that took place during the previous twenty years (from 1974 to 1993).
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the first paragraph directly states what most economists agree on regarding this time period. The text says that between 1994 and 2013, crashes were 'deeper and the resultant troughs longer-lasting' compared to the '20-year period leading up to 1994'. The 20-year period right before 1994 is 1974 to 1993, and having deeper falls that last longer means they were 'more severe'.
Q15 D (Coates does not advocate risk-aversion since risk-taking may jumpstart a depressed market.) Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Coates does not want traders to completely avoid danger or risks, because taking chances can help bring energy back to a market that is down.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that taking risks can occasionally help heal or restart a market that is doing poorly.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the text in the second paragraph. The author explains John Coates' views and points out that Coates is not completely against taking risks because taking risks can restart or revive a struggling, low market. In the text, the phrase "jumpstart" means to assist or restart, and "a depressed market" means a weak or poorly performing market.
Q16 C Another common view is that traders are greedy as well as thrill-seeking Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that another widely held opinion about stock traders is that they want a lot of money and look for excitement and danger.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that many people think traders enjoy risk and actively look for dangerous or exciting situations.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because paragraph 4 mentions a widespread opinion about traders. The text uses the phrase "common view" (which means "popular belief") to describe traders as "thrill-seeking". Someone who is thrill-seeking enjoys excitement and actively seeks out risky or dangerous activities, which directly matches the idea that they love danger and seek it out.
Q17 A blood samples taken from London traders who engaged in simulated risk-taking exercises for him in 2013 confirmed the prevalence of testosterone, cortisol, and dopamine Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when Coates tested blood from traders in London in 2013, he found high amounts of testosterone, cortisol, and dopamine.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that tests showed the traders had a lot of two chemicals in their blood: testosterone and dopamine.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because paragraph 4 clearly mentions tests done on traders in 2013. The text says that "blood samples taken from London traders... in 2013 confirmed the prevalence of testosterone, cortisol, and dopamine". The word "prevalence" means being widespread or present in large amounts, which matches "high levels of testosterone and dopamine".
Q18 B Neuroscientists now see the brain not as a computer that acts neutrally, involved in a process of pure thought, but as a mechanism to plan and carry out a movement, since every single piece of information humans absorb has an attendant pattern of physical arousal Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that scientists who study the brain do not see it as just a calm computer thinking thoughts, but rather as a system designed to organize and do body movements.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that brain scientists currently see the brain as a body part whose job is to direct and manage physical actions.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage explicitly states how neuroscientists understand the brain today. Instead of viewing it simply as a neutral computer focused on pure thought, they view it "as a mechanism to plan and carry out a movement." Here, "mechanism to plan and carry out a movement" matches the meaning of "an organ to control movement."
Q19 C Interestingly, if a patient does not know approximately when he or she will be called for that procedure, cortisol levels are the most elevated of all Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that stress hormone (cortisol) levels are at their highest point when a person has no idea roughly what time they will be called into the room for their treatment.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the patient does not know the exact time they will be seen by the dentist.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the text specifically mentions that cortisol levels rise the highest ("are the most elevated of all") when a patient does not know around what time they will be called in for their treatment. This matches the idea of not having a set or specific appointment time. While worrying about a procedure also causes stress, not knowing the time causes the highest levels.
Q20 volatility When there is too much volatility in the stock market, Coates suspects adrenaline levels decrease while cortisol levels increase, explaining why traders take fewer risks at that time Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when the stock market moves up and down too quickly and unpredictably, traders start to avoid taking risks.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "volatility" means large, rapid, and unpredictable changes in prices or conditions.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "volatility" because the flowchart describes what causes traders to avoid taking risks (risk-aversion). In the passage, Coates explains that traders take fewer risks when "there is too much volatility in the stock market." The phrase in the flowchart, "there is too much in the market," matches the words in the text exactly.
Q21 cortisol When there is too much volatility in the stock market, Coates suspects adrenaline levels decrease while cortisol levels increase, explaining why traders take fewer risks at that time Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when the market changes very quickly and unpredictably, the amount of cortisol in traders' bodies goes up, which leads them to take fewer risks.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is a chemical or hormone produced inside the human body when a person feels stress or uncertainty.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "cortisol" because the flowchart describes what happens inside traders' bodies when the stock market becomes unstable, causing them to avoid risk. The passage explains that when markets have too much volatility, "adrenaline levels decrease while cortisol levels increase." The word "rise" in the flowchart matches the word "increase" in the text, showing that cortisol levels go up.
Q22 Forward Since 1994, the US Federal Reserve has adopted a policy called Forward Guidance Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that starting in 1994, the US Federal Reserve began using a policy with the name "Forward Guidance".
Answer Explanation:
The answer "Forward" is a specific word that describes the name of a policy used by the US Federal Reserve.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "Forward" because the passage explicitly states that the US Federal Reserve introduced a policy named "Forward Guidance". In the flowchart, under the section for the US Federal Reserve policy, the text says: "Known as Guidance, this was supposed to calm the market." Therefore, the missing word directly before "Guidance" is "Forward".
Q23 wild Coates hypothesises these meant traders were on guard and less likely to indulge in wild speculation Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when the Federal Reserve's actions were secret and unpredictable, traders remained alert and were less likely to participate in uncontrolled and risky bets (wild speculation).
Answer Explanation:
The answer "wild" refers to extreme, uncontrolled, or reckless trading choices.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "wild" because the flowchart describes what traders do regarding interest-rate changes and their type of speculation. In paragraph 9, the text explains that in the past, unpredictable rate changes kept traders careful and "less likely to indulge in wild speculation." Under the new predictable policy, traders were not careful and instead engaged in "wild speculation."
Q24 Further In introducing Forward Guidance, the Fed hoped to lower stock and housing prices; instead, before the crash of 2008, the market surged from further risk-taking, like an unleashed pit bull terrier Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that although the Federal Reserve wanted to lower prices, the market actually jumped up due to more and additional risk-taking by traders before the 2008 crash.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means additional, extra, or more.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "further" because the flowchart outlines the sequence of events resulting from the Federal Reserve's policy. The passage states that instead of calming down the markets, the situation before the 2008 crash involved the market surging from "further risk-taking". In the flowchart, this directly fills the blank before "risk-taking".
Q25 NO There are many economists who disagree with Coates Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage clearly states that a large number of economists do not agree with what Coates believes.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement goes against what the text says. In reality, many economists do not share or agree with Coates' views.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because the text explicitly states that many economists do not agree with Coates. The statement claims that Coates' opinions are shared by many other economists, but the text says the opposite by using the phrase "disagree with Coates". Therefore, the statement contradicts the passage.
Q26 NOT GIVEN There are many economists who disagree with Coates, but he has provided some physiological evidence for both traders' recklessness and immobilisation and made the radical proposal of greater opacity at the Fed Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Coates suggested having less transparency (greater opacity) at the Fed, and describes this idea as "radical" while noting that many economists disagree. However, the writer does not say whether this idea is actually good or sound.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether the writer thinks Coates' idea of having less openness at the Fed is good or sensible.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the writer never states their own personal judgment on whether Coates' suggestion is "sound" (sensible or reliable). In the final paragraph, the writer calls it a "radical proposal" and mentions that many economists disagree with Coates, but the author does not give an opinion on whether this proposal is actually a good idea.
Q27 YES Although, as others have noted, we could just let more women onto the floor Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage points out that instead of making complex policy changes, another possible way to deal with the problem is simply allowing more women to work as traders.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means that the text agrees with the statement that having more women working as traders could be a solution to the market crisis issue.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because the final sentence of the text suggests that an alternative solution to the issue is to "let more women onto the floor." Earlier, the author explains that traders are mostly men whose decisions are driven by testosterone, which leads to extreme risk-taking. Therefore, allowing more women onto the trading floor directly matches the idea of "raising the number of female traders" to fix or solve the problem.
