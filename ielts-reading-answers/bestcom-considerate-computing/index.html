Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 14-26, which are based on Reading Passage 2 below.

Bestcom—Considerate Computing

‘Your battery is now fully charged,' announced the laptop to its owner Donald A. Norman in a synthetic voice, with great enthusiasm and maybe even a hint of pride. For the record, humans are not at all unfamiliar with distractions and multitasking. ‘We are used to a complex life that gets constantly interrupted by computers' attention-seeking requests, as much as we are familiar with procreation,' laughs Ted Selker of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) Media Lab.

Humanity has been connected to approximately three billion networked telephones, computers, traffic lights and even fridges and picture frames since these things can facilitate our daily lives. That is why we do not typically turn off the phones, shut down the e-mail system, or close the office door even when we have a meeting coming or a stretch of concentrated work. We merely endure the consequences.

Countless research reports have confirmed that if people are unexpectedly interrupted, they may suffer a drop in work efficiency, and they are more likely to make mistakes. According to Robert G. Picard from the University of Missouri, it appears to build up the feeling of frustration cumulatively, and that stress response makes it difficult to focus again. It is not solely about productivity and the pace of life. For some professionals like pilots, drivers, soldiers and doctors, loss of focus can be downright disastrous. ‘If we could find a way to make our computers and phones realise the limits of human attention and memory, they may come off as more thoughtful and courteous,' says Eric Horvitz of Microsoft Research. Horvitz, Selker and Picard are just a few of a small but prospering group of researchers who are attempting to make computers, phones, cars and other devices to function more like considerate colleagues instead of egocentric oafs.

To do this, the machines need new skills of three kinds: sensing, reasoning and communicating. First, a system must sense or infer where its owner is and what he or she is doing. Next, it must weigh the value of the messages it wants to convey against the cost of the disruption. Then it has to choose the best mode and time to interject. Each of these pushes the limits of computer science and raises issues of privacy, complexity or reliability. Nevertheless, ‘Attentive' Computing Systems have started to make an appearance in the latest Volvos, and IBM has designed and developed a communications software called WebSphere that comes with an underlying sense of busyness. Microsoft has been conducting extensive in-house tests of a way more sophisticated system since 2003. In a couple of years, companies might manage to provide each office employee with a software version of the personal receptionist which is only available to corner-suite executives today.

However, the truth is that most people are not as busy as they claim to be, which explains why we can often stand interruptions from our inconsiderate electronic paraphernalia. To find out the extent to which such disruption may claim people's daily time, an IBM Research team led by Jennifer Lai from Carnegie Mellon University studied ten managers, researchers and interns at the workplace. They had the subjects on videotape, and within every period of a specific time, they asked the subjects to evaluate their ‘interruptibility’. The time a worker spent in leave-me-alone state varied from individual to individual and day to day, and the percentage ranged from 10 to 51. Generally, the employees wished to work without interruption for roughly 1/3 of the time. Similarly, by studying Microsoft workers, Horvitz also came to the discovery that they ordinarily spend over 65 per cent of their day in a low-attention mode.

Obviously, today's phones and computers are probably correct about two-thirds of time by assuming that their users are always available to answer a call, check an email, or click the 'OK' button on an alert box. But for the considerate systems to be functional and useful, their accuracy has to be above 65 per cent in sensing when their users are about to reach their cognitive limits.

Inspired by Horvitz's work, Microsoft prototype Bestcom-Enhanced Telephony (Bestcom-ET) digs a bit deeper into every user's computer to find out clues about what they are dealing with. As I said earlier, Microsoft launched an internal beta test of the system in mid-2003. Horvitz points out that by the end of last October, nearly 3,800 people had been relying on the system to field their incoming calls.

Horvitz is, in fact, a tester himself, and as we have our conversation in his office, Bestcom silently takes care of all the calls. Firstly, it checks if the caller is in his address book, the company directory, or the ‘recent call’ list. After triangulating all these resources at the same time, it attempts to figure out what their relationship is. The calls that get through are from family, supervisors and people he called earlier that day. Other callers will get a message on their screens that says he cannot answer now because he is in a meeting, and will not be available until 3pm. The system will scan both Horvitz's and the caller's calendar to check if it can reschedule a callback at a time which works for both of them. Some callers will take that option, while others simply leave a voicemail. The same happens with e-mails. When Horvitz is not in his office, Bestcom automatically offers to transfer selected callers to his cellphone, unless his calendar implies that he is in a meeting.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q14 NOT GIVEN We are used to a complex life that gets constantly interrupted by computers' attention-seeking requests, as much as we are familiar with procreation,' laughs Ted Selker of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) Media Lab Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that Ted Selker said people are used to a busy life with many computer interruptions, just like they are familiar with having children (procreation). It means people know about both things, but it does not say that having children has been stopped or made difficult.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'NOT GIVEN' means that the passage does not tell us anything about whether human reproduction has been disturbed through history, according to Ted Selker. This information is not in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'NOT GIVEN' because the passage mentions Ted Selker and his thoughts on 'procreation' (which means human reproduction), but it does not say that it has been 'disturbed throughout history'. Ted Selker only says that people are 'familiar with procreation', similar to how they are familiar with computer interruptions. The passage does not provide any information about procreation being disturbed.
Q15 TRUE We merely endure the consequences Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that people simply put up with the bad things that happen when they get interrupted by phones or emails. They don't try to stop these interruptions; they just deal with them.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'TRUE'. This means that the given statement is correct based on the information in the reading passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage explains that people do not usually stop interruptions like phone calls or emails. Instead, they 'endure the consequences'. To 'endure consequences' means to put up with or accept what happens. The passage lists negative effects such as a drop in work efficiency, mistakes, frustration, and stress, but it does not mention people feeling 'sickness' as a direct result. Therefore, the statement that people 'put up with it instead of feeling sickness' is supported.
Q16 TRUE Microsoft has been conducting extensive in-house tests of a way more sophisticated system since 2003. In a couple of years, companies might manage to provide each office employee with a software version of the personal receptionist which is only available to corner-suite executives today Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Microsoft has been trying out a very smart computer program inside its own company since 2003. It also explains that soon, other companies might be able to give this software to every person who works in an office, not just special bosses.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Microsoft is indeed looking into a computer program that can be used in regular offices.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'TRUE' because the passage clearly states that Microsoft has been testing a very advanced system internally since 2003. It further mentions that soon, this type of software could be provided to 'each office employee', suggesting it's being developed for and is compatible with general office environments, not just special settings. The phrase 'in-house tests' implies it's being tested within Microsoft's own offices, which are 'ordinary offices' for its employees.
Q17 TRUE However, the truth is that most people are not as busy as they claim to be, which explains why we can often stand interruptions from our inconsiderate electronic paraphernalia Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that people often say they are very busy, but this is not always true. In reality, they are usually not as busy as they say. This is why they can often handle small breaks or calls from their phones and computers without much problem.
Answer Explanation:
The answer TRUE means that the statement is correct. It means that people often think they are busier than they really are.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage clearly states that many people believe they are busier than they actually are. The text highlights this difference between what people 'claim to be' and the 'truth' of their busyness, indicating a 'misperception'. It also provides research findings that show people spend a significant portion of their day in a 'low-attention mode', further supporting that their self-assessment of busyness can be inaccurate.
Q18 FALSE To find out the extent to which such disruption may claim people's daily time, an IBM Research team led by Jennifer Lai from Carnegie Mellon University studied ten managers, researchers and interns at the workplace Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that a team from IBM, led by someone from Carnegie Mellon University, looked at only ten people who worked as managers, researchers, and interns at IBM to see how much their work was stopped by interruptions. It did not look at every single job type at IBM.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'FALSE' means that the statement is not true. The experts from Carnegie Mellon University did not observe all kinds of jobs at IBM.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage clearly states that the research mentioned only studied a small group of specific employees, not 'all occupations of IBM'. The study focused on 'ten managers, researchers and interns', which is a limited selection of jobs, not every single job type at IBM. This detail in the passage shows that the statement in the question is incorrect.
Q19 NOT GIVEN Obviously, today's phones and computers are probably correct about two-thirds of time by assuming that their users are always available to answer a call, check an email, or click the 'OK' button on an alert box Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that today's phones and computers usually think their users are ready to take calls, read emails, or click a button when a message appears. This shows how current systems expect people to get information but does not talk about shortcut keys.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'NOT GIVEN' means that the passage does not tell us if current phones and computers have special buttons or options (shortcut keys) to help people get information right away.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'NOT GIVEN' because the passage talks about how current phones and computers often interrupt people and expect them to be available to get information, like answering calls or checking emails. It also mentions interacting with these systems by clicking an 'OK' button. However, the passage never mentions or describes any 'shortcut keys' that help people receive information immediately. This specific detail is missing from the text.
Q20 clues Microsoft prototype Bestcom-Enhanced Telephony (Bestcom-ET) digs a bit deeper into every user's computer to find out clues about what they are dealing with Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that the Bestcom system searches carefully inside each user's computer. It does this to find 'clues,' which are small pieces of information that help the system know what the user is working on or doing.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'clues' means small pieces of information or hints that the Bestcom system uses to figure out what a person is busy with.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'clues' because the passage states that the Bestcom system 'digs a bit deeper into every user's computer to find out clues about what they are dealing with.' This directly tells us that the system looks for these pieces of information to understand what users are doing.
Q21 relationship After triangulating all these resources at the same time, it attempts to figure out what their relationship is Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Bestcom looks at all the information it has (like phone books and recent calls) to try and understand the connection between the person calling and the person who owns the phone.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'relationship' means the connection between two people, like if they are family, friends, or work together.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'relationship' because the passage describes how the Bestcom system checks who is calling and how they know the user. It looks at different lists to understand the connection between the caller and the person getting the call. The system tries to 'figure out what their relationship is' to decide which calls to let through.
Q22 message Other callers will get a message on their screens that says he cannot answer now because he is in a meeting, and will not be available until 3pm Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that if someone calls and is not a close contact, Bestcom will show a text on their computer screen. This text tells the caller that the person they are calling is busy, like in a meeting, and cannot talk right now.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'message' means a short piece of information given to someone.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'message' because the passage explains what happens when a caller is not in the user's contact list. It says that these callers will see a message on their screen to let them know the user is busy and cannot answer. This matches the flow-chart which states that if callers are not in the directory, a 'message' will appear on their screen.
Q23 reschedule The system will scan both Horvitz's and the caller's calendar to check if it can reschedule a callback at a time which works for both of them Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that the Bestcom system looks at the schedules of the caller and the person being called. It does this to see if it can arrange a new time for them to talk, a time that is good for both people.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'reschedule' means to change the time or date of a planned event, like a call, to a new time or date.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'reschedule' because the passage explains that the Bestcom system helps callers set a new time for a call if the person they are trying to reach is busy. The system looks at both people's calendars to 'reschedule' the call so it works for both of them. This directly matches the part of the flowchart that says 'The system will ______ a suitable time for both'.
Q24 voicemail Some callers will take that option, while others simply leave a voicemail Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that when someone cannot answer their phone, some people who call will choose to have the system set up a new time to talk later. But other people will just record a message for the person they called.
Answer Explanation:
The answer, 'voicemail,' means a recorded message that people can leave for someone when that person cannot answer their phone.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'voicemail' because the passage explains what happens when a user like Horvitz is busy and cannot take a call. The system gives options to the caller. One option is to let the system 'reschedule a callback,' and the other option is to 'leave a voicemail.' This directly matches the part of the flow chart that says callers can choose to leave a message for the user.
Q25 cellphone When Horvitz is not in his office, Bestcom automatically offers to transfer selected callers to his cellphone, unless his calendar implies that he is in a meeting Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that if the person named Horvitz is away from his office, the Bestcom system will automatically offer to send certain phone calls to his mobile phone. This happens only if his schedule does not show that he is busy in a meeting.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'cellphone'. It means a mobile phone that a person carries with them.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'cellphone' because the passage explains how the Bestcom system helps when a person is not in their office. It says that Bestcom can send calls to the user's cellphone if they are not in a meeting. This matches the flow-chart's description of what Bestcom does when the user is 'out of the office' and has no 'meeting' scheduled.
Q26 meeting When Horvitz is not in his office, Bestcom automatically offers to transfer selected callers to his cellphone, unless his calendar implies that he is in a meeting Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when the user, Horvitz, is not in his office, the Bestcom system will automatically send certain calls to his mobile phone. However, it will not do this if his calendar shows that he has a meeting planned.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'meeting'. This means if the person using Bestcom is not in the office and their schedule does not show they have a meeting, Bestcom will send calls to their mobile phone.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer 'meeting' is found in the last part of the passage, where it describes how the Bestcom system works when a person is 'out of the office'. The passage states that calls will be transferred to a cellphone 'unless' the user's 'calendar implies that he is in a meeting'. This directly answers the blank in the flow-chart, indicating that a meeting is what would prevent the call transfer.
