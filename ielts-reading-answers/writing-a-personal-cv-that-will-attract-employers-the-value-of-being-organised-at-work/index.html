Writing a personal CV that will attract employers

You are unique. No-one has the same behavioural make-up that you have. Likewise, everyone’s career history is also unique. Why is it then that a great many CVs are mostly descriptions of past jobs or standard CV templates and give away very little about the individual behind the CV? It’s almost as if the majority of job seekers are afraid to let their own personality shine through.

Perhaps in a corporate world where everyone feels they have to have the same professional image — dress in dark, formal suits, for example — the same is subconsciously felt to be true for CV writing. But there’s a difficulty here: you want your CV to stand out and yet at the same time are afraid of saying anything that might make you stand out? The problem with the above thinking is painfully clear. Your CV will be dull, and likely to be swiftly passed over by an employer. Surely it is much better to be brave? To define your personal brand, as marketers might say. This isn’t about making unsupportable statements: it’s about choosing words that describe the qualities that drive your success. In short, what makes you good at your job.

Writing about oneself can sometimes be difficult. It involves the ability to see yourself from different people’s viewpoints. Working with a professional CV writer is one way to achieve that and to present your character positively within a CV. Asking a colleague that you trust is also a good way to find out how others see your strengths.

Try not to use classic recruitment clichés. Everyone says they have great ‘communication’ or ‘organisational’ skills. This gets ignored by recruiters. So, instead think carefully about who you are and what you bring and then try to describe yourself. In that way you give recruiters something original to engage with, something that grabs their attention.

==== Read the text below and answer Questions 21-27 ====

The value of being organised at work

Being organised is one of the most effective skills a businessperson can acquire because when we’re organised, we think more clearly. We’re in tune with our targets and know how to reach them. Here are my top tips to bring order to your business life:

Organise your workspace

Don’t underestimate time lost or stress caused from an inefficient working

environment. To restore order:

Cut down documents and stationery to the bare essentials. Be ruthless and remove anything that doesn’t directly serve a function in your day-to-day activity. One or two carefully selected photos to make your desk feel like home are fine, but avoid too many.

Create locations where you will keep all your work materials to make retrieval easy. Do this logically based on where you tend to use the items; for example, store spare copy paper near the printer. Move outside your immediate reach anything you use infrequently. Always return items once you have used them so they’ll be where you expect them next time.

Frustration ensues when you’re searching for a client proposal, but it’s buried among random papers. Eliminate desktop chaos by using trays, magazine files, or whatever you fancy and add clear labels such as ‘In’, ‘Out’, ‘For Action’, ‘Current Projects’ or other relevant categories.

Be brutally honest about what you must keep. Studies suggest that 80% of what we file is never accessed again. Ask: do I know of a tax or legal requirement for retaining it? Why would this be important to me in future? Avoid ‘miscellaneous’ as a category — you won’t remember what’s in there.

Effective planning

Plan your work; work your plan. Time spent planning saves untold hours in execution. Implementing regular planning strategies will sharpen your focus, thereby keeping you on track with your work. Start planning today for tomorrow. Near the close of each work day, implement a 10- to 15-minute routine to wrap up loose ends and prioritise key tasks. A good plan for tomorrow allows you to clear your head and enjoy your evening. Once a week, ring-fence a 60-to 90-minute appointment with yourself for larger scale planning. Use the time to do research or any of the thinking that normally takes a back seat.

Q15 Personality It’s almost as if the majority of job seekers are afraid to let their own personality shine through. Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage talks about how many job seekers do not show their true personality in their CVs. Instead of sharing who they really are, they keep their personality hidden and follow standard templates.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'Personality' means the unique qualities and traits that make someone who they are. It refers to the special mix of behaviors and attitudes that every person has.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'Personality' because the passage emphasizes that many job seekers do not reveal their true self in their CVs. The word 'personality' indicates what makes someone distinct and unique, which relates directly to the idea that revealing this part of themselves could help make their CV stand out.
Q16 Dull Your CV will be dull, and likely to be swiftly passed over by an employer Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that if your CV is not interesting, employers will quickly ignore it. This shows why it is important to be different and personal in your CV.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'dull' means boring or not interesting.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'dull' because the passage explains that if job applicants do not include unique information about themselves in their CVs, their CVs become boring. This makes it likely that employers will ignore them. The passage emphasizes the importance of showcasing one's individual qualities to make a CV stand out.
Q17 Qualities This isn't about making unsupportable statements: it's about choosing words that describe the qualities that drive your success Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that you should use words that show your special good traits. These traits help you succeed in your job.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means the special features or good traits that someone has.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'qualities' because the passage talks about how important it is for job applicants to define what makes them good at their job. These unique traits or characteristics are what help them do well in the workplace.
Q18 Viewpoints It involves the ability to see yourself from different people's viewpoints Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that when writing a CV, it's important to look at yourself from different people's 'viewpoints.' This helps you understand how others see your strengths.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'viewpoints.' This means different ways of seeing or thinking about something.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'viewpoints' because the passage talks about how writing a CV can be hard. It suggests that looking at yourself from different 'viewpoints' can help you understand your strengths better. This way, you can describe yourself positively in your CV.
Q19 Colleague Asking a colleague that you trust is also a good way to find out how others see your strengths Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that asking someone you work with and trust is a good way to learn about your strengths. This helps you with your CV.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'colleague'. A colleague is a person you work with.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'colleague' because in the passage it suggests that asking a trusted colleague about your strengths can help you understand how others see you. This is important for writing a good CV, as it helps you present yourself positively.
Q20 Cliché / Clichés Try not to use classic recruitment clichés. Everyone says they have great 'communication' or 'organisational' skills. This gets ignored by recruiters Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that applicants should not use common phrases that everyone says. If they do, their CVs will not stand out, and recruiters will not pay attention to them.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that clichés are words or phrases that many people use all the time, but they are not special or unique.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'cliché' because the passage explains that job applicants should avoid using the same common phrases that everyone else uses, like saying they have 'great communication skills'. These phrases are known as clichés, and they do not get noticed by recruiters.
Q21 Stationery Cut down documents and stationery to the bare essentials Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says to reduce the amount of documents and stationery to only what is necessary. This means you should only keep the important things you need to do your job.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'stationery' means the supplies you use for writing and office work, like paper, pens, and folders.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer 'stationery' fits in the context of keeping the minimum amount of necessary items at work. The passage says to cut down documents and 'stationery' to the bare essentials. This means you should only keep what you need for your work.
Q22 Photos One or two carefully selected photos to make your desk feel like home are fine, but avoid too many Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says you can have one or two pictures to make your desk nice, but having too many is not good.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'photos.' It means special pictures you keep on your desk.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'photos' because the passage says you can keep one or two photos on your desk to make it feel like home, but you should remove too many things that don't help your work.
Q23 Locations Create locations where you will keep all your work materials to make retrieval easy Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage means you should make special places to keep your work items. This helps you find them quickly.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'locations'. This means places where you keep your work materials.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'locations' because the passage talks about how to store work materials. It says to create spots where you keep everything to make it easy to find what you need. The word 'locations' fits this idea.
Q24 Labels Eliminate desktop chaos by using trays, magazine files, or whatever you fancy and add clear labels such as 'In', 'Out', 'For Action', 'Current Projects' or other relevant categories Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that to avoid mess on your desk, you should use trays and add clear labels. Labels help organize papers into groups like 'In' or 'Out' which makes it easier to find documents.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'labels'. Labels are words or tags used to describe things.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'labels' because the passage talks about using clear labels to organize documents on your desk. It says that using labels like 'In', 'Out', or 'For Action' helps to categorize paperwork and keep things organized. This supports the idea that labeling helps you stay organized at work.
Q25 Requirement Ask: do I know of a tax or legal requirement for retaining it? Why would this be important to me in future? Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage is asking if keeping a document is necessary for the future. This means it is a requirement.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'requirement', which means something that is needed or necessary.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'requirement' because the passage talks about keeping documents only if there is an official reason to keep them. This shows that a requirement is something important you must do.
Q26 Focus Implementing regular planning strategies will sharpen your focus, thereby keeping you on track with your work Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that making plans regularly helps you improve your focus. This means you will understand your tasks better and do them correctly.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'Focus'. This means paying attention to your work and keeping your mind on your tasks.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'Focus' because in the passage, it says that planning ahead helps sharpen your focus. This means that when you plan, you can think more clearly and stay on track with your work.
Q27 Tasks Near the close of each work day, implement a 10- to 15-minute routine to wrap up loose ends and prioritise key tasks. A good plan for tomorrow allows you to clear your head and enjoy your evening Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that making a good plan for the next day helps you relax and feel clear-headed.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'Tasks' means jobs or things you need to do.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'Tasks' because in the passage, it talks about how planning helps keep focus on work. By thinking about what tasks are important for the next day, you ensure they get the attention they need.
