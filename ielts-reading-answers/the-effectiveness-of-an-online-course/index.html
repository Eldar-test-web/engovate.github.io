The Effectiveness Of An Online Course - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From IELTS Mock Tests Academic Reading Test 84 · Part 3 · Questions 27–40

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 27–40, which are based on Reading Passage 3 below.

The effectiveness of an online course

As access to the internet has continued to grow, web-based learning has continued to expand. With approximately half of the households in the United States (or 150 million people) connected to the internet, an estimated 2 million students are taking post-secondary courses that are fully delivered online. Millions of other students at all educational levels (primary, secondary, post-secondary) participate in online courses. However, the effectiveness of online courses, particularly in relation to individual student needs, perceptions, and student outcomes, is sometimes questioned.

Common elements for learning in a typical classroom environment are the social and communicative interactions between student and teacher, and student and student. The ability to ask a question, to share an opinion with a fellow student, or to disagree with the point of view in a reading assignment are all fundamental learning activities. However, effective web-based learning requires adjustments on the part of students and teachers for successful interactions to occur. For example, many online courses provide students and faculty, and students and students with the ability to interact with each other via an electronic bulletin or discussion board.

While most studies show a link between interaction and satisfaction in web-based courses, some observers have cautioned that this is not always the case. Ruberg, Taylor and Moore, for example, observe that in order to interact successfully, students must adjust to the nonlinear nature of web-based learning. Typical face-to-face situations tend to be linear, focusing on a single discussion thread. Web-based learning sessions on an electronic bulletin board can have multiple threads with several discussions and interactions progressing simultaneously. Students respond to a teacher but also to other students, depending on their interest and points of view.

Sproull and Kiesler caution about discussions based on misinformation, that continue because an online instructor cannot necessarily immediately correct or clarify a comment. As a result, students need to have the experience and knowledge base to sift through the discussion for misinformation. In online learning, the amount of student interaction and the number of comments can easily lead to what is described as information overload. Furthermore, comments in online discussions tend to be lengthier than in face-to-face situations. As economist Herbert Simon once said, 'a wealth of information can create a poverty of attention,' meaning that with more information from many sources, students must be more attentive to both who is providing the information and what is being said.

In examinations of interaction, the concept of 'presence', or a sense of being in a place and belonging to a group, has received attention. It is generally assumed that when a student is physically present in a face-to-face course, he or she has a sense of belonging to the class or group enrolled in the course and this sense of belonging is greater than that experienced in an online course. He or she listens to the discussion and may choose to raise a hand to comment, answer, or ask a question. Furthermore, this same student may develop a relationship with other students in the class and discuss topics related to the class during a break. However, this is an assumption and not always true. For a variety of reasons, some students can also feel alienated in a face-to-face class and not feel part of a group.

The idea of presence has been redefined in relation to an online course. The simplest definition of presence for an online course refers to a student's sense of being registered and belonging in a course, and the ability to interact with other students and an instructor even though physical contact is not available. However, as this concept is studied, the definition is expanding and being refined to include telepresence, cognitive presence, social presence, teaching presence, and other forms of presence. The term 'community’ is related to presence, and refers to a group of individuals who belong to a social unit, such as students in a class. In an online course, however, terms such as communities of inquiry, communities of learners, and knowledge-building communities have evolved.

As the definition of presence has expanded and evolved, a distinction has been made between interaction and presence, emphasizing that they are not the same. Interaction may indicate presence, but it is also possible for a student to interact by posting a message on an electronic bulletin board, while not necessarily feeling that he or she is part of a group or a class.

A study conducted by Professor Anthony Picciano used questionnaires to determine students' attitudes in relation to their interactions and sense of purpose in online courses. An attempt was made to go beyond student perceptions of interaction and performance and to include perceptions of social presence, as well as actual participation in class activities. In addition, data was collected on performance measures that related specifically to course objectives. While much of the research, including this study, supports the strong relationship between students' perception of interaction and perceived learning, the results of the study indicated that the relationship between actual measures of interaction and performance is mixed.

The success of many online courses is dependent upon the nature of student-to-student and student-to-faculty interaction. However, how interaction affects learning outcomes and what the relationships between the two are, is in need of further study.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 D Common elements for learning in a typical classroom environment are the social and communicative interactions between student and teacher, and student and student. The ability to ask a question, to share an opinion with a fellow student, or to disagree with the point of view in a reading assignment are all fundamental learning activities Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that in a regular classroom, learning involves talking and communicating with teachers and other students. It highlights that asking questions, sharing opinions, and discussing different points of view are basic and essential learning activities.
Answer Explanation:
The answer choice D means sharing and discussing thoughts, opinions, and viewpoints with other people.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the author describes what normally happens in a standard classroom. In paragraph 2, the text states that typical classroom learning relies on social and communicative interactions, such as asking questions, sharing an opinion with a classmate, or disagreeing with a point of view. These activities directly describe 'the exchange of ideas'. Keywords in the text like 'communicative interactions', 'share an opinion', and 'point of view' match the meaning of 'the exchange of ideas'.
Q28 D Ruberg, Taylor and Moore, for example, observe that in order to interact successfully, students must adjust to the nonlinear nature of web-based learning. Typical face-to-face situations tend to be linear, focusing on a single discussion thread. Web-based learning sessions on an electronic bulletin board can have multiple threads with several discussions and interactions progressing simultaneously Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that these three researchers noticed online learning is different from normal classrooms. While regular classes follow one conversation line, online message boards have many different conversations happening all at the same time, and students need to adapt to that.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that online students have to keep track of several conversations happening at the exact same moment.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the researchers Ruberg, Taylor, and Moore noted that web-based learning is "nonlinear." Unlike normal classes that focus on only one topic at a time, online learning boards have "multiple threads with several discussions and interactions progressing simultaneously." Here, "multiple threads with several discussions" matches "more than one discussion," and "progressing simultaneously" means "at a time."
Q29 D Sproull and Kiesler caution about discussions based on misinformation, that continue because an online instructor cannot necessarily immediately correct or clarify a comment Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that online teachers might not be able to fix errors or explain things at once, allowing incorrect information in online conversations to continue.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that teachers cannot fix mistakes or give helpful answers right away in online chats.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the passage notes that Sproull and Kiesler warned about discussions containing wrong information. This happens because a teacher online cannot "immediately correct or clarify a comment." In other words, there is a time delay before the teacher can give feedback to guide the students.
Q30 B In examinations of interaction, the concept of 'presence', or a sense of being in a place and belonging to a group, has received attention Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that "presence" means having the feeling that you are in a location and that you belong to a group.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that online presence is described as feeling like you belong to and are included in a group of people.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage defines the idea of presence as a student's "sense of being in a place and belonging to a group" (in paragraph 5) and explains that for an online course, it involves "a student's sense of being registered and belonging in a course" (in paragraph 6). The words "sense of ... belonging to a group" directly match the phrase "feeling of being part of a group."
Q31 A An attempt was made to go beyond student perceptions of interaction and performance and to include perceptions of social presence, as well as actual participation in class activities. In addition, data was collected on performance measures that related specifically to course objectives Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the study looked at students' sense of social presence and also gathered information on how well students performed in achieving the course goals.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Anthony Picciano's research looked at how feeling connected or present in an online course relates to how well students achieve their course goals or results.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the text explains that Professor Anthony Picciano's study included "perceptions of social presence" and also gathered data on "performance measures that related specifically to course objectives." Here, "social presence" matches "presence", and "performance measures that related specifically to course objectives" refers to "course outcomes".
Q32 YES However, the effectiveness of online courses, particularly in relation to individual student needs, perceptions, and student outcomes, is sometimes questioned Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that people sometimes have doubts about how well online classes work for the specific needs of each individual student.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means that the author agrees that people are not completely sure whether online classes can satisfy each student's personal needs.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because the passage clearly states that people sometimes doubt or question how effective online learning is when it comes to meeting individual student needs. In this context, the statement's words "individual participant's needs" match the text's "individual student needs", and "is unclear" matches the idea that it "is sometimes questioned". Therefore, the statement agrees with the writer's claims.
Q33 NOT GIVEN Sproull and Kiesler caution about discussions based on misinformation, that continue because an online instructor cannot necessarily immediately correct or clarify a comment Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that researchers warn about wrong information spreading in online classes, but it does not say that they advise against forming strong relationships.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is NOT GIVEN because the text never says that researchers warn people not to make strong friendships or connections online.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because there is no mention in the text of researchers warning or cautioning students against developing strong online relationships. While researchers like Sproull and Kiesler do 'caution' about things like 'misinformation' and information overload in online discussions, the author never discusses researchers warning against forming relationships online.
Q34 NO However, this is an assumption and not always true. For a variety of reasons, some students can also feel alienated in a face-to-face class and not feel part of a group Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that believing students always feel included in an in-person class is just a guess and is not always correct. In reality, some students in physical classrooms can feel separated and not like part of the group.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement is false according to the text. The writer says that what people usually believe about in-person classes is not always true.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "NO" because the statement claims that common ideas about the benefits of in-person learning have been proven true, but the passage states the opposite. The text explains that people usually think students in a physical classroom feel a strong sense of belonging, but this is only an "assumption" and "not always true" because some students can actually feel lonely or left out. Therefore, the statement contradicts the writer's words.
Q35 YES However, as this concept is studied, the definition is expanding and being refined to include telepresence, cognitive presence, social presence, teaching presence, and other forms of presence Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that as experts continue to study the idea of presence, its meaning is growing larger and being improved and made clearer to cover different types of presence.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means that the text agrees that experts are still working to define and make the meaning of "presence" clearer.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "YES" because paragraph 6 clearly explains that as researchers study the concept of "presence," its "definition is expanding and being refined." The words "definition" and "being refined" match the statement's "meaning" and "being clarified." Since this process is actively ongoing, the statement agrees with the writer's claims.
Q36 NO the results of the study indicated that the relationship between actual measures of interaction and performance is mixed Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when researchers checked the real connection between interaction and student grades or performance, the findings were mixed rather than always the same.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement is false according to the passage, because research results are not consistent.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "NO" because the statement claims that research on how interaction affects learning outcomes has shown "consistent results." However, the passage states that the findings on the connection between interaction and actual student performance are "mixed" (varied or not consistent). Because "mixed" is the opposite of "consistent," the statement contradicts what is written in the passage.
Q37 A Ruberg, Taylor and Moore, for example, observe that in order to interact successfully, students must adjust to the nonlinear nature of web-based learning Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that to communicate and participate well online, learners must get used to the different, non-traditional style of online discussions.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that doing well in an internet-based class depends on learning how to change and get used to new and different ways of communicating.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the text explains that doing well in web-based learning requires students to change how they interact. In regular classrooms, discussions are linear (following one topic at a time), but online learning is non-linear and has many conversations happening at once. In the text, the phrase "adjust to" matches "adapting to", and "the nonlinear nature of web-based learning" refers to the "unfamiliar interaction formats".
Q38 F students need to have the experience and knowledge base to sift through the discussion for misinformation
students must be more attentive to both who is providing the information and what is being said		 Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that online learners must have the skills to find and remove false information from discussion boards. Because there is so much information coming from different people, students must pay close attention to who gives the information and what they are saying.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that paying attention to who wrote a comment in an online class helps students figure out if the statement is true and trustworthy.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is F because the passage explains that online discussions can spread incorrect details, so students must filter out false ideas and pay close attention to who is sharing the content. In the passage, being "mindful of the source" matches being "attentive to [...] who is providing the information", and "determining their validity" matches checking whether comments are true or checking them for "misinformation".
Q39 B As the definition of presence has expanded and evolved, a distinction has been made between interaction and presence, emphasizing that they are not the same. Interaction may indicate presence, but it is also possible for a student to interact by posting a message on an electronic bulletin board, while not necessarily feeling that he or she is part of a group or a class Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that communicating (interacting) online is not the same as feeling connected. A student can post a message online, but that action does not guarantee that the student feels like they belong to the group or class.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means that "presence" is the main factor that decides if an online student feels like they belong to a class or group.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage explains the difference between "presence" and "interaction." In paragraph 7, the author points out that interaction and presence are not the same thing. A student can interact by posting messages online without truly feeling that they are part of the group. In paragraphs 5 and 6, "presence" is defined specifically as having "a sense of being in a place and belonging to a group" or "a student's sense of being registered and belonging in a course." Therefore, feeling like one belongs comes from presence, not simply from interaction.
Q40 C However, how interaction affects learning outcomes and what the relationships between the two are, is in need of further study Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that researchers still need to do more work and investigation to see how communicating with others influences what students learn.
Answer Explanation:
The answer C means that we need to do more research to fully understand how interaction connects to student learning and results.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the author concludes the article by stating that the connection between interaction and learning outcomes needs more research. In the text, the phrase "learning outcomes" matches "learning achievement", and the phrase "is in need of further study" matches "is a subject that the writer believes will require more study". Therefore, ending C completes the sentence correctly.
