Population Growth Sentencing Millions To Hydrological Poverty - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From Succeed in IELTS Academic Reading Test 4 · Part 3 · Questions 27–40

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 27 - 40, which are based on Reading Passage 3 below.

Population growth sentencing millions to hydrological poverty

A

At a time when drought in the United States, Ethiopia, and Afghanistan is in the news, it is easy to forget that far more serious water shortages are emerging as the demand for water in many countries simply outruns the supply. Water tables are now falling on every continent; literally scores of countries are facing water shortages as the tables fall and wells go dry. We live in a water-challenged world, one that is becoming more so each year as 80 million additional people stake their claims to the Earth's water resources. Unfortunately, nearly all the projected 3 billion people to be added over the next half century will be born in countries that are already experiencing water shortages. Even now, many in these countries lack enough water to drink, to satisfy cleanliness needs, and to produce food.

B

By 2050, India is projected to have added 519 million people and China 211 million. Pakistan is projected to have added nearly 200 million, going from 151 million at present to 348 million. Egypt, Iran, and Mexico are slated to increase their populations by more than half by 2050. In these and other water-short countries, population growth is sentencing millions of people to hydrological poverty, a local form of poverty that is difficult to escape.

C

Even with today's 6 billion people, the world has a huge water deficit. Using data on over-pumping for China, India, Saudi Arabia, North Africa, and the United States, Sandra Postel, author of Pillar of Sand: Can the Irrigation Miracle Last? reports the annual depletion of aquifers to be at 160 billion cubic meters or 160 billion tons. Using the rule of thumb that it takes 1,000 tons of water to produce 1 ton of grain, this 160-billion-ton water deficit is equal to 160 million tons of grain or one-half the US grain harvest.

D

Average world grain consumption is just over 300 kilograms per person per annum - one third of a ton per person per year - and grain reserves directly or indirectly feed 480 million people globally. Stated otherwise, 480 million of the world's 6 billion people are being fed with grain produced with the unsustainable use of water.

E

Over-pumping is a new phenomenon, one largely confined to the last half century. Only since the development of powerful diesel- and electrically-driven pumps have we had the capacity to pull water out of aquifers faster than it is replaced by precipitation. Some 70 percent of the water consumed worldwide, including both that diverted from rivers and that pumped from underground, is used for irrigation, while some 20 percent is used by industry, and 10 percent for residential purposes. In the increasingly intense competition for water among sectors, agriculture almost always loses. The 1,000 tons of water used in India to produce 1 ton of wheat worth perhaps $200 can also be used to expand industrial output by easily $10,000, or 50 times as much. This ratio helps explain why, in the American West, the sale of irrigation water rights by farmers to cities is an almost daily occurrence.

F

In addition to population growth, urbanisation and industrialisation also expand the demand for water. As developing country villagers, traditionally reliant on the village well, move to urban high-rise apartment buildings with indoor plumbing, their residential water use can easily triple. Industrialisation takes even more water than urbanisation. Rising affluence in itself generates additional demand for water. As people move up the food chain, consuming more beef, pork, poultry, eggs, and dairy products, they use more grain. A US diet rich in livestock products requires 800 kilograms of grain per person a year, whereas diets in India, dominated by a starchy food staple such as rice, typically need only 200 kilograms. Using four times as much grain per person means using four times as much water.

G

Once a localised phenomenon, water scarcity is now crossing national borders via the international grain trade. The world's fastest growing grain import market is North Africa and the Middle East; an area that includes Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Egypt, and Iran. Virtually every country in this region is simultaneously experiencing water shortages and rapid population growth.

H

As the demand for water in the region's cities and industries increases, it is typically satisfied by diverting water from irrigation. The loss in food production capacity is then offset by importing grain from abroad. Since 1 ton of grain represents 1,000 tons of water, this becomes the most efficient way to import water.

I

Last year, Iran imported 7 million tons of wheat, eclipsing Japan to become the world's leading wheat importer. This year, Egypt is also projected to move ahead of Japan. Iran and Egypt have nearly 70 million people each. Both populations are increasing by more than a million a year and both are pressing the limits of their water supplies.

J

The water required to produce the grain and other foodstuffs imported into North Africa and the Middle East last year was roughly equal to the annual flow of the Nile River. Stated otherwise, the fast-growing water deficit of this region is equal to another Nile flowing into the region in the form of imported grain.

K

It is now often said that future wars in the region will more likely be fought over water than oil. Perhaps, but given the difficulty in winning a water war, the competition for water seems more likely to take place in world grain markets. The countries that will "win" in this competition will be those that are financially strongest, not those that are militarily strongest. The world water deficit grows larger with each year, making it potentially more difficult to manage. If we decided abruptly to stabilise water tables everywhere by simply pumping less water, the world grain harvest would fall by some 160 million tons, or 8 percent, and grain prices would go off the chart. If the deficit continues to widen, the eventual adjustment will be even greater.

L

Unless governments in water-short countries act quickly to stabilise their populations and to raise water productivity, their water shortages may soon become food shortages. The risk is that the growing number of water-short countries, including population giants China and India, with rising grain-import needs will overwhelm the exportable supply in food surplus countries, such as the United States, Canada, and Australia. This in turn could destabilise world grain markets. Another risk of delay in dealing with the deficit is that some low-income, water-short countries will not be able to afford to import needed grain, trapping millions of their people in hydrological poverty; thirsty and hungry, unable to escape.

M

Although there are still some opportunities for developing new water resources, restoring the balance between water use and developing a sustainable supply will depend primarily on demand-side initiatives, such as stabilising population and raising water productivity. Governments can no longer separate population policy from the supply of water. And just as the world turned to raising land productivity a half century ago when the frontiers of agricultural settlement disappeared, so it must now turn to raising water productivity. The first step toward this goal is to eliminate the water subsidies that foster inefficiency. The second step is to raise the price of water to reflect its cost. Shifting to more water-efficient technologies, more water-efficient crops, and more water-efficient forms of animal protein offers a huge potential for raising water productivity. These shifts will move faster if the price of water more closely reflects its real value.

Questions 33–36 Matching Information

Reading Passage 3 has 13 paragraphs A - M.

Which paragraph contains information about the following threats to water supplies?

Write the correct letter A - M in spaces 33 - 36 below.

33 The volume of water that is needed for irrigation in grain production.
34 Over-pumping our underground water supplies.
35 Population growth will be responsible for a new type of water-related poverty.
36 Industrialisation demands greater water supplies.

Questions 37–40 One Choice

Choose the correct letter A, B, C or D.

37 Our water supply is running low because
  1. grain is now exported globally.
  2. the world's population is increasing rapidly.
  3. more people are moving to cities.
  4. people waste water foolishly.
38 People who have a high-meat diet cause more water to be used because
  1. it takes more grain to feed livestock than it does a human.
  2. the industrial processes to produce meat require a lot of water.
  3. livestock drink a lot of water.
  4. packaging of meat products goes through an intensive washing process.
39 What would reduce the use of water without adversely affecting the food supply?
  1. growing fewer crops
  2. increasing water subsidies
  3. diverting water from irrigation
  4. falling population levels
40 If there is a water war, who will win?
  1. the driest countries
  2. the richest countries
  3. the countries that are more forceful
  4. the countries that have the biggest population

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 NOT GIVEN A US diet rich in livestock products requires 800 kilograms of grain per person a year, whereas diets in India, dominated by a starchy food staple such as rice, typically need only 200 kilograms. Using four times as much grain per person means using four times as much water Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that eating meat requires much more grain, which in turn uses four times as much agricultural water to produce compared to a rice-based diet, but it says nothing about drinking water.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage never mentions how much water vegetarians actually drink compared to people who eat meat.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the text only talks about the total water required to grow grain for different diets (such as a diet rich in livestock products versus a plant-based diet). It does not provide any information about the amount of water individuals drink directly.
Q28 TRUE A US diet rich in livestock products requires 800 kilograms of grain per person a year, whereas diets in India, dominated by a starchy food staple such as rice, typically need only 200 kilograms Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that a person eating an American diet needs 800 kilograms of grain each year, but a person eating an Indian diet usually needs only 200 kilograms.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means that people in India usually need less grain for their food each year compared to people in the United States.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because Paragraph F directly compares the amount of grain needed for both diets. It states that an American diet needs 800 kilograms of grain for each person every year, while an Indian diet typically needs only 200 kilograms. Because 200 kilograms is much less than 800 kilograms, the statement matches the facts in the text.
Q29 NOT GIVEN As people move up the food chain, consuming more beef, pork, poultry, eggs, and dairy products, they use more grain Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when people eat more meat (such as beef) and dairy, they consume more grain indirectly. However, it does not state whether growing grain uses more water than raising beef.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means the text does not provide enough information to know whether growing grain needs more water than raising beef.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage never directly compares whether growing grain takes more water than raising beef. In Paragraph F, the author mentions beef and grain, stating that eating more animal products like "beef" requires more "grain", which ultimately leads to using more water. However, the author does not state that growing grain consumes more water than raising cattle/beef. Because this specific comparison is missing from the passage, we cannot determine if it is true or false.
Q30 TRUE As developing country villagers, traditionally reliant on the village well, move to urban high-rise apartment buildings with indoor plumbing, their residential water use can easily triple Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when people living in villages move into city apartments with running water, the amount of water they use in their homes can quickly become three times greater.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means that the text confirms that when people leave rural areas to live in cities, the amount of water they use can go up by a large amount.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because Paragraph F specifically states that when villagers move from rural areas to urban apartments with indoor plumbing, their water use can easily triple. The phrase "developing country villagers... move to urban high-rise apartment buildings" matches the statement's "People that move from the country to the city", and "their residential water use can easily triple" matches "increase their water consumption considerably".
Q31 FALSE Perhaps, but given the difficulty in winning a water war, the competition for water seems more likely to take place in world grain markets. The countries that will "win" in this competition will be those that are financially strongest, not those that are militarily strongest Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that it is hard to win a war over water, so the real competition will happen in trade markets using money, rather than through fighting with armies.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE because the statement directly contradicts what the text says.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage explains that future struggles will not be military battles or wars fought over resources like food or water. Instead, the author states that competition will take place in "world grain markets" and will be won by countries that are "financially strongest, not those that are militarily strongest." Therefore, future conflicts will not be fought militarily over food.
Q32 NOT GIVEN Last year, Iran imported 7 million tons of wheat, eclipsing Japan to become the world's leading wheat importer. This year, Egypt is also projected to move ahead of Japan Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Iran bought 7 million tons of wheat from other countries, passing Japan as the top buyer, and that Egypt is also expected to buy more wheat than Japan. It talks about wheat, not oil.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the passage does not provide enough information to know whether Egypt and Japan import 7 million tons of oil each year.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage never mentions the amount of oil that Egypt or Japan imports. While Paragraph I mentions the figure "7 million tons" alongside Iran, Japan, and Egypt, it specifically talks about imports of "wheat", not oil. Oil is only briefly mentioned elsewhere in the text regarding possible future wars, so the statement cannot be verified as true or false.
Q33 C Using the rule of thumb that it takes 1,000 tons of water to produce 1 ton of grain, this 160-billion-ton water deficit is equal to 160 million tons of grain or one-half the US grain harvest Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that, as a standard rule, farmers need 1,000 tons of water to grow every single ton of grain.
Answer Explanation:
The answer identifies Paragraph C as the section discussing how much water is needed to grow grain crops.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Paragraph C directly gives the general measurement or volume of water required to produce grain. It mentions the general guideline that producing 1 ton of grain requires 1,000 tons of water.
Q34 E Over-pumping is a new phenomenon, one largely confined to the last half century. Only since the development of powerful diesel- and electrically-driven pumps have we had the capacity to pull water out of aquifers faster than it is replaced by precipitation Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that taking too much water from underground supplies is a modern problem. With strong electric and diesel pumps, humans now remove groundwater from underground rock layers faster than natural rain can fill them back up.
Answer Explanation:
The answer identifies Paragraph E as the section discussing the excessive pumping of water stored beneath the ground.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because it explains the issue of "over-pumping" underground water reservoirs (called aquifers). The passage mentions that modern pumps allow people to take water out of the ground much faster than rain can replace it. Keywords like "over-pumping," "pull water out of aquifer," and "pumped from underground" clearly match the concept of "over-pumping our underground water supplies."
Q35 B In these and other water-short countries, population growth is sentencing millions of people to hydrological poverty, a local form of poverty that is difficult to escape Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that in places that do not have enough water, having more people forces millions into hydrological poverty, which is a specific type of poverty caused by water shortages that is very hard to get out of.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means that paragraph B has the information showing that an increase in people will lead to a special kind of poverty caused by a lack of water.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the author clearly explains in this paragraph that having more people will cause severe water hardship. The text mentions that "population growth" is forcing millions into "hydrological poverty," which directly matches the statement about population growth causing a new "water-related poverty."
Q36 F In addition to population growth, urbanisation and industrialisation also expand the demand for water Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that, along with having more people, building cities and developing industries makes the need for water grow bigger.
Answer Explanation:
The answer F means that paragraph F discusses how industrial growth needs and uses more water.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is F because this paragraph clearly explains that industry and factories increase the need for water. The passage specifically mentions that "industrialisation" increases or expands "the demand for water" and that it "takes even more water than urbanisation." These phrases directly correspond to the question's statement about industrialisation demanding greater water supplies.
Q37 B We live in a water-challenged world, one that is becoming more so each year as 80 million additional people stake their claims to the Earth's water resources Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that our world has water supply problems, and these problems get worse every year because 80 million more people need and use the Earth's water.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that we are running out of water because the number of people on Earth is growing very fast.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage explains that the world's water shortage is getting worse every year because 80 million new people are added to the planet each year, demanding more water. In the text, "water-challenged world" relates to the "water supply running low," and "80 million additional people" each year directly matches the phrase "the world's population is increasing rapidly."
Q38 A As people move up the food chain, consuming more beef, pork, poultry, eggs, and dairy products, they use more grain. A US diet rich in livestock products requires 800 kilograms of grain per person a year, whereas diets in India, dominated by a starchy food staple such as rice, typically need only 200 kilograms. Using four times as much grain per person means using four times as much water Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that eating more meat means using four times as much grain to feed the animals. Because grain needs water to grow, eating more meat uses four times as much water.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that animals raised for meat need a huge amount of grain to eat, which uses much more grain—and therefore much more water—than feeding grain directly to people.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because Paragraph F explains that when people eat more meat and animal products (moving "up the food chain"), overall grain consumption increases dramatically. For instance, a diet heavy in meat ("livestock products") requires 800 kilograms of grain per person per year, compared to only 200 kilograms for a plant-based diet. Since producing grain requires large quantities of water, eating animals that consume lots of grain leads to using much more water.
Q39 D Unless governments in water-short countries act quickly to stabilise their populations and to raise water productivity, their water shortages may soon become food shortages Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage means that if countries do not keep their population numbers from growing, their lack of water will soon cause a dangerous lack of food.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "D" means having fewer people, or slowing down population growth, would decrease the total need for water while preventing shortages of food.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the author explains that population growth is a major driver of rising water demand and warns that failure to stop population growth will turn water shortages into food shortages. In contrast, growing fewer crops or diverting water from irrigation directly damages food production (adversely affecting the food supply), and the passage recommends eliminating—not increasing—water subsidies. Therefore, controlling population levels allows water demand to fall safely without harming the food supply.
Q40 B The countries that will "win" in this competition will be those that are financially strongest, not those that are militarily strongest Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage clearly states that the nations that succeed in the contest for water will be the ones with the most financial power (money), rather than the ones with the most military power.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that countries with the most money will win.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the text explains that the fight or competition for water will happen in world grain markets. In this competition, the winners will be the nations that are "financially strongest." The phrase "financially strongest" is another way of saying "the richest countries."
