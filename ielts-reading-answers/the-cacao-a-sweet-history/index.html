Reading Passage

The Cacao: a Sweet History

A Chapter 1

Most people today think of chocolate as something sweet to eat or drink that can be easily found in stores around the world. It might surprise you that chocolate was once highly treasured. The tasty secret of the cacao (Kah Kow) tree was discovered 2,000 years ago in the tropical rainforests of the Americas. The story of how chocolate grew from a local Mesoamerican beverage into a global sweet encompasses many cultures and continents.

B Chapter 2

Historians believe the Maya people of Central America first learned to farm cacao plants around two thousand years ago. The Maya took cacao trees from the rainforests and grew them in their gardens. They cooked cacao seeds, then crushed them into a soft paste. They mixed the paste with water and flavorful spices to make an unsweetened chocolate drink. The Maya poured the chocolate drink back and forth between two containers so that the liquid would have a layer of bubbles or foam.

Cacao and chocolate were an important part of Maya culture. There are often images of cacao plants on Maya buildings and art objects. Ruling families drank chocolate at special ceremonies. And, even poorer members of society could enjoy the drink once in a while. Historians believe that cacao seeds were also used in marriage ceremonies as a sign of the union between a husband and a wife.

The Aztec culture in current-day Mexico also prized chocolate. But, cacao plants could not grow in the area where the Aztecs lived. So, they traded to get cacao. They even used cacao seeds as a form of money to pay taxes. Chocolate also played a special role in both Maya and Aztec royal and religious events. Priests presented cacao seeds and offerings to the gods and served chocolate drinks during sacred ceremonies. Only the very wealthy in Aztec societies could afford to drink chocolate because cacao was so valuable. The Aztec ruler Montezuma was believed to drink fifty cups of chocolate every day. Some experts believe the word for chocolate came from the Aztec word “xocolatl” which in the Nahuatl language means “bitter water.” Others believe the word “chocolate” was created by combining Mayan and Nahuatl words.

C Chapter 3

The explorer Christopher Columbus brought cacao seeds to Spain after his trip to Central America in 1502. But it was the Spanish explorer Hernando Cortes who understood that chocolate could be a valuable investment. In 1519, Cortes arrived in current-day Mexico. He believed the chocolate drink would become popular with Spaniards. After the Spanish soldiers defeated the Aztec empire, they were able to seize the supplies of cacao and send them home. Spain later began planting cacao in its colonies in the Americas in order to satisfy the large demand for chocolate. The wealthy people of Spain first enjoyed a sweetened version of chocolate drink. Later, the popularity of the drink spread throughout Europe. The English, Dutch and French began to plant cacao trees in their own colonies. Chocolate remained a drink that only wealthy people could afford to drink until the eighteenth century. During the period known as the Industrial Revolution, new technologies helped make chocolate less costly to produce.

D Chapter 4

Farmers grow cacao trees in many countries in Africa, Central and South America. The trees grow in the shady areas of the rainforests near the Earth’s equator. But these trees can be difficult to grow. They require an exact amount of water, warmth, soil and protection. After about five years, cacao trees start producing large fruits called pods, which grow near the trunk of the tree. The seeds inside the pods are harvested to make chocolate. There are several kinds of cacao trees. Most of the world’s chocolate is made from the seed of the forastero tree. But farmers can also grow criollo or trinitario cacao plants. Cacao trees grown on farms are much more easily threatened by diseases and insects than wild trees. Growing cacao is very hard work for farmers. They sell their harvest on a futures market. This means that economic conditions beyond their control can affect the amount of money they will earn. Today, chocolate industry officials, activists, and scientists are working with farmers. They are trying to make sure that cacao can be grown in a way that is fair to the farmers and safe for the environment.

E Chapter 5

To become chocolate, cacao seeds go through a long production process in a factory. Workers must sort, clean and cook the seeds. Then they break off the covering of the seeds so that only the inside fruit, or nibs, remain. Workers crush the nibs into a soft substance called chocolate liquor. This gets separated into cocoa solids and fat called cocoa butter. Chocolate makers have their own special recipes in which they combine chocolate liquor with exact amounts of sugar, milk and cocoa fat. They finely crush this “crumb” mixture in order to make it smooth. The mixture then goes through two more processes before it is shaped into a mold form.

Chocolate making is big business. The market value of the yearly cacao crop around the world is more than five billion dollars. Chocolate is especially popular in Europe and the United States. For example, in 2005, the United States bought 1.4 billion dollars worth of cocoa products. Each year, Americans eat an average of more than five kilograms of chocolate per person. Speciality shops that sell costly chocolates are also very popular. Many offer chocolate lovers the chance to taste chocolates grown in different areas of the world.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 D The seeds inside the pods are harvested to make chocolate Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage clearly tells us that people collect the seeds from inside the tree's fruit pods to create chocolate.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Chapter D gives information about which piece or part of the cacao tree people collect to make chocolate.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because Chapter D specifically identifies which part of the tree is used: the pods that grow on the tree and the seeds inside them. The text states that "The seeds inside the pods are harvested to make chocolate." Here, "seeds inside the pods" refers to the "part of cacao trees," and "harvested to make chocolate" means "used to produce chocolate."
Q2 E Each year, Americans eat an average of more than five kilograms of chocolate per person Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that every single year, an ordinary person in the United States eats more than five kilograms of chocolate.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "E" means that Chapter 5 contains the detail about how much chocolate an individual in the United States eats every year.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because Chapter 5 mentions the exact amount of chocolate consumed by an American. The text says, "Each year, Americans eat an average of more than five kilograms of chocolate per person." Here, the keywords from the question match directly with the passage: "consumption" corresponds to "eat", "people in the US" matches "Americans", "per year" matches "Each year", and "per person" matches "per person".
Q3 D Cacao trees grown on farms are much more easily threatened by diseases and insects than wild trees. Growing cacao is very hard work for farmers. They sell their harvest on a futures market. This means that economic conditions beyond their control can affect the amount of money they will earn Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that bugs and plant illnesses can easily attack cacao trees on farms. It also explains that farmers cannot control market prices, which can change how much money they make.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is D, which means Chapter 4 talks about the dangers, difficulties, and financial problems that cacao farmers face.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because Chapter 4 directly explains the risks of farming cacao. It states that the trees are easily harmed by "diseases and insects" (biological risks) and explains that "economic conditions beyond their control can affect the amount of money they will earn" (financial risks). Words like "threatened" and "beyond their control" match the idea of "risks".
Q4 C The wealthy people of Spain first enjoyed a sweetened version of chocolate drink Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that rich people living in Spain were the very first people to drink chocolate that had sugar added to make it sweet.
Answer Explanation:
The answer C means that Chapter 3 is the section that talks about where people first drank sweet chocolate.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Chapter 3 describes how Spanish explorers brought cacao to Europe and states that rich people in Spain were the first to enjoy a sweet chocolate drink. Key words in the text include "Spain" (answering "where") and "first enjoyed a sweetened version of chocolate drink" (matching "first sweetened chocolate drink appeared").
Q5 B The Maya took cacao trees from the rainforests and grew them in their gardens
But, cacao plants could not grow in the area where the Aztecs lived. So, they traded to get cacao		 Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the Maya got cacao by moving trees from the rainforest into their own gardens, and the Aztecs got cacao by trading with others because the plant could not grow where they lived.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means that Chapter 2 contains the information about how early cultures in ancient America got their cacao.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because Chapter 2 specifically describes how two ancient American civilizations—the Maya and the Aztecs—obtained cacao. The Maya brought wild cacao trees from the rainforest to plant in their gardens, while the Aztecs could not grow the trees in their area and obtained cacao through trade. Key matching terms include "ancient American civilizations" (referring to the Maya and Aztec cultures) and "obtained" (meaning "took... and grew them" or "traded to get").
Q6 FALSE Ruling families drank chocolate at special ceremonies. And, even poorer members of society could enjoy the drink once in a while. Historians believe that cacao seeds were also used in marriage ceremonies as a sign of the union between a husband and a wife Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that royal leaders drank chocolate at events, but normal or poorer people could also drink it sometimes, and cacao seeds were used in regular marriage ceremonies too.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE because using cacao and chocolate in ceremonies or drinking it was not only for royal families.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage explains that while ruling families drank chocolate at special ceremonies, cacao seeds were also used in marriage ceremonies, and even poorer members of Maya society were able to enjoy the drink from time to time. Therefore, cacao and chocolate were not restricted only to Maya royal families. The word "restricted" means limited to one group, which contradicts the passage.
Q7 NOT GIVEN But it was the Spanish explorer Hernando Cortes who understood that chocolate could be a valuable investment Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Hernando Cortes saw the potential for chocolate to be a good business opportunity, but it does not confirm that he personally invested in it.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether Hernando Cortes actually put his own money into chocolate and chocolate drinks.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage only mentions that Hernando Cortes realized chocolate "could be a valuable investment" and thought it would become popular in Spain. It never tells us whether he actually made a financial investment in chocolate or chocolate drinks himself.
Q8 NOT GIVEN Most of the world’s chocolate is made from the seed of the forastero tree Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that the majority of chocolate around the world comes from the seeds of the forastero tree, but it does not state if it tastes the best or has the highest quality.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the text does not say whether the chocolate made from the forastero tree is the best.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage only tells us that "Most of the world’s chocolate is made from the seed of the forastero tree." It talks about the quantity or amount of chocolate produced from this tree, but it never mentions the quality or says that it makes the "best" chocolate. Therefore, this information is not provided in the text.
Q9 TRUE Then they break off the covering of the seeds so that only the inside fruit, or nibs, remain Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that workers take off the outer layer or skin of the seeds, keeping only the inside parts to make chocolate.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means it is correct to say that certain parts of the cacao seed are removed while making chocolate.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the text clearly mentions that workers remove the outer shell or skin of the seeds during production. In Chapter 5, the passage explains that workers "break off the covering of the seeds" so that only the inner part remains. The phrase "break off the covering" matches the meaning of "get rid of" some parts of the seed.
Q10 TRUE Chocolate is especially popular in Europe and the United States Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that people in Europe and the United States like chocolate very much, more so than people in other places.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means that the statement accurately reflects the facts mentioned in the reading passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage states that chocolate is "especially popular in Europe and the United States." The phrase "welcomed more" matches the idea of being "especially popular" in specific places (countries like the United States and continents like Europe) compared to the rest of the world.
Q11 Covering Then they break off the covering of the seeds so that only the inside fruit, or nibs, remain Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that factory workers take off the outside layer of the seeds, leaving only the inner parts called nibs.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means the outer skin or layer that wraps around the seeds.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "Covering" because the passage describes the steps to prepare cacao seeds before reaching the "nibs" stage. In the flow chart, the step says "ridding seeds of their [blank]" right before getting "Nibs". In Chapter 5, the text states that workers "break off the covering of the seeds so that only the inside fruit, or nibs, remain." The phrase "ridding seeds of" means removing or taking off, which matches the action "break off the covering". Therefore, "covering" correctly fills the blank.
Q12 Chocolate liquor Workers crush the nibs into a soft substance called chocolate liquor Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that workers crush the inner cacao parts (nibs) to create a soft mixture known as chocolate liquor.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to the thick, soft paste produced when cacao nibs are crushed during the chocolate-making process.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is found in Chapter 5 (paragraph E), which outlines the stages of chocolate production in a factory. The text explains that after the outer shell is removed from the seeds to leave only the nibs, workers "crush the nibs into a soft substance called chocolate liquor." Therefore, crushing the nibs results in "chocolate liquor."
Q13 Cocoa fat Chocolate makers have their own special recipes in which they combine chocolate liquor with exact amounts of sugar, milk and cocoa fat Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that chocolate makers mix chocolate liquor together with measured amounts of sugar, milk, and cocoa fat.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means the oily or fatty part that comes from cacao seeds.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "Cocoa fat" because Paragraph E explains the step where the "crumb" mixture is created. The text says that chocolate makers combine chocolate liquor with precise amounts of "sugar, milk and cocoa fat." Therefore, "cocoa fat" is the missing ingredient added along with sugar and milk.
Q14 Mold form / Mold / mould form / mould The mixture then goes through two more processes before it is shaped into a mold form Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that before the chocolate is finished, the mixture undergoes two more steps until it is placed into a mold to take its final shape.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means a container or special shape used to give chocolate its final solid form.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the last sentence describing the chocolate-making process in Chapter 5. The text explains that after the crumb mixture is crushed smoothly and passes through two additional steps, it is finally given its shape using a "mold form". In the flow chart, the phrase "come into a shape in a" directly corresponds to the phrase "shaped into a mold form" in the passage.
