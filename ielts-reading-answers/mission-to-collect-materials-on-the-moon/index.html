From IELTS Mock Tests Academic Reading Test 42 · Part 2 · Questions 14–28

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 14-28 which are based on Reading Passage 2 below.

Mission to Collect Materials on the Moon

A

Whilst the world watched in excitement as Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed on the moon, planetary scientists were focused on something else. For them, the value of the mission was in the cargo they brought back to earth. By the time Armstrong and Aldrin climbed into the lunar module for the last time, they had gathered 22 kilograms of moon rocks, completely filling a small suitcase. Over five Apollo crews brought back a total collection of 382 kilograms of material containing 2,200 samples.

B

The rocks were known at the time as a scientific treasure and they did not disappoint. Paul Spudis, a geologist of the Lunar and Planetary Institute in Houston, Texas, said, “Our ideas about planetary formation and evolution must be rewritten after the discoveries made by the Apollo crews.” Harold Urey, a Nobel prizewinner, and one of the advocates of lunar exploration had predicted that the moon was composed of primitive meteoritic material. But his conclusion was wrong. Some of the rocks looked just like the rocks on earth.

C

Many clues that the lunar rocks contained have taken a couple of years to effectively analyse. Also, some of the conclusions are still debated. A big surprise was the evidence that the early moon was covered by a lot of molten rock. The moon’s mountainous regions are made of anorthosite, a rare rock on earth that forms when light, aluminum-rich minerals float to the top of lava.

D

Nowadays, the smart money is on the idea that the moon was created as a result of something that occurred around 50 million years after the solar system was created when the Earth was in its infancy. From this hypothesis, the earliest Earth ran into a planet that was a similar size to Mars and debris from the collision went into orbit around the Earth which rapidly came together to form the moon.

E

The “giant impact” scenario led to a radical re-evaluation of the history of the early solar system. Before Apollo, planetary scientists watched the collection of objects orbiting the sun like a clockwork mechanism in which collisions were rare and trivial. Now, it is accepted as being a far more active environment, shuffling, colliding or ejecting. This history of all the inner planets has been shaped by collisions and nowhere is that history more visible than the moon.

F

Another surprise was the rocks from the moon’s largest impact craters indicate that all craters are roughly the same age, between 3.8 and 4 billion years old. It never coincided. The moon and, by extension, the Earth must have been caused by a devastating barrage half a billion years after the solar system formed. To cause this process, something big must have been going back to the outer solar system, but what? Surprisingly, this episode in the history of the solar system has come to be known as the last heavy bombardment and ended at roughly the same time as the first signs of life on earth.

G

These key discoveries about our planet’s history may never have been made without the samples taken from the moon for chemical analysis and isotopic dating. So, do the Apollo rocks hide any more secrets? All 2,200 samples have been researched, and Randy Korotev, a lunar geochemist at Washington University in St Louis, Missouri, says that it is unlikely that there will be anything groundbreaking left to find from them. However, they may yet keep some more delicate secrets. Korotev says, “We are steadily developing better tools and asking better questions.” Especially, the instruments for dating mineral samples have been more delicate, enabling researchers to study the age of ever smaller samples, like tiny mineral grains within a rock.

H

These techniques have stimulated a rethink of some key dates in lunar history in the past two years. A team at the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology dated the formation of the moon’s magma oceans. Also, by inference, the creation of the moon itself is estimated to have happened between 20 and 30 million years later than we originally thought, at approximately 4.5 billion years ago. Alexander Nemchin with five colleagues in the Curtin University of Technology in Perth, Western Australia also estimated that a lunar zircon was around 4.417 billion years old when the last of the magma oceans solidified.

I

The Apollo rock samples are not finished answering some of the bigger picture questions. What will we discover on the opposite side of the moon’s surface that we are unable to see from the Earth? Can we put together a detailed history of the lava flows that formed the basalts of the lunar seas? Can we discover any samples from deep inside the moon? These are all seen as very good reasons for coming back to the moon. The big picture needs more samples, more data and more contexts. According to Gary Lofgren, a curator of NASA’s lunar rock collection at Johnson Space Centre in Houston, “There’s no lack of target and scientific questions. It’s not just about the moon but about the solar system’s history. This is the lesson that we have learned from Apollo.”

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q14 i The rocks were known at the time as a scientific treasure and they did not disappoint Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage means that people believed the moon rocks would be very precious for scientific study, and the rocks indeed proved to be extremely useful.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'i' means that the moon rocks were extremely useful and important for science.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading i because Paragraph B focuses on how valuable the moon rocks were to scientists. The paragraph opens by stating that the rocks were considered a "scientific treasure" and explains that studying them forced scientists to completely rewrite their theories about how planets form and change. The phrase "scientific treasure" directly matches the heading's theme of "scientific value".
Q15 v A big surprise was the evidence that the early moon was covered by a lot of molten rock Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that scientists were very surprised to find proof that the moon was covered with melted rock when it was young.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "v" means that the best heading for Paragraph C is "The surprising evidence about the moon".
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is heading v because Paragraph C describes unexpected findings discovered from analyzing the moon rocks. The text directly states that "A big surprise was the evidence that the early moon was covered by a lot of molten rock." The keyword "surprise" directly matches "surprising", and "evidence that the early moon was covered by a lot of molten rock" directly matches "evidence about the moon".
Q16 vi The “giant impact” scenario led to a radical re-evaluation of the history of the early solar system Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that learning about the giant crash caused scientists to completely rethink and change their ideas about the early solar system's past.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "vi" means that the best title for Paragraph E is "The history of the early solar system".
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "vi" because Paragraph E directly discusses how ideas about the early solar system changed. The text explicitly mentions that finding out about the moon's impact led to a major change in how scientists understand the "history of the early solar system". Before, they thought space objects moved calmly like clockwork, but now they understand that the early solar system was an active, violent place with many collisions.
Q17 ii Another surprise was the rocks from the moon’s largest impact craters indicate that all craters are roughly the same age, between 3.8 and 4 billion years old Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that rocks collected from the moon's biggest holes (craters) show that all of these craters are almost the same age.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "ii" means the title for Paragraph F is "The craters of the moon".
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is ii because Paragraph F talks about findings related to the moon's craters. Rocks taken from the biggest impact craters show that the craters were made around the same time during a period called the heavy bombardment. Keywords like "impact craters" and "craters" directly match the heading "The craters of the moon".
Q18 iv These key discoveries about our planet’s history may never have been made without the samples taken from the moon for chemical analysis and isotopic dating Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that important knowledge about the history of our Earth could not have been found without testing the rock samples collected from the moon.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "iv" means that the best title for Paragraph G is "The impact of the rocks discovered".
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "iv" because Paragraph G focuses on the major effect and influence that the collected moon rocks had on science. At the beginning of the paragraph, it explains that crucial discoveries regarding Earth's history were only possible because scientists studied these rock samples. The word "discoveries" matches "discovered", and the great influence of these samples on our knowledge represents the "impact" mentioned in heading iv.
Q19 ix These techniques have stimulated a rethink of some key dates in lunar history in the past two years Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that new scientific methods caused researchers to reconsider and study the timeline and important dates of the moon's past.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "ix" means that the best heading for Paragraph H is "Study of lunar history".
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is ix because Paragraph H focuses on how scientists used new techniques to study and re-evaluate important time periods and events in the moon's timeline. Key phrases to notice are "rethink of some key dates in lunar history", "dated the formation", "creation of the moon itself", and "estimated that a lunar zircon was around 4.417 billion years old". All of these details show scientists studying the historical age and timeline of the moon.
Q20 vii The Apollo rock samples are not finished answering some of the bigger picture questions. What will we discover on the opposite side of the moon’s surface that we are unable to see from the Earth? Can we put together a detailed history of the lava flows that formed the basalts of the lunar seas? Can we discover any samples from deep inside the moon? These are all seen as very good reasons for coming back to the moon Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that existing moon rocks have not answered every major question yet. It asks several new questions about undiscovered areas and depths of the moon, stating that finding these answers is why humans should return to the moon in the future.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "vii" means heading vii, which is "The unknown questions left for future".
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is vii because Paragraph I focuses on questions that scientists still cannot answer with the current Apollo samples. The paragraph lists several unsolved questions, such as what can be found on the far side of the moon and deep inside it. Because these questions remain unanswered, they give strong reasons for future missions to return to the moon. Key phrases like "bigger picture questions", "What will we discover", "Can we discover", and "reasons for coming back to the moon" clearly correspond to "unknown questions left for future".
Q21 A The “giant impact” scenario led to a radical re-evaluation of the history of the early solar system Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the idea of a huge crash (giant impact) completely changed how scientists understand the early history of our solar system.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the "giant impact" idea is about understanding the past of the early solar system.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because paragraph E directly mentions that the "giant impact" scenario caused scientists to rethink and discover the history of the early solar system. In this context, the "re-evaluation of the history of the early solar system" matches the idea of finding or understanding that history.
Q22 C These key discoveries about our planet’s history may never have been made without the samples taken from the moon for chemical analysis and isotopic dating Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that scientists would not have learned these important facts about Earth's past if they did not have the rock and soil samples collected from the moon.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that moon rocks and soil helped scientists discover important facts about Earth's past.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Paragraph G states that important discoveries regarding our planet's history could not have happened without the moon samples. In this context, "our planet's history" refers directly to "the earth's history," and the passage emphasizes that testing the "samples taken from the moon" made these "key discoveries" possible.
Q23 D It’s not just about the moon but about the solar system’s history. This is the lesson that we have learned from Apollo Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains Gary Lofgren's view that missions to the moon teach us important lessons not only about the moon itself, but about the bigger history of our entire solar system.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that studying the history and development of Earth and other celestial bodies forms an important part of understanding the history of the entire solar system.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the final paragraph, where Gary Lofgren states: “It’s not just about the moon but about the solar system’s history. This is the lesson that we have learned from Apollo.” His point highlights that researching these planetary bodies provides broader insights into the overall evolution and history of the solar system, making our understanding of planetary development a key milestone. Keywords like "lesson", "learned", "history", and "solar system" in the passage match the idea of looking back at history and understanding the solar system's development.
Q24 NOT GIVEN By the time Armstrong and Aldrin climbed into the lunar module for the last time, they had gathered 22 kilograms of moon rocks, completely filling a small suitcase Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that Armstrong and Aldrin collected 22 kilograms of moon rocks, but it does not mention Russian astronauts or compare the value of different collections.
Answer Explanation:
The answer NOT GIVEN means that the passage does not provide enough information to know if the rocks collected by Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin were more valuable than rocks collected by Russian astronauts.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because while Paragraph A discusses Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin gathering 22 kilograms of moon rocks, the passage never mentions Russian astronauts or compares their rock samples at all. Therefore, there is no information about whether their rocks were more or less valuable.
Q25 TRUE These key discoveries about our planet’s history may never have been made without the samples taken from the moon for chemical analysis and isotopic dating Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that important facts about our planet's history might not have been found without the moon samples that were tested in labs.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the statement is TRUE, because the passage agrees that the moon rocks are very important for understanding history.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage says the discoveries "may never have been made without the samples." This means the samples were critical. Paragraphs B, E, and I also say the rocks changed our ideas about history. Keywords: "key discoveries," "history," "without the samples."
Q26 FALSE Another surprise was the rocks from the moon’s largest impact craters indicate that all craters are roughly the same age, between 3.8 and 4 billion years old Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says rocks from the moon's biggest craters show that all craters are about the same age, between 3.8 and 4 billion years old.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the statement is false because the passage gives a different age range from the one in the statement.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE. The statement agrees that the craters are a similar age, but it says 'up to 5 billion years old'. The passage says 'between 3.8 and 4 billion years old'. Pay attention to the numbers, because 5 billion does not match 4 billion.
Q27 NOT GIVEN These key discoveries about our planet’s history may never have been made without the samples taken from the moon for chemical analysis and isotopic dating Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that testing rocks from the moon helped scientists learn important facts about the history of Earth, but it never mentions anything about earthquakes.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the passage does not provide any information about whether moon samples provided the main clues for discovering earthquakes.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage never mentions the word "earthquake" or any clues related to earthquakes. Although paragraph G mentions that moon rocks led to "key discoveries about our planet’s history," the text focuses entirely on planetary formation, cosmic collisions, craters, and the early solar system, without discussing earthquakes at all.
Q28 NOT GIVEN What will we discover on the opposite side of the moon’s surface that we are unable to see from the Earth? Can we put together a detailed history of the lava flows that formed the basalts of the lunar seas? Can we discover any samples from deep inside the moon? These are all seen as very good reasons for coming back to the moon. The big picture needs more samples, more data and more contexts. According to Gary Lofgren, a curator of NASA’s lunar rock collection at Johnson Space Centre in Houston, “There’s no lack of target and scientific questions. It’s not just about the moon but about the solar system’s history. This is the lesson that we have learned from Apollo.” Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage asks what scientists might find on the side of the moon that cannot be seen from Earth, but it does not state what is actually there or if that side is directly related to the history of the solar system.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether the far side of the moon is linked to the history of the solar system.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because Paragraph I only raises an unanswered question about the unseen side of the moon by asking, "What will we discover on the opposite side of the moon’s surface that we are unable to see from the Earth?". Although the passage later mentions learning about "the solar system’s history," it never confirms or claims that the hidden side of the moon is specifically related to this history.
