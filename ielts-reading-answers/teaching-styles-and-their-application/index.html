Teaching Styles and Their Application - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From IELTS Simulation Tests Academic Reading Test 02 · Part 1 · Questions 1–13

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 1–13, which are based on Reading Passage 1 below.

READING PASSAGE 1

The way in which information is taught can vary greatly across cultures and time-periods. Entering a British primary school classroom from the early 1900s, for example, one gains a sense of austerity, discipline and a rigid way of teaching. Desks are typically seated apart from one another, with straight-backed wooden chairs that face directly to the teacher and the chalkboard. In the present day, British classrooms look very different. Desks are often grouped together so that students face each other rather than the teacher, and a large floor area is typically set aside for the class to come together for group discussion and learning.

Traditionally, it was felt that teachers should be in firm control of the learning process, and that the teacher's task was to prepare and present material for students to understand. Within this approach, the relationship students have with their teachers is not considered important, nor is the relationship students have with each other in the classroom. A student's participation in class is likely to be minimal, aside from asking questions directed at the teacher, or responding to questions that the teacher has directed at the student. This style encourages students to develop respect for positions of power as a source of control and discipline. It is frequently described as the "formal authority" model of teaching.

A less rigid form of teacher-centred education is the "demonstrator" model. This maintains the formal authority model's notion of the teacher as a "flashlight" who illuminates the material for his or her class to learn, but emphasises a more individualised approach to form. The demonstrator acts as both a role model and a guide, demonstrating skills and processes and then helping students develop and apply these independently. Instructors who are drawn to the demonstrator style are generally confident that their own way of performing a task represents a good base model, but they are sensitive to differing learning styles and expect to provide students with help on an individual basis.

Many education researchers argue for student-centred learning instead, and suggest that the learning process is more successful when students are in control. Within the student-centred paradigm, the "delegator" style is popular. The delegator teacher maintains general authority, but they delegate much of the responsibility for learning to the class as a way for students to become independent thinkers who take pride in their own work. Students are often encouraged to work on their own or in groups, and if the delegator style is implemented successfully they will build not only a working knowledge of course specific topics, but also self-discipline and the ability to co-ordinate group work and interpersonal roles.

Another style that emphasises student-centred education is the "facilitator" mode of learning. Here, while a set of specific curriculum demands are already in place, students are encouraged to take the initiative for creating ways to meet these learning requirements together. The teacher typically designs activities that encourage active learning, group collaboration and problem solving, and students are encouraged to process and apply the course content in creative and original ways. Whereas the delegator style emphasises content, and the responsibility students can have for generating and directing their own knowledge base, the facilitator style emphasises form, and the fluid and diverse possibilities that are available in the process of learning.

Until the 1960s, formal authority was common in almost all Western schools and universities. As a professor would enter a university lecture theatre, a student would be expected to rush up, take his bag to the desk, and pull out the chair for the professor to sit down on. This style has become outmoded over time. Now at university, students and professors typically have more relaxed, collegiate relationships, address each other on a first name basis, and acknowledge that students have much to contribute in class. Teacher-centred education has a lingering appeal in the form of the demonstrator style, however, which remains useful in subjects where skills must be demonstrated to an external standard and the learning process remains fixed in the earlier years of education. A student of mathematics, sewing or metalwork will likely be familiar with the demonstrator style. At the highest levels of education, however, the demonstrator approach must be abandoned in all fields as students are required to produce innovative work that makes unique contributions to knowledge. Thesis and doctoral students lead their own research in facilitation with supervisors.

The delegator style is valuable when the course is likely to lead students to careers that require group projects. Often, someone who has a high level of expertise in a particular field does not make for the best employee because they have not learnt to apply their abilities in a co-ordinated manner. The delegator style confronts this problem by recognising that interpersonal communication is not just a means to learning but an important skill set in itself. The facilitator model is probably the most creative model, and is therefore not suited to subjects where the practical component necessitates a careful and highly disciplined manner, such as training to be a medical practitioner. It may, however, suit more experimental and theoretical fields ranging from English, music and the social sciences, to science and medical research that takes place in research labs. In these areas, "mistakes" in form are important and valuable aspects of the learning and development process.

Overall, a clear evolution has taken place in the West from a rigid, dogmatic and teacher-dominated way of learning, to a flexible, creative and student-centred approach. Nevertheless, different subjects, ages and skill levels suit different styles of teaching, and it is unlikely that there will ever be one recommended approach for everyone.

Questions 1–8 Matching Features

Look at the following statements and the styles of teaching below.

Match each statement with the correct teaching style, A–D.

Write the correct letter, A–D.

NB You may use any letter more than once.

A. Formal authority

B. Demonstrator

C. Delegator

D. Facilitator

1 The emphasis is on students directing the learning process.
2 The teacher shows the class how to do something, then students try it on their own.
3 Student-teacher interaction and student-student interaction is limited.
4 The emphasis is on the process of solving problems together.
5 Students are expected to adjust to the teacher's way of presenting information.
6 The teacher designs group activities that encourage constructive interaction.
7 Time is set aside for one-on-one instruction between teacher and student.
8 Group and individual work is encouraged independently of the teacher.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 C Whereas the delegator style emphasises content, and the responsibility students can have for generating and directing their own knowledge base, the facilitator style emphasises form, and the fluid and diverse possibilities that are available in the process of learning Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that under the delegator style, the main focus is on students taking responsibility to create and guide their own knowledge.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "C" means that the "Delegator" style of teaching matches the idea that students are focused on leading and guiding their own learning.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the passage explicitly states that the "delegator style" focuses on the responsibility students hold for "generating and directing" their own knowledge. Keywords like "directing" directly match the question's phrase "directing the learning process."
Q2 B The demonstrator acts as both a role model and a guide, demonstrating skills and processes and then helping students develop and apply these independently Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that a demonstrator teacher shows the methods and steps first, and then guides students to practice and use those skills by themselves.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "B" means the statement describes the "Demonstrator" teaching style.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage states that the demonstrator teacher shows skills and processes first and then helps learners practice and use them on their own. In the text, "demonstrating skills and processes" matches showing how to do something, and "apply these independently" matches students trying it on their own.
Q3 A Within this approach, the relationship students have with their teachers is not considered important, nor is the relationship students have with each other in the classroom. A student's participation in class is likely to be minimal, aside from asking questions directed at the teacher, or responding to questions that the teacher has directed at the student. This style encourages students to develop respect for positions of power as a source of control and discipline. It is frequently described as the "formal authority" model of teaching Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that in the formal authority model, communication between students and teachers is very low, students rarely interact with each other, and class participation is kept to a minimum.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "A" means that the "formal authority" style of teaching is the one where students rarely talk or interact with their teacher or with their classmates.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the second paragraph discusses the "formal authority" model. The passage explains that under this approach, relationships between students and teachers, as well as relationships among students, are "not considered important" and student participation is "likely to be minimal." In this context, "minimal" participation and unimportant relationships directly mean that student-teacher and student-student interaction is "limited."
Q4 D The teacher typically designs activities that encourage active learning, group collaboration and problem solving, and students are encouraged to process and apply the course content in creative and original ways. Whereas the delegator style emphasises content, and the responsibility students can have for generating and directing their own knowledge base, the facilitator style emphasises form, and the fluid and diverse possibilities that are available in the process of learning Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that in the facilitator approach, teachers create activities for students to work together as a group to solve problems, placing special focus on the actual process and ways of learning.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "D" means the facilitator style of teaching focuses on working together in a team to find answers and on how students learn during that process.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the passage explicitly describes the facilitator style as one where the teacher creates tasks promoting "group collaboration and problem solving" (solving problems together) and states that "the facilitator style emphasises form, and the fluid and diverse possibilities that are available in the process of learning" (emphasis on the process). Therefore, this statement matches the facilitator model.
Q5 A Traditionally, it was felt that teachers should be in firm control of the learning process, and that the teacher's task was to prepare and present material for students to understand. Within this approach, the relationship students have with their teachers is not considered important, nor is the relationship students have with each other in the classroom. A student's participation in class is likely to be minimal, aside from asking questions directed at the teacher, or responding to questions that the teacher has directed at the student. This style encourages students to develop respect for positions of power as a source of control and discipline. It is frequently described as the "formal authority" model of teaching Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that under the "formal authority" style, the teacher fully controls the class and presents the lesson, while students participate very little and simply follow the teacher's direction.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "A" means that in the "formal authority" style of teaching, learners must adapt to how the instructor delivers lessons.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the passage explains that in the "formal authority" model, the teacher maintains complete control over the learning process, preparing and presenting the lesson for learners to understand without adapting to individual student needs or relationships. In contrast, the "demonstrator" model considers differing learning styles, while the student-centred models give control to the students.
Q6 D Another style that emphasises student-centred education is the "facilitator" mode of learning. Here, while a set of specific curriculum demands are already in place, students are encouraged to take the initiative for creating ways to meet these learning requirements together. The teacher typically designs activities that encourage active learning, group collaboration and problem solving, and students are encouraged to process and apply the course content in creative and original ways Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that in the facilitator style, teachers create activities that get students to participate actively, work together in groups, and solve problems creatively.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "D" means that the "Facilitator" style of teaching matches the description of a teacher who creates group tasks that help students work well and solve problems together.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because paragraph 5 specifically discusses the "facilitator" mode of learning. It states that in this style, "The teacher typically designs activities that encourage active learning, group collaboration and problem solving". Here, "designs activities that encourage... group collaboration" directly corresponds to the question's phrase "designs group activities that encourage constructive interaction."
Q7 B Instructors who are drawn to the demonstrator style are generally confident that their own way of performing a task represents a good base model, but they are sensitive to differing learning styles and expect to provide students with help on an individual basis Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that demonstrator-style teachers recognize different ways of learning and expect to help each student one by one.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means that the "demonstrator" teaching style is the one where a teacher gives personal, individual help to a student.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because paragraph 3 explains that teachers using the "demonstrator" style understand that students learn differently and plan to give help to each student separately. In this context, "one-on-one instruction" means giving help "on an individual basis."
Q8 C The delegator teacher maintains general authority, but they delegate much of the responsibility for learning to the class as a way for students to become independent thinkers who take pride in their own work. Students are often encouraged to work on their own or in groups, and if the delegator style is implemented successfully they will build not only a working knowledge of course specific topics, but also self-discipline and the ability to co-ordinate group work and interpersonal roles Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that delegator teachers give students the freedom and duty to manage their own learning, motivating them to work independently by themselves or together in groups.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "C" means the delegator teaching style is the one where students are encouraged to do tasks alone or in teams without relying on the teacher.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because paragraph 4 explains that in the "delegator" style, teachers give most of the learning responsibility to the students so they can become "independent thinkers". The text directly mentions that students are "encouraged to work on their own or in groups" under this style.
Q9 FALSE Until the 1960s, formal authority was common in almost all Western schools and universities. As a professor would enter a university lecture theatre, a student would be expected to rush up, take his bag to the desk, and pull out the chair for the professor to sit down on. This style has become outmoded over time Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the formal authority teaching style was used in Western schools and universities in the past (up to the 1960s), but it is now out of date rather than popular.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "FALSE" means that the statement directly contradicts the facts given in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage states that the formal authority style was common in Western schools and universities only until the 1960s and has since become "outmoded" (old-fashioned and no longer commonly used). Therefore, it does not remain popular today. Key terms to pay attention to include "formal authority", "Western schools and universities", and "outmoded".
Q10 NOT GIVEN At the highest levels of education, however, the demonstrator approach must be abandoned in all fields as students are required to produce innovative work that makes unique contributions to knowledge Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that the demonstrator model cannot be used at the very highest levels of education where students do advanced independent research, but it does not say that it is never used at university level at all.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the passage does not provide enough information to know if the demonstrator model is completely absent at the university or college level.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the text only mentions that the demonstrator style is not used at the "highest levels of education" (such as thesis and doctoral research). It does not say whether or not it is used at earlier or other stages of tertiary education (like undergraduate studies). Therefore, there is no statement confirming that it is "never" used at the tertiary level.
Q11 TRUE The delegator style confronts this problem by recognising that interpersonal communication is not just a means to learning but an important skill set in itself Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that delegator-style teaching helps students learn how to communicate and interact effectively with others as an essential skill.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is TRUE because the statement matches the information provided in the passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the text explains that the delegator teaching style helps students develop communication abilities. It notes that this method addresses workforce issues by treating "interpersonal communication" as a key skill set that students acquire, which means students who finish this style of education learn to communicate well.
Q12 FALSE It may, however, suit more experimental and theoretical fields ranging from English, music and the social sciences, to science and medical research that takes place in research labs Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that the facilitator style can be suitable for certain areas of medicine, specifically medical research done in laboratories.
Answer Explanation:
The answer FALSE means that the statement directly contradicts the facts given in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage explains that while the facilitator model is not suitable for practical training to become a doctor, it can work well for "medical research that takes place in research labs." Therefore, saying that it is not appropriate in the whole field of medicine is incorrect.
Q13 A Overall, a clear evolution has taken place in the West from a rigid, dogmatic and teacher-dominated way of learning, to a flexible, creative and student-centred approach. Nevertheless, different subjects, ages and skill levels suit different styles of teaching, and it is unlikely that there will ever be one recommended approach for everyone Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage summarizes that teaching has changed over time, but different kinds of subjects, student ages, and skill levels match different ways of teaching, meaning no single method works best for all situations.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'Teaching styles and their application' means the text explains different methods of teaching and where or how each method is used.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the entire passage describes four major teaching models—formal authority, demonstrator, delegator, and facilitator—and explains where each style is appropriately applied across different fields, subjects, and education levels. The final paragraph confirms this main theme by stating that 'different subjects, ages and skill levels suit different styles of teaching'. Choices like 'Teaching: then and now' or 'Why student-centred learning is best' are too narrow because the passage discusses much more than just historical changes and explicitly states that no single style is best for everyone.
