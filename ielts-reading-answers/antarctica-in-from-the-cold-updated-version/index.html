Reading Passage

Antarctica - in from the cold? (Updated version)

A A little over a century ago, men of the ilk of Scott, Shackleton and Mawson battled against Antarctica's blizzards, cold and deprivation. In the name of Empire and in an age of heroic deeds they created an image of Antarctica that was to last well into the 20th century - an image of remoteness, hardship, bleakness and isolation that was the province of only the most courageous of men. The image was one of a place removed from everyday reality, of a place with no apparent value to anyone.

B As we enter the 21st century, our perception of Antarctica has changed. Although physically Antarctica is no closer and probably no warmer, and to spend time there still demands a dedication not seen in ordinary life, the continent and its surrounding ocean are increasingly seen to be an integral part of Planet Earth, and a key component in the Earth System. Is this because the world seems a little smaller these days, shrunk by TV and tourism, or is it because Antarctica really does occupy a central spot on Earth's mantle? Scientific research during the past half century has revealed - and continues to reveal – that Antarctica's great mass and low temperature exert a major influence on climate and ocean circulation, factors which influence the lives of millions of people all over the globe.

C Antarctica was not always cold. The slow break-up of the super-continent Gondwana with the northward movements of Africa, South America, India and Australia eventually created enough space around Antarctica for the development of an Antarctic Circumpolar Current (ACC), that flowed from west to east under the influence of the prevailing westerly winds. Antarctica cooled, its vegetation perished, glaciation began and the continent took on its present-day appearance. Today the ice that overlies the bedrock is up to 4km thick, and surface temperatures as low as -89.2deg c have been recorded. The icy blast that howls over the ice cap and out to sea -the so-called katabatic wind - can reach 300 km/hr, creating fearsome wind chill effects.

D Out of this extreme environment come some powerful forces that reverberate around the world. The Earth's rotation, coupled to the generation of cells of low pressure off the Antarctic coast, would allow Astronauts a view of Antarctica that is as beautiful as it is awesome. Spinning away to the northeast, the cells grow and deepen, whipping up the Southern Ocean into the mountainous seas so respected by mariners. Recent work is showing that the temperature of the ocean may be a better predictor of rainfall in Australia than is the pressure difference between Darwin and Tahiti - the Southern Oscillation Index. By receiving more accurate predictions, graziers in northern Queensland are able to avoid overstocking in years when rainfall will be poor. Not only does this limit their losses but it prevents serious pasture degradation that may take decades to repair. CSIRO is developing this as a prototype forecasting system, but we can confidently predict that as we know more about the Antarctic and Southern Ocean we will be able to enhance and extend our predictive ability.

E The ocean's surface temperature results from the interplay between deepwater temperature, air temperature and ice. Each winter between 4 and 19 million square km of sea ice form, locking up huge quantities of heat close to the continent. Only now can we start to unravel the influence of sea ice on the weather that is experienced in southern Australia. But in another way the extent of sea ice extends its influence far beyond Antarctica. Antarctic krill - the small shrimp-like crustaceans that are the staple diet for baleen whales, penguins, some seals, flighted sea birds and many fish - breed well in years when sea ice is extensive and poorly when it is not. Many species of baleen whales and flighted sea birds migrate between the hemispheres and when the krill are less abundant they do not thrive.

F The circulatory system of the world's oceans is like a huge conveyor belt, moving water and dissolved minerals and nutrients from one hemisphere to the other, and from the ocean's abyssal depths to the surface. The ACC is the longest current in the world, and has the largest flow. Through it, the deep flows of the Atlantic, Indian and Pacific Oceans are joined to form part of a single global thermohaline circulation. During winter, the howling katabatics sometimes scour the ice off patches of the sea's surface leaving large ice-locked lagoons, or 'polynyas'. Recent research has shown that as fresh sea ice forms, it is continuously stripped away by the wind and may be blown up to 90km in a single day. Since only fresh water freezes into ice, the water that remains becomes increasingly salty and dense, sinking until it spills over the continental shelf. Cold water carries more oxygen than warm water, so when it rises, well into the northern hemisphere, it reoxygenates and revitalises the ocean. The state of the northern oceans, and their biological productivity, owe much to what happens in the Antarctic.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q14 D Recent work is showing that the temperature of the ocean may be a better predictor of rainfall in Australia than is the pressure difference between Darwin and Tahiti - the Southern Oscillation Index. By receiving more accurate predictions, graziers in northern Queensland are able to avoid overstocking in years when rainfall will be poor Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that new research uses sea temperatures to predict rainfall in Australia, allowing cattle farmers (graziers) to prepare when there will not be enough rain.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is paragraph D, which describes how studying ocean conditions helps forecast rainfall to assist farmers.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because paragraph D explicitly mentions an example where scientific research predicts rain ("rainfall in Australia") and how these weather predictions help cattle farmers ("graziers in northern Queensland") manage their livestock and farmland effectively.
Q15 F Cold water carries more oxygen than warm water, so when it rises, well into the northern hemisphere, it reoxygenates and revitalises the ocean Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that cold water holds a lot of oxygen. When this cold water moves up to the northern side of the planet, it gives oxygen back to the ocean and makes it healthy and full of life again.
Answer Explanation:
The answer F means that Paragraph F contains the matching information.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is F because Paragraph F explains how the process of sea ice forming leaves behind cold, oxygen-rich water that travels to the northern hemisphere and "reoxygenates and revitalises the ocean." This directly matches the idea of bringing life and health back to the world oceans' vitality.
Q16 E Antarctic krill - the small shrimp-like crustaceans that are the staple diet for baleen whales, penguins, some seals, flighted sea birds and many fish - breed well in years when sea ice is extensive and poorly when it is not. Many species of baleen whales and flighted sea birds migrate between the hemispheres and when the krill are less abundant they do not thrive Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that small sea animals called krill are the main food for many larger animals. The krill reproduce better when there is more sea ice. When there is less sea ice, there is less krill, which makes it hard for migrating animals to live successfully.
Answer Explanation:
The answer E means that Paragraph E contains the information about a food chain and how animal living patterns depend on sea ice.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because paragraph E explains a key food chain involving Antarctic krill, which are the main food ('staple diet') for many animals like whales, penguins, seals, and birds. It describes how these krill grow better when there is a lot of sea ice. It also mentions how animals that travel between different parts of the world ('migrate') do not do well ('do not thrive') when there is less krill, showing how sea ice directly affects animal living patterns.
Q17 C created enough space around Antarctica for the development of an Antarctic Circumpolar Current (ACC), that flowed from west to east under the influence of the prevailing westerly winds. Antarctica cooled, its vegetation perished, glaciation began and the continent took on its present-day appearance Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that wind forces moved the ocean current around Antarctica, which made the land cold, killed its plants, and turned it into ice.
Answer Explanation:
The answer shows that Paragraph C is the paragraph containing the explanation of how atmospheric conditions and winds around Antarctica affect climate change.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Paragraph C explains how winds ('prevailing westerly winds') affected the ocean current around Antarctica, causing the area to cool, plants to die ('vegetation perished'), and ice to form ('glaciation began'). Key words to focus on include 'westerly winds', 'Antarctica cooled', and 'glaciation'.
Q18 A The image was one of a place removed from everyday reality, of a place with no apparent value to anyone Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that people used to view Antarctica as a place that was completely separate from normal daily life and had no clear value to anyone.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is paragraph A.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because this paragraph describes how people used to think of Antarctica in the past. It shows that people viewed it as a far-away place that had no importance or usefulness to anyone. The phrase 'once thought to be' matches 'The image was', while 'forgotten and insignificant' matches being 'removed from everyday reality' and having 'no apparent value to anyone'.
Q19 D Scientific research during the past half century has revealed - and continues to reveal – that Antarctica's great mass and low temperature exert a major influence on climate and ocean circulation, factors which influence the lives of millions of people all over the globe Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that scientists have found that Antarctica's enormous size and cold temperatures have a powerful effect on global weather and ocean water movement.
Answer Explanation:
The answer choice D means temperature, which is the measure of how hot or cold something is.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because Paragraph B directly states that Antarctica's large size (great mass) and low temperature strongly affect the world's climate and ocean movements. Keywords like 'great mass' correspond to 'size', 'low temperature' matches 'temperature' (option D), and 'exert a major influence on climate' matches 'influence climate change'.
Q20 A The slow break-up of the super-continent Gondwana with the northward movements of Africa, South America, India and Australia eventually created enough space around Antarctica for the development of an Antarctic Circumpolar Current (ACC), that flowed from west to east under the influence of the prevailing westerly winds Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when the large landmass Gondwana broke apart and moved north, it opened up space around Antarctica for an ocean stream called the Antarctic Circumpolar Current (ACC) to form and flow along with the west-to-east winds.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is option A, which refers to the Antarctic Circumpolar Current (ACC).
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because Paragraph C explains how the movement of landmasses created space around Antarctica, allowing the Antarctic Circumpolar Current (ACC) to develop and flow driven by the prevailing westerly winds. Key matching terms to pay attention to include 'Antarctic Circumpolar Current (ACC)' and 'westerly winds'.
Q21 C Recent work is showing that the temperature of the ocean may be a better predictor of rainfall in Australia than is the pressure difference between Darwin and Tahiti - the Southern Oscillation Index Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that pressure differences (called the Southern Oscillation Index) can be used to tell in advance how much rain (rainfall) will fall in Australia.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "C" means "rainfall".
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because paragraph D explains that the Southern Oscillation Index, which uses air pressure differences, is used to forecast or predict rainfall in Australia. Therefore, rainfall is the correct match.
Q22 C Scientific research during the past half century has revealed - and continues to reveal – that Antarctica's great mass and low temperature exert a major influence on climate and ocean circulation, factors which influence the lives of millions of people all over the globe Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that scientific studies show Antarctica's large size and freezing temperature have a major effect on weather and water currents all around the world.
Answer Explanation:
The answer choice C means that the author wants to show how important Antarctica is because it plays a key role in affecting the world's weather patterns and ocean flows.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Paragraph B states that studies over the last fifty years show Antarctica's size and cold weather strongly affect world climate and ocean movement. The keywords 'climate and ocean circulation' in the passage directly match 'global climate and current' in option C.
Q23 A By receiving more accurate predictions, graziers in northern Queensland are able to avoid overstocking in years when rainfall will be poor. Not only does this limit their losses but it prevents serious pasture degradation that may take decades to repair Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that when farmers in Queensland get better weather forecasts, they do not put too many farm animals on the land during low rainfall years. This stops them from losing money and protects the grass and soil from long-term damage.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that knowing the Antarctic ocean temperature helps farmers in Australia protect their money (economic) and their land/nature (ecological) from harm.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because Paragraph D explains that understanding ocean temperature helps predict rainfall in Australia. With better weather forecasts, farmers (graziers) can avoid putting too many animals on their land during dry years. This helps them 'limit their losses' (reduce economic losses) and 'prevents serious pasture degradation' (reduce ecological/environmental losses).
Q24 B Recent research has shown that as fresh sea ice forms, it is continuously stripped away by the wind and may be blown up to 90km in a single day Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that strong wind keeps removing new sea ice as it forms, blowing it far away in one day.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says katabatic winds finally make the water near the ocean surface saltier.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B. The passage says the wind blows the fresh ice away. Only fresh water freezes, so the water left behind becomes 'increasingly salty and dense'. Then it sinks. So the final effect is more salt in the surface water. Watch the words 'stripped away', 'only fresh water freezes', and 'salty'.
Q25 A Antarctica was not always cold Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says Antarctica was not always cold. This line is about how the continent changed over time, not about salt in the water or the continental shelf.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says that more salt and higher density cause the event at the continental shelf.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because paragraph F says only fresh water freezes, so the water left behind becomes "increasingly salty and dense." This water sinks "until it spills over the continental shelf." Look for the keywords "salty," "dense," and "continental shelf." The excerpt about Antarctica being cold is not the real support. Options B, C and D do not match the passage.
Q26 C Antarctic krill - the small shrimp-like crustaceans that are the staple diet for baleen whales, penguins, some seals, flighted sea birds and many fish - breed well in years when sea ice is extensive and poorly when it is not. Many species of baleen whales and flighted sea birds migrate between the hemispheres and when the krill are less abundant they do not thrive Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that krill are the main food for whales and sea birds. Krill do not reproduce well when there is less sea ice, so when krill numbers are low, the whales and birds do not do well.
Answer Explanation:
The answer choice C means that having less sea ice lowers the amount of food (krill) produced, which negatively affects whales and seabirds.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the text states that krill are the main food ('staple diet') for baleen whales and flighted sea birds. Krill breed poorly when sea ice is not extensive (when there is less sea ice). As a result, when krill are 'less abundant' (less available as a food source), whales and seabirds 'do not thrive' (they struggle to survive and grow).
