Reading Passage

==== Read the text below and answer questions 1-5 ====

Maps Showing Walks Starting From Bingham Town Hall

A The walk described in this leaflet takes you to one of the many places in the district where bricks were made for hundreds of years, until it was closed in the late 19th century. This brickworks is now the largest and best-known nature reserve in the area. Please note that the ground is very uneven, and under-sixes should not be taken on this walk.

B This walk will take you to the top of Burley Hill, along a nice easy path that people of all ages will be able to manage. From the summit you can see for a great distance to the north and west, across a landscape that includes half a dozen lakes and the entrance to Butter Caves. Bear in mind, though, that mist often comes in from the sea and covers the hilltop.

C This route leads you through the village of Cottesloe, which was created in the 1930s and is famous for its strange-looking houses and ceramics factory, which is still the largest employer in the area. An artificial lake was originally created beside the village, and has since been filled in and turned into an adventure playground. After you leave Cottesloe, you have a choice of routes to return to the starting point, so either continue via Thurley Park, or if it’s raining, take the shorter direct route.

D This walk is ideal in fine weather, as it takes you to the shore of a lake, at a spot convenient for swimming. Children will want to enjoy themselves in the adventure playground nearby. From there you continue to Starling Cottage, which draws people from around the world to visit the home, from 1920 to 1927, of the poet Barbara Cottam.

E If you want an easy, undemanding walk over flat ground, this walk will suit you perfectly. It passes the entrance to the famous Butter Caves visitor attraction, so you can combine a visit there with the walk, or just take shelter if it starts raining! On the final stage of the walk you pass through Wimpole, the village where Richard Merton, the architect of a number of local buildings, lived for much of his life.

==== Read the text below and answer Questions 6-14 ====

The Maplehampton Scarecrow Competition – A Great Success!

There was once a time when farmers all over the country put scarecrows in fields of growing crops. A traditional scarecrow was a model – usually life-size – of a man or woman dressed in old clothes, and their purpose was to frighten the birds away; though how successful they were is a matter of opinion!

Maplehampton’s scarecrow competition took place on September 12th. Local farmers supplied everything needed to make a scarecrow – like pieces of wood to form a frame, and straw to stuff the scarecrow. The scarecrows were dressed in old clothes which the competitors brought with them.

The festival was held in the village hall, instead of outdoors as planned, due to the unusually high temperature. There were two classes, one for adults and one for children, all of them working in small teams. Over 20 teams took part, each creating one scarecrow. They were encouraged by an audience of around 50, and had ideas and guidance from local artist Tracey Sanzo.

The scarecrows were judged by a team of people from the village. The winning children’s team made a scarecrow that looked like a giant bird – which would surely keep every real bird away! The winning adult team’s scarecrow was dressed as an alien from another planet, and its face was painted to make it look very frightening – at least to human beings!

After the judging, many of the participants and the spectators had a picnic which they had brought. Some of the scarecrows then went home to their creators’ gardens. Alice Cameron, a local farmer, liked one of the scarecrows so much, she bought it to stand on her balcony: she said she didn’t need it to scare birds away from her crops, as only bird-scarers that made a noise were effective. She just wanted to be able to see it! The event raised over £300 for village funds.

Questions

Questions 1–5 Matching Information

The text has five paragraphs, A-E. Which paragraph mentions the following? Write the correct letter, A-E. NB You may use any letter more than once.

1 the chance to go into caves
2 the chance to spend time beside a lake
3 some unusual architecture
4 unsuitability for young children
5 the length of the walk depending on the weather

Questions 6–14 True / False / Not Given

Do the following statements agree with the information given in the text? Write

TRUE                  if the statement agrees with the information

FALSE                if the statement contradicts the information

NOT GIVEN     if there is no information on this

6 Traditionally, most scarecrows were the same size as a human being.
7 The competition in September was the first one in Maplehampton.
8 The farmers who provided materials could take part in the competition.
9 Old clothes were supplied to the people who made the scarecrows.
10 The venue for the competition was changed because of the weather.
11 Competitors could get advice on making their scarecrows.
12 In the judges’ opinion, the scarecrow dressed as an alien was better than the giant bird.
13 The competition organisers supplied a picnic for the competitors and spectators.
14 Alice Cameron bought a scarecrow to frighten birds away from her crops.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 E it passes the entrance to the famous Butter Caves visitor attraction Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that the walk goes by the entrance to Butter Caves, which is a special place you can visit, so it gives a chance to go into the caves.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is E. This means the paragraph that talks about going into caves is letter E.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because this paragraph mentions that the walk passes the entrance to the famous Butter Caves, which means you have a chance to visit the caves. It also says you can take shelter there if it rains.
Q2 D This walk is ideal in fine weather, as it takes you to the shore of a lake, at a spot convenient for swimming Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says this walk is great when the weather is nice because it goes to the edge of a lake where swimming is easy.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is D. This means that paragraph D talks about being near a lake.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because this paragraph describes a walk that goes to the shore of a lake. It also says that it is a good place for swimming and that there is an adventure playground nearby. This clearly shows the chance to spend time beside a lake.
Q3 C This route leads you through the village of Cottesloe, which was created in the 1930s and is famous for its strange-looking houses and ceramics factory Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage talks about a place called Cottesloe. It says that Cottesloe is well-known for its houses that look different and strange. This is why it is connected to unusual architecture.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is C. This means that paragraph C talks about buildings that look different from normal.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because it mentions Cottesloe, which is famous for its 'strange-looking houses'. This shows that paragraph C talks about unusual buildings or architecture.
Q4 A the ground is very uneven, and under-sixes should not be taken on this walk Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that the ground is not even, which means it is rough and can be difficult to walk on. It also says that children under six years old should not go on this walk, which means it is not suitable for them.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is A. This means paragraph A talks about why the walk is not good for young children.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because in this paragraph it says that the ground is uneven and that children who are under six years old should not go on this walk. This shows that it is not safe for very young children.
Q5 C After you leave Cottesloe, you have a choice of routes to return to the starting point, so either continue via Thurley Park, or if it's raining, take the shorter direct route Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that when you leave Cottesloe, you can choose different ways to go back. If it rains, you can take a shorter way, which means the weather can change how long your walk is.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is C. This means that the paragraph C talks about how the weather can change the path you take on a walk.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because this paragraph explains that after leaving the village of Cottesloe, you can choose different paths to return depending on the weather. If it rains, there is a shorter route to take. This shows that the weather affects the length of the walk.
Q6 TRUE A traditional scarecrow was a model – usually life-size – of a man or woman dressed in old clothes Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says a traditional scarecrow was usually a model of a man or woman. It was usually life-size and wore old clothes.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the statement is true. Most old scarecrows were as big as real people.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because "usually life-size" means the same as "most ... the same size as a human being." Notice the matching words: "traditional" = "traditionally", "usually" = "most", and "life-size" = "same size as a human being".
Q7 NOT GIVEN Maplehampton’s scarecrow competition took place on September 12th Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says the Maplehampton scarecrow competition happened on September 12th. It gives the date only.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the text does not tell us if this was the first competition in Maplehampton.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the text only gives the date (September 12th) and details about the event. It never says if there were earlier competitions, and it has no words like "first", "before", or "previous".
Q8 NOT GIVEN Local farmers supplied everything needed to make a scarecrow – like pieces of wood to form a frame, and straw to stuff the scarecrow Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says local farmers gave all the things needed to make a scarecrow, such as wood and straw.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means the text does not say if the farmers could join the competition.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the text only says farmers 'supplied' materials. It never says if they could or could not take part. Other words like 'competitors' and 'teams' do not say who they were. So we cannot say TRUE or FALSE.
Q9 FALSE The scarecrows were dressed in old clothes which the competitors brought with them Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says the people who made the scarecrows brought their own old clothes to put on them.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the statement is false. It does not match the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the question says old clothes were 'supplied' to the makers. The passage says the competitors 'brought' the clothes themselves. Only things like wood and straw were supplied by local farmers. Pay attention to 'supplied' and 'brought with them'.
Q10 TRUE The festival was held in the village hall, instead of outdoors as planned, due to the unusually high temperature Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says the festival was inside the village hall, not outside as planned, because the weather was very hot.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the statement is true: the place of the competition changed because of the weather.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because 'instead of outdoors as planned' means the venue changed, and 'unusually high temperature' is a weather reason. Match 'venue' with 'held in the village hall' and 'weather' with 'temperature'.
Q11 TRUE They were encouraged by an audience of around 50, and had ideas and guidance from local artist Tracey Sanzo Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says the teams had people watching and cheering them. It also says a local artist, Tracey Sanzo, gave them ideas and help.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the statement is TRUE. The text agrees that competitors could get advice on making their scarecrows.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because 'guidance' in the passage means the same as 'advice' in the statement. The artist Tracey Sanzo helped the teams, so they could get advice. Also notice 'ideas', which supports this.
Q12 NOT GIVEN The winning children’s team made a scarecrow that looked like a giant bird – which would surely keep every real bird away! Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says the children's team won with a scarecrow that looked like a giant bird. The writer thinks it would scare real birds away.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the text does not tell us if the judges liked the alien scarecrow more than the giant bird.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the judges chose a winner in the children's class (giant bird) and a winner in the adult class (alien). The words "two classes" and "winning" show they were judged separately. The text never compares the two scarecrows or says which one the judges liked better.
Q13 FALSE After the judging, many of the participants and the spectators had a picnic which they had brought Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that after the judging, many competitors and spectators ate a picnic that they brought themselves.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the statement is FALSE because the passage says the opposite of what the statement says.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the statement says the organisers 'supplied' the picnic, but the passage says people 'had a picnic which they had brought'. So the food came from the people, not the organisers. Pay attention to 'supplied' and 'brought'.
Q14 FALSE she said she didn't need it to scare birds away from her crops, as only bird-scarers that made a noise were effective Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says Alice Cameron did not need the scarecrow to scare birds from her crops. She said only things that make noise really work.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the statement is wrong because Alice did not buy the scarecrow to scare birds.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage says she bought it to stand on her balcony and 'just wanted to be able to see it'. The words 'didn't need it to scare birds away' directly contradict the statement.
