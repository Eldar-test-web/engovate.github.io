Fear Of The Unknown - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From IELTS Vol 3 Academic Reading Test 1 · Part 3 · Questions 27–40

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 27-40, which are based on Reading Passage 3 below.

Fear of the Unknown

American companies fear that innovation is the secret of success - and that they cannot innovate

A In the small Umagic office in midtown Manhattan, a team of 30 computer programmers are working on setting up websites that will allow subscribers to feed in details about themselves and their problems and to receive advice from 'virtual' versions of personalities regarded as experts in their fields: for example, a well-known dietician, a celebrity fitness trainer, a psychologist well known in the media for her work on parent-child relationships. Umagic Systems is a young firm and it's hard to predict how far they'll go. In ten years' time, consulting a computer about your diet problems might seem natural or it might seem absurd. But the company and others like it are beginning to seriously worry large American firms, who see such half-crazy new and innovative ideas as a threat to their own future success.

B Innovation has become a major concern of American management. Firms have found that it is increasingly difficult to redesign existing products or to produce them more economically. The stars of American business tend today to be innovators such as Amazon (the internet bookstore) and Wal-Mart (the supermarket chain) which have produced completely new ideas or products that have changed their industries.

C Over the past 15 years, the firms which have achieved the greatest profits have been the ones which have had the most innovations. But such profits aren't easy to come by. One of the reasons for the increasing number of mergers between companies is a desperate search for new ideas. And a fortune is spent nowadays on identifying and protecting intellectual property: other people's ideas. According to the Pasadena-based Patent & License Exchange in the United States, trading in intangible assets such as intellectual property rose from $15 billion in 1990 to $100 billion in 1998, with an increasing proportion of the rewards going to small firms and individuals.

D And therein lies the terror for big companies: that innovation seems to work best outside them. Many of the large established companies have been struggling to come up with new products recently. 'In the management of creativity, size is your enemy,' argues Peter Chernin, who runs Fox TV and film empire for News Corporation. 'One person managing 20 movies is never going to be as involved as one doing five movies.' He has thus tried to break down the studio into smaller units, even at the risk of incurring higher costs.

E It is easier for ideas to develop outside big firms these days. In the past, if a clever scientist had an idea he wanted to commercialise, he would take it first to a big company. Now, with the banks encouraging individuals to set up new businesses through offering special loans, innovators are more likely to set up on their own. Umagic has already raised $5 million and is about to raise $25 million more. Even in capital-intensive businesses such as pharmaceuticals, entrepreneurs can conduct profitable, early-stage research, selling out to the big firms when they reach expensive, risky clinical trials.

F Some giants, including General Electric and Cisco, have been remarkably successful at buying up and integrating scores of small companies. But many others worry about the prices they have to pay and the difficulty in keeping hold of the people who dreamt up the ideas. Everybody would like to develop more ideas in-house. Procter & Gamble is now changing the entire direction of its business from global expansion to product development; one of its new aims is to get innovations accepted across the company. Elsewhere, the search for innovation has led to a craze for 'intrapreneurship' - giving more power to individuals in the company and setting up internal ideas-factories so that talented staff will not leave.

G And yet innovation does not happen just because the chief executive wills it. Indeed, it is extremely difficult to come up with new ideas year in, year out, especially brilliant ones. Underneath all experts' diagrams, lists and charts, most of the available answers seem to focus on two strengths that are difficult to impose: a culture that looks for new ideas, and leaders who know which ones to back. Companies have to discredit the widespread view that jobs working on new products are for 'those who can't cope in the real business'. They have to change the culture by introducing hard incentives, such as giving more generous bonuses to those who come up with successful new ideas and, particularly, not punishing those whose experiments fail.

H Will all this reorganisation and culture tweaking make big firms more creative? David Post, the founder of Umagic, isn't so sure. He also recalls with glee the looks of total incomprehension when he tried to sell his 'virtual experts' idea three years ago to firms such as IBM, though, as he cheerfully adds, 'of course, they could have been right'. Apparently, innovation - unlike diet, fitness and parenting - is one area where a computer cannot tell you what to do.

Questions

Questions 27–33 Matching Information

Reading Passage 3 has eight paragraphs, A-H.

Which paragraph contains the following information?

You may use any letter more than once.

27 the methods some companies use to try to keep their most creative employees
28 a new way of getting help with your personal difficulties
29 how much investment goes into safeguarding the ideas of individuals
30 two examples of companies which have succeeded through being innovative
31 how some innovators manage to avoid spending large sums of money on testing out their ideas
32 a commonly held opinion about product designers that needs to be proved wrong
33 the target of one large company that has changed its business focus

Questions 34–37 True / False / Not Given

Do the following statements agree with the information given in Reading Passage 3?

Write

TRUE if the statement agrees with the information

FALSE if the statement contradicts the information

NOT GIVEN if there is no information on this

34 Umagic Systems is an example of a new innovative company.
35 Amazon and Wal-Mart have exchanged successful ideas on innovation.
36 Using financial rewards to encourage innovation is an outdated practice.
37 IBM failed to understand David Post's 'virtual experts' idea.

Questions 38–40 One Choice

Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D.

38 What point does the writer make about intellectual property?
  1. It can be lost when firms merge.
  2. It tends to belong to companies rather than individuals.
  3. It is valued more than it used to be.
  4. It is not usually owned by small companies.
39 Peter Chernin is an example of someone who has realised that
  1. large companies are less innovative than small ones.
  2. other businesses are more innovative than the film business.
  3. his employees need more experience of innovation.
  4. he is the best person to encourage innovation.
40 In conclusion, the writer suggests that
  1. computer-based industries cannot be innovative.
  2. big firms are right to be cautious about innovation.
  3. small firms should not worry about early failures.
  4. innovation will always involve some uncertainty.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 F Elsewhere, the search for innovation has led to a craze for 'intrapreneurship' - giving more power to individuals in the company and setting up internal ideas-factories so that talented staff will not leave Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says some companies give more power to staff and set up internal idea factories so talented people stay.
Answer Explanation:
The answer F means paragraph F describes how companies try to keep creative employees.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is F because 'talented staff will not leave' matches 'keep their most creative employees'.
Q28 A In the small Umagic office in midtown Manhattan, a team of 30 computer programmers are working on setting up websites that will allow subscribers to feed in details about themselves and their problems and to receive advice from 'virtual' versions of personalities regarded as experts in their fields Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says Umagic builds websites where people describe their problems and get advice from 'virtual' experts.
Answer Explanation:
The answer A means paragraph A describes a new way to get help with personal difficulties.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because 'their problems' matches 'personal difficulties' and advice from 'virtual' experts is the new way.
Q29 C And a fortune is spent nowadays on identifying and protecting intellectual property: other people's ideas Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says a lot of money is now spent on finding and protecting other people's ideas.
Answer Explanation:
The answer C means paragraph C says how much is spent on protecting ideas.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because 'a fortune is spent' matches 'how much investment' and 'protecting intellectual property' matches 'safeguarding ideas'.
Q30 B The stars of American business tend today to be innovators such as Amazon (the internet bookstore) and Wal-Mart (the supermarket chain) which have produced completely new ideas or products that have changed their industries Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says Amazon and Wal-Mart are business stars because their new ideas changed their industries.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means paragraph B gives two successful innovative companies.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because Amazon and Wal-Mart are 'two examples' of firms that succeeded by being innovative.
Q31 E Even in capital-intensive businesses such as pharmaceuticals, entrepreneurs can conduct profitable, early-stage research, selling out to the big firms when they reach expensive, risky clinical trials Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says entrepreneurs do early research cheaply and sell to big firms before costly trials.
Answer Explanation:
The answer E means paragraph E shows how innovators avoid big testing costs.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because 'selling out ... when they reach expensive, risky clinical trials' matches avoiding large sums on testing.
Q32 G Companies have to discredit the widespread view that jobs working on new products are for 'those who can't cope in the real business' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says companies must prove wrong the common idea that new-product jobs are for people who cannot cope.
Answer Explanation:
The answer G means paragraph G mentions a common opinion that needs to be proved wrong.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is G because 'widespread view' matches 'commonly held opinion' and 'discredit' matches 'proved wrong'.
Q33 F Procter & Gamble is now changing the entire direction of its business from global expansion to product development; one of its new aims is to get innovations accepted across the company Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says Procter & Gamble is changing from global expansion to product development and wants ideas accepted across the company.
Answer Explanation:
The answer F means paragraph F names the goal of a big company that changed its focus.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is F because 'changing the entire direction' matches 'changed its business focus' and 'new aims' matches 'target'.
Q34 TRUE Umagic Systems is a young firm and it's hard to predict how far they'll go Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says Umagic Systems is a young company.
Answer Explanation:
The answer TRUE means Umagic is an example of a new innovative company.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because 'young firm' matches 'new' and the paragraph describes its new ideas.
Q35 NOT GIVEN The stars of American business tend today to be innovators such as Amazon (the internet bookstore) and Wal-Mart (the supermarket chain) which have produced completely new ideas or products that have changed their industries Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says Amazon and Wal-Mart both created new ideas. It does not say they shared ideas with each other.
Answer Explanation:
The answer NOT GIVEN means the passage does not say they exchanged ideas.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage names both companies as innovators but never says they swapped ideas.
Q36 FALSE They have to change the culture by introducing hard incentives, such as giving more generous bonuses to those who come up with successful new ideas and, particularly, not punishing those whose experiments fail Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says companies should change by giving bigger bonuses for successful ideas.
Answer Explanation:
The answer FALSE means rewarding innovation with money is not outdated.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the writer says companies must introduce incentives like bonuses, so it is a recommended practice.
Q37 TRUE He also recalls with glee the looks of total incomprehension when he tried to sell his 'virtual experts' idea three years ago to firms such as IBM, though, as he cheerfully adds, 'of course, they could have been right' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says firms such as IBM looked totally confused when Post tried to sell his 'virtual experts' idea.
Answer Explanation:
The answer TRUE means IBM did not understand Post's idea.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because 'total incomprehension' matches 'failed to understand'.
Q38 C trading in intangible assets such as intellectual property rose from $15 billion in 1990 to $100 billion in 1998, with an increasing proportion of the rewards going to small firms and individuals Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says trade in intellectual property grew from $15 billion to $100 billion in eight years.
Answer Explanation:
The answer C means intellectual property is valued more than it used to be.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the big rise in money traded shows it is worth more. 'Small firms and individuals' also gain, so B and D are wrong.
Q39 A 'In the management of creativity, size is your enemy,' argues Peter Chernin, who runs Fox TV and film empire for News Corporation Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says Peter Chernin believes that in managing creativity, being big is a problem.
Answer Explanation:
The answer A means Chernin knows that large companies are less innovative than small ones.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because 'size is your enemy' means big companies find it harder to be creative.
Q40 D Will all this reorganisation and culture tweaking make big firms more creative? David Post, the founder of Umagic, isn't so sure Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says nobody is sure that changes will make big firms more creative, and the paragraph ends by saying a computer cannot tell you what to do about innovation.
Answer Explanation:
The answer D means innovation will always involve some uncertainty.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because 'isn't so sure' and 'cannot tell you what to do' show that no one can be certain about innovation.
