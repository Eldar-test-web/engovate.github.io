Mending Broken Hearts

A Although hearts suffer many maladies-valves leak, membranes become inflamed – coronary heart disease, which can lead to heart attack and ultimately to heart failure, is the number one killer of both men and women in the United States, where 500,000 die annually. Worldwide, it kills 7.2 million people every year. Exacerbated by the export of Western lifestyle – motorized transport, abundant meat and cheese, workdays conducted from the comfort of a well-padded chair – incidence of the disease is soaring.

B To help stem this lethal tide, cardiologists can prescribe such cholesterol-lowering drugs as statins to help keep arteries clear. They can advise patients to change their habits, or they can operate to fix an immediate problem. Angioplasty is one procedure, and surgery to bypass the diseased arteries is another – each year more than 400,000 bypasses are performed in the U.S. Transplants can replace severely damaged hearts, and artificial ones can keep people alive while they wait for a donor heart. But in the face of an impending global epidemic, none of these stopgap measures addresses the essential question: Who gets heart attacks and why?

C The human heart beats 100,000 times a day, propelling six quarts (5.6 liters) of blood through 60,000 miles (96,560.6 kilometers) of vessels – 20 times the distance across the U.S. from coast to coast. The blood flows briskly, surging out of a ten-ounce (283.5 gram) heart so forcefully that large arteries, when severed, can send a jet of blood several feet into the air. Normally the relentless current helps keep blood vessels clean. But where an artery bends, tiny eddies form, as in a bend in a river. This is where bits of sticky, waxy cholesterol and fat can seep into the artery wall and oxidize, like butter going rancid. Other matter piles up too. Eventually, the whole mass calcifies into a kind of arterial stucco, or plaque.

D Until recently, cardiologists approached heart disease as a plumbing problem. Just as mineral deposits restrict the flow of water through a pipe, an accretion of plaque impedes the flow of blood through an arterial channel. The more crud in the system, the greater likelihood that a dammed artery will trigger a heart attack. Doctors now dismiss this "clogged-pipes model" as an idea whose time has passed. It's just not that simple.

E Most heart attacks are caused by plaque embedded within the artery wall that ruptures, cracking the wall and triggering the formation of a blood clot. The clot blocks the flow of blood to the heart muscle, which can suffer from the lack of oxygen and nutrients. Suddenly, the pump stops pumping.

F Contrary to the clogged pipes model, heart attacks generally occur in arteries that have minimal or moderate blockage, and their occurrence depends more on the kind of plaque than on the quantity. Scientists have been struggling to figure out what type is most responsible. Paradoxically, findings suggest that immature, softer plaques rich in cholesterol are more unstable and likely to rupture than the hard, calcified, dense plaques that extensively narrow the artery channel. But understanding the root cause of the disease will require much more research. For one thing, human hearts, unlike plumbing fixtures, are not stamped from a mold. Like the rest of our body parts, they are products of our genes.

G Don Steffensen was putting duck-hunting decoys out on a small lake one fall afternoon in South Western Iowa when his heart attack hit. The infarction was massive and unexpected. It's likely that Steffensen survived only because a buddy was carrying nitroglycerin tablets and quickly slipped one under his friend's tongue. Nitroglycerin is used to make dynamite; in the body, a heavily diluted form releases nitric oxide, which signals the smooth muscle cells in veins and arteries to relax, dilating the vessels.

H The Steffensen clan is enormous: more than 200 relatives spread over three generations, many of the youngest are now dispersed from Iowa to New York and beyond. Although heart trouble is common in the family, it had never struck anyone as unusual. "I attributed it to diet," shrugs Tina, a slim 38-year-old and the family's only vegetarian.

I It was a reasonable conclusion. The Steffensens were raised on the kind of farm food that the state is famous for – ham balls, meatloaf, pie, macaroni and cheese – and still popular even as careers have moved indoors. Driving north through cornfields to meet some of the family in Buffalo Center, I dined at a restaurant offering deep-fried sandwiches. A single ham and cheese hoagie – dunked in hot fat and served sizzling – seemed capable of stopping a heart all on its own.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 YES coronary heart disease, which can lead to heart attack and ultimately to heart failure, is the number one killer of both men and women in the United States Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that coronary heart disease causes more deaths than anything else for both men and women in the United States.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means that the statement is completely true according to the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because Paragraph A clearly states that coronary heart disease is the "number one killer" of both men and women in the United States. In the question, the phrase "the largest culprit behind the deaths" has the exact same meaning as the phrase "the number one killer" in the text. Since the statement matches the passage, the answer is YES.
Q28 NOT GIVEN Exacerbated by the export of Western lifestyle – motorized transport, abundant meat and cheese, workdays conducted from the comfort of a well-padded chair – incidence of the disease is soaring Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that spreading the Western way of life—such as using cars, eating a lot of meat and cheese, and sitting in chairs all day—makes the rate of heart disease climb quickly. However, it does not claim that this lifestyle is the biggest single reason for the increase.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the passage does not provide enough information to know if the Western way of living is the biggest or main reason why heart disease is increasing.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because Paragraph A mentions that the spread of the "Western lifestyle" has made the number of heart disease cases rise faster ("exacerbated"). However, the text never compares it to other causes or states that it is the "largest cause". Because the passage does not say whether it is the number-one reason or just one of several contributing factors, the statement cannot be confirmed as true or false.
Q29 NO none of these stopgap measures addresses the essential question: Who gets heart attacks and why Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that none of the temporary treatments used by doctors actually answer the most important question of who gets heart attacks and the reason why they get them.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement is false according to the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because the passage clearly states that current medical actions do not answer the main questions about heart attacks. The text mentions several treatments used by doctors, but explains that "none of these stopgap measures addresses the essential question: Who gets heart attacks and why?". Because the statement claims that experts have "successfully answered" these questions, it directly contradicts the information in the text.
Q30 YES The human heart beats 100,000 times a day, propelling six quarts (5.6 liters) of blood through 60,000 miles (96,560.6 kilometers) of vessels – 20 times the distance across the U.S. from coast to coast Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that in a single day, blood travels a very long path in our blood vessels, which is 20 times longer than traveling completely across the United States.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means that the statement is true and matches the information in the reading passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because paragraph C states that each day, the heart pushes blood through 60,000 miles of vessels, which is "20 times the distance across the U.S. from coast to coast." The phrase "a day" corresponds to "on a daily basis", and being "20 times the distance" clearly means it travels "much more distance" than the trip from coast to coast.
Q31 NOT GIVEN This is where bits of sticky, waxy cholesterol and fat can seep into the artery wall and oxidize, like butter going rancid Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that sticky pieces of cholesterol and fat enter into the artery walls, but it does not mention anything about cholesterol giving energy to help the body work.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the passage does not provide enough information to know whether cholesterol is kept in arteries to give energy to the body.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because although the passage talks about cholesterol getting into the artery walls, it never mentions that the body stores cholesterol there to provide energy. Because this specific reason or function is not mentioned anywhere in the text, we cannot say if the statement is true or false.
Q32 NO Doctors now dismiss this "clogged-pipes model" as an idea whose time has passed Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says doctors now reject the 'clogged-pipes model'. They think it is old and no longer a good idea.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is NO. The statement says most doctors accept the model, but the passage says the opposite.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because the statement says 'accepted by most doctors', but the passage says doctors 'dismiss' it and its 'time has passed'. 'Accepted' is the opposite of 'dismiss', so the statement contradicts the passage.
Q33 YES Scientists have been struggling to figure out what type is most responsible Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that researchers are still working hard and trying to understand which kind of plaque is mainly to blame for causing heart attacks.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "YES" means that the statement is correct according to the passage: researchers still do not know for certain which main type or factor is most responsible for causing heart attacks.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because the author states in Paragraph F that heart attacks depend on the kind of plaque rather than the total amount, but researchers have not reached a final conclusion. The phrase "have yet to decide" matches the passage's statement that scientists "have been struggling to figure out", and "the most likely factor that leads to heart attack" refers to "what type is most responsible".
Q34 NO "I attributed it to diet," shrugs Tina, a slim 38-year-old and the family's only vegetarian
It was a reasonable conclusion		 Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that a family member believed their heart problems came from what they ate, and the author agrees that this was a sensible and believable explanation.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NO" means that the statement is incorrect because it contradicts what is written in the reading passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because Don Steffenson's family case suggests that bad eating habits definitely do contribute to severe heart attacks. In the passage, family members and the author link their frequent heart problems to their typical heavy, high-fat farm diet, calling this a "reasonable conclusion." Therefore, saying that his case suggests unhealthy eating does not cause serious heart attacks is the opposite of what the text says.
Q35 D To help stem this lethal tide, cardiologists can prescribe such cholesterol-lowering drugs as statins to help keep arteries clear Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that doctors can give patients special pills that reduce cholesterol so that their blood vessels stay clear and open.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that heart doctors give patients medicine that lowers cholesterol to keep their blood tubes (arteries) open, clean, and unblocked.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because Paragraph B explains the purpose of giving cholesterol-lowering drugs. Heart doctors (cardiologists) give these medicines, like statins, to "keep arteries clear," which matches the idea of smoothing or clearing the arteries so blood can flow easily.
Q36 G Transplants can replace severely damaged hearts, and artificial ones can keep people alive while they wait for a donor heart Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that doctors can replace very sick hearts with new ones, and man-made hearts can save patients' lives while they wait to receive a heart given by another person.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that man-made hearts are used to keep very sick people alive until a real human heart from a donor becomes ready for them.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is G because Paragraph B explains what doctors can do for patients with severe heart problems. It mentions that "artificial ones" (referring to artificial hearts) are used to keep patients alive during the time they must wait for a donor heart to become available. In choice G, "help patients survive" means the same as "keep people alive", and "until the availability of a donated human heart" matches "while they wait for a donor heart".
Q37 C Just as mineral deposits restrict the flow of water through a pipe, an accretion of plaque impedes the flow of blood through an arterial channel Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that plaque blocking blood inside an artery is like mineral dirt blocking water inside a pipe.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "C" means that heart problems in blood vessels are compared to mineral buildup that stops water from moving smoothly inside a pipe.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because paragraph D explains how doctors previously compared heart disease to plumbing issues. Specifically, the text notes: "Just as mineral deposits restrict the flow of water through a pipe, an accretion of plaque impedes the flow of blood through an arterial channel." In this comparison, mineral buildup stopping water flow matches plaque stopping blood flow in arteries. Key words from the text include "mineral deposits restrict the flow of water through a pipe", which directly matches choice C's "deposits of minerals limiting the flow of water through a pipe".
Q38 F The clot blocks the flow of blood to the heart muscle, which can suffer from the lack of oxygen and nutrients Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when a blood clot appears, it stops blood from reaching the heart muscle, which leaves the heart without necessary oxygen and food.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that a blood clot stops blood from reaching the heart muscle, preventing it from getting needed oxygen and nutrients.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is F because Paragraph E explains what happens when a blood clot forms. It states that the clot stops or blocks the blood from traveling to the heart muscle, which leads to a lack of oxygen and vital nutrients. In choice F, the idea of stopping blood from moving and keeping away oxygen and nutrients directly matches the text.
Q39 B Contrary to the clogged pipes model, heart attacks generally occur in arteries that have minimal or moderate blockage, and their occurrence depends more on the kind of plaque than on the quantity Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that, unlike the old idea of blocked pipes, whether someone gets a heart attack depends more on the type of blockage than on how much blockage is present.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that having a heart attack is related to the specific type and characteristics of the plaque blockage, rather than how much of it there is.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because paragraph F clearly states that, unlike the old clogged-pipes idea, heart attacks depend more on the "kind of plaque" than on the amount. The passage explains that different types and qualities of plaque (such as softer, cholesterol-rich plaque versus hard, calcified plaque) behave differently. Here, "kind" corresponds to "categories" and traits like "softer" or "hard" correspond to "properties".
Q40 D Although heart trouble is common in the family, it had never struck anyone as unusual. "I attributed it to diet," shrugs Tina, a slim 38-year-old and the family's only vegetarian Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that heart problems often happen in the Steffensen family. Tina, who is the only person in the family who does not eat meat, thought this was caused by the food they eat.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that what people eat is seen as the reason for heart problems in this family.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because paragraphs H and I discuss why heart trouble frequently affects the Steffensen family. In paragraph H, Tina explains that she connects their family's heart problems to their "diet" (what they eat). Paragraph I agrees that this is a "reasonable conclusion" because the family grew up eating heavy, fatty foods like ham balls, cheese, and deep-fried sandwiches. Therefore, their eating habits and diets are presented as the likely factor.
