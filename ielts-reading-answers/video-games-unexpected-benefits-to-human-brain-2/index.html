Video Games’ Unexpected Benefits To Human Brain - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From IELTS Mock Tests Academic Reading Test 29 · Part 3 · Questions 28–40

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 28-40 which are based on Reading Passage 3 below.

Video Games’ Unexpected Benefits to Human Brain

A

James Paul Gee, professor of education at the University of Wisconsin-Madison, played his first video game years ago when his six-year-old son Sam was playing Pajama Sam: No Need to Hide When It’s Dark Outside. He wanted to play the game so he could support Sam’s problem-solving. Though Pajama Sam is not an “educational game”, it is replete with the types of problems psychologists study when they study thinking and learning. When he saw how well the game held Sam’s attention, he wondered what sort of beast a more mature video game might be.

B

Video and computer games, like many other popular, entertaining and addicting kid’s activities, are looked down upon by many parents as time-wasters, and worse, parents think that these games rot the brain. Violent video games are readily blamed by the media and some experts as the reason why some youth become violent or commit extreme anti-social behavior. Recent content analyses of video games show that as many as 89% of games contain some violent content, but there is no form of aggressive content for 70% of popular games. Many scientists and psychologists, like James Paul Gee, find that video games actually have many benefits – the main one being making kids smart. Video games may actually teach kids high-level thinking skills that they will need in the future.

C

“Video games change your brain,” according to University of Wisconsin psychologist Shawn Green. Video games change the brain’s physical structure the same way as do learning to read, playing the piano, or navigating using a map. Much like exercise can build muscle, the powerful combination of concentration and rewarding surges of neurotransmitters like dopamine, which strengthens neural circuits, can build the player’s brain.

D

Video games give your child’s brain a real workout. In many video games, the skills required to win involve abstract and high-level thinking. These skills are not even taught at school. Some of the mental skills trained by video games include: following instructions, problem-solving, logic, hand-eye coordination, fine motor and spatial skills. Research also suggests that people can learn iconic, spatial, and visual attention skills from video games. There have been even studies with adults showing that experience with video games is related to better surgical skills. Jacob Benjamin, a doctor from Beth Israel Medical Center NY, found a direct link between skill at video gaming and skill at keyhole or laparoscopic surgery. Also, a reason given by experts as to why fighter pilots of today are more skillful is that this generation’s pilots are being weaned on video games.

E

The players learn to manage resources that are limited and decide the best use of resources, the same way as in real life. In strategy games, for instance, while developing a city, an unexpected surprise like an enemy might emerge. This forces the player to be flexible and quickly change tactics. Sometimes the player does this almost every second of the game giving the brain a real workout. According to researchers at the University of Rochester, led by Daphne Bavelier, a cognitive scientist, games simulating stressful events such as those found in battle or action games could be a training tool for real-world situations. The study suggests that playing action video games primes the brain to make quick decisions. Video games can be used to train soldiers and surgeons, according to the study. Steven Johnson, author of Everything Bad is Good For You: How Today’s Popular Culture Is Actually Making Us Smarter, says gamers must deal with immediate problems while keeping their long-term goals on their horizon. Young gamers force themselves to read to get instructions, follow storylines of games, and get information from the game texts.

F

James Paul Gee, professor of education at the University of Wisconsin-Madison, says that playing a video game is similar to working through a science problem. Like students in a laboratory, gamers must come up with a hypothesis. For example, players in some games constantly try out combinations of weapons and powers to use to defeat an enemy. If one does not work, they change the hypothesis and try the next one. Video games are goal-driven experiences, says Gee, which is fundamental to learning. Also, using math skills is important to win in many games that involve quantitative analysis like managing resources. In higher levels of a game, players usually fail the first time around, but they keep on trying until they succeed and move on to the next level.

G

Many games are played online and involve cooperation with other online players in order to win. Video and computer games also help children gain self-confidence and many games are based on history, city building, and governance and so on. Such games indirectly teach children about aspects of life on earth.

H

In an upcoming study in the journal Current Biology, authors Daphne Bavelier, Alexandre Pouget, and C. Shawn Green report that video games could provide a potent training regimen for speeding up reactions in many types of real-life situations. The researchers tested dozens of 18 to 25-year-olds who were not ordinarily video game players. They split the subjects into two groups. One group played 50 hours of the fast-paced action video games “Call of Duty 2” and “Unreal Tournament,” and the other group played 50 hours of the slow-moving strategy game “The Sims 2.” After this training period, all of the subjects were asked to make quick decisions in several tasks designed by the researchers. The action game players were up to 25 percent faster at coming to a conclusion and answered just as many questions correctly as their strategy game playing peers.

Questions

Questions 28–31 One Choice

Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D.

28 What is the main purpose of paragraph ONE?
  1. Introduction of professor James Paul Gee.
  2. Introduction of the video game: Pajama Sam.
  3. Introduction of types of video games.
  4. Introduction of the background of this passage.
29 What does the author want to express in the second paragraph?
  1. Video games are widely considered harmful for children’s brain.
  2. Most violent video games are a direct reason for juvenile delinquency.
  3. Even there is a certain proportion of violence in most video games; scientists and psychologists see its benefits of children’s intellectual abilities.
  4. Many parents regard video games as time-wasters, which rot children’s brain.
30 What is correctly mentioned in paragraph four?
  1. Some schools use video games to teach students abstract and high-level thinking.
  2. Video games improve brain ability in various aspects.
  3. some surgeons have better skills because they play more video games.
  4. Skillful fighter pilots in this generation love to play video games.
31 What is the expectation of the experiment the three researchers did?
  1. Gamers have to make the best use of the limited resource.
  2. Gamers with better math skills will win in the end.
  3. Strategy game players have a better ability to make quick decisions.
  4. Video games help increase the speed of the players’ reaction effectively.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q28 D James Paul Gee, professor of education at the University of Wisconsin-Madison, played his first video game years ago when his six-year-old son Sam was playing Pajama Sam: No Need to Hide When It’s Dark Outside. He wanted to play the game so he could support Sam’s problem-solving. Though Pajama Sam is not an “educational game”, it is replete with the types of problems psychologists study when they study thinking and learning. When he saw how well the game held Sam’s attention, he wondered what sort of beast a more mature video game might be Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows how a professor started observing how games involve thinking and learning while watching his son play. This personal story introduces the background context for why scientists began researching the benefits of video games.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the first paragraph gives the opening story or background context that leads into the main topic of the article.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because Paragraph A tells a story about how Professor James Paul Gee became interested in video games after seeing how a game helped his son think and learn. This story serves as an introductory background setting up the main discussion of the entire passage, which is how video games can benefit the human brain. It does not exist merely to introduce the professor as a biography (option A), describe only one game (option B), or list categories of video games (option C).
Q29 C Recent content analyses of video games show that as many as 89% of games contain some violent content, but there is no form of aggressive content for 70% of popular games. Many scientists and psychologists, like James Paul Gee, find that video games actually have many benefits – the main one being making kids smart Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that most video games have violent parts, yet researchers still see that these games bring important benefits, especially by helping children become smarter.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that although most games have some violence, researchers believe video games help children become smarter.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because paragraph B presents both the negative view of games and the research that balances it. The author notes that 89% of video games contain violence, but contrasts this by stating that many scientists and psychologists discover clear benefits, especially in improving intelligence. Here, "violent content" matches "proportion of violence", and "making kids smart" corresponds to "children's intellectual abilities". Choices A and D only mention the parents' negative view without the author's main point, and B incorrectly treats the media's blame as a proven fact.
Q30 B Some of the mental skills trained by video games include: following instructions, problem-solving, logic, hand-eye coordination, fine motor and spatial skills Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that video games help practice and improve many different kinds of thinking and coordination skills.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that playing video games helps the brain grow and work better in many different ways.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because paragraph D specifically lists a wide range of mental skills that games help train. The author notes that video games exercise abilities such as following instructions, solving problems, thinking logically, coordinating hands and eyes, and using spatial and visual attention. This shows that video games improve brain ability in "various aspects" (many different areas). In contrast, option A is incorrect because the text says these skills are not taught in school; option C mistakes a correlation/link for a proven cause; and option D claims pilots "love" playing games, which the passage never states.
Q31 D In an upcoming study in the journal Current Biology, authors Daphne Bavelier, Alexandre Pouget, and C. Shawn Green report that video games could provide a potent training regimen for speeding up reactions in many types of real-life situations Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the three researchers believe video games can be a very powerful way to help people make faster reactions in real-life moments.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the researchers expected video games to help people react and respond more quickly and successfully.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because paragraph H mentions the three researchers (Daphne Bavelier, Alexandre Pouget, and C. Shawn Green) and their study. The text states they reported that video games could be "a potent training regimen for speeding up reactions". Here, "potent training regimen" means an effective way to train, and "speeding up reactions" means increasing the speed of the players' reactions.
Q32 NOT GIVEN Recent content analyses of video games show that as many as 89% of games contain some violent content, but there is no form of aggressive content for 70% of popular games Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that most games have some violence, while most popular games actually have no aggressive content. However, it does not explain the reason why players like these games or why they become popular.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage does not tell us why video games are popular, so we cannot know if violence is the reason for their popularity.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the text discusses how many games have violent content versus how many popular games do not, but it never mentions the reason behind their popularity. Paragraph B notes that 70% of popular games do not have aggressive content and 89% of games have some violent content. However, the author never states whether violent content makes video games popular. Because this cause-and-effect relationship is missing from the text, the statement cannot be confirmed as true or false.
Q33 FALSE The action game players were up to 25 percent faster at coming to a conclusion and answered just as many questions correctly as their strategy game playing peers Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the players of action games made decisions faster, but they got the same number of correct answers as the players of strategy games.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "FALSE" means that the statement directly contradicts the information provided in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage explains that the action game players "answered just as many questions correctly as their strategy game playing peers." This means their accuracy level was the same as the strategy game players, not that they reduced or minimized their errors. Therefore, stating that they reduced or minimized the percentage of mistakes is incorrect.
Q34 NOT GIVEN These skills are not even taught at school Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage only mentions school to explain that schools do not teach certain high-level skills, but it never talks about using video games in classrooms.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether or not schools should use video games in classrooms.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage never discusses whether schools should adopt or use video games in their classrooms. The text mentions that some skills trained by video games "are not even taught at school," but it never gives an opinion or recommendation about bringing video games into actual school classrooms.
Q35 TRUE Much like exercise can build muscle, the powerful combination of concentration and rewarding surges of neurotransmitters like dopamine, which strengthens neural circuits, can build the player’s brain Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that playing video games triggers strong rushes and high levels of a brain chemical called dopamine, which helps build and strengthen the player's brain.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means the statement accurately reflects the passage, agreeing that gaming leads to high amounts of dopamine in the brain.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage explains in Paragraph C that playing video games creates "rewarding surges of neurotransmitters like dopamine". The word "surge" refers to a sudden large increase or rush, which directly corresponds to having "lots of dopamine". Therefore, the statement matches the facts presented in the text.
Q36 C “Video games change your brain,” according to University of Wisconsin psychologist Shawn Green. Video games change the brain’s physical structure the same way as do learning to read, playing the piano, or navigating using a map Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Shawn Green said video games physically change the shape and parts of the brain in the same way that everyday learning tasks—like reading or playing an instrument—do.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "C" means that psychologist Shawn Green is the person who stated that video games change how the brain is physically built, just like other everyday activities do.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because in Paragraph C, Shawn Green explains this exact idea. The text states that according to Shawn Green, video games change the brain's physical structure just like learning to read, playing music, or using a map. Here, the word "alter" in the question means "change", and "other daily life skills" refers to regular activities mentioned like "learning to read, playing the piano, or navigating using a map".
Q37 D According to researchers at the University of Rochester, led by Daphne Bavelier, a cognitive scientist, games simulating stressful events such as those found in battle or action games could be a training tool for real-world situations. The study suggests that playing action video games primes the brain to make quick decisions Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Daphne Bavelier and her team discovered that action games featuring stressful events help prepare the human brain to make fast choices.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'D' means that Daphne Bavelier is the person connected to the idea that intense or stressful games prepare the brain to choose and act quickly.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because Paragraph E mentions research led by cognitive scientist Daphne Bavelier. Her research shows that games copying stressful events, like battle or action games, prepare ('prime') the brain to make fast choices ('make quick decisions'). Here, 'simulating stressful events' matches 'playing stressful games', and 'primes the brain to make quick decisions' matches 'the brain is ready to make decisions without hesitation'.
Q38 B Video games are goal-driven experiences, says Gee, which is fundamental to learning Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that, according to Gee, video games give players experiences focused on reaching specific targets, and having such goals is basic and necessary for learning.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B points to James Paul Gee as the person who expressed this idea.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because in Paragraph F, James Paul Gee explains that video games are based on achieving goals, which is a core part of learning. The phrase "purpose-motivated experience" matches the passage's "goal-driven experiences," and "plays an essential role in studying" matches "is fundamental to learning."
Q39 E Steven Johnson, author of Everything Bad is Good For You: How Today’s Popular Culture Is Actually Making Us Smarter, says gamers must deal with immediate problems while keeping their long-term goals on their horizon Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that according to Steven Johnson, players have to solve quick, current problems in games while still focusing on what they want to achieve later on.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Steven Johnson (Option E) is the person who believes that video game players handle problems happening right now while remembering their future plans.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because paragraph E mentions author Steven Johnson and his view on how players think. The statement "tackling prompt issues" matches the text "deal with immediate problems", and "with future intentions" matches the text "while keeping their long-term goals on their horizon". Therefore, Steven Johnson matches the opinion described in the question.
Q40 A Video and computer games also help children gain self-confidence and many games are based on history, city building, and governance and so on. Such games indirectly teach children about aspects of life on earth Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that playing these games gives children more confidence and teaches them about different real-world topics, broadening their understanding of life.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "A" means that this idea is stated directly by the author of the text, rather than by a named expert or researcher.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because in Paragraph G, the text states that video games help kids build confidence and learn about different topics like history and city building. Since no specific researcher or doctor is cited or quoted in this paragraph, these remarks represent the writer's own opinion.
