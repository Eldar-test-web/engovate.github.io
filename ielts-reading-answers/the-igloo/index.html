Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 1-13 which are based on Reading Passage 1 below.

The Igloo

The igloo is the traditional shelter of Inuit living in the far northern regions. They are built of blocks of snow in a circular shape, in which the walls curve inward towards the top to form a snow vault with a self-supporting arched ceiling. An outstanding example of human ingenuity and adaptability to the environment, the igloo retains heat and protects against the cold, since snow and ice act as excellent insulation. The design includes a tunnel entrance that forms a cold trap to preserve heat inside. The sleeping and sitting areas are raised above this and so maintain a higher temperature.

A similar construction is the ‘quinzhee’, which is a shelter made by hollowing out a pile of settled snow, and is only for temporary use. In contemporary times, this type of snow shelter has become popular among those who enjoy winter camping, as well as in survival situations. Some contemporary Inuit though continue to use igloos, especially as temporary shelters while hunting. However, the warming climate of the early twenty-first century has reduced the availability of appropriate snow for igloo construction. Although the traditional art of igloo construction by Inuit natives may have declined, the igloo and variations upon it, such as ice hotels, have gained in popularity among those who enjoy the winter experience.

An igloo in the Inuit language simply means house and the Inuit do not restrict the use of this term exclusively to snow houses, but include traditional tents, sod houses, homes constructed of driftwood, and modern buildings. Although the origin of the igloo may have been lost in antiquity, it is known that Inuit have constructed snow igloos for hundreds of years. Living in an area where snow and ice predominate, particularly in the long dark winter above the Arctic Circle, the igloo is the perfect shelter. Snow is used because the air pockets trapped in it make it an excellent insulator. Outside, temperatures may be as low as minus 45 degrees Celsius, but inside an igloo, the temperature may range from minus 7 degrees Celsius to 16 degrees Celsius when warmed by body heat alone. A highly functional shelter, the igloo is also aesthetically pleasing, with its shape being both strong and beautiful.

In order to build an igloo, there is a fairly standard procedure. The first thing to do is to find a good spot. It is vital to choose a safe location away from avalanche prone slopes. Next, mark a circle in the snow. For two people, a circle of around two metres diameter is needed, and for four people, around four metres. The igloo body is the next step. Shovel a pile of snow into a large, reasonably steep mound and try and keep the sloping sides at an angle of around 35 degrees or higher, which is best for stability. Wide, short snow shelters are more prone to collapse. If possible, mix snow of different temperatures to help it to harden. The entrance is done by digging a trench downwards into the snow towards the mound. This should be on the downhill side and out of the wind. The snow that is being removed from the trench should be placed on top of the mound. In these conditions, make the trench as deep as a standing man. Then, leave everything for about 90 minutes if possible. Next, finish the tunnel entrance. Make the tunnel slightly wider than a body’s width and dig at a slightly upward angle. Ideally, the floor of the snow shelter should be at least 30 centimetres above the entrance, which will help prevent warm air from escaping the shelter. After the initial entrance is made, it is easier to hollow the inside from the top down. The walls should be thirty to sixty centimetres thick, and if the inside walls are smoothed, this will help prevent dripping. Leave an elevated platform for sleeping on. As heat rises, the occupants will be in the warmest part of the igloo for sleeping. A very important point is to make an air vent in the wall of the shelter, which will prevent the occupants from suffocating in the night. Finally, block the entrance with a block of snow or a rucksack.

Igloo hotels are a new variation on the traditional igloo. In several winter destinations, villages of igloos are built for tourists, where the guests use sleeping bags that sit on top of reindeer hides in overnight stays. Ice hotels are found in many places in Norway, Finland, and Sweden, and are constructed each winter and melt in the spring. The Ice Hotel in the village of Jukkasjarvi, located next to the town of Kiruna in Sweden, is a famous attraction. Originally, the creators started out building a simple igloo, which later turned into the elaborate and now famous ‘hotel’. It is made from the waters of the adjacent river Torne, the pure waters of which produce beautiful clear ice used to create interior decorations, which are made entirely of snow and ice.

The igloo is a subject that has fascinated people from all over the world. Although the traditional art of igloo construction by Inuit natives may have declined, the igloo and variations on it have gained in popularity among those who enjoy the novelty and winter experience.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 FALSE A similar construction is the ‘quinzhee’, which is a shelter made by hollowing out a pile of settled snow, and is only for temporary use Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that a quinzhee is made from a pile of snow and is only meant to be used for a short period of time.
Answer Explanation:
The answer FALSE means that the statement directly contradicts the facts given in the reading text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the statement claims that a quinzhee is built for long-term living, whereas the passage states that it is intended only for short-term or temporary use. The phrase 'temporary use' directly contradicts 'living in long-term'.
Q2 NOT GIVEN Although the traditional art of igloo construction by Inuit natives may have declined, the igloo and variations upon it, such as ice hotels, have gained in popularity among those who enjoy the winter experience Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that fewer Inuit people may be building traditional igloos today, but it says nothing about the Canadian government giving money or grants to keep this practice alive.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the passage does not mention whether the Canadian government gives any money or support to protect the skill of building igloos.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the text never mentions the Canadian government or financial grants. While the text notes that "the traditional art of igloo construction by Inuit natives may have declined," it provides no information about any government actions or funding to preserve it.
Q3 FALSE Outside, temperatures may be as low as minus 45 degrees Celsius, but inside an igloo, the temperature may range from minus 7 degrees Celsius to 16 degrees Celsius when warmed by body heat alone Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that while it can be minus 45 degrees Celsius outside, the inside of an igloo can warm up to anywhere between minus 7 and 16 degrees Celsius just from people's body heat.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "FALSE" means that the statement is wrong based on the information in the reading passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the statement claims that the temperature inside an igloo will never go above freezing (0 degrees Celsius). However, the passage clearly states that the temperature inside an igloo can reach up to 16 degrees Celsius simply from body heat. Since 16 degrees Celsius is well above freezing, the statement contradicts the text.
Q4 avalanche It is vital to choose a safe location away from avalanche prone slopes Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when selecting a place to build an igloo, one must pick a safe spot that is not near hillsides where dangerous snow slides can happen.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means a sudden, heavy fall of snow sliding down a mountain or slope.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the text explaining the first step in building an igloo. The flow chart mentions choosing a spot away from places with certain danger potential. The passage states that it is essential to pick a safe place away from slopes that are "avalanche prone" (likely to experience an avalanche). Therefore, "avalanche" fits the blank.
Q5 stability Shovel a pile of snow into a large, reasonably steep mound and try and keep the sloping sides at an angle of around 35 degrees or higher, which is best for stability Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that building the pile of snow with sides tilted at about 35 degrees or more gives the shelter the best strength so it will not fall down.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "stability" means being firm, strong, and not easily falling or breaking down.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "stability" because the flow chart asks why the snow mound should have 35-degree sloping sides. The text mentions that keeping the sides at an angle of around 35 degrees or higher is "best for stability". In the flow chart, the phrase "robust stability" matches this idea of making the structure strong and safe from collapsing.
Q6 temperatures If possible, mix snow of different temperatures to help it to harden Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when building the igloo, mixing together snow of differing warmth or coldness helps the snow become firm and solid.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "temperatures" refers to how hot or cold something is, meaning you should use snow that has different levels of coldness.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "temperatures" because the instructions in the passage say to combine snow that has different warmth or coldness levels so that it becomes firm. In the flow chart, the phrase "various to help it set" matches the text's "different temperatures to help it to harden," where "various" means "different" and "set" means "harden."
Q7 warm air / warm Ideally, the floor of the snow shelter should be at least 30 centimetres above the entrance, which will help prevent warm air from escaping the shelter Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says the igloo floor should be at least 30 cm higher than the entrance. This stops warm air from leaving the shelter.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'warm air'. It is the thing the raised floor keeps inside the igloo.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'warm air' because the flow chart says the floor is higher 'to keep the ___ in', and the passage says this helps 'prevent warm air from escaping'. 'Keep in' means the same as 'prevent from escaping'. Note: 'warm' alone also fits the two-word limit.
Q8 dripping The walls should be thirty to sixty centimetres thick, and if the inside walls are smoothed, this will help prevent dripping Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that making the inner surface of the walls smooth will stop water from dripping inside the shelter.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means water drops falling down from the ceiling or walls.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "dripping" because the text explains that smoothing the inside walls helps to stop melted water from falling. In the flow chart, the phrase "to stop" means the same thing as "prevent" in the passage.
Q9 elevated / raised Leave an elevated platform for sleeping on Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that when you build an igloo, you should leave a high platform where people can sleep.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the sleeping area should be high up, not on the ground level. The words are "elevated" or "raised".
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "elevated" because the flow chart says "the sleeping area should be ___", and the passage says "Leave an elevated platform for sleeping on." Pay attention to the keywords "sleeping" and "elevated". The first paragraph also says the sleeping areas are "raised", which has the same meaning.
Q10 air vent / vent A very important point is to make an air vent in the wall of the shelter, which will prevent the occupants from suffocating in the night Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that it is extremely important to make a hole in the igloo wall for air so people inside do not run out of fresh air to breathe while they sleep.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means a small opening or hole in the wall that lets fresh air come in and out.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "air vent" (or "vent") because the text outlines the steps for building an igloo and highlights that creating an air opening is essential. In the text, the phrase "A very important point is to make an air vent" directly corresponds to the flow chart's statement "it is vital to make an ". The word "vital" means "very important".
Q11 reindeer hides / hides In several winter destinations, villages of igloos are built for tourists, where the guests use sleeping bags that sit on top of reindeer hides in overnight stays Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that visitors staying overnight in tourist igloo villages put their sleeping bags on reindeer skins.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means the animal skins of reindeer that guests lie on.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "(reindeer) hides" because the text describes how tourists sleep when staying in igloo hotels. The passage mentions that visitors use sleeping bags placed directly on top of "reindeer hides." In the summary, the phrase "guests can sleep in sleeping bags on" matches the text phrase "guests use sleeping bags that sit on top of reindeer hides".
Q12 melt Ice hotels are found in many places in Norway, Finland, and Sweden, and are constructed each winter and melt in the spring Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that ice hotels exist in cold northern nations like Norway, Finland, and Sweden. People build them every winter, and when warmer spring weather arrives, they turn into water.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "melt" means to turn from solid ice into water because the temperature gets warmer.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "melt" because the text explains what happens to ice hotels as the seasons change. The summary mentions that these hotels in northern countries are built each year and do something "when it warms." In paragraph 5, the text notes that ice hotels "are constructed each winter and melt in the spring." Therefore, "melt" fits the blank perfectly.
Q13 interior decorations / decorations It is made from the waters of the adjacent river Torne, the pure waters of which produce beautiful clear ice used to create interior decorations, which are made entirely of snow and ice Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the hotel is made using water from the nearby river, and this clean ice is also used to make the beautiful things decorating the inside.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means items or ornaments used to make the inside of a building or room look attractive.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the text's description of the famous Ice Hotel in Jukkasjarvi. The summary mentions that the hotel is completely made from nearby river water, "even the ". The passage explains that the clear ice from the nearby river is used not just for the building itself, but also "to create interior decorations". Therefore, "decorations" or "interior decorations" correctly completes the blank within the two-word limit.
