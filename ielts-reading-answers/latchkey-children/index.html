From Succeed in IELTS Academic Reading Test 6 · Part 3 · Questions 27–40

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 27 - 40, which are based on Reading Passage 3 below.

LATCHKEY CHILDREN

Latchkey child was a term coined to describe children who wore or carried house keys to school so that they could let themselves into their home when they returned from school. The term came into use during the Second World War, when fathers had gone off to war, and mothers had gone into industry, making the tanks, planes, uniforms and bullets the soldiers needed. The children went home with keys on chains, ribbons or a piece of string tied around their necks. Some mothers chose to work the night shift, called the "swing shift", and tucked their children into bed, locked the door and went to the factory. The country's response was prompt and comprehensive. Programmes were set up in factories, in schools and community centres, to gather in all the children whose parents were busy with the war effort. These programmes closed promptly when the war ended, and women resumed their roles as housewives. More than sixty years on, there are large numbers of working mothers, but unlike in wartime, the country isn't organised to care for their children.

Sadly, finding young children at home without adult supervision has become much too commonplace. Latchkey children were once found only among the lower classes, but the situation has gradually spread to the middle and upper classes. The same is true of adolescent violence. In the past, shootings and stabbings were associated primarily with inner city, or poverty stricken areas permeated with abusive families and neglectful schools. However, in recent times, the "teen violence" epidemic has penetrated society at every economic level. An increase in the number of working mothers, as well as single-parent families, combined with a decrease in extended families that once helped with childcare, has contributed to the growing ranks of latchkey kids.

According to one census, one-third of all school-age children in the United States are, for some part of the week, latchkey kids, that is, they go home to an empty house or apartment. The total number may be between five and seven million children between five and thirteen years old. Marian Wright Edelman, the director of the Children's Defence Fund, thinks it's close to 16 million children. The Census Bureau found that 15% were home alone before school, 76% after school and 9% at night. Presumably, the 9% have parents who work night shifts.

One-half of all children in the country aged 12 to 14 are home alone for an average of seven hours a week. The very poor in America are less likely to leave their children alone at home, or allow them to go home alone, than families who earn twice their level of income. This is probably because the very poor live in less safe neighbourhoods, and have fewer friends or family who can step in, in case of emergency. In spite of the hours spent on the job, working mothers spend an average of five-and-a-half hours a day with their children.

When latchkey children are functioning well, we don't hear about them. But we do hear about the one-third of all complaints to child welfare agencies which involve latchkey children. We know about the 51% who are doing poorly in school. Most teachers believe that being alone at home is the number one cause of school failure. The afternoon hours are the peak time for juvenile crime. In the last 11 years, juvenile crime has increased 48%. The Carnegie Council on Adolescent Development found that 8th graders who are alone 11 hours a week are twice as likely to abuse drugs as adolescents who are busy after school.

Unsupervised children are more likely to become depressed, smoke cigarettes and marijuana and drink alcohol. They are also more likely to be the victims of crimes. When home alone, latchkey children generally watch television, eat snacks, play with pets and fight with siblings.

Adolescents who fall under the classification of latchkey children are more likely than others of the same age group to experience feelings of rage and isolation and to express those emotions in a physically aggressive manner. While there are certainly genetic and biological factors involved in the development of an adolescent's propensity towards acting out their feelings of rage and isolation, environment also plays a key role in this arena. Sociologists have found that many latchkey children, because they are frequently raised in dysfunctional families, are taught by example to be manipulative, secretive and unpredictable. They often instinctively develop a sense of timing and management of their emotions. These are skills that can be easily and directly used to portray a false picture of themselves and their living situation.

Making the decision: When is a child ready to be home alone? Personality characteristics, skills, and maturity are useful criteria for determining a child's readiness to be home alone. Personality doesn't generally change much with age, although children can learn to modify some of their reactions as they learn what is expected of them. There are some children who find it very difficult to be alone, some who need time and gradual exposure to become accustomed to being by themselves, and some who adapt easily.

The personality characteristics of the child who is ready to be home alone are those of a child who

– is not fearful, feels at ease in the world and is self-confident

– is calm, and is not excitable when something unexpected happens

– is outgoing and talks about his or her feelings and thoughts readily with parents and others

– admits wrongdoing, even when expecting disapproval

– has courage enough to resist pressure from friends and others.

In many communities there are activities for school-age children whose parents work and cannot be at home in the afternoon. The importance of looking into these is stressed by child development professionals. According to James Comer of Yale University, "the period between 10 and 15 years is a time when young people re-examine their attitudes and values. They are being pressured by peers. They need to be protected by responsible adults who will help them examine and counter some of these attitudes."

The activities available vary as does the cost. Some are more popular with children than others, and some are more rewarding, but all are preferable to sitting at home in front of the television. These programmes can vary in cost or are free, depending upon the particular activity and the age of the child. All of them offer the opportunity to acquire skills and knowledge that are useful throughout life. Children who are not learning anything for hours every week are at a distinct disadvantage compared to children who are engaged in enriching activities. In the words of T. Berry Brazelton, of Harvard University: "During these all important bridge years between childhood and adulthood, kids really do need something constructive to do, and they also still need to have their activities supervised. Most of all, they need to know that their parents care about them, are involved in their lives, and have their best interests at heart."

The passage shows that youth violence now reaches all financial levels of society (supporting A), unsupervised children can suffer from crimes done to them (supporting F), and standing up to pressure from friends shows the readiness and maturity needed to be left alone (supporting H).
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that three statements are true according to the text: violence among teenagers now happens across all wealth levels (A), latchkey children can be harmed by crime as well as commit it (F), and being able to say no to peer pressure shows that a young person is mature enough to stay alone (H).

The correct answer consists of A, F, and H because each matches information in the text:
- Statement A matches the text where it says that "teen violence" has entered every economic group, not just poor areas.

- Statement H matches the section discussing maturity and signs a child is ready to stay home alone, which includes having the "courage enough to resist pressure from friends and others."
Q30 A Programmes were set up in factories, in schools and community centres, to gather in all the children whose parents were busy with the war effort. These programmes closed promptly when the war ended, and women resumed their roles as housewives. More than sixty years on, there are large numbers of working mothers, but unlike in wartime, the country isn't organised to care for their children Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that during the war, special programs cared for children while their parents worked. Today, however, society is not set up to provide that same level of care for the children of working mothers.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that during World War II, children whose parents were away working had better care organized for them than latchkey children have today.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the text states that during the war, the country took quick action by setting up special programmes in factories, schools, and community centers to take care of children whose parents were busy working. In contrast, the author notes that today, "unlike in wartime, the country isn't organised to care for their children." Therefore, children back then were looked after better by society than children in the present day.

Key words to notice:
- "unlike in wartime" = different from the war period
- "isn't organised to care for their children" = lacks organized support today compared to the war
Q31 C The Census Bureau found that 15% were home alone before school, 76% after school and 9% at night Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage clearly states that 76% of the children were by themselves after school finished, which is much more than the percentages for before school or during the night.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the largest group of latchkey children stayed home by themselves during the afternoon, which is the time after their school day ends.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the passage gives the exact numbers from the Census Bureau: 15% were alone before school (morning), 9% at night, and 76% after school. Since 76% is by far the highest percentage, it represents "most children." The time "after school" corresponds directly to "in the afternoon."
Q32 TRUE Sociologists have found that many latchkey children, because they are frequently raised in dysfunctional families, are taught by example to be manipulative, secretive and unpredictable. They often instinctively develop a sense of timing and management of their emotions. These are skills that can be easily and directly used to portray a false picture of themselves and their living situation Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that many latchkey children learn to be secretive and manage their emotions, giving them skills they can use to show an untrue picture of who they are and how they live.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means the passage agrees that latchkey children can be very good at concealing what is really happening in their lives.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage explains that latchkey children frequently develop skills such as being "secretive" and managing their feelings, which allows them to show a false image of their reality. In the text, the phrase "portray a false picture of themselves and their living situation" matches the idea of "hiding the truth about their situation."
Q33 NOT GIVEN Latchkey child was a term coined to describe children who wore or carried house keys to school so that they could let themselves into their home when they returned from school Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that "latchkey children" are kids who carry keys to open their own doors when returning home after school, but it does not talk about them moving away from home early in life.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether latchkey children leave their family home to live on their own at a very young age.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage discusses children who stay at home alone without adult supervision after school, but it never mentions whether they permanently move out or "leave home at a very early age". Since this topic is not discussed anywhere in the text, we cannot say if it is true or false.
Q34 FALSE While there are certainly genetic and biological factors involved in the development of an adolescent's propensity towards acting out their feelings of rage and isolation, environment also plays a key role in this arena Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that even though genes and biology influence how teenagers express anger and loneliness, their environment is also very important.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "FALSE" means that the statement is incorrect according to the passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage states that both biology and the surrounding environment are important factors. The word "principally" in the question means "mainly" or "chiefly." However, the text explains that although genetic and biological traits are involved, the "environment also plays a key role" in causing latchkey teens to act out their anger and loneliness. Therefore, their aggressive behavior is not caused principally by biology alone.
Q35 TRUE The personality characteristics of the child who is ready to be home alone are those of a child who– is not fearful, feels at ease in the world and is self-confident– is calm, and is not excitable when something unexpected happens– is outgoing and talks about his or her feelings and thoughts readily with parents and others Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that a child who is ready to stay by themselves at home can easily talk with parents and other people about their thoughts and feelings. This demonstrates that talking well with others is a sign of being ready to stay alone.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means that the text confirms that being able to communicate well is one way to judge if a child is ready to stay at home alone without adult care.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage explains what makes a child ready to stay home alone. It specifically lists being "outgoing and talks about his or her feelings and thoughts readily with parents and others" as a key sign of readiness. In the statement, "good communication skills" matches talking about thoughts and feelings easily, and "a measure of a child's ability to be left unsupervised" corresponds to judging whether a child is ready to be home alone.
Q36 industry The term came into use during the Second World War, when fathers had gone off to war, and mothers had gone into industry, making the tanks, planes, uniforms and bullets the soldiers needed Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that during World War II, fathers went away to fight, so mothers went to work in factories and manufacturing to make supplies for the army.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means factories, manufacturing, or business work where goods and materials are made.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "industry" because the text explains what happened during the Second World War. When fathers left for the war, mothers took on jobs making items like tanks, planes, and uniforms. In the passage, this is described as mothers having "gone into industry." In the summary sentence, "fill the places of men in " directly corresponds to mothers entering "industry" to do the work that men typically did.
Q37 response The country's response was prompt and comprehensive Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the nation acted quickly and thoroughly to address the need for childcare during the war.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "response" means an action or reaction taken to deal with a specific problem or situation.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the first paragraph of the passage. The summary describes how the authorities reacted quickly when mothers started working during the war ("there was a quick to this by the authorities"). In the text, the author states that "The country's response was prompt and comprehensive." Here, "prompt" means "quick," matching the sentence structure and confirming that "response" is the word needed.
Q38 adult supervision / supervision Sadly, finding young children at home without adult supervision has become much too commonplace Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that it is now very common to find young children at home with no adult watching them.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means the missing word is 'supervision', which means an adult watching and taking care of children.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'supervision' because the summary says the problem of children alone at home 'without ___' is 'very common', and the passage says 'without adult supervision has become much too commonplace'. Notice the matching words: 'without', 'commonplace' = 'very common'.
Q39 physically aggressive / aggressive Adolescents who fall under the classification of latchkey children are more likely than others of the same age group to experience feelings of rage and isolation and to express those emotions in a physically aggressive manner Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that teenage latchkey children are more inclined than other kids to show strong anger and act violently toward others.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to acting in a violent, forceful, or attacking way toward others.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer fills the blank describing the type of behaviour shown by latchkey children. In paragraph 7, the text discusses teenagers who are latchkey kids and points out that they are more prone to showing their anger and loneliness in a 'physically aggressive manner'. In the summary sentence, 'display [physically] aggressive behaviour' directly paraphrases expressing emotions in a 'physically aggressive manner'. Therefore, '(physically) aggressive' correctly completes the sentence.
Q40 personality Personality characteristics, skills, and maturity are useful criteria for determining a child's readiness to be home alone Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that a child's character, abilities, and how mature they are help parents decide if the child is ready to stay home alone.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "personality" means the special set of qualities, character traits, and feelings that make a person who they are.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "personality" because the passage mentions that personality traits and maturity are key factors in deciding whether a child can handle staying at home by themselves. In the summary sentence, the blank is paired with "and level of maturity" to describe what determines how well a child handles staying alone, matching the text's list of criteria: "Personality characteristics, skills, and maturity".
