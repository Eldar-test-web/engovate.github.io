Agriculture and Tourism

A Linkages between the Agri-Food Sector and Tourism offer significant opportunities for the development of both sectors within the region. These linkages could lead to ensuring the sustainability of the region's tourism product thus ensuring its preservation. Agriculture and tourism — two of Wisconsin's most important industries — are teaming up. A study in southwestern Wisconsin has found that tourists, rural communities, and some farmers could benefit from stronger efforts to promote and market agricultural tourism there. In 1990, agricultural tourism project members surveyed 290 visitors to the annual Monroe Cheese Festival and 164 visitors to the Picnic on the Farm, a one-time event held in Platteville in conjunction with the Chicago Bears summer training camp. More than one-half of those surveyed responded favorably to a proposed tour, saying they would be interested in participating in some type of agricultural tour in southwestern Wisconsin. Survey respondents reported that they would prefer to visit cheese factories, sausage processing plants, dairy farms, and historical farm sites, as well as enjoy an old-fashioned picnic dinner. The study also found strong interest in visiting specialty farms (strawberries, cranberries, poultry, etc.). More than 75 percent of the Cheese Day visitors planned ahead for the trip, with 37 percent planning at least two months in advance.

B More than 40 percent of the visitors came to Monroe for two- or three-day visits. Many stopped at other communities on their way to Cheese Days. Visitors at both events indicated that they were there to enjoy themselves and were willing to spend money on food and arts and crafts. They also wanted the opportunity to experience the “country” while there. The study found that planning around existing events should take into account what brought visitors to the area and provide additional attractions that will appeal to them. For example, visitors to Cheese Days said they were on a holiday and appeared to be more open to various tour proposals. Picnic visitors came specifically to see the Chicago Bears practice. They showed less interest in a proposed agricultural tour than Cheese Day visitors, but more interest in a picnic dinner.

C The study identified three primary audiences for agricultural tourism: 1) elderly people who take bus tours to see the country; 2) families interested in tours that could be enjoyed by both parents and children; and 3) persons already involved in agriculture, including international visitors. Agricultural tourism can serve to educate urban tourists about the problems and challenges facing farmers, says Andy Lewis, Grant county community development agent. While agriculture is vital to Wisconsin, more and more urban folk are becoming isolated from the industry. In fact, Lewis notes, farmers are just as interested in the educational aspects of agricultural tours as they are in any financial returns.

D “Farmers feel that urban consumers are out of touch with farming,” Lewis says. “If tourists can be educated on issues that concern farmers, those visits could lead to policies more favorable to agriculture.” Animal rights and the environment are examples of two issues that concern both urban consumers and farmers. Farm tours could help consumers get the farmer's perspective on these issues, Lewis notes. Several Wisconsin farms already offer some type of learning experience for tourists. However, most agricultural tourism enterprises currently market their businesses independently, leading to a lack of a concerted effort to promote agricultural tourism as an industry.

E Lewis is conducting the study with Jean Murphy, assistant community development agent. Other participants include UW-Platteville Agricultural Economist Bob Acton, the Center for Integrated Agricultural Systems, UW-Extension Recreation Resources Center, the Wisconsin Rural Development Center, and Hidden Valleys, a Southwestern Wisconsin regional tourism organization. This past fall, Murphy organized several workshops with some Green and Grant County farmers, local business leaders, and motor coach tour operators to discuss how best to organize and put on farm tours. Committees were formed to look at the following: tour site evaluations, inventory of the area's resources, tour marketing, and familiarization of tours. The fourth committee is organizing tours for people such as tour bus guides and local reporters to help better educate them about agricultural tourism. Green County farmers already have experience hosting visitors during the annual Monroe Cheese Days. Green county Tourism Director Larry Lindgren says these farmers are set to go ahead with more formal agricultural tours next year. The tours will combine a farm visit with a visit to a local cheese factory and a picnic lunch.

F Another farm interested in hosting an organized tour is Sinsinawa, a 200-acre Grant County farm devoted to sustainable agriculture and run by the Dominican Sisters. Education plays a major role at the farm, which has an orchard, dairy and beef cows, and hogs. Farm tours could be combined with other activities in the area such as trips to the Mississippi River and/or visits to historical towns or landmarks, Lewis says. The project will help expose farmers to the tourism industry and farm vacations as a way to possibly supplement incomes, he adds. While farm families probably wouldn't make a lot of money through farm tours, they would be compensated for their time, says Lewis. Farmers could earn additional income through the sale of farm products, crafts, and recreational activities.

4 Agriculture tour assist tourists to understand more issues concerning animal and environment.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 B More than 40 percent of the visitors came to Monroe for two- or three-day visits Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that a little under half of the tourists traveled to Monroe to stay for two or three days.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is B.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because Paragraph B mentions that over 40 percent of the people visiting Monroe stayed for two or three days. In this text, 'More than 40 percent' means 'about half', and 'two- or three-day visits' means spending 'several days'.
Q2 A More than one-half of those surveyed responded favorably to a proposed tour, saying they would be interested in participating in some type of agricultural tour in southwestern Wisconsin Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that over half of the people who answered the questions liked the idea of a tour and said they would like to join an agricultural tour.
Answer Explanation:
The answer choice A means that paragraph A contains the information about most visitors answering positively to a survey about farm tours.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because paragraph A mentions that a survey was given to visitors at two events, and "more than one-half" (which means most) of the people asked "responded favorably" (responded positively) to a proposed agricultural tour.
Q3 E Lewis is conducting the study with Jean Murphy, assistant community development agent. Other participants include UW-Platteville Agricultural Economist Bob Acton, the Center for Integrated Agricultural Systems, UW-Extension Recreation Resources Center, the Wisconsin Rural Development Center, and Hidden Valleys, a Southwestern Wisconsin regional tourism organization Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows that different groups and organizations are participating together to carry out the research study about tourism and agriculture.
Answer Explanation:
The answer choice E means that paragraph E contains information about different groups and organizations working together on research about farm tours.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because paragraph E explains that the research ("study") is being done by several people and groups working together ("participants"). These include university researchers, agricultural centers, extension centers, rural development groups, and regional tourism organizations, showing cooperation across multiple organizations.
Q4 D Animal rights and the environment are examples of two issues that concern both urban consumers and farmers. Farm tours could help consumers get the farmer's perspective on these issues, Lewis notes Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that animal welfare and the environment are important subjects for both city people and farmers. It highlights that going on farm tours can help visitors see these subjects from the farmer's point of view.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is paragraph D.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because paragraph D explains that farm tours can help consumers (tourists) learn about the farmer's perspective on key topics such as animal rights and the environment. In the text, 'animal rights and the environment' connects directly to 'animal and environment' in the statement, and 'help consumers get the farmer's perspective on these issues' corresponds to 'assist tourists to understand more issues'.
Q5 B Picnic visitors came specifically to see the Chicago Bears practice Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that people who attended the picnic came to the area for one exact reason, which was to watch the Chicago Bears team practice.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means that 'Picnic visitors' refers to the group of tourists who traveled with a very specific purpose and location in mind.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage states that the picnic visitors traveled for a clear, single reason—to watch the Chicago Bears practice. The word 'specifically' indicates that they had a 'focused destination' or goal for their trip, unlike the Cheese Days visitors who were on a general holiday and open to different tours.
Q6 A More than 75 percent of the Cheese Day visitors planned ahead for the trip, with 37 percent planning at least two months in advance Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that most of the people who visited the Cheese Day festival made their plans in advance before taking the trip.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "A" means that the statement applies to the Cheese Festival visitors.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the text states that over 75 percent of the Cheese Day visitors planned ahead for their trip. In English, "more than 75 percent" means the majority (most of the people), and "planned ahead" means to prepare before going.
Q7 A They showed less interest in a proposed agricultural tour than Cheese Day visitors, but more interest in a picnic dinner Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Picnic visitors were more interested in eating a picnic dinner than Cheese Day visitors were. Therefore, Cheese Day visitors were comparatively less interested in a picnic meal.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "A" means that the Cheese Festival visitors are the group that is less interested in having a picnic meal compared to the Picnic visitors.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the passage states that people visiting the Picnic had "more interest in a picnic dinner" than the Cheese Day visitors. This means that, by comparison, the Cheese Festival (Cheese Day) visitors were less interested or less keen on having a picnic meal.
Q8 C In 1990, agricultural tourism project members surveyed 290 visitors to the annual Monroe Cheese Festival and 164 visitors to the Picnic on the Farm, a one-time event held in Platteville in conjunction with the Chicago Bears summer training camp. More than one-half of those surveyed responded favorably to a proposed tour, saying they would be interested in participating in some type of agricultural tour in southwestern Wisconsin. Survey respondents reported that they would prefer to visit cheese factories, sausage processing plants, dairy farms, and historical farm sites, as well as enjoy an old-fashioned picnic dinner. The study also found strong interest in visiting specialty farms (strawberries, cranberries, poultry, etc.) Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that researchers asked visitors from both the Cheese Festival and the Picnic on the Farm questions about tours. More than half of all people surveyed wanted to go on farm tours, including trips to cheese factories and farms that grow fruits like strawberries and cranberries.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'C' means that both the Cheese Festival visitors and the Picnic visitors showed interest in visiting places like factories and fruit farms.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the text mentions that over half of the people surveyed from both events (the Cheese Festival and the Picnic on the Farm) were interested in agricultural tours. The survey respondents wanted to visit cheese factories, sausage processing plants, and specialty farms growing fruit like strawberries and cranberries. Since this applies to people surveyed from both events, the correct category is 'Both of them'. Key words to look for are 'cheese factories' (factory tour) and 'strawberries, cranberries' (fruit).
Q9 A For example, visitors to Cheese Days said they were on a holiday and appeared to be more open to various tour proposals Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that people visiting Cheese Days were on vacation and were ready to accept different tour suggestions.
Answer Explanation:
The answer choice 'A' refers to Cheese Festival visitors.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the text explains that visitors to Cheese Days (the Cheese Festival) were more open to trying different tour ideas. In the passage, being 'open to various tour proposals' means the same thing as being 'willing to accept a variety of tour recommendation'.
Q10 animal rights Animal rights and the environment are examples of two issues that concern both urban consumers and farmers Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that animal rights and the environment are two key topics that matter to both consumers and farmers.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means the fair and safe treatment of animals.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'animal rights' because the text lists it together with the environment as two important topics that both city visitors and farmers care about, which consumers can learn about on farm tours.
Q11 workshops This past fall, Murphy organized several workshops with some Green and Grant County farmers, local business leaders, and motor coach tour operators to discuss how best to organize and put on farm tours Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that last autumn, Murphy arranged several group meetings (workshops) with local farmers, business owners, and tour operators to talk about how to plan farm visits.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means group meetings where people gather to learn and talk about a specific topic.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'workshops' because the text states that during the autumn season ('this past fall'), Murphy set up ('organized') group meetings ('workshops') with farmers, business leaders, and tour operators to talk about farm tours.
Q12 picnic lunch / picnic The tours will combine a farm visit with a visit to a local cheese factory and a picnic lunch Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the planned tours will put three things together: visiting a farm, seeing a cheese factory, and eating an outdoor meal called a picnic lunch.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means a meal eaten outside while taking a break, which will be included in the farm tour.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'picnic' (or 'picnic lunch') because paragraph E mentions tourism director Larry Lindgren talking about the upcoming tours. He explains that these farm trips will link together a farm visit, a trip to a local cheese factory, and a 'picnic lunch'. This directly matches the summary text mentioning factory visits and a picnic.
Q13 dominican Sisters Another farm interested in hosting an organized tour is Sinsinawa, a 200-acre Grant County farm devoted to sustainable agriculture and run by the Dominican Sisters Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that the Sinsinawa farm, which covers 200 acres and uses sustainable farming, is managed and run by the Dominican Sisters.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to the Dominican Sisters, a group of religious women who manage and run the farm located in Sinsinawa.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "dominican Sisters" because the text explains that the Sinsinawa farm is run by the Dominican Sisters. The phrase "managed and operated by" in the summary means the same thing as "run by" in the passage.
Q14 income / incomes The project will help expose farmers to the tourism industry and farm vacations as a way to possibly supplement incomes, he adds Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says the farm tour project will show farmers the tourism industry and farm vacations. This can help them earn more money.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says the project will probably bring extra money, called income, to local farmers.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "income/incomes" because the summary's "bring extra" matches "supplement incomes" in the passage. Lewis says farm families would be paid for their time and could earn more from selling farm products. Pay attention to the words "supplement" (= extra) and "incomes".
