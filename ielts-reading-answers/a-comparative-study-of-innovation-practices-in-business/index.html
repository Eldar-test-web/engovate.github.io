From Achieve IELTS Practice Tests Academic Reading Test 1 · Part 3 · Questions 27–40

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 27 – 40, which are based on Reading Passage 3.

A Comparative Study of Innovation Practices in Business

Companies want to be innovative, but what does innovation mean?

Results of interviews with corporate executives and senior innovation officers in four of the largest publicly-traded companies and one government agency in the Chicago-area, provide some insights into how businesses approach innovation.

The dictionary defines innovation as ‘the introduction of something new’. Regardless of the type of innovation – whether it be product, process, or service – it results in significant change. This change could be as simple as ‘changing the way we do something routine,’ a breakthrough which provides a substantial benefit to the customer, or one that dramatically increases the revenue or profitability of the company.

Participants interested in breakthrough innovation believe ‘if innovation doesn’t deliver bottom-line results, it is just creativity’. Indeed, the very definition of innovation for Afuah (2003) is ‘invention plus commercialization.’ The relationship of innovation to financial performance was well demonstrated by Kirn and Mauborgne (1997). In manufacturing environments, they found that while 86% of product launches involved some small improvements to existing models – that is, incremental changes – they accounted for only 62% of total revenues and 39% of total profits. The remaining 14% of launches – the real breakthrough innovations – generated 38% of total revenues and a huge 61% of total profits.

Innovation may offer one significant way that companies can gain advantage. Utterback’s (1994) concept of ‘dominant design’ provides insight into how an innovation can create a temporary monopoly situation that will weaken competitive forces; however, when an innovative product or service is launched, rivals typically begin to copy it (once patents run out). Hence, it is necessary for the company to continuously seek further ways to innovate.

Every innovation process has its strengths and weaknesses, but it seems that when a company sets up a systematized innovation process it communicates the importance of innovation to the entire organization. In these companies, more resources are devoted to development. The best companies have learned to systematize the process (Hargadorn & Sutton, 2000).

The primary disadvantage to having a structured innovation process is speed to market – the more structure, the longer the lead time is from idea to product. The only company that described its process as ‘quick’ did not have such a process. Employees were empowered to solve problems and create new products for the customer by responding to demand. While this benefits customers, the company stated it lacks systems to share learning with other segments of the organization. A potential disadvantage of this approach, according to Utterback, is that evolutionary change can be missed when companies are too focused on pleasing customers.

The most challenging aspect of any innovation is determining marketability. No company said it lacked creative ideas or creative people, but many ideas require significant resources to test, develop, and launch. Millions of dollars are at stake, so an element of risk-taking is required.

Taking risks is generally defined as being able to drive new ideas forward in the face of adversity. Publicly-traded companies have a major dilemma. To guarantee a leadership position, they have to stay on the leading-edge of innovation. This requires a long-term approach and a high tolerance for risk. Investors, especially in a down economy, want short-term results. As investors’ tolerance for risk decreases, so does the company’s ability to take the significant financial risk necessary to create breakthrough change; however, most recognize that investing in innovation is the ‘right thing to do’.

One company actively pursues a rather unusual strategy of ‘acquiring’ innovation by purchasing other smaller companies or partnering with specialized companies. This enables the acquiring company to bring a product to market more quickly and gives the smaller company access to funds it might not otherwise have.

How can a company involve all its employees in the innovation process? It may be as simple as requesting new ideas. A brainstorming session during a staff meeting need only take 30 minutes. Another system is to use existing ‘suggestion box’ processes. Involving employees in idea-generation can reap some large benefits at a very low cost. Only modest monetary rewards are necessary for successful innovation ideas, especially since many companies have found that employees place high value on recognition.

In most organizations, teams are extensively used to evaluate ideas, but rarely to generate them. Companies need to learn how to construct teams for the purpose of innovation. A team member should be selected based on their tendency to be more creative or more risk-taking. This could markedly increase innovation output. According to Hargadorn and Sutton, using teams to capture and share ideas is one method of keeping ideas alive – a key step in the innovation process. Good ideas need to be nurtured by teams and incorporated into the information and communication systems of the company.

In conclusion, innovation can be difficult to structure. It is the authors’ perception that even the most innovative companies in the sample underinvest in market research during the concept refining phase. Risk could be reduced considerably by adoption of this strategy, but, of course, it could not be eliminated.

Most of the ‘problems’ cited by participants were due to a low tolerance for risk – by employees (what they would or would not say), and by committees (being afraid to invest money without knowing the return on investment). Raising the risk tolerance would reduce the amount of analysis required to bring a new idea to market, thus shortening the cycle time of new product/service development. According to psychologists Kahn and Hirshorn, people come alive when they feel safe. It is threat and anxiety that inhibit them. It would follow that in order for people in organizations to take risks, lack of success must be tolerated. The organizations that manage risk most effectively transform those risks into challenges and opportunities.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 C Utterback’s (1994) concept of ‘dominant design’ provides insight into how an innovation can create a temporary monopoly situation that will weaken competitive forces; however, when an innovative product or service is launched, rivals typically begin to copy it (once patents run out). Hence, it is necessary for the company to continuously seek further ways to innovate Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Utterback shows how a new product only gives a company a short-term advantage because other companies will soon copy it. For this reason, a business must keep looking for new ideas and cannot stop after just one success.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "C" means that the expert Utterback is the one who proposed the idea that a company cannot depend only on a single successful new creation.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the text discusses Utterback's concept of "dominant design." It explains that an innovation provides only a "temporary monopoly" because competitors ("rivals") will start to copy the idea over time. Therefore, a business cannot simply rely on just one successful invention; instead, "it is necessary for the company to continuously seek further ways to innovate." These points clearly match the statement in the question.
Q28 D According to Hargadorn and Sutton, using teams to capture and share ideas is one method of keeping ideas alive – a key step in the innovation process Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Hargadorn and Sutton believe working in groups (teams) to gather and share ideas is an essential and effective way to help the innovation process succeed.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "D" means that the experts Hargadorn and Sutton support the idea that working in teams or groups is an effective method for creating innovation.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the text directly credits Hargadorn and Sutton with the concept that teams help preserve and develop new ideas during the innovation process. In this context, the phrase "using teams" corresponds to a "group approach," and "a key step in the innovation process" matches "an effective way of generating innovation."
Q29 E According to psychologists Kahn and Hirshorn, people come alive when they feel safe. It is threat and anxiety that inhibit them. It would follow that in order for people in organizations to take risks, lack of success must be tolerated Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that psychologists Kahn and Hirshorn believe people perform well and take chances when they feel secure, meaning companies must accept that not every new idea will succeed.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "E" refers to Kahn and Hirshorn, who believe that workers do their best and take creative risks when the workplace accepts mistakes and does not punish failure.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because the passage attributes this psychological idea to Kahn and Hirshorn. In the final paragraph, the text explains that according to Kahn and Hirshorn, people feel energized and active ("come alive") when they feel safe from threats or worry. It notes that for people at work to take risks, the company must accept and forgive failure ("lack of success must be tolerated").
Q30 B The relationship of innovation to financial performance was well demonstrated by Kirn and Mauborgne (1997). In manufacturing environments, they found that while 86% of product launches involved some small improvements to existing models – that is, incremental changes – they accounted for only 62% of total revenues and 39% of total profits. The remaining 14% of launches – the real breakthrough innovations – generated 38% of total revenues and a huge 61% of total profits Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Kirn and Mauborgne discovered that small changes make up only 39% of total profits, while major breakthrough innovations produce 61% of total profits, showing that major new products make more money.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B refers to the researchers Kirn and Mauborgne.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage explains that Kirn and Mauborgne showed that major breakthrough innovations bring in a much larger share of profits compared to small, incremental improvements. In the text, "radical innovations" corresponds to "real breakthrough innovations" and "income" relates to "profits".
Q31 D The best companies have learned to systematize the process (Hargadorn & Sutton, 2000) Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that the top-performing companies have learned how to make their innovation process organized and structured, according to Hargadorn and Sutton.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "D" means that the experts Hargadorn and Sutton are linked to the idea that successful companies use an organized or structured method for innovation.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because the passage specifically mentions that "The best companies have learned to systematize the process" right before citing "(Hargadorn & Sutton, 2000)". In this context, "systematize the process" corresponds to having a "structured approach to innovation," and "The best companies" relates to businesses that are successful or more likely to succeed.
Q32 A Indeed, the very definition of innovation for Afuah (2003) is ‘invention plus commercialization.’ Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that according to Afuah, innovation means creating something new (invention) together with turning it into a successful commercial business (commercialization).
Answer Explanation:
The answer "A" refers to the researcher Afuah.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the passage specifically mentions that Afuah defined innovation as 'invention plus commercialization.' In this context, "invention" corresponds to "a new idea," "plus" matches "combined with," and "commercialization" refers to "business potential" (bringing an idea to the market to make a profit).
Q33 C A potential disadvantage of this approach, according to Utterback, is that evolutionary change can be missed when companies are too focused on pleasing customers Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that, according to Utterback, if a business only focuses on making its customers happy, it might fail to see and make bigger, progressive improvements.
Answer Explanation:
The answer C refers to Utterback, the expert who stated that focusing too much on satisfying customers can cause a company to miss broader, long-term changes.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because paragraph 7 directly attributes this viewpoint to Utterback. In the text, 'too focused on pleasing customers' corresponds to the statement's 'concentrates on responding to clients’ needs', and 'evolutionary change can be missed' matches 'may overlook the need for wider development.' Therefore, Utterback is the correct expert.
Q34 E The primary disadvantage to having a structured innovation process is speed to market – the more structure, the longer the lead time is from idea to product Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that the main problem with a step-by-step innovation system is that it takes more time, meaning there is a longer wait between having an idea and making the product available.
Answer Explanation:
The answer E means that setting up a formal or planned system for innovation can cause a long delay from the time an idea is created until the final product is sold.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because the text explains the main downside of having a structured or organized system for innovation. In paragraph 7, the author states that more structure leads to a longer "lead time" from "idea to product" (the period between initial concept and launch). The word "Unfortunately" matches "primary disadvantage", "organised innovation process" matches "structured innovation process", and "lengthy period between initial concept and launch" matches "longer the lead time is from idea to product".
Q35 G The most challenging aspect of any innovation is determining marketability Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage clearly says that the hardest part of introducing any new idea or product is deciding whether it can be sold successfully in the market.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that a very hard part of creating something new is figuring out if people will actually want to buy it.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is G because the text states that the hardest part of innovation is figuring out whether an idea can be successfully sold. In the passage, 'The most challenging aspect' matches the prompt's 'One of the most difficult issues', 'determining' matches 'to work out', and 'marketability' matches 'the saleability of a future product'.
Q36 B To guarantee a leadership position, they have to stay on the leading-edge of innovation. This requires a long-term approach and a high tolerance for risk Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that to make sure a company stays a leader in the market, it must keep innovating over a long period of time and be willing to take major risks.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that a business that wants to stay ahead of its competitors needs to spend time and invest money in creating new ideas and products.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage states that for companies to "guarantee a leadership position" (which matches "maintain a leading position in business"), they must remain at the forefront of innovation. The text explains that doing this needs a "long-term approach" (putting in time) and taking "financial risk" or "investing in innovation" (putting in money). Therefore, B correctly finishes the thought.
Q37 I One company actively pursues a rather unusual strategy of ‘acquiring’ innovation by purchasing other smaller companies or partnering with specialized companies Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that one business uses an uncommon method to gain innovation by buying smaller businesses or teaming up with companies that have specific knowledge.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "I" means that a unique way to create new ideas or products is to work together with another business that has special skills or knowledge.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is I because the passage describes an uncommon method where a business gets new innovations by "partnering with specialized companies." Here, "unusual strategy" corresponds to "different approach," "partnering" matches "collaboration," and "specialized companies" matches "another company with particular expertise."
Q38 C Involving employees in idea-generation can reap some large benefits at a very low cost Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when companies include their workers in creating new ideas, they can gain big advantages while spending very little money.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "C" means that asking workers to create and share new ideas can give big results without spending much money.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the text explains that asking workers for their ideas brings great advantages while costing very little. In the passage, "Involving employees in idea-generation" matches the prompt "Getting staff to come up with new ideas", and "reap some large benefits at a very low cost" matches the idea of being a "very cost-effective way of achieving innovation".
Q39 H It is the authors’ perception that even the most innovative companies in the sample underinvest in market research during the concept refining phase Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that even businesses that are very good at innovation do not spend enough money researching the market when they are developing their new ideas.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "H" means that businesses that already focus on creating new products should spend more money studying what customers want and need.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is H because the author mentions that even leading innovative companies spend too little money on market research when developing their ideas. Therefore, the advice or recommendation for them is to invest more into understanding customer demand. Key matching words include "even the most innovative companies" (matching "companies already committed to innovation"), "underinvest" (suggesting the recommendation to "put more money into"), and "market research" (meaning "analysis of customer demand").
Q40 F Most of the ‘problems’ cited by participants were due to a low tolerance for risk – by employees (what they would or would not say), and by committees (being afraid to invest money without knowing the return on investment) Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the main difficulties reported by the study participants were caused by employees and committees being scared to take risks and invest money without guaranteed returns.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the difficulties faced by the businesses in the study happened because people did not want to take risks.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is F because the text directly states what caused the issues reported by the participants. In the final paragraph, the passage mentions that most of the problems described by the participants happened because of a low tolerance for risk. Here, "problems cited by participants" corresponds to "Problems experienced by companies participating in the study," "were due to" matches "could be attributed to," and "a low tolerance for risk" means "an unwillingness to accept risk."
