India’s Modern Women - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From Expert On Cambridge IELTS 07 Academic Reading Test 1 · Part 1 · Questions 1–13

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 1-13, which are based on Reading Passage 1 below.

India’s Modern Women

The country's younger generation is shedding submissive attitudes, wants careers, and longs for wealth. And marketers are paying attention. When the first American music videos and popular TV shows began appearing in Indian homes in the early 1990s thanks to satellite and cable, many pundits predicted Indian society would never be the same. For the first time, female viewers saw independent, successful women and fun, sensitive guys. Sex and divorce were openly discussed in these TV imports and couples kissed passionately – then still a taboo in Indian TV shows and movies.

Indeed, the impact on younger generations of Indian women has been profound. Whereas Indian women traditionally have been submissive to parents and husbands and valued frugality and modesty, a number of sociological studies show that young Indian females now prize financial independence, freedom to decide when to marry and have children, and glamorous careers. A generation back, women would sacrifice themselves and believed in saving. Today, it is spend, spend, spend. It is O.K. for a woman to want something for herself, and people will accept it if she goes out into a man's world making a statement.

Because today's young women are the key consumer group of tomorrow, these shifts have big implications for marketing companies.

And the trends come out clearly in two recent studies. One study examined 3400 unmarried women aged 19-22 of different income and social levels. Altogether, the project involved 40 focus groups in five large urban areas and five smaller cities. In some cases, the researchers lived with the women for a while to study them more closely. The researchers supplemented this data with interviews of journalists, teachers, and psychologists.

Among the findings:

Guilt-free materialism. 51% of young single women in major urban areas say it's necessary to have a big house and big car to be happy. In smaller cities, 86% agreed with this statement. This shows that the less women have, the greater are their aspirations. One woman interviewed was making just $200 a year but said she wants to own a Jet plane. A typical comment in recent interviews was, 'I want money, fame and success'.

Parental ties. Traditionally, parents regarded girls as somebody else's future property. They arranged marriages for their daughters, and then the daughters would go away and take care of their in-laws, so parents needed and doted on sons. However, today's young women are rebelling against that. 67% say they plan to take care of their parents into their old age – and that means they need money. The company Unilever played on that sentiment with a recent controversial – but successful – ad for its Fair and Lovely line of beauty products. A daughter came home and found that her parents had no sugar for coffee because they couldn't afford it. She became an airline hostess after using the Fair and Lovely products to make her beautiful. She then visited her parents and took them to a first-class restaurant.

Marital freedom. Now many women say they'll marry when ready – not when their parents decide to marry them off. 65% say dating is essential, and they also want to become financially independent before they marry. 76% say they want to maintain that independence afterward. 60% say they'll decide how to spend their own salaries. What's more, 76% say they'll decide when to have children. They now regard this as the woman's decision completely. In big metro areas, 24% say they never want children, and that number reaches 40% in smaller cities.

Individualism. Female role models in Indian culture used to personify perfections. Now, 62% of girls say it's O.K. if they have faults and that people see them. They don't want to be seen as Mrs. Perfect. Popular TV role models are like Phoebe in 'Friends' – women who commit blunders.

Careerism. A decade ago, most young women saw themselves as housewives. After that, most said they wanted to be teachers or doctors. If they had a profession at all, it had to be a noble cause. Now, it is about glamour, money, and fame. A surprising 45% of young single females say they would like to be journalists. This may be because prominent female Indian journalists, especially TV reporters, are seen as very glamorous. Another 39% say they would like to be managers, 38% are interested in design, and 20% think they want to be teachers. Interestingly, 13% say they would like to be in the military. The percentage of those saying they want to be a full-time housewife was minuscule.

Modern husbands. The relationship with the husband used to be one of awe. Now, women want a partner and a relationship of equals. A recent Whirlpool ad shows a man washing the family clothes before his wife comes home from work, while a Samsung home-appliance ad shows a husband and wife cooking together.

Questions

Questions 1–4 Matching Features

The text refers to 6 main findings when young Indian women were surveyed.

Which finding contains each of the following pieces of information?

Guilt-free materialism

Parental ties

Marital freedom

Individualism

Careerism

Modern husbands

1 Young Indian women who want more tend to be poorer.
2 Few young Indian women want to be housewives.
3 Most young Indian women want to take care of their retired parents.
4 Most young Indian women want to be financially independent.

Complete the following sentences using NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS from the text.

  • 5 are freely talked about on American TV shows.
  • Young women are considered to be the future's most important 6 by many companies.
  • Most young Indian women surveyed agree that 7 before marriage is necessary.
  • In the past, young Indian women who wanted careers were most likely to consider teaching or becoming doctors because each of these is considered to be 8.

Questions 9–13 True / False / Not Given

Do the following statements agree with the information given in Reading Passage 1?

TRUE if the information in the text agrees with the statement

FALSE if the information in the text contradicts the statement

NOT GIVEN if there is no information on this

9 The effect of American culture on young Indian women was not forecast when satellite and cable TV arrived in India.
10 Researchers used three methods to get their data.
11 Most young Indian women are aiming for perfection.
12 Most of the best journalists on TV in India are women.
13 Modern men and women in India cook together.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 Guilt-free materialism This shows that the less women have, the greater are their aspirations Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that women who possess less wealth have even bigger dreams and desires for material things.
Answer Explanation:
The answer identifies the section titled "Guilt-free materialism" as the finding that talks about poorer young Indian women having bigger desires for wealth and success.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is found in the section labeled "Guilt-free materialism". In this paragraph, the text states that when women have less money and fewer possessions (meaning they are poorer), their desires and ambitions for things like big houses, cars, or private planes become even higher (meaning they "want more"). Keywords to note are "the less women have" (which matches "tend to be poorer") and "greater are their aspirations" (which matches "want more").
Q2 Careerism The percentage of those saying they want to be a full-time housewife was minuscule Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that an extremely small number of young women said they want to stay home and be full-time housewives.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the section titled "Careerism" contains the information about very few young women wanting to stay at home as housewives.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is found under the heading "Careerism." In this paragraph, the text talks about women's job choices and states that only a tiny fraction of women want to stay home. Specifically, the word "few" in the question matches the phrase "percentage ... was minuscule" in the passage, and "housewives" matches "full-time housewife."
Q3 Parental ties 67% say they plan to take care of their parents into their old age – and that means they need money Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that more than half (67%) of the women want to support and look after their parents when their parents become elderly.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to the section called "Parental ties", which talks about the relationship between young women and their parents.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is found in the section "Parental ties." The passage explains that 67% of young women—which means the majority or "most" of them—intend to look after their mothers and fathers when they grow old. In the text, "67%" corresponds to "most," and "take care of their parents into their old age" means taking care of their retired parents.
Q4 Marital freedom 65% say dating is essential, and they also want to become financially independent before they marry. 76% say they want to maintain that independence afterward Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that young women wish to earn their own money before marriage, and a high percentage (76%) want to keep that financial independence after they get married.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "Marital freedom" names the specific section in the text that talks about most young Indian women wanting to have and control their own money.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is found under the section titled "Marital freedom." In this section, the text notes that women "want to become financially independent before they marry" and adds that "76% say they want to maintain that independence afterward." Because 76% represents a large majority, this corresponds directly to the statement that "most" young Indian women want financial independence.
Q5 Sex and divorce Sex and divorce were openly discussed in these TV imports Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that shows brought in from America talked freely and openly about sex and divorce.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to physical relationships between people and the legal ending of marriages.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "Sex and divorce" because the text explains that these two topics were openly talked about in imported American television programs. In the sentence, the phrase "freely talked about" directly matches the passage's phrase "openly discussed", and "American TV shows" corresponds to "TV imports" mentioned in the first paragraph.
Q6 consumer group Because today's young women are the key consumer group of tomorrow, these shifts have big implications for marketing companies Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that young women today will be the main group of buyers in the future, which is very important for businesses that sell products.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "consumer group" refers to a collection of shoppers or buyers who purchase products and services.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "consumer group" because the passage explains that young women will be the most important buyers in the future. The sentence from the test matches the text's words: "today's young women" means "Young women", "key" means "most important", "of tomorrow" corresponds to "the future's", and "marketing companies" refers to "companies".
Q7 dating 65% say dating is essential, and they also want to become financially independent before they marry Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that 65% of the women think going on dates is very important, and they want to make their own money before getting married.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means going on dates or spending romantic time with someone to see if you want to marry them.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "dating" because the text reports that 65% of the surveyed young women consider dating to be essential before marriage. In this context, "65%" matches "most", and "essential" means the same as "necessary". Therefore, "dating" is the exact word from the passage that completes the sentence.
Q8 a noble cause After that, most said they wanted to be teachers or doctors. If they had a profession at all, it had to be a noble cause Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that in the past, when young women decided to work, they wanted to be teachers or doctors because working had to serve a good and honorable purpose.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means an honorable, good, and selfless job that helps other people.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "a noble cause" because the passage discusses past career preferences among young Indian women. It states that after the time when most women were housewives, young women primarily wanted to become teachers or doctors, explaining that any career they chose had to be "a noble cause." In the question, "consider teaching or becoming doctors" directly matches "wanted to be teachers or doctors," and "each of these is considered to be" corresponds to "it had to be a noble cause."
Q9 FALSE When the first American music videos and popular TV shows began appearing in Indian homes in the early 1990s thanks to satellite and cable, many pundits predicted Indian society would never be the same Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when American television programs first entered Indian households, many experts predicted that Indian society would change forever.
Answer Explanation:
The answer FALSE means that the statement is wrong according to the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage states that experts did in fact predict that American media would change Indian society. The statement claims that this effect was 'not forecast' (not predicted). However, the text explicitly says that 'many pundits predicted' life would change when American TV shows and music videos first arrived via satellite and cable. Therefore, the statement directly contradicts the facts in the passage.
Q10 TRUE Altogether, the project involved 40 focus groups in five large urban areas and five smaller cities. In some cases, the researchers lived with the women for a while to study them more closely. The researchers supplemented this data with interviews of journalists, teachers, and psychologists Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the study collected data by using focus groups, staying in the women's homes to watch their lives, and speaking to professionals like teachers and psychologists.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means it is correct that researchers gathered their information using three different ways.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage mentions three distinct ways the researchers collected information: first, by organizing "40 focus groups"; second, by living with the women to observe them closely; and third, by conducting "interviews" with professionals such as journalists, teachers, and psychologists. Because these three approaches match the statement, the statement agrees with the text.
Q11 FALSE Now, 62% of girls say it's O.K. if they have faults and that people see them. They don't want to be seen as Mrs. Perfect Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that more than half of young women are comfortable making mistakes and having flaws, and they do not try to be completely perfect.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE because the statement directly contradicts the facts given in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage states that the majority (62%) of young women are fine with having faults and do not want to be viewed as perfect. In the passage, "62% of girls" corresponds to "Most young Indian women", and "its O.K. if they have faults" plus "don't want to be seen as Mrs. Perfect" directly contradicts the idea that they "are aiming for perfection".
Q12 NOT GIVEN This may be because prominent female Indian journalists, especially TV reporters, are seen as very glamorous Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that well-known female reporters on TV are seen as exciting and fashionable, but it does not state whether most of the best TV journalists in India are women.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not contain enough information to say whether the statement is true or false.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage only mentions that well-known female journalists, especially TV reporters, look glamorous and attractive to young women. However, the text never discusses whether "most of the best journalists" on Indian television are women or men. Because this comparison is missing, the statement cannot be verified.
Q13 NOT GIVEN while a Samsung home-appliance ad shows a husband and wife cooking together Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage tells us that a television commercial for Samsung home appliances shows a husband and wife cooking together, but it never mentions whether real Indian couples cook together.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether modern men and women in India actually cook together in real life.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage only talks about an advertisement showing a couple cooking. Under the heading "Modern husbands", it mentions that "a Samsung home-appliance ad shows a husband and wife cooking together." Showing something in a commercial or advertisement (ad) does not confirm that Indian men and women actually do this in everyday life. Therefore, there is not enough information to know if the statement is true or false.
