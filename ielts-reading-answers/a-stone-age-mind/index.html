A Stone Age Mind - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From IELTS Vol 5 Academic Reading Test 7 · Part 3 · Questions 27–40

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 27-40, which are based on Reading Passage 3 below.

A Stone Age mind

Charles Darwin, the brilliant anthropologist and creator of the theory of evolution, is not normally associated with the modern business world. Nevertheless, Darwinian evolutionary theory is the foundation of a new wave of ideas about human behavior in general and particularly the way people behave in the workplace; these ideas have been given the title of 'evolutionary psychology'. Evolutionary psychology revolves around the notion that our brains, like our bodies, have an inherited evolutionary design that has scarcely changed for 10,000 years. As respected evolutionary psychology experts Leda Cosmides and John Tooby comment, 'our modern skulls house a Stone Age mind'. The US biologist Edward O Wilson sees evolutionary psychology as being a discipline which is based on both socio-biology, which is the study of the biological basis of social behavior, and psychology, which is the systematic study of human behavior.

Nigel Nicholson, an organisational psychologist from the London Business School, is a strong supporter of evolutionary psychology and on this subject has published Managing the Human Animal. His book takes the reader on a journey from the Stone Age plains of the savannah to the modern office, and includes a discussion of Darwinism and behavioural psychology together with a dissection of dysfunctional organisational behavior. It is an effective approach to explaining why people behave as they do, particularly at work. Evolutionary psychology is increasingly being cited in management circles, where managers are trying to understand puzzling aspects of human behaviour and by doing so improve the workplace. Nicholson believes that evolutionary psychology can help managers understand what goes wrong in organisational life and what they can do about it.

Nicholson maintains that evolutionary psychology dismisses the long-held assumption that our minds are like blank pages just waiting for culture and experience to write on them and shape our nature. He points out that sophisticated research shows the brain actually houses a store of knowledge when we are born, and now genetic research is establishing there are certain genes that account for abilities, tastes and tendencies. The stored knowledge in the human brain has not changed much since the Stone Age. As Tooby and Cosmides stress, there have not been enough generations for a brain that is well adapted to our post-industrial life to evolve through natural selection.

The evolutionary psychology version of human nature revolves around some key elements which we have inherited from our hunter-gatherer minds. One key element is emotion. Emotion was originally essential to keep early man alive and safe from predators. Emotion was, and continues to be, our radar, guiding us throughout today's techno-defined business world. Despite this, the business world emphasises rational not emotional behaviour, and does not admit the importance of emotion. We still use the emotional part of our minds to make sense of other people's behaviour and to create an impression, so we can often be taken in by appearances. This mental predisposition actually works best in small communities (the tribe), not in much larger environments filled with people we barely know (the modern workplace). Our minds naturally try to re-create our ancestral communities with networks of no more than 150 people, where there are clear hierarchies and leaders. As a consequence, it takes very little to trigger people's innate distrust of others because our safety in antiquity depended on supporting our near family and friends whom we valued more than other people.

So what advice does Nicholson have for the corporate world? He thinks that by knowing the reasons for people's behaviour it is possible to mould corporate environments into places that have more chance of working efficiently and being pleasant places to work in. Nicholson admits that not everybody in the business world agrees with his belief in the effectiveness of evolutionary psychology in the workplace. One group that resist the theory of evolutionary psychology is young MBA graduates who are just beginning their careers and feel that evolutionary psychology will make their lives at work more difficult. Older and wiser executives point out that they still tend to cling to the idea of a magic formula to bring people into line with corporate strategy. But that is back-to-front thinking according to Nicholson, who contends that we should be reinventing our business structures, not our fundamental human nature.

At the end of his book, Nicholson gives his forecast of what will and will not change in the business world. He believes that most people will still prefer more traditional forms of work and throughout their lives will continue to aim at lifelong status advancement. He also maintains that the line between work and home will be less defined, but that people will prefer traditional working patterns if working from home leaves them isolated from their work community. He doubts that the high-tech ideas of virtual companies will ever be very successful because people will still want to meet each other face-to-face. Nicholson describes his ideal organisation in the future: it would be decentralized, with small sub-units; the staff would be from diverse backgrounds and be allowed a high degree of self-determination. New endeavours and creativity would replace systems and rationality. Nicholson acknowledges that there is a long way to go in terms of the translation of his ideas of evolutionary psychology into practical propositions, but he is confident more and more people will come round to his way of thinking.

Questions

Questions 27–31 One Choice

Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D.

27 The writer's purpose in the first paragraph is to
  1. oppose the views of Charles Darwin.
  2. compare experts' opinions of Darwin's theory.
  3. explain the theory of evolutionary psychology.
  4. name experts in the field of evolutionary psychology.
28 In the third paragraph which view about evolutionary psychology matches Nicholson's opinion?
  1. Our characters determine our career choices.
  2. We begin life without any preconceived notions.
  3. Our interests and skills depend on our environment.
  4. We inherit ideas and characteristics from our ancestors.
29 The writer discusses the key element of emotion in order to
  1. criticise primitive survival strategies.
  2. explain attitudes and actions at work.
  3. demonstrate the slowness of evolution.
  4. suggest companies today are poorly structured.
30 Which of the following does Nicholson predict will happen in the business world?
  1. Companies will remain in city centres.
  2. Promotion will no longer motivate people.
  3. Employees will be less independent than now.
  4. Social interaction will remain important to workers.
31 Which of the following is the most suitable title for Reading Passage 3?
  1. How successful companies manage change.
  2. Understanding the origins of workplace behavior.
  3. Darwin's theories rejected by modern management.
  4. Why post-industrial organisations need to evolve more quickly.

Questions 32–35 Yes / No / Not Given

Do the following statements agree with the views of the writer in Reading Passage 3?

YES if the statement agrees with the views of the writer

NO if the statement contradicts the views of the writer

NOT GIVEN if it is impossible to say what the writer thinks about this

32 Nicholson makes a persuasive argument in his book.
33 Tooby and Cosmides believe natural selection through the generations has prepared our brains for modern working life.
34 Our reliance on technology causes emotional problems in the workplace.
35 People today are more trusting than they used to be.

Questions 36–40 Summary Completion

Complete the summary using the list of words, A-I, below.

A.  business leaders    |    B.  MBA graduates    |    C.  promotion structures

D.  reward strategy    |    E.  magic formula    |    F.  strategic planning

G.  back-to-front thinking    |    H.  business environments    |    I.  human nature

Nicholson's advice to the corporate world

Nicholson believes that if we know why people act the way they do, we can change 36 so employees will work more efficiently. Nicholson's ideas are unwelcome to 37 but some executives are more open to what evolutionary psychology says. However, these executives still believe that there is a 38 that will make employees act according to the company's practices. According to Nicholson, these senior executives are engaging in 39 and we should not try to change 40 but instead we should change our business structures.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 C Nevertheless, Darwinian evolutionary theory is the foundation of a new wave of ideas about human behavior in general and particularly the way people behave in the workplace; these ideas have been given the title of 'evolutionary psychology' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Darwin's theory is the base of new ideas about how people behave, especially at work, and these ideas are called 'evolutionary psychology'.
Answer Explanation:
The answer C means the writer's purpose is to explain the theory of evolutionary psychology.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the paragraph explains what 'evolutionary psychology' is and where it comes from. It does not oppose Darwin or only list experts.
Q28 D He points out that sophisticated research shows the brain actually houses a store of knowledge when we are born, and now genetic research is establishing there are certain genes that account for abilities, tastes and tendencies Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Nicholson thinks babies are born with stored knowledge, and that genes explain some abilities, tastes and habits.
Answer Explanation:
The answer D means we inherit ideas and characteristics from our ancestors.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because 'store of knowledge when we are born' and 'genes' match 'inherit'. This is opposite to the old idea of a blank page.
Q29 B Emotion was originally essential to keep early man alive and safe from predators Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that emotion was needed in early times to keep people alive and safe from animals that hunt.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means the writer discusses emotion to explain attitudes and actions at work.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the paragraph links emotion to how we judge others, trust people and behave 'in the modern workplace'.
Q30 D He doubts that the high-tech ideas of virtual companies will ever be very successful because people will still want to meet each other face-to-face Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Nicholson doubts virtual companies will succeed, because people will still want to meet in person.
Answer Explanation:
The answer D means that social interaction will stay important to workers.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because 'meet each other face-to-face' matches 'social interaction', and 'still' matches 'remain'.
Q31 B Nevertheless, Darwinian evolutionary theory is the foundation of a new wave of ideas about human behavior in general and particularly the way people behave in the workplace Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Darwin's theory is the base for new ideas about why people behave as they do at work.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means the best title is about understanding where workplace behavior comes from.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the whole passage explains the origins of behavior, from the Stone Age to the modern office, which matches 'origins of workplace behavior'.
Q32 YES It is an effective approach to explaining why people behave as they do, particularly at work Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Nicholson's book is a good way to explain why people behave as they do, especially at work.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is YES because the writer agrees that Nicholson's argument is convincing.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is YES because the writer calls the book 'an effective approach', which matches 'persuasive argument'.
Q33 NO As Tooby and Cosmides stress, there have not been enough generations for a brain that is well adapted to our post-industrial life to evolve through natural selection Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Tooby and Cosmides stress that there has not been enough time for our brains to adapt to modern life.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is NO because they believe our brains are not yet ready for modern working life.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because 'there have not been enough generations' is the opposite of the statement that natural selection 'has prepared our brains'.
Q34 NOT GIVEN Emotion was, and continues to be, our radar, guiding us throughout today's techno-defined business world Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that emotion still guides us in today's business world, which is full of technology.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is NOT GIVEN because the writer does not say that technology causes emotional problems.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage mentions technology and emotion together, but never says that technology creates emotional problems.
Q35 NO As a consequence, it takes very little to trigger people's innate distrust of others because our safety in antiquity depended on supporting our near family and friends whom we valued more than other people Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that it is easy to make people distrust others, because long ago safety depended on helping family and friends.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is NO because people today are not more trusting than before; they distrust easily.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NO because 'takes very little to trigger people's innate distrust' is the opposite of 'more trusting than they used to be'.
Q36 H He thinks that by knowing the reasons for people's behaviour it is possible to mould corporate environments into places that have more chance of working efficiently and being pleasant places to work in Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Nicholson thinks that knowing why people behave as they do lets us shape company environments so they work well.
Answer Explanation:
The answer H, 'business environments', is what we can change so employees work more efficiently.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is H because 'mould corporate environments' matches 'change business environments'.
Q37 B One group that resist the theory of evolutionary psychology is young MBA graduates who are just beginning their careers and feel that evolutionary psychology will make their lives at work more difficult Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that young MBA graduates are one group that does not accept evolutionary psychology.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B, 'MBA graduates', is the group to which Nicholson's ideas are unwelcome.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because 'resist the theory' matches 'unwelcome', and the next part of the summary talks about 'some executives'.
Q38 E Older and wiser executives point out that they still tend to cling to the idea of a magic formula to bring people into line with corporate strategy Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that older executives still hold on to the idea of a magic formula that makes people follow company plans.
Answer Explanation:
The answer E, 'magic formula', is what these executives believe will make employees follow company practices.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because 'bring people into line with corporate strategy' matches 'act according to the company's practices'.
Q39 G But that is back-to-front thinking according to Nicholson Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Nicholson thinks the executives' idea is 'back-to-front thinking', which means thinking the wrong way round.
Answer Explanation:
The answer G, 'back-to-front thinking', is what Nicholson says the senior executives are doing.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is G because the passage uses these exact words for the executives' idea of a magic formula.
Q40 I who contends that we should be reinventing our business structures, not our fundamental human nature Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that Nicholson thinks we should change business structures, not human nature.
Answer Explanation:
The answer I, 'human nature', is what we should not try to change.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is I because 'not our fundamental human nature' matches 'we should not try to change' in the summary.
