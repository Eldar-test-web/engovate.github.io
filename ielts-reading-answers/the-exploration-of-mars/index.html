The Exploration Of Mars - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From IELTS Past Papers Academic Reading Test 13 · Part 3 · Questions 27–40

Reading Passage

Read the text below and answer questions 27-40.

The Exploration of Mars

A In 1877, Giovanni Schiaparelli, an Italian astronomer, made drawings and maps of the Martian surface that suggested strange features. The images from telescopes at this time were not as sharp as today's. Schiaparelli said he could see a network of lines, or canali. In 1894, an American astronomer, Percival Lowell, made a series of observations of Mars from his own observatory at Flagstaff, Arizona, USA. Lowell was convinced a great network of canals had been dug to irrigate crops for the Martian race! He suggested that each canal had fertile vegetation on either side, making them noticeable from Earth. Drawings and globes he made show a network of canals and oases all over the planet.

B The idea that there was intelligent life on Mars gained strength in the late 19th century. In 1898, H.G. Wells wrote a science fiction classic, The War of the Worlds about an invading force of Martians who try to conquer Earth. They use highly advanced technology (advanced for 1898) to crush human resistance in their path. In 1917, Edgar Rice Burroughs wrote the first in a series of 11 novels about Mars. Strange beings and rampaging Martian monsters gripped the public's imagination. A radio broadcast by Orson Welles on Halloween night in 1938 of The War of the Worlds caused widespread panic across America. People ran into the streets in their pyjamas-millions believed the dramatic reports of a Martian invasion.

C Probes are very important to our understanding of other planets. Much of our recent knowledge comes from these robotic missions into space. The first images sent back from Mars came from Mariner 4 in July 1965. They showed a cratered and barren landscape, more like the surface of our moon than Earth. In 1969, Mariners 6 and 7 were launched and took 200 photographs of Mars's southern hemisphere and pole on fly-by missions. But these showed little more information. In 1971, Mariner 9's mission was to orbit the planet every 12 hours. In 1975, The USA sent two Viking probes to the planet, each with a lander and an orbiter. The Landers had sampler arms to scoop up Martian rocks and did experiments to try and find signs of life. Although no life was found, they sent back the first colour pictures of the planet’s surface and atmosphere from pivoting cameras.

D The Martian meteorite found in Earth aroused doubts to the above analysis. ALH84001 meteorite was discovered in December 1984 in Antarctica, by members of the ANSMET project; The sample was ejected from Mars about 17 million years ago and spent 11,000 years in or on the Antarctic ice sheets. Composition analysis by NASA revealed a kind of magnetite that on Earth, is only found in association with certain microorganisms. Some structures resembling the mineralized casts of terrestrial bacteria and their appendages fibrils or by-products occur in the rims of carbonate globules and pre-terrestrial aqueous alteration regions. The size and shape of the objects is consistent with Earthly fossilized nanobacteria but the existence of nanobacteria itself is still controversial.

E In 1965, the Mariner 4 probe discovered that Mars had no global magnetic field that would protect the planet from potentially life-threatening cosmic radiation and solar radiation; observations made in the late 1990s by the Mars Global Surveyor confirmed this discovery. Scientists speculate that the lack of magnetic shielding helped the solar wind blow away much of Mars's atmosphere over the course of several billion years. After mapping cosmic radiation levels at various depths on Mars, researchers have concluded that any life within the first several meters of the planet's surface would be killed by lethal doses of cosmic radiation. In 2007, it was calculated that DNA and RNA damage by cosmic radiation would limit life on Mars to depths greater than 7.5 metres below the planet's surface. Therefore, the best potential locations for discovering life on Mars may be at subsurface environments that have not been studied yet. Disappearance of the magnetic field may have played a significant role in the process of Martian climate change. According to the valuation of the scientists, the climate of Mars gradually transits from warm and wet to cold and dry after magnetic field vanished.

F NASA's recent missions have focused on another question: whether Mars held lakes or oceans of liquid water on its surface in the ancient past. Scientists have found hematite, a mineral that forms in the presence of water. Thus, the mission of the Mars Exploration Rovers of 2004 was not to look for present or past life, but for evidence of liquid water on the surface of Mars in the planet's ancient past. Liquid water, necessary for Earth life and for metabolism as generally conducted by species on Earth, cannot exist on the surface of Mars under its present low atmospheric pressure and temperature, except at the lowest shaded elevations for short periods and liquid water does not appear at the surface itself. In March 2004, NASA announced that its rover Opportunity had discovered evidence that Mars was, in the ancient past, a wet planet. This had raised hopes that evidence of past life might be found on the planet today. ESA confirmed that the Mars Express orbiter had directly detected huge reserves of water ice at Mars’ south pole in January 2004.

G Researchers from the Center of Astrobiology (Spain) and the Catholic University of the North in Chile have found an ‘oasis’ of microorganisms two meters below the surface of the Atacama Desert, thanks to SOLID, a detector for signs of life which could be used in environments similar to subsoil on Mars. “We have named it a ‘microbial oasis’ because we found microorganisms developing in a habitat that was rich in rock salt and other highly hygroscopic compounds that absorb water” explained Victor Parro, researcher from the Center of Astrobiology in Spain. “If there are similar microbes on Mars or remains in similar conditions to the ones we have found in Atacama, we could detect them with instruments like SOLID” Parro highlighted.

H Even more intriguing, however, is the alternative scenario by Spanish scientists: If those samples could be found to use DNA, as Earthly life does, as their genetic code. It is extremely unlikely that such a highly specialised, complex molecule like DNA could have evolved separately on the two planets, indicating that there must be a common origin for Martian and Earthly life. Life based on DNA first appeared on Mars and then spread to Earth, where it then evolved into the myriad forms of plants and creatures that exist today. If this was found to be the case, we would have to face the logical conclusion: we are all Martian. If not, we would continue to search for signs of life.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q27 D The Martian meteorite found in Earth aroused doubts to the above analysis. ALH84001 meteorite was discovered in December 1984 in Antarctica, by members of the ANSMET project; The sample was ejected from Mars about 17 million years ago and spent 11,000 years in or on the Antarctic ice sheets Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that a piece of rock that originated on Mars came to Earth and was discovered in Antarctica, providing scientists with physical material from Mars to study on Earth.
Answer Explanation:
The answer identifies Paragraph D as the section describing clues or materials from Mars that were discovered on Earth.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because Paragraph D discusses a space rock from Mars (a "Martian meteorite") that was found on Earth in Antarctica. Scientists studied this rock because it contained signs and structures that could be evidence of tiny living things from Mars. Key matching phrases include "Martian meteorite found in Earth" and "discovered... in Antarctica".
Q28 H It is extremely unlikely that such a highly specialised, complex molecule like DNA could have evolved separately on the two planets, indicating that there must be a common origin for Martian and Earthly life Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that DNA is so complex that it probably did not develop on both planets by itself, meaning life on Mars and life on Earth likely came from the same source.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is H because Paragraph H talks about the idea that living things on Mars and Earth could have started from the very same beginning.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is H because the paragraph discusses the possibility that DNA-based life began on Mars and then traveled to Earth. It directly states that finding DNA in Martian samples would mean there is a "common origin for Martian and Earthly life." In this context, the words "common origin" mean the same thing as "share the same life origin."
Q29 A Lowell was convinced a great network of canals had been dug to irrigate crops for the Martian race! He suggested that each canal had fertile vegetation on either side, making them noticeable from Earth. Drawings and globes he made show a network of canals and oases all over the planet Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Percival Lowell believed Martians built a water canal system to supply water for growing food, and he drew pictures and models of Mars displaying these canals.
Answer Explanation:
The answer A means that Paragraph A is the section of the text that describes drawings showing specific man-made structures built for farming.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because Paragraph A describes the astronomer Percival Lowell, who believed that a large system of canals was built ("had been dug") to water farm plants ("to irrigate crops") on Mars. Furthermore, these agricultural structures were clearly shown and mapped ("Drawings and globes he made show a network of canals"). In this context, "agricultural construction" refers to canals dug to irrigate crops, and "depicted" matches the drawings and globes Lowell made.
Q30 G Researchers from the Center of Astrobiology (Spain) and the Catholic University of the North in Chile have found an ‘oasis’ of microorganisms two meters below the surface of the Atacama Desert, thanks to SOLID, a detector for signs of life which could be used in environments similar to subsoil on Mars Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that scientists found living microbes deep under the desert and have a tool named SOLID that detects life in environments resembling the ground on Mars.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "G" means that Paragraph G is the section of the text describing research and a tool designed to search for signs of life in places with conditions like those on Mars.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is G because the paragraph describes a life detector called SOLID that was tested on underground microorganisms in the Atacama Desert. The text mentions that this instrument is designed to search for signs of life in conditions similar to the subsurface of Mars.
Q31 E Disappearance of the magnetic field may have played a significant role in the process of Martian climate change. According to the valuation of the scientists, the climate of Mars gradually transits from warm and wet to cold and dry after magnetic field vanished Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when Mars lost its magnetic field, its climate went through a big change, shifting from warm and wet to cold and dry.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "E" means that Paragraph E contains the information about the major and harsh climate change that happened on Mars.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is E because this paragraph explains how Mars went through a major climate change after losing its magnetic field. The text uses terms such as "climate change" and describes how the weather on Mars "transits from warm and wet to cold and dry", which directly matches the idea of a "climate transformation".
Q32 F Thus, the mission of the Mars Exploration Rovers of 2004 was not to look for present or past life, but for evidence of liquid water on the surface of Mars in the planet's ancient past Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the scientific goal of the 2004 rover mission was to search for proof of liquid water on Mars from long ago.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is paragraph F.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is F because paragraph F discusses space missions aimed at discovering if Mars once had liquid water. The text mentions that NASA's scientific investigations, specifically the 2004 Mars Exploration Rovers mission, were designed to search for "evidence of liquid water on the surface of Mars".
Q33 C Lowell was convinced a great network of canals had been dug to irrigate crops for the Martian race! He suggested that each canal had fertile vegetation on either side, making them noticeable from Earth Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that Lowell believed there were man-made waterways used for watering farm plants on Mars, with green plant life growing alongside them.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Percival Lowell described Mars as having water channels (canals) and signs of farming (crops and plants), much like Earth.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Paragraph A explains that Percival Lowell believed a system of canals had been created to supply water to crops for Martians, and that these canals were lined with plant life. In the text, "network of canals" and "irrigate" relate to "water", while "crops" and "fertile vegetation" relate to "agriculture trace". Therefore, he viewed Mars as having water and farming features similar to Earth.
Q34 B In 1898, H.G. Wells wrote a science fiction classic, The War of the Worlds about an invading force of Martians who try to conquer Earth. They use highly advanced technology (advanced for 1898) to crush human resistance in their path. In 1917, Edgar Rice Burroughs wrote the first in a series of 11 novels about Mars. Strange beings and rampaging Martian monsters gripped the public's imagination Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that science fiction novels and stories written by authors like H.G. Wells and Edgar Rice Burroughs captured the public's imagination and shaped what people thought about Mars.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that people formed their ideas and beliefs about Mars by reading books, stories, and novels.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because Paragraph B explains that during the late 19th and early 20th centuries, ideas about intelligent life and monsters on Mars became popular through famous written books (literature), such as H.G. Wells's science fiction novel 'The War of the Worlds' and Edgar Rice Burroughs's series of novels.
Q35 B The first images sent back from Mars came from Mariner 4 in July 1965. They showed a cratered and barren landscape, more like the surface of our moon than Earth Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that in 1965, the spacecraft Mariner 4 took the first photos of Mars, revealing that the planet's surface was dry, empty, and covered in craters like the moon, rather than like Earth.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that pictures taken by spacecraft in the 1960s showed that Mars was empty and lifeless compared to Earth.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the passage states that Mariner 4 sent back the first pictures of Mars in July 1965, which revealed a "cratered and barren landscape" resembling the moon rather than Earth. The word "barren" means empty and without life, which matches the idea that Mars was not as vivid or full of life as Earth.
Q36 A Researchers from the Center of Astrobiology (Spain) and the Catholic University of the North in Chile have found an ‘oasis’ of microorganisms two meters below the surface of the Atacama Desert, thanks to SOLID, a detector for signs of life which could be used in environments similar to subsoil on Mars Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says researchers found a small area of tiny living things two meters under the Atacama Desert. They used SOLID, a tool that finds signs of life and can be used in places like the ground on Mars.
Answer Explanation:
The answer says SOLID can be used to look for living things in places with Mars-like conditions.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer matches the words 'could be used in environments similar to subsoil on Mars.' 'Employed' means 'used,' and 'Martian condition' means 'similar to Mars.' Choice B says the opposite. Choice C is wrong because the passage never says the Atacama is the only place with life. Choice D is wrong because microorganisms were found there.
Q37 NOT GIVEN In 1898, H.G. Wells wrote a science fiction classic, The War of the Worlds about an invading force of Martians who try to conquer Earth. They use highly advanced technology (advanced for 1898) to crush human resistance in their path Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that in the story, Martians used very advanced machines and weapons to defeat humans, but it does not say their technology was superior in every single area or field.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the passage does not provide enough information to say if the statement is true or false.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because Paragraph B mentions that Martians in the novel *The War of the Worlds* used "highly advanced technology" to fight humans, but it never mentions whether this technology was better than human technology "in every field". Since this detail is missing, we cannot know if the statement is completely true or false.
Q38 FALSE The Martian meteorite found in Earth aroused doubts to the above analysis Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that a rock from Mars discovered on Earth made scientists doubt and question the earlier results and analyses that found no evidence of life.
Answer Explanation:
The answer FALSE means that the statement is incorrect according to the passage, because the findings or conclusions from the Viking probes have actually been questioned or challenged.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the text shows that findings from the earlier missions, including the Viking probes' analysis where no life was found, were later questioned. Paragraph D states that a Martian meteorite found on Earth "aroused doubts to the above analysis," meaning it created doubt and challenged the previous conclusion that Mars had no signs of life.
Q39 TRUE Composition analysis by NASA revealed a kind of magnetite that on Earth, is only found in association with certain microorganisms Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when NASA tested the make-up of the Martian rock, they found a type of mineral (magnetite) that on Earth exists only alongside certain tiny living germs.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means that the text confirms that testing a rock from Mars showed a material related to tiny living things (microorganisms or germs).
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because Paragraph D explains that scientists tested the ALH84001 Martian meteorite. During this testing, NASA discovered a material called magnetite, which on Earth is linked only to specific microscopic living organisms (germs). Here, the statement matches the passage through the following key synonyms and ideas:
- "Analysis on meteorite from Mars" corresponds to "Composition analysis" of the "ALH84001 meteorite" which was "ejected from Mars".
- "a substance" corresponds to "a kind of magnetite".
- "connected to" corresponds to "found in association with".
- "some germs" corresponds to "certain microorganisms".
Q40 NOT GIVEN “If there are similar microbes on Mars or remains in similar conditions to the ones we have found in Atacama, we could detect them with instruments like SOLID” Parro highlighted Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage shows what Victor Parro actually said: that instruments like SOLID could detect microbes if they exist on Mars. It does not mention any plan or condition about deploying the project only after identifying DNA on Earth.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "NOT GIVEN" means that the text does not say whether Victor Parro said the project would be sent to Mars after finding DNA on Earth.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because Paragraph G introduces researcher Victor Parro and his detector SOLID, which can detect signs of microbes. However, nowhere in the passage does Victor Parro state that their project will be sent or deployed to Mars specifically after identifying DNA substances on Earth. Because this condition is never mentioned, the statement cannot be confirmed as true or false.
