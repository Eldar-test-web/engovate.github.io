Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 1-13, which are based on Reading Passage 1 below.

Studies in the History of Art and Visual Culture

General Information

The Department of the History of Art, which operates within the Faculty of History, offers a one-year postgraduate course in the History of Art and Visual Culture. We welcome applicants from a broad range of backgrounds and do not require students to have a first degree in art history. In addition to a compulsory course on Theory and Methods, students take two one-term optional courses, and write a 15,000-word dissertation on a topic of their choice. The course aims to redefine art history and includes a broader range of objects and visual media than other art history courses. Images and objects produced in many contexts – ranging from the scientific to the popular – have been brought together to show how visual styles at different periods and in different places can be understood in relation to the aesthetic, intellectual and social facets of various cultures.

Theory and Methods

This core course provides an advanced introduction to the major methodological issues and traditions of art history. The course is organised around a series of issues that relate to the production of art and people's responses to it. Students will discuss these issues and assigned readings in small weekly classes. In lectures, the tutors will consider works of art from a variety of cultures in order to demonstrate that context and artistic method are important aspects of art history.

Choosing Courses

The optional courses listed below are not available every year and prospective students are advised to check on the availability of specific courses. Students should also note there may be some restrictions on combining particular courses within their degree programme. Students wishing to take a course that does not appear in the list but is offered by the Faculty of History will need to get permission from the relevant Course Tutor. An application should then be made by the candidate's supervisor.

Authenticity and Replication

The course is designed to give students exposure to a central issue of the visual arts in an explicitly inter-disciplinary and cross-cultural framework. It will use a series of case studies to explore the idea of ‘the real’ or 'the authentic' in both images and objects. The historical and geographical contexts to be addressed will range widely, from ancient Greece and Rome, to early modern China and to contemporary art, including works seen as genuine as well as works regarded as fraudulent. The course will make particularly extensive use of trips to museums to view actual objects and images.

French Painting 1880-1912

The course examines the development of Post-impressionist painting between 1880 and 1912, at which point Cubism began to have a major impact on the thinking of French artists. Rather than tracing a history of styles or individuals, we consider how artistic practices were closely linked to contemporary developments. The rise of new forms of image distribution, including cinema, will be addressed, as well as the changing role of painting in the public sphere. The writings of artists and their contemporaries will be examined alongside recent art-historical work.

Medieval European Art

This course addresses two problems central to the history of art: the roots of artistic invention, and suitable methods for instructing on technique. During this period, young, would-be artists acted as assistants to a master painter in his workshop. With this type of training, what was the scope for originality, and how was stylistic change encouraged?

These issues are brought into sharp focus by the changing visual culture of late-medieval Europe, between the twelfth and the fifteenth centuries. The available literature on these themes is rich, yet inconsistent: the course therefore addresses questions that are very much open.

Reception of Classical European Art

From the fourteenth century the discovery of classical antiquities inspired contemporary artists, some copied closely, some restored the ancient in a contemporary style, while others reinterpreted freely. The course focuses on sculpture, painting and architecture in Britain, with a selection of other European works. Sculpture is examined in life-size marble statues, and in miniature with porcelain figurines. Painting is studied through the designs painted on vases, particularly Athenian and South Italian. Architecture is examined through the ancient Greek temples that were excavated in the nineteenth century and also replicated in miniature for sale to travelers.

Women, Art and Culture in Early Modern Europe

This course will explore the various roles played by women in the production and reception of art and architecture in fifteenth-to seventeenth-century Europe. After many decades of relative neglect, the significance of the way women contributed to the art and culture of Early Modern Europe has come to light. By drawing on this wealth of new research, the course will examine the careers of professional women artists working in Northern and Southern Europe. We also consider how famous women patrons, such as Isabella d'Este and Catherine de Medici, influenced the careers of artists and wider perceptions about art. Another topic that will be addressed is the representation of women in the visual arts, for example as sitters for state portraits and marriage paintings.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 FALSE We welcome applicants from a broad range of backgrounds and do not require students to have a first degree in art history Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that students from many different study backgrounds are welcome to apply, and they do not need to already have a university degree in art history.
Answer Explanation:
The answer FALSE means the statement does not agree with the text; in fact, the text says the opposite.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage explicitly states that they do not require students to have studied art history before. The statement claims that applicants "must have previously studied art history," which directly contradicts the passage's words "do not require students to have a first degree in art history."
Q2 FALSE In addition to a compulsory course on Theory and Methods, students take two one-term optional courses, and write a 15,000-word dissertation on a topic of their choice Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that all students are required to take the Theory and Methods class, and as well as taking that class, they also choose two optional classes and write a long paper.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "FALSE" means that the statement directly disagrees with the facts given in the text.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because students cannot replace the Theory and Methods course with optional courses. The text states that Theory and Methods is a "compulsory" (mandatory, must-do) course. Furthermore, it says "In addition to" this course, students take two optional courses. This means they must take both the Theory and Methods course and the two optional courses together, not one "instead of" the other.
Q3 NOT GIVEN Images and objects produced in many contexts – ranging from the scientific to the popular – have been brought together to show how visual styles at different periods and in different places can be understood in relation to the aesthetic, intellectual and social facets of various cultures Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that art pieces and images from different time periods and places were gathered together for the study. However, it does not state whether collecting objects from some periods was harder than from others.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the text does not say whether the course organisers had difficulty finding or gathering art items from certain time periods compared to others.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is NOT GIVEN because the passage never mentions the course organisers having trouble or "problems collecting" artworks. In the 'General Information' section, the text notes that items from different times and places "have been brought together", but it says nothing about how hard or easy it was to gather them.
Q4 TRUE The course is organised around a series of issues that relate to the production of art and people's responses to it Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that this class centers on topics about how art is made (produced) and how people react to or view (respond to) that art.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means that the statement accurately reflects the facts mentioned in the reading passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the text clearly states that the "Theory and Methods" course is based on topics connected to how art is created and how people react to it. In the text, "the production of art" means the way art is made, and "people's responses to it" means how art is perceived. Because these match the statement completely, the answer is TRUE.
Q5 FALSE The optional courses listed below are not available every year and prospective students are advised to check on the availability of specific courses Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage means that the elective classes on the list are not taught every single year, so students must check if a specific class is open before choosing it.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE because students cannot simply choose any optional course from the list, as not all of them are offered every year.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the statement claims students can take "any" of the listed optional courses. However, under the section titled "Choosing Courses", the passage clearly states that the optional courses "are not available every year" and that there may be "restrictions". Because the courses are not all open every year, students cannot take any course they want.
Q6 TRUE Students wishing to take a course that does not appear in the list but is offered by the Faculty of History will need to get permission from the relevant Course Tutor Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that if students want to enroll in a course not found on the provided list, they must first ask for and receive permission from the teacher of that course.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "TRUE" means that it is true and correct that students can choose a course not mentioned on the list if they receive the right permission.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage clearly states that students who want to choose a course not on the list are allowed to do so if they receive permission. In the passage, the phrase "does not appear in the list" matches "unlisted course", and "get permission from the relevant Course Tutor" matches "get the appropriate approval". Therefore, the statement completely agrees with the text.
Q7 fraudulent including works seen as genuine as well as works regarded as fraudulent Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that students will study art that is real (genuine) along with art that is fake or dishonest (fraudulent).
Answer Explanation:
The answer "fraudulent" means fake, dishonest, or not real art.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the section "Authenticity and Replication." The notes state that "genuine art is studied in addition to that considered [blank]". In the passage, it mentions that the course includes "works seen as genuine as well as works regarded as fraudulent." The word "regarded" matches "considered," and "as well as" matches "in addition to," which makes "fraudulent" the exact one-word answer required.
Q8 museums The course will make particularly extensive use of trips to museums to view actual objects and images Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that students in this course will go on many trips to museums in order to look at real artworks and objects.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "museums" means places or buildings where historical and artistic objects are kept, shown to the public, and studied.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is confirmed by the section titled "Authenticity and Replication." The notes state that "art objects are studied in ". The text states that the course will take students on visits to "museums" so they can see and examine real items ("actual objects and images"). Therefore, "museums" perfectly completes the note.
Q9 Cubism The course examines the development of Post-impressionist painting between 1880 and 1912, at which point Cubism began to have a major impact on the thinking of French artists Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the course focuses on art up to 1912, when the art style called Cubism started to become very influential among French artists.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to an art style called Cubism.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is confirmed by the section titled 'French Painting 1880-1912'. The text states that the course studies painting up to the year 1912, which was the moment when 'Cubism began to have a major impact'. In the notes, the phrase 'started to be prominent' corresponds directly to 'began to have a major impact', meaning Cubism is the movement that became significant at the end of the studied period.
Q10 cinema Rather than tracing a history of styles or individuals, we consider how artistic practices were closely linked to contemporary developments. The rise of new forms of image distribution, including cinema, will be addressed, as well as the changing role of painting in the public sphere Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that the course studies how art was connected to new changes happening at that time, and gives cinema as an example of a new way images were shared.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "cinema" means movies or films as a new way to show pictures.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is confirmed by the section titled "French Painting 1880-1912". The notes ask for a new development that connects with art. The passage mentions that artistic practices were "closely linked to contemporary developments" and then gives "cinema" as an example of "new forms of image distribution". Therefore, "cinema" fits the blank perfectly.
Q11 workshop During this period, young, would-be artists acted as assistants to a master painter in his workshop. With this type of training, what was the scope for originality, and how was stylistic change encouraged Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that young people who wanted to be artists trained by assisting an expert painter in his workshop, raising the question of how original their work could actually be under that kind of instruction.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "workshop" refers to the workroom where a skilled painter taught and worked with young learner artists.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is confirmed by the section titled "Medieval European Art". The notes ask where artists were taught by an expert ("master painter") while questioning how they could still be original ("scope for originality"). The text explains that during this time, young learner artists worked as assistants to a master painter in his "workshop". Therefore, "workshop" completes the sentence accurately.
Q12 vases Painting is studied through the designs painted on vases, particularly Athenian and South Italian Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that students learn about painting by looking at artwork and patterns drawn on clay pots (vases).
Answer Explanation:
The answer means decorative containers or pots, often made of clay or ceramic.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "vases" because the text explains how painting is analyzed in the course "Reception of Classical European Art". In the passage, it states that "Painting is studied through the designs painted on vases". The note paraphrases "is studied" as "is examined", and "designs painted on" corresponds to "decoration of". Therefore, the missing word that fits the blank is "vases".
Q13 travelers / travellers Architecture is examined through the ancient Greek temples that were excavated in the nineteenth century and also replicated in miniature for sale to travelers Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that students study architecture by looking at ancient Greek buildings that were dug up in the nineteenth century, as well as small copies of them made to sell to visitors.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "travelers" refers to people who were visiting or journeying to different places.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "travelers" because the text explains that in the 19th century, small copies (models) of ancient Greek temples were made to be sold to people on journeys. In the notes, the phrase "19th century models bought by" matches the passage's description "excavated in the nineteenth century and also replicated in miniature for sale to travelers". Here, "replicated in miniature" corresponds to "models", and "for sale to" corresponds to "bought by".
