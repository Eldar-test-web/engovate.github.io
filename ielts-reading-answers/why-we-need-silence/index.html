Why We Need Silence - IELTS Reading Answers & Explanations

From Cambridge IELTS 21 Academic Reading Test 1 · Part 2 · Questions 14–26

Reading Passage

You should spend about 20 minutes on Questions 14-26, which are based on Reading Passage 2 below.

Why we need silence

A Humans are finely attuned to noise, and for good reason. From an evolutionary perspective, sounds give us vital information, helping us navigate the world and avoid danger. To help ensure loud or unexpected noises get the attention they deserve, our internal chemistry alters in response to them. Our blood pressure goes up, muscles tense and glands release hormones that prepare us for fight or flight. In the short term, this is a good thing. When we are exposed to too much noise over the long term, however, those responses can lead to a multitude of health issues, from sleep disturbance to even cardiovascular disease.

B The World Health Organization has recently designated excessive noise as an 'underestimated threat' to public health, and has said that people living in cities such as Mumbai, Tokyo and Buenos Aires are being exposed to far more than the recommended 40 decibels of noise at night. A report from the European Environment Agency concluded that noise was an ongoing and widespread issue in Europe, with at least 1 in 5 people consistently exposed to levels considered harmful to health. 'There are no "earlids" that can protect your brain from noise,' says Nick Antonio, an acoustic consultant who has contributed to the British and international standards for noise.

C The good news is that several cities have been working to turn the volume down. One of the first to do so was London. 'By providing recommendations for quieter buses, reducing noise from roads and also controlling noise from aircraft, they were able to make the city quieter,' says Antonio. Other cities have introduced noise-reducing road coatings, for instance, alongside greenery that muffles sound. Some solutions are more specific: Washington DC's ban on petrol-powered leaf blowers came into effect recently, while in New York City, legislation has been approved to fine people who modify their vehicles to make them noisier. 'People are seeing the benefits of these more quiet environments in their cities,' Antonio says. 'I expect we will see much more of this in the future.'

D Researchers are also seeking to understand what aspects of silent experiences are most beneficial to our health. One of the best-researched is the flotation tank: a lightproof, soundproof tank of salt water in which a person floats as a form of deep relaxation. While some people experience altered perception in the tanks, involving subtle humming sounds and visual effects, these effects are benign and do not detract from the benefits of the experience, says Justin Feinstein, a clinical neuropsychologist. 'When you don't have external sensory stimuli coming in, the brain tries to fill the void to make sense of this dark and silent world,' he explains. 'In these tanks, some people can even hear the sound of their eyes blinking,' says Feinstein. 'But it is the ability to focus on the breath that helps people reach a relaxed or meditative state.'

E To further explore flotation tanks as a therapeutic tool, Feinstein and his colleagues recruited 50 people with a variety of conditions related to stress and had them answer a questionnaire prior to and following a flotation session. Participants reported decreases in muscle tension, pain and symptoms of their conditions after a single, 1-hour float, alongside an increase in feelings of relaxation and overall wellbeing.

Less is known about what effects sensory deprivation can have on the brain. To investigate, Feinstein's team had 48 people participate in either three 90-minute float sessions or three 90-minute periods of relaxing on a chair which reclined. Participants had their brains scanned using functional magnetic resonance imaging at the beginning and end of the trial. Float sessions uniquely decreased activity in the default mode network (DMN), a collection of brain regions commonly linked with depression. Feinstein says it is an exciting finding, because flotation tanks seem to offer a way of 'resetting' our nervous system to prevent it from getting out of balance.

F Neurobiologist Tal Dotan Ben-Soussan is also an advocate of silence as a therapy. 'When we find ways to be quiet, we are not only quiet in our environment, but quiet in our inner selves,' she explains. 'This allows us to be more aware of what is happening around us and what the situation may require from us so we can provide [a more] adequate response.'

Not everyone will benefit from silence to the same extent, but Ben-Soussan says one characteristic is key: the person must need to want to engage in the experience. 'We see from animal models and human studies that volition and intentionality is important,' she says. 'When people do not want silence, it can be very distressing.'

G Eric Pfeifer, a psychotherapy researcher, also concedes that some people may not benefit from silence, particularly those who are in a heightened state of stress. 'People in these states may not be able to relax or calm down in a silent condition,' he says. Professional guidance can be useful, he adds, allowing people to approach silence slowly so that they can gradually enjoy the benefits. And Pfeifer is convinced that silence is more attainable in everyday life than people think. First, complete silence isn't necessary. In a recent study, he found that participants reported more relaxation and less boredom when they sat quietly in an outdoor garden compared with a completely silent room. Second, Pfeifer believes we don't need a lot of silence to gain benefits. 'You don't need to spend hours in silence,' he says. 'It is likely better to have more frequency of silence for a few minutes at a time than a longer period of silence only once a week. Just finding those places in your daily life where you can find some silence ... can make a big difference.'

Feinstein and his colleagues conducted an experiment in which 50 people, who were all suffering from stress and related issues, were given a 19 to complete before and after using a flotation tank. Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q14 C 'By providing recommendations for quieter buses, reducing noise from roads and also controlling noise from aircraft, they were able to make the city quieter,' says Antonio. Other cities have introduced noise-reducing road coatings, for instance, alongside greenery that muffles sound. Some solutions are more specific: Washington DC's ban on petrol-powered leaf blowers came into effect recently, while in New York City, legislation has been approved to fine people who modify their vehicles to make them noisier Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage gives several examples of things cities do to be less noisy, like using quiet buses, fixing roads, using plants to soak up sound, and creating rules against loud machines and loud car parts.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Section C is the part of the text that describes different plans and rules cities use to make life quieter for people.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is section C because it provides a list of several methods or 'strategies' used to lower sound levels. These include making buses quieter, using special road surfaces, planting trees to block sound, and passing laws against loud tools and modified cars. These examples directly address the 'strategies to decrease the noise' mentioned in the question.
Q15 B A report from the European Environment Agency concluded that noise was an ongoing and widespread issue in Europe, with at least 1 in 5 people consistently exposed to levels considered harmful to health Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage uses a specific statistic—"1 in 5 people"—as data to explain that a large number of people are dealing with noise levels that can hurt their health.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is Section B, because this part of the text gives numbers and facts to show how big the problem of loud noise is.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because it provides specific "data" or statistics to show the "extent" (how widespread and serious) of the "problem of excessive noise." It includes information from global organizations like the World Health Organization and the European Environment Agency. Key phrases that indicate the scale of the problem include "40 decibels" and "1 in 5 people," which prove that noise is a common and harmful issue.
Q16 A To help ensure loud or unexpected noises get the attention they deserve, our internal chemistry alters in response to them. Our blood pressure goes up, muscles tense and glands release hormones that prepare us for fight or flight Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that when we hear a loud or surprising noise, the chemicals inside our bodies change. It says our blood pressure rises, our muscles get tight, and our body sends out special chemicals (hormones) to help us deal with the situation.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is Section A because it describes the physical ways our bodies react to loud or surprising sounds.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because this section lists several 'physiological changes'—which are physical changes inside the body—that happen when we hear sudden or loud sounds. It mentions that our 'internal chemistry' changes, our 'blood pressure' increases, our 'muscles' become tight, and our bodies release 'hormones' to get us ready for action. These details match the description of bodily changes mentioned in the question.
Q17 G In a recent study, he found that participants reported more relaxation and less boredom when they sat quietly in an outdoor garden compared with a completely silent room Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that research showed people felt more calm and less tired of their surroundings when they were in a quiet garden instead of a room that was 100% silent.
Answer Explanation:
The answer identifies Section G as the part of the text that shows why a place with a little bit of sound can be better for a person than a place with no sound at all.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is G because it describes a study by Eric Pfeifer. In this study, he looked at how people feel in different environments. He found that people felt more relaxed and had a better time in an outdoor garden, which is quiet but not silent, than they did in a room that was completely silent. This provides the evidence that a relatively quiet place can be more beneficial than a totally silent one.
Q18 breath 'But it is the ability to focus on the breath that helps people reach a relaxed or meditative state.' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage tells us that when a person pays attention to their breathing, it allows them to become very calm and relax their mind.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means the air that you take into your body and let out when you are breathing.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'breath' because the passage in paragraph D explains that focusing on this specific action is what allows people to become calm. In the summary, the phrase 'concentrate on' is used, which has the same meaning as 'focus on' in the text. The passage confirms that this focus is what helps a person reach a 'relaxed or meditative state'.
Q19 questionnaire To further explore flotation tanks as a therapeutic tool, Feinstein and his colleagues recruited 50 people with a variety of conditions related to stress and had them answer a questionnaire prior to and following a flotation session Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that researchers looked for 50 people with stress and asked them to fill out a questionnaire before and after they stayed in the tank to see if they felt better.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to a list of written questions used to collect information from people about their health and feelings.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'questionnaire' because the passage mentions it as the specific tool used to gather data from the 50 participants before and after their time in the tank. The text uses the phrase 'answer a questionnaire' to describe what the participants did to report on their stress-related conditions. Key terms to note are '50 people', 'stress', and 'answer'.
Q20 wellbeing Participants reported decreases in muscle tension, pain and symptoms of their conditions after a single, 1-hour float, alongside an increase in feelings of relaxation and overall wellbeing Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that after spending one hour in a flotation tank, people felt better physically and mentally, reporting that their general health and happiness (wellbeing) improved.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to the general state of feeling healthy, happy, and comfortable.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'wellbeing' because the passage describes a study where people used a flotation tank for one hour. After the session, the participants said they felt less pain and stress, had more feelings of relaxation, and noticed an increase in their 'overall wellbeing.' In the summary, the word 'general' is used as a synonym for 'overall.'
Q21 depression Float sessions uniquely decreased activity in the default mode network (DMN), a collection of brain regions commonly linked with depression Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that using the flotation tank made a specific part of the brain less active. It then explains that these same brain areas are usually connected to the illness of depression.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'depression' refers to a mental health condition where a person feels very sad and loses interest in things they usually enjoy.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'depression' because the passage explains a study where 48 people either sat in a chair or used a flotation tank. The researchers used brain scans to see what happened to their brains. They found that the people who were in the tanks had less activity in a part of the brain called the 'default mode network' (DMN). The text clearly states that this specific group of brain regions is 'linked with depression.'
Q22 C 'When people do not want silence, it can be very distressing.' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that when people are in a quiet environment without wanting to be there, it can make them feel extremely upset or uncomfortable.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that if a person is forced to be in a quiet place and they do not want to be there, they will feel very unhappy and upset.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Tal Dotan Ben-Soussan explains that a person's choice to be in a silent environment is very important. She uses the words 'volition and intentionality' to describe this choice. She states that when people do not want to be in silence, it can be 'distressing,' which means it causes a lot of worry or pain. This matches the idea of silence being 'unpleasant and upsetting' when it is against someone's 'will' (their choice).
Q23 A 'People are seeing the benefits of these more quiet environments in their cities,' Antonio says. 'I expect we will see much more of this in the future.' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that people like the good results of having less noise in their cities. Because of this, Nick Antonio believes that we will see many more of these quiet changes in the time ahead.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is A (Nick Antonio).
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is Nick Antonio because he suggests that the move towards making cities quieter will continue to grow. In the text, he points out that people are already noticing the positive effects of quieter urban areas. Using keywords like 'expect' and 'future,' he explains that he believes more cities will adopt these noise-reducing changes in the years to come.
Q24 B While some people experience altered perception in the tanks, involving subtle humming sounds and visual effects, these effects are benign and do not detract from the benefits of the experience, says Justin Feinstein, a clinical neuropsychologist. 'When you don't have external sensory stimuli coming in, the brain tries to fill the void to make sense of this dark and silent world,' he explains Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage states that when there is no information coming from our eyes or ears, the brain tries to give the person something to focus on by creating its own sounds and images. This happens because the brain is trying to understand a world that has become completely dark and quiet.
Answer Explanation:
The answer identifies Justin Feinstein as the researcher who explained that our brain creates imaginary sounds and sights when we are in a place with no sensory input.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because Justin Feinstein studies flotation tanks, which are designed to remove light and sound. He notes that when the brain has no 'external sensory stimuli' (information from the outside world), it tries to 'fill the void' (make up for the silence and darkness). This leads to 'altered perception,' where people might hear humming or see visual effects that are not actually there, which is the mind's way of compensating for the lack of input.
Q25 D Second, Pfeifer believes we don't need a lot of silence to gain benefits. 'You don't need to spend hours in silence,' he says. 'It is likely better to have more frequency of silence for a few minutes at a time than a longer period of silence only once a week Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains that Pfeifer thinks we don't need to be quiet for a long time to stay healthy. He says it is better to find quiet for just a few minutes many times rather than being quiet for many hours only once a week.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that Eric Pfeifer is the person who says that even a little bit of quiet time can be very helpful for people.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is D because Eric Pfeifer specifically says that we do not need to stay in silence for hours to get the benefits. He explains that having quiet moments for just a few minutes more often is better than one long period of silence. His mentions of 'a few minutes' and 'not a lot of silence' match the phrase 'short amount' in the question, and 'gain benefits' matches 'positive impact'.
Q26 C 'When we find ways to be quiet, we are not only quiet in our environment, but quiet in our inner selves,' she explains. 'This allows us to be more aware of what is happening around us and what the situation may require from us so we can provide [a more] adequate response.' Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that when we are quiet both in the world around us and inside our own minds, we can pay more attention to what is happening near us and act in the way that the situation needs.
Answer Explanation:
The answer identifies Tal Dotan Ben-Soussan as the person who believes that having a quiet environment and a quiet mind helps us notice things better and react the right way.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because Tal Dotan Ben-Soussan explains the relationship between "quiet in our environment" (external) and being "quiet in our inner selves" (internal). She links this silence to being "more aware" (conscious) of what is happening "around us" (surroundings), which allows for an "adequate response" (reacting appropriately).
