Reading Passage

How tennis rackets have changed

In 2016, the British professional tennis player Andy Murray was ranked as the world’s number one. It was an incredible achievement by any standard – made even more remarkable by the fact that he did this during a period considered to be one of the strongest in the sport’s history, competing against the likes of Rafael Nadal, Roger Federer and Novak Djokovic, to name just a few. Yet five years previously, he had been regarded as a talented outsider who entered but never won the major tournaments.

Of the changes that account for this transformation, one was visible and widely publicised: in 2011, Murray invited former number one player Ivan Lendl onto his coaching team – a valuable addition that had a visible impact on the player’s playing style. Another change was so subtle as to pass more or less unnoticed. Like many players, Murray has long preferred a racket that consists of two types of string: one for the mains (verticals) and another for the crosses (horizontals). While he continued to use natural string in the crosses, in 2012 he switched to a synthetic string for the mains. A small change, perhaps, but its importance should not be underestimated.

The modification that Murray made is just one of a number of options available to players looking to tweak their rackets in order to improve their games. ‘Touring professionals have their rackets customised to their specific needs,’ says Colin Triplow, a UK-based professional racket stringer. ‘It’s a highly important part of performance maximisation.’ Consequently, the specific rackets used by the world’s elite are not actually readily available to the public; rather, each racket is individually made to suit the player who uses it. Take the US professional tennis players Mike and Bob Bryan, for example: ‘We’re very particular with our racket specifications,’ they say. ‘All our rackets are sent from our manufacturer to Tampa, Florida, where our frames go through a … thorough customisation process.’ They explain how they have adjusted not only racket length, but even experimented with different kinds of paint. The rackets they use now weigh more than the average model and also have a denser string pattern (i.e. more crosses and mains).

The primary reason for these modifications is simple: as the line between winning and losing becomes thinner and thinner, even these slight changes become more and more important. As a result, players and their teams are becoming increasingly creative with the modifications to their rackets as they look to maximise their competitive advantage.

Racket modifications mainly date back to the 1970s, when the amateur German tennis player Werner Fischer started playing with the so-called spaghetti-strung racket. It created a string bed that generated so much topspin that it was quickly banned by the International Tennis Federation. However, within a decade or two, racket modification became a regularity. Today it is, in many ways, an aspect of the game that is equal in significance to nutrition or training.

Modifications can be divided into two categories: those to the string bed and those to the racket frame. The former is far more common than the latter: the choice of the strings and the tension with which they are installed is something that nearly all professional players experiment with. They will continually change it depending on various factors including the court surface, climatic conditions, and game styles. Some will even change it depending on how they feel at the time.

At one time, all tennis rackets were strung with natural gut made from the outer layer of sheep or cow intestines. This all changed in the early 1990s with the development of synthetic strings that were cheaper and more durable. They are made from three materials: nylon (relatively durable and affordable), Kevlar (too stiff to be used alone) or co-polyester (polyester combined with additives that enhance its performance). Even so, many professional players continue to use a ‘hybrid set-up’, where a combination of both synthetic and natural strings are used.

Of the synthetics, co-polyester is by far the most widely used. It’s a perfect fit for the style of tennis now played, where players tend to battle it out from the back of the court rather than coming to the net. Studies indicate that the average spin from a co-polyester string is 25% greater than that from natural string or other synthetics. In a sense, the development of co-polyester strings has revolutionised the game.

However, many players go beyond these basic adjustments to the strings and make changes to the racket frame itself. For example, much of the serving power of US professional player Pete Sampras was attributed to the addition of four to five lead weights onto his rackets, and today many professionals have the weight adjusted during the manufacturing process.

Other changes to the frame involve the handle. Players have individual preferences for the shape of the handle and some will have the handle of one racket moulded onto the frame of a different racket. Other players make different changes. The professional Portuguese player Gonçalo Oliveira replaced the original grips of his rackets with something thinner because they had previously felt uncomfortable to hold.

Racket customisation and modification have pushed the standards of the game to greater levels that few could have anticipated in the days of natural strings and heavy, wooden frames, and it’s exciting to see what further developments there will be in the future.

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q1 FALSE Yet five years previously, he had been regarded as a talented outsider who entered but never won the major tournaments. Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that five years before 2016, people thought Andy Murray was a talented player, but they did not expect him to be the best. They believed he could play well but he entered many big matches and did not win any of them.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE because it says that people expected Andy Murray to be the best player for five years before 2016, which is not true according to the passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage indicates that five years before 2016, people did not expect him to win; they thought he was talented but only an outsider and not the top player.
Q2 FALSE Another change was so subtle as to pass more or less unnoticed. Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that one of the changes Andy Murray made was very small and went mostly unnoticed by people. This means that not many people paid attention to it.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE because it says the change attracted a lot of attention, but the passage says the opposite.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage indicates that the change was so subtle that it passed almost unnoticed, which means it did not attract a lot of attention.
Q3 NOT GIVEN ‘Touring professionals have their rackets customised to their specific needs,’ says Colin Triplow, a UK-based professional racket stringer. Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that professional players get their rackets made special just for them by a stringer.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'NOT GIVEN' means that we don’t know if the top players have a racket stringer with them while they travel.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'NOT GIVEN' because the passage does not tell us if most of the top players take a stringer with them on tour or not. It only mentions that they customize their rackets.
Q4 FALSE The rackets they use now weigh more than the average model and also have a denser string pattern (i.e. more crosses and mains). Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that the rackets Mike and Bob Bryan use are heavier than most other rackets. They have a denser string pattern, meaning there are more strings crossing and running in their rackets.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is FALSE because the statement says their rackets are light compared to others, but the passage says their rackets are heavier.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is FALSE because the passage clearly states that their rackets weigh more than the average racket, which means they are not light in comparison.
Q5 NOT GIVEN Racket modifications mainly date back to the 1970s, when the amateur German tennis player Werner Fischer started playing with the so-called spaghetti-strung racket. Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage talks about a German tennis player named Werner Fischer. It says that he played with a new type of racket called a spaghetti-strung racket and that this started in the 1970s.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'NOT GIVEN' means there is no information in the passage that tells us whether Werner Fischer designed the spaghetti-strung racket himself.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'NOT GIVEN' because the passage only says that Werner Fischer played with the spaghetti-strung racket; it does not say if he made it or designed it himself.
Q6 TRUE The former is far more common than the latter: the choice of the strings and the tension with which they are installed is something that nearly all professional players experiment with. They will continually change it depending on various factors including the court surface, climatic conditions, and game styles. Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage says that professional players often change the strings and how tight they are on their rackets. They do this because of different things like the type of court, the weather, and how they play their game.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is TRUE, which means it agrees with what the passage says.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is TRUE because the passage explains that players change their racket strings based on climatic conditions, which means the weather can affect how they adjust the strings.
Q7 TRUE For example, much of the serving power of US professional player Pete Sampras was attributed to the addition of four to five lead weights onto his rackets, and today many professionals have the weight adjusted during the manufacturing process. Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage tells us that Pete Sampras, a famous US tennis player, was thought to be able to serve very strongly because he added extra weights to his rackets. Many tennis players today also change the weight of their rackets when they are made.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'TRUE' means that the statement agrees with what is said in the passage.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'TRUE' because the passage says that the serving power of Pete Sampras was believed to come from adding weights to his rackets. This shows that people thought the change in his rackets helped him serve better.
Q8 paint They explain how they have adjusted not only racket length, but even experimented with different kinds of paint. Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage explains how Mike and Bob Bryan have made changes to their tennis rackets, including making them longer and trying out different kinds of paint.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'paint' refers to the special color or finish that can be put on the outside of the tennis racket.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'paint' because the passage specifically mentions that they experimented with different kinds of paint, which shows that it is a material used on the rackets.
Q9 topspin It created a string bed that generated so much topspin that it was quickly banned by the International Tennis Federation. Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage talks about a tennis racket that made a special string bed. This special string bed made the ball spin a lot, which caused it to be banned from use in tennis competition.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'topspin'. Topspin means the ball spins forward as it moves, which affects how it behaves when it bounces.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'topspin' because the passage mentions that the strings created so much topspin that the racket was banned. This shows how important topspin is in tennis and why changes to rackets can have a big impact.
Q10 training However, within a decade or two, racket modification became a regularity. Today it is, in many ways, an aspect of the game that is equal in significance to nutrition or training. Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage talks about how changing rackets has become very important in tennis. It says that modifying rackets is just as important as taking care of what players eat and how they train their bodies.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'training' means the exercises and activities that players do to improve their skills and fitness in tennis.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'training' because the passage compares the importance of racket changes to the importance of training. Both are seen as key parts of being a successful player.
Q11 intestines At one time, all tennis rackets were strung with natural gut made from the outer layer of sheep or cow intestines. This all changed in the early 1990s with the development of synthetic strings that were cheaper and more durable. Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage talks about how tennis rackets used to be made with natural gut. This gut came from the outer layer of the intestines of sheep or cows. These types of strings were used a long time ago, but in the early 1990s, new synthetic strings were made that were cheaper and lasted longer.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'intestines' refers to the inside parts of sheep or cows. In this case, it means the material that used to be used for the strings of tennis rackets.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'intestines' because the passage specifically says that natural strings were made from the outer layer of sheep or cow intestines.
Q12 weights For example, much of the serving power of US professional player Pete Sampras was attributed to the addition of four to five lead weights onto his rackets, and today many professionals have the weight adjusted during the manufacturing process. Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage talks about a famous tennis player named Pete Sampras. It says that part of his power when serving the ball comes from adding extra small pieces of metal called lead weights to his tennis rackets. Many professional players these days also make changes to the weight of their rackets when they are made.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is 'weights', which means the extra metal pieces added to the rackets to make them heavier.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'weights' because the excerpt specifically describes how adding lead weights to Pete Sampras's rackets helped him serve stronger. It shows that the weight of the racket is important for players.
Q13 grips The professional Portuguese player Gonçalo Oliveira replaced the original grips of his rackets with something thinner because they had previously felt uncomfortable to hold. Excerpt/Passage Explanation:
The passage talks about a tennis player named Goncalo Oliveira. He changed the grips on his tennis rackets to something thinner because the original grips were uncomfortable for him to hold.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'grips' refers to the part of the racket that a player holds onto, which can affect how comfortable and easy it is to use the racket.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 'grips' because the excerpt specifically mentions that Oliveira replaced the original grips with thinner ones to make it more comfortable for him to hold the rackets.
