Oxford Practice Grammar Advanced Chapter 4 – Negative Questions and Question Tags

Choose or type the answer to each question, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. Which of these is a negative question?
    A negative question uses a negative form such as didn't before the subject, after the wh-word in a wh-question. The question about the sink is a positive question with a negative infinitive, and the first sentence is a negative statement, not a question.
  2. Which sentence has a question tag?
    A question tag is a short question added after a statement, such as 'isn't it?' after a positive statement. 'Isn't the museum free on Sundays?' is a negative question, not a statement with a tag.
  3. Most of the time there ___ any complaints about the hotel's breakfast, so the manager was surprised by the bad review.
    Complaints is plural, so the negative verb before any is aren't. Isn't is singular, and no or not alone can't replace the verb.
  4. Eggs slide right out of a ___ frying pan, so you need very little oil to cook them.
    A nonstick pan has a surface that food doesn't stick to. Nonstop means without stopping, which doesn't describe a pan.
  5. If a tenant ___ living in the apartment full-time, the landlord may ask for a higher deposit.
    Living is an -ing form, so it needs a form of be, and a tenant is singular: isn't living. Doesn't and won't are followed by the base form, and aren't is plural.
  6. Because Hannah lives in Canada but owns a small apartment in Spain, she pays Spanish taxes as a ___.
    A nonresident is someone who doesn't live permanently in a country. Nonevent and nondescript don't describe a person's status in a country.
  7. Everyone expected the new manager to be strict, but she's ___, so the team relaxed quickly.
    In a reduced negative after be, we use not, not no: she's not (= she isn't strict). No needs a noun after it, and isn't and doesn't can't follow she's.
  8. The new shopping mall was supposed to attract huge crowds on opening day, but only a handful of people showed up, so the whole thing was a ___.
    A nonevent is something that was expected to be big or exciting but turned out to be disappointing. Nondescript is an adjective meaning ordinary, so it can't end the sentence after 'a' like this.
  9. A ___ deposit can't be returned to you, even if you cancel your reservation.
    Nonrefundable means the money won't be given back. Nondescript means ordinary-looking, which doesn't describe money paid in advance.
  10. This coupon expires at the end of the month, ___ it?
    The statement is positive and in the present simple (expires), so the tag is negative with does: doesn't it? Isn't would only match a statement with is.
  11. The thieves drove off in a ___ gray van, so none of the witnesses could describe it well.
    Nondescript means having no special or unusual features, which is why the van was hard to describe. Nonrefundable and nonresident don't describe how a vehicle looks.
  12. The town has ___ interesting buildings, but the hiking trails nearby are wonderful.
    We use no before a noun (with or without an adjective) to make a negative with a positive verb: has no interesting buildings. Not can't be used directly before a noun like this.
  13. We took a ___ flight from Chicago to Tokyo, so we didn't have to change planes.
    A nonstop flight goes straight to its destination without landing anywhere on the way. Nonstick describes pans, not flights.
  14. If you leave your passport at home, you ___ be allowed to board the plane.
    Won't (will not) is followed by the base form be and gives the result of the if clause. Doesn't and aren't can't come before be, and not needs an auxiliary or modal.
  15. The bus can't leave until every child has a seat belt on. ___ wearing a seat belt?
    Who is the subject and refers to a person, so the verb follows it directly: Who isn't wearing a seat belt? Doesn't can't come before an -ing form, why needs a subject after isn't, and what is not used for people.
  16. The concert is free and you love jazz. ___ you come with us tonight?
    Why don't you + base form is used to make a suggestion or invitation. Aren't can't be followed by the base form come.
  17. The car keys weren't on the hook this morning. ___ you say you had left them?
    The question asks about a place, and say is a base form, so it needs did: Where did you say you had left them? Were can't be followed by say, and what doesn't work because them is already the object.
  18. Your cousins both work at the airport. ___ they do there exactly?
    The question asks about their job, and the main verb do is a base form, so we need What + the auxiliary do. Where doesn't work because there already gives the place, and are can't come before a base form.
  19. Ana told me she and her husband stayed in Kyoto on their honeymoon. ___ they there, in spring or in fall?
    The answer is a time (in spring or in fall), and there is no main verb after they, so we need when + were. Did would need a base-form verb after the subject.
  20. I found these sunglasses on the back seat of my car after the party. ___ sunglasses are they?
    Whose is used with a noun to ask who something belongs to. Who can't be followed directly by a noun like umbrellas.
  21. Those muddy boots by the front door aren't mine. Whose boots ___ they?
    Boots is plural and the question is about now, so the verb is are. Do and did can't be used because there is no other verb, and isn't is singular.
  22. Correct the words in square brackets: Before the trip, most of the group [didn't ever flown] on a plane, so they were quite nervous.
    Accepted answers: hadn't ever flown / had not ever flown / had never flown
    Flown is a past participle, so it needs had, not did. The past perfect is right for experience up to a time in the past (before the trip).
  23. Correct the words in square brackets: The instructions were all in Japanese, so we [didn't really could understand] them.
    Accepted answers: really couldn't understand / couldn't really understand / really could not understand / could not really understand
    Negative forms of do are not used with modals; we add n't or not to the modal itself: couldn't understand.
  24. Correct the words in square brackets: [What you think] causes most of the traffic jams in this city?
    Answer: What do you think
    A question with an ordinary verb like think needs do before the subject: What do you think causes ...?
  25. Correct the words in square brackets: Honestly, [I not mind] which restaurant we choose tonight.
    Accepted answers: I don't mind / I do not mind
    Ordinary verbs like mind form the negative with do + not/n't before the base form: I don't mind.
  26. Correct the words in square brackets: [Why we need] a password for the guest Wi-Fi?
    Answer: Why do we need
    Questions with ordinary verbs need do before the subject: Why do we need ...?
  27. Correct the words in square brackets: [They not use] the old printer anymore, so we can throw it away.
    Accepted answers: They don't use / They do not use
    With an ordinary verb like use, the negative is formed with do + not/n't before the base form: They don't use.
  28. Correct the words in square brackets: We don't have to bring our own towels, [don't we]?
    Answer: do we
    A negative statement takes a positive tag: We don't have to ..., do we?
  29. Correct the words in square brackets: My roommate thinks frozen vegetables [not healthy], but many of them keep their vitamins well.
    Accepted answers: aren't healthy / are not healthy
    Healthy is an adjective, so the sentence needs the verb be, with not after it: aren't healthy.
  30. Correct the words in square brackets: The concert tickets cost a lot, but [we not sorry] that we bought them.
    Accepted answers: we're not sorry / we aren't sorry / we are not sorry
    An adjective like sorry needs the verb be, and not goes after be: we're not sorry or we aren't sorry.
  31. Correct the words in square brackets: Nobody here has seen my blue scarf, [has he]?
    Answer: have they
    After nobody or no one, the tag is positive and uses they; because the sentence uses has seen, the tag is have they?
  32. Correct the words in square brackets: I sent her three messages, but she [didn't replied].
    Accepted answers: didn't reply / did not reply
    After didn't, the verb is in the base form: didn't reply, not didn't replied.
  33. Correct the words in square brackets: Because of the building's noise rules, the students [could play not] music after ten o'clock.
    Accepted answers: couldn't play / could not play
    Not goes directly after the modal, before the main verb: couldn't play.
  34. Correct the words in square brackets: [Why do some phones can't connect] to the hotel Wi-Fi?
    Accepted answers: Why can't some phones connect / Why can some phones not connect
    Do is not used with modals; the modal can't itself goes before the subject: Why can't some phones connect ...?
  35. Correct the words in square brackets: [Does this jacket] warm enough for a winter in Chicago?
    Answer: Is this jacket
    Warm is an adjective and there is no other verb, so the question uses be, not do, before the subject: Is this jacket warm enough ...?
  36. Correct the words in square brackets: [When will be repaired the bridge]?
    Answer: When will the bridge be repaired
    In a passive question, the modal goes before the subject and be + past participle comes after it: When will the bridge be repaired?
  37. Correct the words in square brackets: Who [is look after] your dog when you travel for work?
    Accepted answers: looks after / will look after
    When who is the subject, the verb follows it directly without be or do, and a regular arrangement takes the present simple: Who looks after ...?
