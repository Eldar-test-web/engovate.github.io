Understanding and Using English Grammar Chart 20-4 – Unreal (Contrary To Fact) In The Past

Choose or type the answer to each question, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. If my brother were in town, he would drive us to the airport ___.
    "Were" with "would drive" describes an unreal situation in the present, so the time is now. For yesterday, the sentence would need "had been" and "would have driven."
  2. If the plumber had been free, he would have fixed our leaking sink ___.
    "Had been" with "would have fixed" describes an unreal situation in the past, so the time is yesterday. "Now" would need "were" and "would fix."
  3. If Priya had ___ (go) to bed earlier, she wouldn't have felt so sleepy during her exam.
    Answer: gone
    A past unreal if-clause uses the past perfect: had + past participle. The past participle of "go" is "gone."
  4. If Diego had gone to the train station earlier, he ___
    Arriving at the station earlier would logically have given him a seat, so this past result fits. The other endings have nothing to do with going to the station.
  5. If the airline hadn't ___ (lose) my suitcase, I wouldn't have had to buy new clothes for the conference.
    Answer: lost
    After "hadn't" the past participle is needed to form the past perfect. The past participle of "lose" is "lost."
  6. If Hana hadn't lost her phone, she ___
    Without her phone Hana could not reply, so the logical past result is that she would have answered the messages. "Their seats" does not match "she," and a sunburn has nothing to do with a phone.
  7. If I hadn't ___ (stay) at the office so late, I wouldn't have missed my son's school concert.
    Answer: stayed
    The past perfect is hadn't + past participle. "Stay" is regular, so the past participle is "stayed."
  8. If Leo hadn't stayed in the sun so long, he ___
    Too much time in the sun causes a sunburn, so this is the logical past result. The other endings have no link to staying in the sun, and "their" does not match "he."
  9. If more volunteers had ___ (help) at the beach cleanup, we would have collected twice as much trash.
    Answer: helped
    The past perfect in a past unreal if-clause is had + past participle. "Help" is regular, so it becomes "helped."
  10. If my roommate had helped me clean, I ___
    Help with cleaning would logically have made the work faster, so not staying up so late is the result. The other endings do not follow from getting help with cleaning.
  11. If the hotel room had ___ (be) cleaner, we would have stayed another night.
    Answer: been
    After "had" the past participle is needed. The past participle of "be" is "been."
  12. If the movie had been shorter, the children ___
    A long movie can make children fall asleep, so a shorter one would logically have kept them awake. The other endings have nothing to do with the length of a movie.
  13. If the hikers had ___ (have) a map, they wouldn't have gotten lost in the forest.
    Answer: had
    The past perfect of "have" is "had had": the first "had" is the helping verb and the second is the past participle.
  14. If we had had an umbrella, we ___
    An umbrella keeps you dry, so the logical past result is not getting soaked. "Their" does not match "we," and an umbrella has nothing to do with finishing before guests arrive.
  15. If the printer ___ (be, not) broken, I would have handed in my essay on time.
    Accepted answers: hadn't been / had not been
    A past unreal condition uses the past perfect. The printer was broken, so the if-clause is "hadn't been."
  16. If Ellie had checked the weather forecast, she ___ (get, not) caught in the storm.
    Accepted answers: wouldn't have gotten / would not have gotten / wouldn't have got / would not have got
    The result of a past unreal condition is would (not) have + past participle. In US English the past participle of "get" is "gotten."
  17. If the workers ___ (follow) the safety rules, nobody would have been hurt.
    Answer: had followed
    The result clause has "would have been," so this is a past unreal condition. The if-clause needs the past perfect: "had followed."
  18. If the city had maintained the bridge properly, it ___ (spend, not) millions on emergency repairs last spring.
    Accepted answers: wouldn't have spent / would not have spent
    After a past perfect if-clause, the result uses would (not) have + past participle. The past participle of "spend" is "spent."
  19. If the road ___ (be, not) so icy, the truck wouldn't have slid into the ditch.
    Accepted answers: hadn't been / had not been
    The road was icy in the past, so the unreal if-clause uses the past perfect: "hadn't been."
  20. If Mr. Kim had known about the late fee, he ___ (pay) the bill on time.
    Accepted answers: would have paid / would've paid / 'd have paid / could have paid / could've paid
    The if-clause is past perfect, so the result is would have + past participle. The past participle of "pay" is "paid."
  21. If we ___ (rent) bikes, we could have seen more of the city.
    Accepted answers: had rented / 'd rented
    "Could have seen" is a past result, so the if-clause needs the past perfect: "had rented."
  22. If the museum had put up clearer signs, the visitors ___ (find) the exit by now.
    Accepted answers: would have found / would've found / could have found / could've found
    The result of a past unreal condition is would have + past participle, even with "by now." The past participle of "find" is "found."
  23. If the students ___ (listen) more carefully, they would have known when the assignment was due.
    Answer: had listened
    The students did not listen carefully in the past, so the unreal if-clause uses the past perfect: "had listened."
  24. If the coach had checked the schedule, the team ___ (drive, not) three hours to an empty field.
    Accepted answers: wouldn't have driven / would not have driven
    The result of a past unreal condition is would (not) have + past participle. The past participle of "drive" is "driven."
  25. If you ___ (take) the medicine the way the doctor told you, you would have felt better by the weekend.
    Accepted answers: had taken / 'd taken
    "Would have felt" shows a past unreal result, so the if-clause uses the past perfect. The past participle of "take" is "taken."
  26. If the concert had started on time, we ___ (be) home before midnight.
    Accepted answers: would have been / would've been / 'd have been / could have been / could've been
    After a past perfect if-clause, the result is would have + past participle: "would have been."
  27. "If Rosa had entered the photo contest, she would have won a prize." Did Rosa enter the photo contest?
    A past perfect if-clause describes something that did not happen. Rosa did not enter the contest.
  28. "If Rosa had entered the photo contest, she would have won a prize." Did Rosa win a prize?
    "Would have won" is the imagined result of something that did not happen, so she did not win a prize.
  29. "If Rosa had entered the photo contest, she would have won a prize." Is the meaning present or past?
    Had + past participle in the if-clause and would have + past participle in the result refer to past time.
  30. "If Jamal had saved his receipt, he could have returned the shoes." Did Jamal save his receipt?
    "Had saved" in an unreal if-clause shows the opposite of the facts: Jamal did not save his receipt.
  31. "If Jamal had saved his receipt, he could have returned the shoes." Did Jamal return the shoes?
    "Could have returned" is a possibility that did not happen, so he did not return the shoes.
  32. "If Jamal had saved his receipt, he could have returned the shoes." Is the meaning present or past?
    The past perfect in the if-clause and "could have" + past participle in the result refer to the past.
  33. "If my aunt had a passport, she would join us on the cruise." Does my aunt have a passport?
    The simple past "had" in an unreal if-clause shows the opposite of the present facts: she does not have a passport.
  34. "If my aunt had a passport, she would join us on the cruise." Is she going to join us on the cruise?
    "Would join" is only an imagined result. Without a passport she is not going to join the cruise.
  35. "If my aunt had a passport, she would join us on the cruise." Is the meaning present or past?
    Here "had" is the simple past of the main verb "have," not the past perfect. With "would join" it describes an unreal present or future situation.
  36. "If Tomas knew the answer, he would tell us." Does Tomas know the answer?
    The simple past "knew" in an unreal if-clause means the opposite is true now: Tomas does not know the answer.
  37. "If Tomas knew the answer, he would tell us." Is Tomas going to tell us the answer?
    "Would tell" is the imagined result of an unreal condition, so he is not going to tell us.
  38. "If Tomas knew the answer, he would tell us." Is the meaning present or past?
    A simple past if-clause with would + base verb describes an unreal present situation, not the past.
  39. "If Nadia didn't speak French, she would need a translator in Paris." Does Nadia need a translator in Paris?
    "Would need" is the imagined result, and the real situation is the opposite: she does not need a translator.
  40. "If Nadia didn't speak French, she would need a translator in Paris." Does Nadia speak French?
    A negative unreal if-clause means the real situation is positive: Nadia does speak French.
  41. "If Nadia didn't speak French, she would need a translator in Paris." Is the meaning present or past?
    The simple past "didn't speak" with "would need" describes an unreal present situation.
  42. "The birthday cake would have turned out perfectly last night if the oven hadn't broken halfway through." Did the oven break?
    A negative past perfect if-clause means the real past event was positive: the oven did break.
  43. "The birthday cake would have turned out perfectly last night if the oven hadn't broken halfway through." Did the cake turn out perfectly?
    "Would have turned out" is an imagined past result, so in reality the cake did not turn out perfectly.
  44. "The birthday cake would have turned out perfectly last night if the oven hadn't broken halfway through." Is the meaning present or past?
    Would have + past participle and the past perfect "hadn't broken" refer to past time, here last night.
  45. "If we had a bigger car, we would bring the dog on our trip." Do we have a bigger car?
    The simple past "had" in an unreal if-clause means the opposite is true now: we do not have a bigger car.
  46. "If we had a bigger car, we would bring the dog on our trip." Will we bring the dog on our trip?
    "Would bring" is only an imagined result, so we will not bring the dog.
  47. "If we had a bigger car, we would bring the dog on our trip." Is the meaning present or past?
    "Had" is the simple past of the main verb "have" here, and with "would bring" it describes an unreal present or future situation.
  48. "If the museum is open on Monday, we'll take the kids there." Is the condition real or unreal?
    The if-clause uses the simple present and the result uses "will," so the condition is real: it may really happen.
  49. "If the museum is open on Monday, we'll take the kids there." Does the sentence refer to present/future time or past time?
    A real condition with the simple present and "will" talks about the present or future, here next Monday.
  50. "If the museum were open on Monday, we would take the kids there." Is the condition real or unreal?
    "Were" in the if-clause with "would" in the result shows an unreal condition: the museum is not open on Monday.
  51. "If the museum were open on Monday, we would take the kids there." Does the sentence refer to present/future time or past time?
    "Were" with would + base verb describes an unreal present or future situation. It does not refer to the past.
  52. "If the museum had been open on Monday, we would have taken the kids there." Is the condition real or unreal?
    "Had been" with "would have taken" shows something that did not happen, so the condition is unreal.
  53. "If the museum had been open on Monday, we would have taken the kids there." Does the sentence refer to present/future time or past time?
    Had + past participle and would have + past participle refer to past time.
  54. "If Kevin had a car, he would drive to work." Is the condition real or unreal?
    The simple past "had" with "would drive" shows an unreal condition: Kevin does not have a car.
  55. "If Kevin had a car, he would drive to work." Does the sentence refer to present/future time or past time?
    In an unreal if-clause the simple past refers to now, not to the past. "Would drive" also points to the present or future.
  56. "If Kevin had had a car, he would have driven to work." Is the condition real or unreal?
    "Had had" is the past perfect, which shows a past situation that was not true. The condition is unreal.
  57. "If Kevin had had a car, he would have driven to work." Does the sentence refer to present/future time or past time?
    The past perfect "had had" and "would have driven" refer to past time.
  58. "If the twins don't have to take the bus, they can sleep a little later." Is the condition real or unreal?
    The simple present "don't have to" with "can" describes a real possibility, so the condition is real.
  59. "If the twins don't have to take the bus, they can sleep a little later." Does the sentence refer to present/future time or past time?
    A real condition in the simple present with "can" talks about the present or future.
  60. "If the twins hadn't had to take the bus, they could have slept a little later." Is the condition real or unreal?
    "Hadn't had to" with "could have slept" shows the opposite of what happened: the twins did have to take the bus.
  61. "If the twins hadn't had to take the bus, they could have slept a little later." Does the sentence refer to present/future time or past time?
    The past perfect in the if-clause and "could have" + past participle refer to past time.
  62. "If the twins didn't have to take the bus, they could sleep a little later." Is the condition real or unreal?
    The simple past "didn't have to" with "could sleep" shows an unreal condition: in reality the twins do have to take the bus.
  63. "If the twins didn't have to take the bus, they could sleep a little later." Does the sentence refer to present/future time or past time?
    The simple past in an unreal if-clause with could + base verb refers to now or the future, not the past.
  64. If I have a day off, ___
    A simple present if-clause (have) is a real condition, so the result uses "will." "Would" belongs with an unreal if-clause.
  65. If I had a day off, ___
    The simple past "had" here is an unreal present condition, so the result is would + base verb. "Would have visited" needs "had had."
  66. If I had had a day off, ___
    "Had had" is the past perfect, an unreal past condition, so the result is would have + past participle.
  67. If we win the final, ___
    "Win" is the simple present, so this is a real future possibility and the result uses "will."
  68. If we won the final, ___
    The simple past "won" makes an unreal present or future condition, so the result is would + base verb, not "will."
  69. If we had won the final, ___
    "Had won" is the past perfect, an unreal past condition, so the result is would have + past participle.
  70. If I had felt sick, ___
    "Had felt" is the past perfect, so the condition is unreal in the past and the result is would have + past participle.
  71. If I feel sick, ___
    "Feel" is the simple present, so this is a real condition and the result uses "will."
  72. If I felt sick, ___
    The simple past "felt" in an unreal if-clause matches would + base verb. "Will" needs a present if-clause, and "would have stayed" needs "had felt."
  73. The kids would swim in the lake ___
    "Would swim" is an unreal present or future result, so the if-clause uses the simple past: "were." "Is" belongs with "will," and "had been" with "would have swum."
  74. The kids would have swum in the lake ___
    "Would have swum" is an unreal past result, so the if-clause needs the past perfect: "had been."
  75. The kids will swim in the lake ___
    "Will swim" is a real future result, so the if-clause uses the simple present: "is."
  76. We will repaint the fence ___
    A result with "will" is a real condition, so the if-clause uses the simple present: "have."
  77. We would have repainted the fence ___
    "Would have repainted" is a past unreal result, so the if-clause needs the past perfect "had had."
  78. We would repaint the fence ___
    "Would repaint" is an unreal present or future result, so the if-clause uses the simple past "had."
  79. If I ___ (have) time after my shift, I will stop by the bakery.
    Answer: have
    The result uses "will," so this is a real future condition and the if-clause takes the simple present.
  80. If you ___ (have) a garden, you would grow your own tomatoes.
    Answer: had
    "Would grow" shows an unreal present situation, so the if-clause uses the simple past: "had."
  81. If they ___ (have) better equipment, they would have reached the top of the mountain.
    Accepted answers: had had / 'd had
    "Would have reached" is an unreal past result, so the if-clause needs the past perfect of "have": "had had."
  82. If today were a holiday, our whole family ___ (go) to the beach.
    Accepted answers: would go / could go
    Today is not a holiday, so "were" makes an unreal present condition and the result is would + base verb.
  83. If the library had been open last Sunday, I ___ (go) there to study.
    Accepted answers: would have gone / would've gone / 'd have gone / could have gone / could've gone
    "Had been" with "last Sunday" is an unreal past condition, so the result is would have + past participle.
  84. If the snow stops by tomorrow morning, the kids ___ (go) sledding.
    Accepted answers: will go / are going to go
    "Stops" is the simple present, so this is a real future condition and the result uses "will."
  85. The pharmacy was closed last night. If it ___ (be) open, I would have bought some cough syrup.
    Accepted answers: had been / 'd been
    The pharmacy was closed in the past, so the unreal if-clause uses the past perfect: "had been."
  86. My cousins didn't come to the city last summer. If they had come, I ___ (visit) the science museum with them.
    Accepted answers: would have visited / would've visited / 'd have visited / could have visited / could've visited
    The if-clause is past perfect and the visit did not happen, so the result is would have + past participle.
  87. If the sky ___ (be) clear tomorrow night, we'll be able to see the meteor shower.
    Answer: is
    This is a real future possibility with "will" in the result, so the if-clause uses the simple present, not "will be."
  88. If Grandma feels well enough on Sunday, we ___ (visit) her at the nursing home.
    Accepted answers: will visit / 'll visit / are going to visit / 're going to visit
    "Feels" is the simple present in a real future condition, so the result uses "will."
  89. The manager isn't in the building right now. If she ___ (be) here, she would sign the form for you.
    Answer: were
    This is an unreal present condition, so the if-clause uses "were." In standard English "were" is used with all subjects in this pattern.
  90. The art gallery is closed today. If it were open, I ___ (visit) it with my students.
    Accepted answers: would visit / 'd visit / could visit
    "Were" makes an unreal present condition, so the result is would + base verb.
  91. Your tooth has been hurting for a week? If I ___ (be) you, I'd see a dentist today.
    Answer: were
    To give advice, English uses the unreal condition "If I were you." "Were" is used even with "I."
  92. If I were in your position, I ___ (tell) the landlord about the leak right away.
    Accepted answers: would tell / 'd tell
    "If I were in your position" is an unreal present condition, so the result is would + base verb.
  93. If we ___ (have) a flashlight during the blackout last night, we wouldn't have tripped over the furniture.
    Accepted answers: had had / 'd had
    The situation is in the past and did not happen, so the if-clause needs the past perfect of "have": "had had."
  94. If the driver had seen the warning sign, he ___ (take) the other road.
    Accepted answers: would have taken / would've taken / 'd have taken / could have taken / could've taken
    After a past perfect if-clause, the unreal past result is would have + past participle. The past participle of "take" is "taken."
  95. I'm not sure how much paint is left. If I ___ (have) any extra, I'll give it to you.
    Answer: have
    The speaker may really have extra paint, so this is a real condition: simple present in the if-clause and "will" in the result.
  96. I might have an extra ticket for Saturday's concert. If I do, I ___ (give) it to you.
    Accepted answers: will give / 'll give / am going to give / 'm going to give
    Having an extra ticket is a real possibility, so the result of this real condition uses "will."
  97. Our town has only one gas station. If it ___ (have) two, people wouldn't have to wait so long to fill up.
    Answer: had
    The town really has one station, so imagining two is an unreal present condition with the simple past: "had."
  98. Nora lives far from her job. If she lived closer, she ___ (have to, not) get up at five every morning.
    Accepted answers: wouldn't have to / would not have to
    "Lived" makes an unreal present condition, so the result is would + base verb: "wouldn't have to."
  99. I ___ (come) to your graduation if you had invited me.
    Accepted answers: would have come / would've come / 'd have come / could have come / could've come
    "Had invited" is an unreal past condition, so the result is would have + past participle. The past participle of "come" is "come."
  100. We would have stayed late and ___ (help) you set up the chairs if we had known.
    Accepted answers: helped / would have helped / would've helped
    The second verb continues "would have stayed," so it is a past participle. "Would have" can be left out after "and."
  101. The neighbors would have watered our plants while we were away if we ___ (tell) them about the trip.
    Accepted answers: had told / 'd told
    "Would have watered" is an unreal past result, so the if-clause needs the past perfect: "had told."
  102. I'm sure the firefighters ___ (come) sooner if someone had reported the smoke.
    Accepted answers: would have come / would've come / could have come / could've come
    The if-clause is past perfect, so the unreal past result is would have + past participle: "would have come."
  103. Your sister would have picked you up from the airport if you ___ (call) her.
    Accepted answers: had called / 'd called
    "Would have picked up" is an unreal past result, so the if-clause needs the past perfect: "had called."
  104. The river is very low. If it ___ (rain, not) this week, the boats won't be able to sail.
    Accepted answers: doesn't rain / does not rain
    This is a real future possibility with "won't" in the result, so the if-clause uses the simple present, not "won't rain."
  105. If you don't feed the goldfish while I'm away, it ___ (die).
    Accepted answers: will die / 'll die / is going to die / 's going to die
    "Don't feed" is the simple present in a real condition, so the result uses "will."
  106. If the bees ___ (die), many fruit trees won't produce any fruit.
    Answer: die
    The result uses "won't," so this is a real future condition and the if-clause takes the simple present.
  107. If the factory closes, hundreds of workers ___ (go) without a paycheck.
    Accepted answers: will go / are going to go
    "Closes" is the simple present in a real future condition, so the result uses "will."
  108. If I ___ (realize) the paint was still wet, I wouldn't have leaned against the wall.
    Accepted answers: had realized / 'd realized / had realised / 'd realised
    "Wouldn't have leaned" is an unreal past result, so the if-clause needs the past perfect: "had realized."
  109. If Jenna had read the instructions, she ___ (make, not) so many mistakes on the form.
    Accepted answers: wouldn't have made / would not have made
    After a past perfect if-clause, the unreal past result is would (not) have + past participle.
  110. What ___ (we, use) instead of plastic bags if stores stopped giving them out?
    Answer: would we use
    "Stopped" makes an unreal present condition, so the result is would + base verb. In a question, "would" comes before the subject.
  111. How would people tell the time if they ___ (have, not) clocks or phones?
    Accepted answers: didn't have / did not have
    "Would tell" shows an unreal present situation, so the if-clause uses the simple past: "didn't have."
  112. Sara doesn't own a bike, but if she ___, she would ride it to work.
    Answer: did
    The full clause would be "if she owned a bike." A simple past main verb is shortened to the helping verb "did."
  113. The baby is sick today, but if she ___, we would take her to the park.
    Accepted answers: weren't / were not
    The full clause would be "if she weren't sick." With "be," the short form keeps "were" and the negative.
  114. Martin didn't apply for the scholarship, but if he ___, he probably would have gotten it.
    Answer: had
    The full clause would be "if he had applied." A past perfect if-clause is shortened to "had."
  115. We don't have a dishwasher, but if we ___, we'd save a lot of time.
    Answer: did
    The full clause would be "if we had a dishwasher," an unreal present with the main verb "have." It is shortened with "did."
  116. Our office printer is slow, but if it ___, we wouldn't waste so much time waiting for copies.
    Accepted answers: weren't / were not
    The full clause would be "if it weren't slow." The printer really is slow, so the unreal clause is negative: "weren't."
  117. Kenji didn't join us for dinner, but if he ___, he would have loved the food.
    Answer: had
    The full clause would be "if he had joined us," a past perfect condition, so it is shortened to "had."
  118. Diamonds are extremely hard. If they ___, they wouldn't be used in cutting tools.
    Accepted answers: weren't / were not
    The full clause would be "if they weren't extremely hard." The fact is positive, so the unreal clause is negative.
  119. My roommate isn't very tidy, but if she ___, our apartment would look much nicer.
    Answer: were
    The full clause would be "if she were very tidy." The fact is negative, so the unreal clause is positive: "were."
  120. Ana didn't wear sunscreen, but if she ___, her shoulders wouldn't have burned.
    Answer: had
    The full clause would be "if she had worn sunscreen," a past perfect condition, so it is shortened to "had."
  121. Our cat always uses the litter box. If she ___, the apartment would smell terrible.
    Accepted answers: didn't / did not
    The full clause would be "if she didn't use the litter box." The fact is positive, so the unreal clause is negative with "didn't."
  122. Luis set two alarms last night. If he ___, he would have overslept.
    Accepted answers: hadn't / had not
    The full clause would be "if he hadn't set two alarms," a negative past perfect condition, so it is shortened to "hadn't."
