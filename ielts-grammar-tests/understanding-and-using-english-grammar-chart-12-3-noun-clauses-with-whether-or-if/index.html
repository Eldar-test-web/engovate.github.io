Understanding and Using English Grammar Chart 12-3 – Noun Clauses With Whether or If

Choose or type the answer to each question, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. The question "Is the pool open today?" has become a noun clause. Which words are the noun clause in "Ask the front desk if the pool is open today."?
    When a yes/no question becomes a noun clause, if is added at the start, and if is part of the clause: if the pool is open today.
  2. The question "Is the pool open today?" has become a noun clause. Which words are the noun clause in "I can't remember whether the pool is open today."?
    Whether can introduce the noun clause instead of if, and it is part of the clause: whether the pool is open today.
  3. The question "Is the pool open today?" has become a noun clause. Which words are the noun clause in "Let's find out whether or not the pool is open today."?
    Or not can come right after whether. The whole noun clause is whether or not the pool is open today.
  4. The question "Is the pool open today?" has become a noun clause. Which words are the noun clause in "Do you know whether the pool is open today or not?"?
    Or not can also come at the end of the clause. The noun clause runs from whether to or not.
  5. The question "Is the pool open today?" has become a noun clause. Which words are the noun clause in "I'll check if the pool is open today or not."?
    If can also be used with or not at the end. The noun clause runs from if to or not.
  6. Is the museum open on Mondays? → Let me know if ___ open on Mondays.
    Accepted answers: the museum is / the museum's
    A yes/no question becomes a noun clause with if, and the clause uses statement word order. The subject (the museum) comes before is.
  7. Will they deliver the sofa on Friday? → Can you find out if ___ the sofa on Friday?
    Accepted answers: they will deliver / they'll deliver
    In a noun clause the subject comes before the helping verb, as in a statement: they will deliver, not will they deliver.
  8. Does this bus stop at the airport? → Ask the driver if ___ at the airport.
    Answer: this bus stops
    Does is dropped in a noun clause, so the verb itself takes the -s ending: this bus stops.
  9. Is the baby still sleeping? → Let me know if ___ still sleeping.
    Accepted answers: the baby is / the baby's
    The noun clause uses statement word order, so the subject comes before is: the baby is still sleeping.
  10. Do we have enough eggs for the cake? → Can you check if ___ enough eggs for the cake?
    Answer: we have
    Do is dropped in a noun clause, and the clause uses statement word order: we have enough eggs.
  11. Are you going to join us for dinner? → Let me know if ___ join us for dinner.
    Accepted answers: you are going to / you're going to
    In the noun clause the subject comes before are: you are going to join, not are you going to join.
  12. Did the kids eat lunch? → Please check whether ___ lunch.
    Answer: the kids ate
    Did is dropped in a noun clause, so the main verb shows the past itself: the kids ate.
  13. Is it raining again? → Please check whether ___ raining again.
    Accepted answers: it is / it's
    After whether, the clause uses statement word order: it is raining, not is it raining.
  14. Is there a pharmacy near the hotel? → Could you find out whether ___ a pharmacy near the hotel?
    Accepted answers: there is / there's
    In a noun clause, there comes before is, as in a statement: whether there is a pharmacy.
  15. Is my passport still valid? → Please check whether ___ still valid.
    Accepted answers: my passport is / my passport's
    The noun clause uses statement word order, so the subject comes before is: my passport is still valid.
  16. Did the plane take off on time? → I need to know whether ___ off on time.
    Answer: the plane took
    Did is dropped in a noun clause, so the verb changes to its past form: the plane took off.
  17. Are we going to stop for gas on the way? → Please ask Dad whether ___ for gas on the way.
    Accepted answers: we are going to stop / we're going to stop
    In the noun clause the subject comes before are, as in a statement: we are going to stop.
  18. Where is the remote control? → I wonder ___.
    Accepted answers: where the remote control is / where the remote is / where it is
    In a noun clause the subject comes before is: where the remote control is, not where is the remote control.
  19. When is the plumber coming? → I wonder ___.
    Accepted answers: when the plumber is coming / when the plumber's coming
    In the noun clause the subject (the plumber) comes before the helping verb is: when the plumber is coming.
  20. Is the train running late? → I wonder ___ running late.
    Accepted answers: if the train is / whether the train is / whether or not the train is / if the train's / whether the train's
    A yes/no question becomes a noun clause with if or whether, and the subject comes before is: if the train is running late.
  21. How long should we boil the eggs? → I wonder ___.
    Accepted answers: how long we should boil the eggs / how long we should boil them
    In the noun clause the subject we comes before the modal should: how long we should boil the eggs.
  22. Did anyone feed the cat? → I wonder ___ the cat.
    Accepted answers: if anyone fed / whether anyone fed / whether or not anyone fed
    Did is dropped in the noun clause, so the verb itself is past: if anyone fed the cat.
  23. Did she oversleep? → I wonder ___.
    Accepted answers: if she overslept / whether she overslept / whether or not she overslept / if she overslept or not / whether she overslept or not
    A yes/no question becomes a noun clause with if or whether. Did is dropped, so the verb is past: if she overslept.
  24. What makes the sky blue? → I wonder ___.
    Answer: what makes the sky blue
    What is the subject of the question, so the word order stays the same in the noun clause: what makes the sky blue.
  25. When was the telephone invented? → I wonder ___.
    Accepted answers: when the telephone was invented / when it was invented
    In the noun clause the subject (the telephone) comes before the passive verb was invented.
  26. Why did the Titanic sink? → I wonder ___.
    Accepted answers: why the Titanic sank / why it sank
    Did is not used in a noun clause, so the verb changes to its past form: why the Titanic sank.
  27. Is there a cure for the common cold? → I wonder ___ a cure for the common cold.
    Accepted answers: if there is / whether there is / whether or not there is / if there's / whether there's
    A yes/no question becomes a noun clause with if or whether, and there comes before is: if there is a cure.
  28. How did humans learn to make fire? → I wonder ___ to make fire.
    Accepted answers: how humans learned / how they learned / how humans learnt / how they learnt
    Did is dropped in the noun clause, so learn becomes past: how humans learned to make fire.
  29. Will cars drive themselves someday? → I wonder ___ themselves someday.
    Accepted answers: if cars will drive / whether cars will drive / whether or not cars will drive
    A yes/no question becomes a noun clause with if or whether, and the subject comes before will: if cars will drive themselves.
  30. At a hotel: Where is the nearest subway station? → Let's ask ___.
    Accepted answers: where the nearest subway station is / where the subway station is
    In the noun clause the subject (the nearest subway station) comes before is, so is goes at the end.
  31. At a hotel: How much does a day pass cost? → Let's ask ___.
    Accepted answers: how much a day pass costs / how much the day pass costs
    Does is dropped in the noun clause, so cost takes -s after the singular subject: how much a day pass costs.
  32. At a hotel: Is there a pool here? → Let's ask ___ a pool here.
    Accepted answers: if there is / whether there is / whether or not there is / if there's / whether there's
    A yes/no question becomes a noun clause with if or whether, and there comes before is: if there is a pool.
  33. At a hotel: Is this map up to date? → Let's ask ___ up to date.
    Accepted answers: if this map is / whether this map is / whether or not this map is
    Use if or whether for a yes/no question, with the subject before the verb: if this map is up to date.
  34. Planning a day trip: How far is it from the hotel to the beach? → We need to figure out ___ from the hotel to the beach.
    Answer: how far it is
    In the noun clause the subject it comes before is: how far it is, not how far is it.
  35. Planning a day trip: How much does it cost to rent a car for a week? → We need to figure out ___ to rent a car for a week.
    Answer: how much it costs
    Does is dropped in the noun clause, so cost takes -s: how much it costs.
  36. Planning a day trip: Is there free parking near the stadium? → We need to figure out ___ free parking near the stadium.
    Accepted answers: if there is / whether there is / whether or not there is / if there's / whether there's
    A yes/no question becomes a noun clause with if or whether, and there comes before is: whether there is free parking.
  37. Running late: Where did I park the car? → I don't know ___ the car.
    Answer: where I parked
    Did is not used in a noun clause, so the verb itself is past: where I parked the car.
  38. Running late: Is my passport in my backpack? → I don't know ___ in my backpack.
    Accepted answers: if my passport is / whether my passport is / whether or not my passport is / if it is / whether it is / if it's / whether it's
    A yes/no question becomes a noun clause with if or whether, and the subject comes before is: if my passport is in my backpack.
  39. Running late: Where is my phone charger? → I don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: where my phone charger is / where my charger is / where it is
    The subject (my phone charger) comes before is, so is goes at the end of the noun clause.
  40. Running late: What did I do with my umbrella? → I don't know ___ with my umbrella.
    Answer: what I did
    In a noun clause the helping verb did is dropped, and do becomes past: what I did with my umbrella.
  41. A new teammate: Is she a beginner or an experienced player? → I'll find out ___ a beginner or an experienced player.
    Accepted answers: if she is / whether she is / if she's / whether she's
    A yes/no question becomes a noun clause with if or whether, and the subject comes before is: whether she is a beginner or an experienced player.
  42. A new teammate: What does she study? → I'll find out ___.
    Answer: what she studies
    Does is dropped in the noun clause, so study takes -s after she: what she studies.
  43. A new teammate: Where does she usually practice? → I'll find out ___.
    Accepted answers: where she usually practices / where she practices
    Does is dropped in the noun clause, so practice takes -s after she: where she usually practices.
  44. A new teammate: Would she like to carpool to the games with us? → I'll find out ___ to carpool to the games with us.
    Accepted answers: if she would like / whether she would like / whether or not she would like / if she'd like / whether she'd like
    A yes/no question becomes a noun clause with if or whether, and the subject comes before would: if she would like to carpool.
