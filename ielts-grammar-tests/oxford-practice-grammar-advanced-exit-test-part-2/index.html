Oxford Practice Grammar Advanced Exit Test (Part 2)

Choose the best option to complete each sentence, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. I closed the door behind ___ and switched on the light.
    After a preposition of place, we use an object pronoun (behind me), even when it refers to the subject. Myself is not used after prepositions of place like this.
  2. My two cats share one cushion, each curled up against ___.
    When there are only two, the second one is the other. Another is used when there are more than two, and other on its own needs a noun after it.
  3. The new neighbors came over and asked if ___ could park their van in our driveway for the weekend.
    We don't leave out the subject after a conjunction like if, so the clause needs they (the neighbors). Then is not a pronoun, and it can't refer to people.
  4. 'Will the store still be open at nine?' 'I don't think ___. It usually closes at eight.'
    So replaces a whole clause after think (I don't think so = I don't think it will be open). It and that are not used to replace a clause in this short answer.
  5. Collectors are excited because these are the ___ sports cars to come out of the Turin factory in ten years.
    Ordinal numbers like first come before other adjectives, and general adjectives like new come before origin adjectives like Italian. So the order is first new Italian.
  6. I read the report twice, but there wasn't ___ in it; it had the same figures as last year.
    Adjectives come after indefinite pronouns like anything, and very comes right before the adjective it modifies. So it is anything very new.
  7. Our neighbor runs one of the best pizza places ___ town.
    After a superlative, we use in before a place (the best ... in town). Of is used before a group or a period, such as of all or of the year.
  8. A good coach does ___ just shout instructions; she makes the players believe they can win.
    Do more than + base form means do something extra beyond that action. As much as, the best and the most can't be followed by a base form like shout here.
  9. My sister works as a nurse at a hospital ___ Boston.
    In is used with cities and countries. At is used for a point or a building (at a hospital), not a whole city, and into shows movement.
  10. The museum was closed ___ two weeks while the roof was repaired.
    For + a length of time says how long something lasted. During is followed by the name of an event or period (during the repairs), not a length of time like two weeks.
  11. We're planning to repaint the kitchen ___ next spring.
    No preposition is used before time expressions with next, last or this (next spring, last summer). In is used with seasons only without next (in the spring).
  12. The dog across the street barks ___ night, so nobody on our block gets much sleep.
    We say at night for the time when it is dark. During and in need the (during the night), and by night is a literary phrase used mainly in contrast with by day.
  13. My uncle lives alone ___ the mountains, a two-hour drive from the nearest town.
    In is used for a place inside a large area such as the mountains. On would mean on the surface of a mountain, and above and over mean higher than.
  14. The tour costs $80 per person, ___ lunch and entry to the castle.
    Including is used like a preposition to add items that are part of a total. Includes would need its own subject, and included would have to come after the nouns (lunch included).
  15. My sister left the party twenty minutes after us but drove fast and caught ___ before we got to the highway.
    Catch up with someone means reach someone who is ahead, and the object goes after with. In US English, catch us up means tell us the latest news, which doesn't fit here.
  16. If the package still hasn't arrived ___ Friday, call the delivery company.
    By means no later than a time, so hasn't arrived by Friday sets a deadline. Since would mean from Friday until now, which doesn't fit a future deadline.
  17. My doctor told me to avoid ___ to bed right after a big meal.
    Avoid is followed by an -ing form, not an infinitive, so to go is wrong.
  18. There's no use ___ the rules to the kids now; they're far too excited to listen.
    No use is followed by an -ing form (no use trying), not by to. Try meaning make an effort is then followed by to + base form.
  19. The new interns are eager ___ how the lab works, so they follow the scientists everywhere.
    Eager is followed by to + base form. Eager for is followed by a noun, not a verb, and eager in or eager + -ing is not used.
  20. The landlord refused ___ until the end of the month to pay the rent.
    Refuse is followed by to + base form, and let is followed by an object and a base form without to. So it is refused to let me wait.
  21. For many young people today, it's very important ___ in their careers before they start a family.
    After it + be + adjective, we use to + base form for the action (it's important to succeed). Succeeding and the noun success can't follow important in this pattern.
  22. Given how little he studied, Omar wasn't ___ to see his low grade.
    The -ed adjective describes how a person feels (Omar wasn't surprised). Surprising describes the thing that causes the feeling, such as the grade.
  23. Lots of new drivers are nervous about ___ the test on their first attempt.
    After a preposition such as about, a verb takes the -ing form. To fail can't follow about.
  24. The mouse in our kitchen is so clever that it's unlikely ___ in an ordinary trap.
    The mouse doesn't catch anything; it is the one that would be caught, so we need the passive infinitive (to be caught). To catch is active and would need an object.
  25. My roommates and I had a long talk about ___ we should get a dog or not.
    After a preposition like about, we use whether, not if, for a yes/no choice (whether ... or not). That can't follow about here, and why doesn't fit with or not.
  26. The mechanic told me ___ the car until he had checked the brakes.
    A reported negative command is tell + person + not to + base form. Don't use is the form of the direct command and can't follow told me.
  27. My little brother always argues ___ me about whose turn it is to do the dishes.
    We argue with a person and about a subject. To and for are not used after argue for the other person.
  28. When my sister started planning a picnic, I reminded ___ was supposed to rain all afternoon.
    Remind needs a person object before the clause (remind her (that) it ...). Reminded that it is wrong because the person being reminded is missing.
  29. Jake laughed when he heard the rumor ___ the principal's car.
    Nouns such as rumor, charge and accusation can be followed by a that-clause giving their content (the rumor that he stole). Which would start a relative clause, but this clause says what the rumor is, and rumor is not followed by -ing or to + verb.
  30. Dad gave my brother $50 for school supplies, but now he can't explain where ___.
    In an indirect question after explain, we use statement word order with no did (where the money went). Did the money go is direct question order.
  31. Nobody knows who ate the cake, but my mom has suggested that the dog ___ it off the counter while we were out last night.
    Here suggest means put forward the idea that something happened, so the past simple describes the past event (last night). Should take and takes would be advice about what to do, which doesn't fit last night.
  32. Doctors consider ___ the virus will spread beyond the small town.
    With consider + adjective + that-clause, we use empty it as the object (consider it unlikely that). Without it, there is no object before the adjective.
  33. The museum has a painting ___ said to be worth over ten million dollars.
    The relative clause needs a relative pronoun and a verb: that's said = that is said. Which said has no verb, and was can't follow a noun without a relative pronoun.
  34. At the conference I met a woman ___ husband used to teach at my old school.
    Whose shows possession in a relative clause (her husband becomes whose husband). Which and where can't be followed by a noun that belongs to the person.
  35. The person you need to talk ___ is the manager on the second floor.
    In a relative clause with no relative pronoun, the preposition stays at the end (the person you need to talk to). To her repeats the person, and to whom must come before the subject (to whom you need to talk).
  36. I met a student at the library ___ offered to lend me her notes.
    Who is the subject of the relative clause (who offered). The relative pronoun can't be left out when it is the subject, and whom is used only as an object.
  37. Most of the coffee ___ in this region is exported to Europe and Japan.
    A reduced relative clause uses the past participle for a passive meaning (coffee grown = coffee that is grown). That growing and which grown mix a relative pronoun with a reduced form.
  38. Teachers should try to avoid situations ___ students feel they have to cheat to pass.
    Where can introduce a relative clause after nouns like situation or case. Which would need a preposition (in which), and that can't be used because the clause after it is already complete.
  39. Anyone ___ a phone while driving can be fined up to $500.
    A reduced relative clause uses the -ing form for an active meaning (anyone using = anyone who uses). Uses would need who before it.
  40. The factory was fined for dumping water ___ containing high levels of lead into the river.
    Containing already starts a reduced relative clause, so no relative pronoun is needed. Which containing is wrong; it would have to be which contained.
  41. ___ the hotel was cheap, it was clean and very close to the beach.
    Even though introduces a fact that contrasts with the main clause. Unless means except if, and if only expresses a wish.
  42. In the 1800s, women were often refused jobs if they ___ young children at home.
    In a real conditional about the past, the if-clause uses the past simple. Have is present, and would have and will have are not used in an if-clause like this.
  43. Most people don't mind dogs in the park, ___ they bark all the time.
    Unless means except if, giving the one case when people do mind. If and only if would say that people don't mind dogs that bark all the time, which is the opposite.
  44. If the city kept adding people at this rate, it ___ to twice its current size by 2050.
    An unreal or unlikely condition (if + past simple) is followed by would + base form. Will grow would need if + present simple (if it keeps adding).
  45. I'll ask the waiter for the check when he ___ back to our table.
    After when referring to the future, we use the present simple, not will. Came is past.
  46. 'Let me know how the interview goes.' 'Don't worry, ___ you the moment it's over.'
    Will is used for promises made at the moment of speaking. I'm calling describes an arrangement or something happening now, and the present simple I call can't make a promise.
  47. It's nearly six months ___ my brother moved to Canada.
    It's + period + since + past simple measures the time from a past event until now. Later, once and when don't link a period of time to a past event like this.
  48. Patients aren't allowed to drive until a full week ___ since the surgery.
    After until referring to the future, we use a present tense, not will, and the present perfect stresses that the week is complete. Since also needs the present perfect here.
  49. ___ my sister loves spicy food, I can't eat anything hotter than black pepper.
    Whereas joins two clauses that contrast two people or things. In spite of and instead of must be followed by a noun or -ing form, not a clause, and even although is not used.
  50. Many students take part-time jobs ___ pay for their textbooks.
    In order to + base form expresses purpose. For can't be followed by a base form, and so that needs a subject and verb (so that they can pay).
  51. The challenge is to cross the river ___ only a rope and two wooden planks.
    An -ing form can follow the main verb to show how something is done (cross the river using a rope). Uses and used would need their own subject.
  52. ___ two words are spelled the same but have different meanings, they're called homographs.
    When introduces the situation in a general fact or definition (when this happens, it's called ...). As and since here would mean because, and while is used for contrast or things happening at the same time.
  53. ___ our team lost the final on Saturday, the whole town was quiet for days.
    After introduces the earlier of two past events. Although would need a contrast, and if is not used for a single event that really happened.
  54. The concert was moved indoors because ___ the strong winds.
    Because of is followed by a noun phrase (the strong winds). Because on its own must be followed by a clause with a subject and verb.
  55. I spent the whole morning ___ for the plumber, who never showed up.
    Spend + time is followed by an -ing form for the activity (spent the morning waiting). A second main verb like waited can't follow without and.
  56. ___ being the youngest player on the team, Rosa scored the winning goal.
    Despite is a preposition, so it can be followed by an -ing form (despite being). Although needs a full clause (although she was), and as would give a reason, not a contrast.
  57. What surprises me most about this city ___ nobody seems to be in a hurry.
    In a sentence that begins with a what-clause, the what-clause is the subject, followed by is and then a that-clause. It is and is it add a second subject that isn't needed.
  58. Some common foods are dangerous for dogs. ___, chocolate can make them seriously ill.
    For example introduces a particular case of the general point before it. In addition would add a different point, therefore introduces a result, and on the other hand a contrast.
  59. The storm brought down several trees and, ___ the damage, the main road was closed for two days.
    As a result of is a preposition, so it is followed by a noun phrase (the damage). Afterwards, as a consequence and subsequently are adverbs and can't be followed directly by a noun.
  60. Not until the plane had landed safely ___ announced that one of the engines had failed.
    When a sentence begins with not until, the main clause uses question word order (was it announced). It was is normal order, and was alone has no subject.
  61. It was only after midnight ___ the band finally came on stage.
    In a sentence that starts with it was to give emphasis, the emphasized part is followed by that. Which can't follow a time phrase like after midnight in this pattern, and what is not used here.
  62. My brother speaks Spanish, and he ___ understands a little Portuguese.
    Also usually goes before the main verb (he also understands). As well goes at the end of a clause, and besides and moreover normally begin a sentence or clause.
  63. Summers in Phoenix are extremely hot and dry. ___, summers in Seattle are mild and often cloudy.
    In contrast introduces a point that is very different from the one before. In other words would repeat the same idea differently, and in particular would give a special case.
  64. Regular exercise strengthens the heart ___ helps people manage stress.
    And joins two verbs that share the same subject (strengthens ... and helps). Consequently and has the words in the wrong order, and as a result of must be followed by a noun.
