Oxford Practice Grammar Advanced Chapter 12 – Reporting Verbs

Choose or type the answer to each question, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. Complete the second sentence: "Dr. Patel, the printer on the second floor isn't working." → I mentioned ___ that the printer on the second floor wasn't working.
    Accepted answers: to Dr. Patel / to Dr Patel
    Mention can take to + the hearer before a that-clause, but not the hearer directly (not mentioned Dr. Patel that). The person spoken to becomes the object of to.
  2. Complete the second sentence: "Sofia, you'll need your passport for the visa interview." → I reminded ___ would need her passport for the visa interview.
    Accepted answers: Sofia that she / Sofia she
    Remind must have an object (the hearer) before the that-clause. You becomes she because Sofia is now talked about, not talked to.
  3. Complete the second sentence: Turning to the children, the baker said, "Don't open the oven door while the bread is baking." → The baker warned ___ the oven door while the bread was baking.
    Accepted answers: the children not to open / them not to open / the children that they shouldn't open / the children that they should not open / them that they shouldn't open / them that they should not open
    Warn is followed by an object and then not + to-infinitive to report a negative warning. The object (the children) names the people who were warned.
  4. Complete the second sentence: "I didn't break the window," Kevin insisted. → Kevin denied ___ the window.
    Accepted answers: breaking / having broken / that he had broken / that he'd broken / that he broke / he had broken / he'd broken / he broke
    Deny is followed by a gerund (breaking, having broken) or a that-clause (that he had broken). It is not followed by a to-infinitive.
  5. The lifeguard stood up on his tall chair, ___ to the swimmers to come back closer to the beach.
    Shout can be followed by to + the people addressed. Tell and ask take the person directly as an object (telling the swimmers), never to.
  6. When I arrived in Lisbon, I remembered that a colleague ___ a small hotel near the harbor where I could stay cheaply.
    Mention can be followed directly by the thing talked about (a small hotel). Tell needs a person as its object, and talk needs about before a topic.
  7. Staring out the window, I ___ why the train had stopped in the middle of nowhere.
    Wonder is used for a question someone asks themselves and is followed by a wh-clause. Think is not followed by why, and told needs a person as its object.
  8. After the lecture, I waited by the stage so that I could ___ to the speaker for a few minutes.
    Talk to someone describes a conversation in a summary report. Tell and ask take the person directly as an object, so to the speaker does not fit them.
  9. When I called the landlord back, he ___ me if I was still interested in the apartment.
    Ask takes the hearer as an object before an if-clause (asked me if). Tell is not used to report a yes/no question, and wonder does not take a person as its object.
  10. The mechanic ___ me that the brakes would need replacing before winter.
    Tell takes the hearer directly before a that-clause (told me that). Explain and mention need to before the hearer (explained to me, mentioned to me).
  11. The bridge would be closed for at least three months, the engineer ___.
    Explain can come after the reported words with no object. Told needs an object (told us), and talk is not used to report what someone said.
  12. "Too often, when companies ___ that their products are eco-friendly, they only mean the packaging," the reporter said.
    Claim is followed directly by a that-clause and shows that the statement may not be true. Tell needs a person before that, and talk is not followed by a that-clause.
  13. "Please, please don't tell Mom about the broken vase," my little brother ___ me.
    Beg reports an urgent request and takes the person directly as an object (begged me). Explain and talk cannot take a person directly as an object.
  14. This is going to be a long night, I ___ to myself as I opened my laptop.
    Think to yourself reports words that are not spoken aloud. Tell needs a direct object (told myself), and talk is not used to report the words someone said.
  15. Correct the words in square brackets: My cousin always [says me] that city life is too stressful for her.
    Accepted answers: says / says to me / tells me
    Say is not followed directly by the hearer before a that-clause. Leave the hearer out, use to (says to me that), or use tell (tells me that).
  16. Correct the words in square brackets: Most people on the committee think the park should stay open later, and I [agree them] that it would make the area safer.
    Answer: agree with them
    Agree uses with before the person (agree with them) when a that-clause follows. It cannot take the person directly as an object.
  17. Correct the words in square brackets: When my car battery died outside the library, a student [offered me to give] a jump start.
    Accepted answers: offered to give me / offered me
    Offer is followed directly by a to-infinitive, with no object before it, so the person helped goes after the infinitive: offered to give me. Offer + person + noun (offered me a jump start) is also correct.
  18. Correct the words in square brackets: At the front desk, I [explained them] that our flight had been delayed by six hours.
    Answer: explained to them
    Explain needs to before the hearer (explained to them). It cannot take the person directly as an object.
  19. Correct the words in square brackets: When the repairman finally arrived, I told him that I [can't] turn on the heat in the bedroom, and he fixed it in five minutes.
    Accepted answers: couldn't / could not
    After a past reporting verb, can changes to could. The problem was fixed, so the situation is no longer true and the present cannot stay.
  20. Correct the words in square brackets: The nurse [told that] the doctor would see me in about ten minutes.
    Accepted answers: told me that / told me / said that / said / said to me that
    Tell must have the hearer as an object before a that-clause (told me that). Say does not need an object (said that).
  21. Correct the words in square brackets: Our tour guide [assured that] we would be back at the hotel before dark.
    Accepted answers: assured us that / assured us
    Assure, like tell, must have the hearer as an object before a that-clause: assured us that.
  22. Correct the words in square brackets: My doctor [suggested me to take] a short walk after every meal.
    Accepted answers: suggested taking / suggested that I take / suggested that I should take / suggested I take / suggested I should take
    Suggest is followed by a gerund (suggested taking) or a that-clause (suggested that I (should) take). It is never followed by an object + to-infinitive.
  23. Correct the words in square brackets: Before my first trip abroad, my mom [warned me keep] a close eye on my bag on the subway.
    Accepted answers: warned me to keep / warned me that I should keep / warned me I should keep
    Warn takes an object and a to-infinitive (warned me to keep) or a that-clause (warned me that I should keep). The bare verb keep cannot follow the object.
  24. Correct the words in square brackets: In his wedding speech, the best man [joked] the groom's terrible sense of direction.
    Answer: joked about
    Joke is used with about to give a summary of what was said: joked about something. It cannot take the topic directly as an object.
  25. Correct the words in square brackets: After the firefighters rescued our cat from the tree, we [spoke them thanks].
    Accepted answers: thanked them / said thanks to them / said thank you to them
    Thank is a summary reporting verb that takes the person as an object: thanked them. Speak is only used for summaries like spoke to them and cannot report the words said.
  26. Correct the words in square brackets: Because of the heavy snow, the taxi driver [refused take] us to the airport.
    Answer: refused to take
    Refuse is followed by a to-infinitive (refused to take), not by the bare verb.
  27. Correct the words in square brackets: When the restaurant lost our reservation, the manager [offered pay] for our drinks while we waited.
    Answer: offered to pay
    Offer is followed by a to-infinitive (offered to pay), not by the bare verb.
