Understanding and Using English Grammar Chapter 4 – Review Of Verb Tenses

Choose or type the answer to each question, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. Look out the window! Everyone on the street ___ up at the hot-air balloon.
    An action happening right now needs the present progressive (is + -ing). The -ing form alone has no helping verb, so it is not a complete verb.
  2. I only ___ my driver's license three days ago, so I'm still a nervous driver.
    Ago marks a finished time in the past, so the simple past got is needed. The simple present get does not fit a past event.
  3. We just heard that the band ___ to our town on its next tour, and tickets go on sale Friday.
    After heard that, you need a full clause with a subject and a verb. Is coming expresses a planned future event; to come is not a complete verb.
  4. When ___ to switch your major from biology to music?
    Deciding happens at one moment in the past, so use the simple past question did you decide. The past progressive is for actions in progress over a period.
  5. My parents ___ in Florida for the last three months, and they don't want to come home.
    For the last three months describes a situation that began in the past and continues now, so it takes the present perfect. The simple present are cannot be used with this kind of for-phrase.
  6. Since joining the hiking club in May, I ___ six different mountains.
    The period since May continues up to now, so the present perfect is correct. The past perfect had climbed needs a later past event to look back from.
  7. Since I moved here in June, I ___ the ocean only twice.
    Since I moved here in June marks a period that continues up to now, so use the present perfect have seen. The simple past saw is for a finished past time.
  8. By the time this hike ends, we ___ over 2,000 photos.
    The future perfect (will have + past participle) shows an action completed before a future time. After will, use have, not had.
  9. At the moment, Carla ___ (study) Italian online because she's planning a trip to Rome in the spring.
    Accepted answers: is studying / 's studying
    Use the present progressive for an activity in progress during the present period. At the moment shows the studying is going on now, not a permanent habit.
  10. Marcus has a full-time job, and right now he ___ (take, also) an evening course in accounting.
    Accepted answers: is also taking / 's also taking
    Right now points to a temporary activity in progress, so use the present progressive. Also goes after the helping verb is.
  11. On weekdays, the free guided tours of the museum ___ (begin) at 11:00 and 2:00.
    Answer: begin
    A regular schedule takes the simple present. Tours is plural, so the verb has no -s.
  12. The fire alarm went off at 6:15 this morning, but luckily I ___ (get up, already) and was dressed.
    Accepted answers: had already gotten up / 'd already gotten up / had gotten up already / had already got up / 'd already got up / had got up already
    The past perfect shows that getting up happened before another past event, the alarm. Already usually goes between had and the past participle.
  13. Yesterday my phone ___ (ring) just as I stepped into the shower.
    Answer: rang
    A completed action at a past moment takes the simple past. Ring is irregular: ring, rang, rung.
  14. My uncle ___ (work) at a car dealership during the week and plays in a band on weekends.
    Answer: works
    The simple present describes a regular routine, like plays later in the sentence. Uncle is third person singular, so add -s.
  15. Before a big game, Jordan ___ (eat, always) a huge bowl of pasta for energy.
    Answer: always eats
    A habit takes the simple present with -s for a singular subject. Frequency adverbs like always go before a one-word main verb.
  16. I usually take out the trash before my roommate ___ (leave) for work.
    Answer: leaves
    Both actions are part of a daily routine, so the time clause after before uses the simple present. Roommate is singular, so the verb needs -s.
  17. My brother works two jobs, so he ___ (have, not, usually) enough time to cook at home.
    Accepted answers: doesn't usually have / does not usually have / usually doesn't have / usually does not have
    The negative of a simple present habit is doesn't + base form. Usually can go after doesn't or before it.
  18. Last night I ___ (call) the pizza place twice, but the line was busy both times.
    Answer: called
    Last night is a finished past time, so use the simple past. Call is regular and adds -ed.
  19. I texted Raj at noon, but he ___ (answer, not) until the evening.
    Accepted answers: didn't answer / did not answer
    The negative of the simple past is didn't + base form. Answer stays in the base form after didn't.
  20. Someone stole my bike from outside the library while I ___ (attend) a lecture inside.
    Answer: was attending
    The past progressive shows an activity in progress when another past event (the theft) happened. With I, use was.
  21. At this time tomorrow, I ___ (attend) my cousin's wedding in San Diego.
    Accepted answers: will be attending / 'll be attending / am going to be attending / 'm going to be attending
    The future progressive shows an activity that will be in progress at a particular future time. At this time tomorrow names that moment.
  22. When the firefighters ___ (get) to the warehouse, smoke was pouring out of the windows.
    Answer: got
    The when-clause names a completed past action, so use the simple past. The smoke was already pouring out (past progressive) when they arrived.
  23. When I walked into the living room, my grandfather ___ (sleep) in his armchair with the TV on.
    Answer: was sleeping
    The sleeping was already in progress when I walked in, so use the past progressive.
  24. By the time the plane landed in Lisbon, Marco ___ (sleep) for almost seven hours.
    Accepted answers: had been sleeping / 'd been sleeping / had slept / 'd slept
    The past perfect progressive shows how long an activity continued before another past event (the landing). The past perfect had slept is also possible with for.
  25. Please keep your voice down. The baby ___ (sleep) in the next room.
    Accepted answers: is sleeping / 's sleeping
    The sleeping is happening right now, so use the present progressive.
  26. Exhausted after the long hike, Priya ___ (fall) asleep in the car twenty minutes ago.
    Answer: fell
    Ago marks a finished past time, so use the simple past. Fall is irregular: fall, fell, fallen.
  27. Our dog ___ (sleep) in that sunny spot by the window since lunch.
    Accepted answers: has been sleeping / 's been sleeping / has slept
    Since lunch shows an activity that began in the past and is still going on, so use the present perfect progressive (or the present perfect).
  28. After Grandpa ___ (have) his nap this afternoon, he'll take us fishing.
    Accepted answers: has / has had
    A future time clause after after uses the simple present (or the present perfect), not will. Grandpa is singular, so use has.
  29. Tomorrow after class, Ines ___ (meet) me at the library to study for our exam.
    Accepted answers: will meet / 'll meet / is going to meet / 's going to meet / is meeting / 's meeting
    Tomorrow is in the future, so use will, be going to, or the present progressive for an arranged plan.
  30. By the time the movie starts, we ___ (have) dinner, so we won't need any snacks.
    Accepted answers: will have had / 'll have had
    The future perfect (will have + past participle) shows an action finished before a future time. By the time the movie starts names that time.
  31. Mr. Okafor will have read every report by the time he ___ (meet) the new clients on Friday.
    Answer: meets
    A time clause with by the time uses the simple present even for a future event. He is third person singular, so add -s.
  32. Back in 2015, I ___ to open my own restaurant, but I couldn't get a loan.
    Back in 2015 is a finished past time, so use the simple past wanted. The present perfect have wanted cannot be used with a specific past time.
  33. My sister ___ at the party on Saturday because she had the flu.
    The past negative of be is wasn't or weren't; be does not use did. Not alone leaves the sentence without a verb.
  34. Our new neighbors ___ in the building since June, but we haven't met them yet.
    Since June shows a situation that started in the past and continues now, so use the present perfect. Had been would mean the situation ended before another past time.
  35. I just got into a graduate program in Japan. I ___ there for two years.
    Be going to expresses a future plan. The simple present study describes a present habit, and will is never followed by an -ing form.
  36. I work on a cargo ship. When we dock in Miami next week, I ___ at sea for three months.
    The future perfect will have been shows a period that will be complete at a future moment, when the ship docks. Am going to be would mean the three months start then.
  37. Listen! Someone ___ knocking at the back door.
    The present progressive (is + -ing) shows an action happening now. Has cannot be followed by an -ing form, and am goes only with I.
  38. Don't call Grandma before noon tomorrow; she ___ be at her doctor's appointment.
    Will + base form (be) talks about a future situation. Is and has cannot be followed by the base form be.
  39. My little nephew ___ never ridden a roller coaster, so he's both excited and nervous.
    The present perfect is has/have + past participle (ridden). Nephew is singular, so use has, not have.
  40. The washing machine ___ been making a strange noise all week.
    The present perfect progressive is has/have been + -ing. The singular subject needs has; is cannot come before been.
  41. I ___ trying a new recipe tonight, so dinner might be a little late.
    The present progressive with I is am + -ing. Is goes with he, she or it, and have cannot be followed by an -ing form.
  42. According to the news, the new subway line ___ be open to the public by next spring.
    Will + base form expresses a future event. Is and has cannot be followed by the base form be.
  43. We ___ watching a movie at home last night when the power suddenly went out.
    The past progressive with we is were + -ing. Was goes with singular subjects, and had cannot be followed by an -ing form.
  44. Until her trip to Tokyo last spring, Elena ___ never used chopsticks.
    Until her trip last spring points to the time before a past event, so use the past perfect had + past participle. Has never used would connect to the present, not to last spring.
  45. The new traffic light on Pine Street ___ made the intersection much safer.
    The present perfect shows a past change with a result now. Traffic light is singular, so use has + made.
  46. Ticket prices for the concert ___ been going up every week since the band announced the tour.
    Since shows an activity continuing up to now, so use the present perfect progressive. Prices is plural, so use have, not has.
  47. When I ___ jogging in the park this morning, I saw a family of deer.
    The past progressive with I is was + -ing. Were goes with you, we and they, and had cannot be followed by an -ing form.
  48. When I got to the station, my train ___ just left.
    The train left before I got there, an earlier past event, so use the past perfect had left. Has just left would connect to the present, not to a past moment.
  49. This ___ been the best semester of my college life so far.
    So far shows a period continuing up to now, so use the present perfect has been. This takes has, not have, and is cannot come before been.
  50. My grandparents ___ never left Ohio until they retired and bought a camper van.
    Until they retired names a past time, and the past perfect had left describes the time before it. Have never left cannot be used with a finished past point.
  51. Our twins ___ only six, but they already read chapter books.
    Be is used for age, and the plural subject twins needs are. Is goes with a singular subject, and English uses be, not have, for age.
  52. Correct the words in square brackets: My aunt [was emigrating] to Australia in 1998 and opened a small cafe in Perth.
    Answer: emigrated
    A completed action at a specific past time takes the simple past, like opened later in the sentence. The past progressive is for an action in progress.
  53. Correct the words in square brackets: Before his trip to Peru last year, Diego [has never flown] on a plane.
    Answer: had never flown
    The time before a past event (last year's trip) takes the past perfect. Has never flown would connect to the present.
  54. Correct the words in square brackets: After years of moving from city to city, the Nguyens finally [settle] in Denver in 2010.
    Answer: settled
    In 2010 is a finished past time, so the verb must be simple past: settled.
  55. Correct the words in square brackets: As a child, Ravi [is growing up] in a small fishing village, so he still loves the sea.
    Answer: grew up
    His childhood is finished, so use the simple past grew up. The present progressive describes something happening now.
  56. Correct the words in square brackets: Two months ago, Kim [had gone] skiing in Colorado and broke her wrist.
    Answer: went
    Two months ago is a finished past time with no earlier past event to look back from, so use the simple past went, like broke.
  57. Correct the words in square brackets: Lena finally bought a piano last spring; she [had always want] to learn to play.
    Answer: had always wanted
    The past perfect is had + past participle, so want needs -ed. It describes her wish before she bought the piano.
  58. Correct the words in square brackets: After three failed attempts, Omar [was finally passing] his driving test last Tuesday.
    Answer: finally passed
    Passing the test happened at one moment, so use the simple past. The past progressive is for an action in progress over a period.
  59. Two months ago, Hana ___ (start) taking piano lessons.
    Answer: started
    Ago marks a finished past time, so use the simple past started.
  60. At the moment, my dad ___ (read) a thick history of the Roman Empire that his friend lent him.
    Accepted answers: is reading / 's reading
    At the moment shows an activity in progress now: he has started the book but not finished it. Use the present progressive.
  61. This apartment is small, but it ___ (have) a beautiful view of the river.
    Answer: has
    Have meaning possess is a non-progressive verb, so use the simple present. It is singular, so use has.
  62. Dmitri started his science project last week, but he ___ (finish, not) it yet.
    Accepted answers: hasn't finished / has not finished
    Not ... yet goes with the present perfect for something expected that has not happened up to now.
  63. Since September, Rosa ___ (finish) four online courses in graphic design.
    Accepted answers: has finished / 's finished
    Since September names a period that continues up to now, so use the present perfect for the completed courses.
  64. In his long career, Dr. Hughes ___ (read) thousands of X-rays, and he still double-checks every one.
    Accepted answers: has read / 's read
    His career continues up to now, so use the present perfect for the total so far.
  65. According to Sofia, this is the most confusing textbook she ___ (read, ever).
    Accepted answers: has ever read / 's ever read
    After a superlative or the first, use the present perfect with ever for all experience up to now. Ever goes between has and the past participle.
  66. When my brother finishes this TV series, he ___ (start) a cooking show that his friends recommended.
    Accepted answers: is going to start / 's going to start / will start / 'll start / is starting / 's starting
    The main clause describes a future plan, so use be going to, will, or the present progressive. Only the when-clause uses the simple present.
  67. I ___ for this company since 2019.
    Since shows an activity that began in the past and continues now, so use the present perfect progressive. Am working cannot be used with since.
  68. By the time the astronauts returned to Earth, they ___ in space for six months.
    By the time + a past event takes the past perfect for the earlier period. Have been would connect to the present, not to the past return.
  69. Which sentence about my sister's plans is correct?
    A future time clause after as soon as uses the simple present with -s (gets), and be going to needs is. The first sentence wrongly uses will in the time clause.
  70. The new manager started on Monday, but I ___ her yet because I've been working from home.
    Yet goes with the present perfect for something that has not happened up to now. Hadn't met needs a past time to look back from.
  71. Which sentence about calling the bank is correct?
    A number of times takes the present perfect, not the progressive, and want is a non-progressive verb. So have called and want are both needed.
  72. Which set of sentences about the kids' eating habits is correct?
    Like, want and believe are non-progressive verbs, so they take the simple present. The last set breaks agreement (they wants) and wrongly uses the past (believed).
  73. The janitor locked the building after the last students ___.
    Leaving was a completed past action before the locking, so use the simple past left. Are going to leave refers to the future, which does not fit locked.
  74. Which sentence about Grace and Tomas is correct?
    A situation that started in the past and continues now takes the present perfect in the main clause, and the since-clause uses the simple past. The simple present know cannot be used with since.
  75. Don't worry about food at the conference; there ___ a free lunch for everyone.
    Will + base form talks about the future. Going to be needs is before it, and will is never followed by to.
  76. By the end of the summer, the divers ___ dozens of old shipwrecks along this coast.
    By + a future time takes the future perfect: will have + past participle. After will, use the base form have.
  77. I ___ a postcard from my aunt in Peru last week.
    Last week is a finished past time, so use the simple past got.
  78. My neighbors ___ to sell their old car ever since they bought the new one.
    Ever since shows an activity that started in the past and is still going on, so use the present perfect progressive. Were trying would mean it is over.
  79. I ___ really busy since the semester started.
    Since shows a situation from a past time up to now, so use the present perfect. The simple present am cannot be used with since.
  80. This month we ___ two power outages, and another storm is coming.
    This month is not finished, and the outages are counted up to now, so use the present perfect. Were having describes an action in progress in the past.
  81. My cousin ___ on our couch since last weekend.
    Since last weekend shows an activity that started in the past and continues now, so use the present perfect progressive. The simple past cannot be used with since this way.
  82. Since my grandparents retired, they ___ a lot of time in their garden.
    The period since they retired continues up to now, so use the present perfect progressive. Were spending would describe a finished past period.
  83. So far on this trip, we ___ to three different beaches and a lighthouse.
    So far counts visits up to now, so use the present perfect have been to. Were going describes an action in progress, not completed visits.
  84. Last Saturday my roommates and I ___ to a street festival downtown.
    Last Saturday is a finished past time, so use the simple past. The present perfect cannot be used with a specific past time.
  85. Last night we ordered pizza and ___ an old movie on TV.
    Both actions happened last night, so the second verb is also simple past, like ordered.
  86. My new job keeps me so busy that I ___ a chance to unpack since I moved.
    Since I moved shows a period from the past up to now, so use the present perfect. The present progressive am barely having cannot show that period.
  87. It ___ almost midnight, and the neighbors are still playing loud music.
    Telling the time right now uses the simple present: it is. Has been would need a period of time, not a clock time.
  88. At the moment I ___ on a crowded train, so I can't talk for long.
    At the moment means now, so use the present progressive. Was sitting refers to the past.
  89. My back hurts because I ___ in this waiting room for three hours.
    For three hours gives the length of an activity from the past up to now, so use the present perfect progressive. Am sitting cannot express that duration.
  90. Hurry up! The last shuttle to the airport ___ in ten minutes.
    The simple present can express a scheduled future event. Shuttle is singular, so the verb needs -s.
  91. My flight was canceled, so I ___ to rent a car and drive home instead.
    The decision was made at one moment in the past, after the flight was canceled, so use the simple past. Am deciding would mean the decision is still in progress now.
  92. I ___ to you from a little cafe in Lisbon while I wait for my train.
    The writing is happening right now, so use the present progressive. The simple present write is for habits.
  93. I ___ hungry, so let's stop for lunch soon.
    Get + adjective in the present progressive shows a change happening now. The simple present get would describe a habit.
  94. Tonight I ___ a long hot shower as soon as I get home from the gym.
    Tonight shows a future plan, so the main clause needs be going to. The simple present take would describe a habit.
  95. I'll call you after I ___ home from the dentist.
    A future time clause after after uses the simple present, not will.
  96. You started your new job on Monday. How ___ along so far?
    The question asks about a situation in progress now, so use the present progressive. Do you get would ask about a general habit.
  97. How ___ things going with your new roommate?
    The present progressive needs be + -ing, so are must come before going. Without are, going has no helping verb.
