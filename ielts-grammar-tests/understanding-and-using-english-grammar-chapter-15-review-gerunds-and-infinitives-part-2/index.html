Understanding and Using English Grammar Chapter 15 Review – Gerunds and Infinitives, Part 2

Choose or type the answer to each question, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. Kenji went to the bank ___ out a loan for his new business.
    To + the simple form gives the purpose (in order to). For is never placed before an infinitive.
  2. Everyone was delighted ___ that the new library will open in May.
    Adjectives of feeling such as delighted, sorry and happy are followed by an infinitive: delighted to hear. A gerund does not follow them.
  3. The new interns seem eager ___ with any project we give them.
    Eager is followed by an infinitive, which is to + the simple form: eager to help. To helping is wrong because to here is part of the infinitive, not a preposition.
  4. The soup isn't ___ to eat yet.
    Enough comes after an adjective: cool enough to eat. It comes before a noun, not before an adjective.
  5. Nobody likes ___ by fake online reviews.
    A passive infinitive is to be + past participle: to be fooled. Fool is the simple form and cannot follow to be here.
  6. During the interview, the candidate mentioned ___ in Japan for two years.
    Mention is followed by a gerund. The past gerund having worked shows that the work happened before the interview. Mention is not followed by an infinitive.
  7. The young scientist was proud ___ to give a talk at the international conference.
    The scientist received the invitation earlier, so the past passive infinitive to have been invited is used. To have invited is active and would need an object.
  8. These wine glasses can't go in the dishwasher. They need ___ by hand.
    The glasses receive the action, so need is followed by the passive infinitive to be + past participle: to be washed.
  9. Late at night, we could hear the wind ___ through the old windows.
    After a verb of perception like hear + object, use the -ing form or the simple form, not an infinitive.
  10. My little brother helped our dad ___ the new bookshelf.
    Help is usually followed by the simple form of a verb: helped our dad assemble. An -ing form does not follow help + object.
  11. Our landlord lets tenants ___ small pets in the building.
    Let is followed by the simple form of a verb, not an infinitive: lets tenants keep.
  12. Our coach makes us ___ twenty push-ups if we arrive late.
    Causative make is followed by the simple form of a verb, not an infinitive: makes us do.
  13. We really appreciate ___ us a ride to the airport.
    Appreciate is followed by a gerund, and in formal English a possessive comes before it: your giving. Appreciate is not followed by an infinitive.
  14. Choose the correct completion(s): Two of my coworkers helped me ___ my desk to the new office on the third floor.
    Help can be followed by the simple form (move) or, less often, an infinitive (to move). Moving and being moved are wrong after help + object.
  15. Choose the correct completion(s): Waiting for the bus in the storm without an umbrella, I could feel rainwater ___ down the back of my neck.
    After a verb of perception like feel + object, use the simple form (run) or the -ing form (running). An infinitive is not used.
  16. We all watched the firefighters ___ a hole in the roof to let the smoke out.
    After a verb of perception like watch + object, use the simple form for a complete action: watched the firefighters make a hole. An infinitive or a tense form cannot follow it.
  17. At our book club meeting, we spent two hours ___ about the ending of the novel.
    Spend + time is followed by an -ing form: spent two hours talking. Being talked is passive, but we did the talking.
  18. More than 300 bands sent in recordings, so our band was fortunate ___ for the festival's main stage.
    Fortunate is followed by an infinitive. The band receives the action, so the passive infinitive to be chosen is needed; to choose would mean the band did the choosing.
  19. The CEO wants ___ about every customer complaint, no matter how small.
    Want is followed by an infinitive. The CEO receives the information, so the passive infinitive to be told is needed. A gerund such as being told does not follow want.
  20. Grandpa sat on the porch all afternoon ___ for the mail carrier.
    Sit + place is often followed by an -ing form that tells what the person is doing at the same time: sat on the porch waiting. Waited would need and before it.
  21. Choose the correct completion(s): ___ forgetting the passports at home almost ruined our vacation. (Ø = no word)
    A gerund subject can have a possessive before it (My forgetting) or no word at all (Forgetting). We and I are subject pronouns and cannot come before a gerund.
  22. After the dentist gave me a strong painkiller, I had my sister ___ me home.
    Causative have + person is followed by the simple form of a verb: had my sister drive. An infinitive is not used after have + person.
  23. The student who reported the cheating was afraid of her classmates' reaction, so she asked not ___ by name at the meeting.
    Ask is followed by an infinitive, with not before it. She does not want anyone to name her, so the passive to be identified is needed; to identify would need an object.
  24. Most employees enjoy ___ (allow) to work from home on Fridays.
    Answer: being allowed
    Enjoy is followed by a gerund. The employees receive the permission, so the passive gerund being allowed is needed.
  25. After the marathon, Nadia lay on the grass near the finish line ___ (think) about how far she had come.
    Answer: thinking
    Lie + place is often followed by an -ing form that tells what the person is doing at the same time: lay on the grass thinking.
  26. At sixteen, my niece is old enough ___ (have) a part-time job.
    Answer: to have
    Adjective + enough is followed by an infinitive: old enough to have.
  27. Our son is too young ___ (know) what a mortgage is.
    Answer: to know
    Too + adjective is followed by an infinitive: too young to know.
  28. Nobody in the office knew how ___ (handle) the angry customer.
    Answer: to handle
    A question word such as how can be followed by an infinitive: knew how to handle.
  29. The builders don't anticipate ___ (have) any problems with the new permit.
    Answer: having
    Anticipate is followed by a gerund, not an infinitive: anticipate having.
  30. New employees often have difficulty ___ (adjust) to the night shift.
    Accepted answers: adjusting / in adjusting
    Have difficulty is followed by an -ing form; in before it is possible but not needed.
  31. The cat sat by the window and watched the birds ___ (play) in the birdbath.
    Accepted answers: play / playing
    After watch + object, use the simple form or the -ing form. The -ing form stresses that the action was in progress.
  32. Instead of ___ (join) a gym, Leo bought a bike and rides it to work.
    Answer: joining
    Instead of ends in a preposition, so it is followed by a gerund: instead of joining.
  33. Halfway through the long meeting, our manager stopped ___ (let) everyone get a cup of coffee.
    Accepted answers: to let / in order to let
    Here the infinitive gives the purpose: the manager stopped in order to let everyone get coffee. Stopped letting would mean she no longer allowed it.
  34. The farmer opened the gate and let the sheep ___ (run) into the field.
    Answer: run
    Let is followed by the simple form of a verb: let the sheep run.
  35. I wouldn't mind ___ (stay) at the office a little later if it meant getting Friday off.
    Answer: staying
    Mind is followed by a gerund: wouldn't mind staying.
  36. Our grandparents wouldn't mind selling their big house and ___ (get) a small apartment near us.
    Answer: getting
    Two verbs joined by and after mind are both gerunds: selling ... and getting.
  37. Nobody likes ___ (force) to work on weekends.
    Accepted answers: being forced / to be forced
    Like can be followed by a gerund or an infinitive. The person receives the action, so a passive form is needed: being forced or to be forced.
  38. After the fire, the tenants were forced ___ (leave) the building for a week.
    Answer: to leave
    Force, in the active or the passive, is followed by an infinitive: were forced to leave.
  39. My brother goes to a cafe ___ (study) because our house is too noisy.
    Accepted answers: to study / in order to study
    An infinitive of purpose tells why someone does something: he goes to a cafe (in order) to study.
  40. Whenever the weather is nice, our boss decides ___ (have) the team meeting outdoors.
    Answer: to have
    Decide is followed by an infinitive, not a gerund: decides to have.
  41. Let's ___ (have) a pizza delivered tonight instead of cooking.
    Answer: have
    Let's (let us) is followed by the simple form of a verb: let's have.
  42. The principal had a firefighter ___ (join) the class for a talk about fire safety.
    Answer: join
    Causative have + person is followed by the simple form of a verb: had a firefighter join.
  43. Her ___ (come) to the meeting unprepared made the whole team look bad.
    Answer: coming
    A possessive such as her is followed by a gerund. Here her coming to the meeting unprepared is the subject of the sentence.
  44. Thank you for cooking dinner and ___ (leave) some for me in the fridge.
    Answer: leaving
    For is a preposition, so both verbs after it are gerunds: cooking ... and leaving.
  45. These rubber toys are made for ___ (chew), so the puppy won't damage the furniture.
    Answer: chewing
    For is a preposition, so it is followed by a gerund: for chewing.
  46. The robot's arm has two metal fingers for ___ (grab) small parts on the assembly line.
    Answer: grabbing
    For is a preposition, so it is followed by a gerund. Grab doubles the b before -ing: grabbing.
  47. This backpack has a side pocket for ___ (hold) a water bottle.
    Answer: holding
    For is a preposition, so it is followed by a gerund: for holding.
  48. Our paper shredder has sharp metal teeth for ___ (tear) old bank statements into thin strips.
    Answer: tearing
    For is a preposition, so it is followed by a gerund: for tearing.
  49. Many people ___ (swallow) pills more easily with a full glass of water.
    Answer: swallow
    Here the verb is the main verb of the sentence, not a gerund or an infinitive. It states a general fact with a plural subject, so the simple present swallow is used.
  50. Since moving to Tokyo, I can't seem ___ (get) used to the crowded trains.
    Answer: to get
    Seem is followed by an infinitive: can't seem to get.
  51. At the dog park, you can see dozens of dogs ___ (run) around without leashes.
    Accepted answers: run / running
    After see + object, use the simple form or the -ing form; -ing stresses that the action is in progress.
  52. We're considering ___ (have) the old carpet replaced with wood floors.
    Answer: having
    Consider is followed by a gerund: considering having.
  53. Every summer, the city has all the public parks ___ (spray) for mosquitoes.
    Answer: sprayed
    Have + thing + past participle has a passive meaning: workers spray the parks because the city arranges it.
  54. In January, the roads are too icy for most people ___ (commute) by bike.
    Answer: to commute
    Too + adjective (+ for + person) is followed by an infinitive: too icy for most people to commute.
  55. The company is considering ___ (move) its headquarters to Denver.
    Answer: moving
    Consider is followed by a gerund: considering moving.
  56. Ines took a job at the local hospital ___ (be) near her elderly father.
    Accepted answers: to be / in order to be
    An infinitive of purpose (in order to) explains why she took the job: to be near her father.
  57. Since he retired, my uncle spends most of his time ___ (do) crossword puzzles.
    Answer: doing
    Spend + time is followed by an -ing form: spends his time doing.
  58. The long flight made my legs ___ (feel) stiff and heavy.
    Answer: feel
    Make + object is followed by the simple form of a verb: made my legs feel.
  59. By Friday evening, I never have enough energy ___ (get) dressed up and go out.
    Answer: to get
    Enough + noun is followed by an infinitive: enough energy to get.
  60. During the concert, I could hear the man behind me ___ (cough) every few minutes.
    Accepted answers: cough / coughing
    After hear + object, use the simple form or the -ing form, not an infinitive.
  61. Through the thin wall, I could hear my brother ___ (sneeze) all night because of his allergies.
    Accepted answers: sneeze / sneezing
    After hear + object, use the simple form or the -ing form, not an infinitive.
  62. It was thoughtful of your neighbor ___ (ask) about your mother's health.
    Answer: to ask
    It + be + adjective (+ of + person) is followed by an infinitive: It was thoughtful of your neighbor to ask.
  63. The loud music from the party made our neighbors' dog ___ (go) crazy.
    Answer: go
    Make + object is followed by the simple form of a verb: made the dog go crazy.
  64. Correct the words in square brackets: My cousin moved to Boston [for study] engineering.
    Accepted answers: to study / in order to study
    Purpose is shown with to + the simple form (in order to), not with for: moved to Boston to study.
  65. Correct the words in square brackets: The comedy show was so funny that it made the whole audience [laughing] for an hour.
    Answer: laugh
    Make + object is followed by the simple form of a verb, not an -ing form: made the whole audience laugh.
  66. Correct the words in square brackets: The bus driver slowed down [for letting] a family of ducks cross the road.
    Accepted answers: to let / in order to let
    Purpose is shown with an infinitive (in order to), not with for + gerund: slowed down to let.
  67. Correct the words in square brackets: Our neighbors let their kids [playing] in the street until it gets dark.
    Answer: play
    Let is followed by the simple form of a verb, not an -ing form: let their kids play.
  68. Correct the words in square brackets: We're saving money [for to visit] our cousins in Brazil next summer.
    Accepted answers: to visit / in order to visit
    An infinitive of purpose is to + the simple form; for is never used before it.
  69. Correct the words in square brackets: The coach made every player [to promise contact] her before missing a practice.
    Answer: promise to contact
    Make + object is followed by the simple form (promise), and promise is followed by an infinitive (to contact).
  70. Correct the words in square brackets: My coworker let me [borrowed] her phone charger during the meeting.
    Answer: borrow
    Let is followed by the simple form of a verb, even when the sentence is in the past: let me borrow.
  71. Correct the words in square brackets: In the middle of the night, we heard the gate [to creak and banging] in the wind.
    Accepted answers: creaking and banging / creak and bang
    After hear + object, use the -ing form or the simple form, not an infinitive. Two verbs joined by and should have the same form.
  72. Correct the words in square brackets: The school had the library [to lend] each student a laptop for the semester.
    Answer: lend
    Causative have + person is followed by the simple form of a verb, not an infinitive: had the library lend.
  73. Correct the words in square brackets: Do we have enough volunteers [for beginning] the beach cleanup, or should we wait for more?
    Answer: to begin
    Enough + noun is followed by an infinitive, not for + gerund: enough volunteers to begin the cleanup.
  74. Correct the words in square brackets: My boss [wants that I finish] the report by Friday.
    Answer: wants me to finish
    Want is followed by an object + infinitive, not a that-clause: wants me to finish.
  75. Correct the words in square brackets: After weeks of rumors, the workers deserve [to be tell] whether the factory is closing.
    Answer: to be told
    A passive infinitive is to be + past participle, and the past participle of tell is told: deserve to be told.
  76. Correct the words in square brackets: I took my laptop to the repair shop [for having the screen to be replaced].
    Accepted answers: to have the screen replaced / in order to have the screen replaced / to get the screen replaced
    Purpose needs an infinitive, and have + thing + past participle gives the passive meaning: to have the screen replaced.
  77. Correct the words in square brackets: You look exhausted. Sit down and [let me to make] you a cup of tea.
    Answer: let me make
    Let is followed by the simple form of a verb, not an infinitive: let me make.
  78. Correct the words in square brackets: The blood test results were unclear, so the doctor had another sample [taking] the next day.
    Answer: taken
    Have + thing + past participle has a passive meaning: had another sample taken.
  79. Correct the words in square brackets: After the hike, our muddy boots [need to washing] before we bring them inside.
    Accepted answers: need washing / need to be washed
    Need with a passive meaning is followed by a gerund (need washing) or a passive infinitive (need to be washed).
  80. Correct the words in square brackets: I found my brother lying on the sofa [look] at old photos on his phone.
    Answer: looking
    Lie + place is followed by an -ing form for an action happening at the same time: lying on the sofa looking at photos.
  81. Correct the words in square brackets: The long silence on the phone made my mother [to worry] that something was wrong.
    Answer: worry
    Make + object is followed by the simple form of a verb, not an infinitive: made my mother worry.
  82. Read the passage: "Watering houseplants too often is the most common mistake. Most plants prefer soil that dries out a little. Checking the soil with your finger is an easy test. Placing the pot near a sunny window also helps. If the leaves turn yellow, the plant may need less water." How many sentences begin with a gerund?
    Three sentences begin with a gerund as the subject: Watering, Checking and Placing. Most, If and the other first words are not gerunds.
  83. Read the passage: "Getting a good night's sleep is not always easy. Experts advise people to avoid screens for an hour before bed. It also helps to keep the bedroom cool and dark. If sleep doesn't come, try reading a book instead of checking your phone. Some people find it useful to write down their worries before turning off the light." How many infinitives (to + verb) are in the passage?
    The infinitives are to avoid, to keep and to write. Getting, reading, checking and turning are gerunds, not infinitives.
