Understanding and Using English Grammar Chapter 2 Review – Perfect and Perfect Progressive Tenses

Choose or type the answer to each question, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. How long has your brother ___ how to drive?
    The present perfect is have/has + past participle. The past participle of know is known, so has ... know is wrong.
  2. The kids ___ at the pool since lunchtime.
    Since gives the starting point of a situation that continues up to now, so the present perfect is needed. The simple present (are) can't be used with since.
  3. ___ you ever ridden a camel?
    Questions with ever about experience up to now use the present perfect: have + subject + past participle. Did is followed by the base form (ride), not the past participle ridden.
  4. ___ since your surgery? I heard it went well.
    In fast speech have can sound very weak, but it can't be left out: the present perfect question is question word + have + subject + been.
  5. Nadia ___ her own bakery since 2021, and business is good.
    Since 2021 shows a situation that began in the past and continues now, so the present perfect is needed. The simple past (owned) is for situations that are finished.
  6. How long ___ studying Korean? Your accent is excellent.
    In a question, the helping verb have comes before the subject: How long have you been ...? You have been is statement word order.
  7. Leo ___ video games since he finished dinner, and he still hasn't started his homework.
    Since he finished dinner shows an activity from a past time up to now, so the present perfect progressive is needed. The present progressive (is playing) can't be used with since.
  8. Grace ___ three cups of coffee that afternoon, so she couldn't fall asleep.
    The past perfect shows an action that came before another past event (not being able to sleep). Has drunk is wrong because the story is in the past and that afternoon is finished.
  9. ___ to see the northern lights, but the sky stayed cloudy all week.
    Here 'd is the short form of had, and had is followed by a past participle (hoped). We'd hope means we would hope, which doesn't fit a finished past story.
  10. The kitchen floor was covered in flour because the kids ___ cookies all afternoon.
    The past perfect progressive explains a past situation (the floor was covered in flour) with an activity in progress just before it. Have been baking is wrong because the situation is in the past.
  11. Read the sentences: (a) When the bell rang, Maya was eating her lunch. (b) When the bell rang, Leon ate his lunch. Who started eating before the bell rang?
    The past progressive (was eating) shows an activity already in progress when the bell rang. The simple past (ate) shows Leon started after the bell.
  12. Read the sentences: (a) Mr. Reyes coached the swim team for six years. (b) Ms. Okafor has coached the swim team for six years. Who is coaching the swim team now?
    The present perfect (has coached) shows a situation that began in the past and continues now. The simple past (coached) shows Mr. Reyes's job has ended.
  13. Read the sentences: (a) Hiro was unlocking the gate when the delivery truck pulled up. (b) Theo walked out to the gate after the delivery truck pulled up. Who had been expecting the delivery?
    Hiro was already unlocking the gate when the truck arrived, so he knew it was coming. Theo only went to the gate after the truck arrived.
  14. Read the sentences: (a) Rosa worked at the city library for ten years. (b) Victor has been working at the city library for ten years. Who still works at the library?
    The present perfect progressive (has been working) shows an activity that continues up to now. The simple past (worked) shows Rosa's job is finished.
  15. Read the sentences: (a) Nina took off her jacket because she had been raking leaves. (b) Ella took off her jacket because she was raking leaves. Who took off her jacket after she finished raking?
    The past perfect progressive (had been raking) shows an activity that went on before another past event, so Nina had stopped raking. Was raking shows Ella was still raking.
  16. Read the sentences: (a) I turned around. My brother was laughing at me. (b) I turned around. My sister laughed at me. Who started laughing before I turned around?
    Was laughing shows an activity already in progress when I turned around. Laughed shows my sister started laughing after I turned around.
  17. Read the sentences: (a) Carla was driving out of the parking lot when I arrived. (b) Mark had driven out of the parking lot when I arrived. Who did I see when I arrived?
    Was driving shows Carla was still leaving at the moment I arrived, so I saw her. Had driven shows Mark was already gone before I arrived.
  18. Read the sentences: (a) Dev ordered more printer paper because the office was running out of it. (b) Lisa ordered more printer paper because the office had run out of it. Who is better at planning ahead?
    Was running out shows Dev ordered before the paper was gone. Had run out shows the paper was already gone when Lisa ordered more.
  19. Read the sentences: (a) Amir had been taking piano lessons since he was six. He played beautifully by the time he went to college. (b) Ben has been taking piano lessons since he was six. He's getting better every year. Who is still taking piano lessons?
    The present perfect progressive (has been taking) shows an activity that continues up to now. Had been taking describes a time up to a past event (going to college).
  20. Relax, it's not an earthquake. Our upstairs neighbor ___ just moving some furniture around.
    The noise is happening right now, so the present progressive (is + -ing) is needed. Has and had can't be followed by an -ing verb to make a tense.
  21. I can't get into my apartment because I ___ left my keys at the office.
    The present perfect (have + past participle) shows a past action with a result now: the speaker can't get in. Had left would need a later past event, and was can't come before left here.
  22. The lights went out while the students ___ taking their final exam.
    The past progressive (were + -ing) shows an activity in progress when another past event happened. Had and have can't be followed by taking to make a tense.
  23. I ___ never been on a sailboat, so I'm a little nervous about tomorrow's trip.
    Have never + past participle talks about experience up to now. Had never is wrong because the speaker is talking about the present (I'm nervous), not about a time before a past event.
  24. My neighbor ___ never used a smartphone before his kids gave him one for his eightieth birthday.
    The past perfect (had + past participle) shows something that was true up to a past event, the birthday gift. Have never can't be used with a finished past time like before his kids gave him one.
  25. The soup ___ starting to boil when the doorbell rang.
    Was + -ing shows an activity in progress at the moment another past event happened. Had and have can't be followed by starting to make a tense.
  26. My mom thought I ___ forgotten her birthday because my card arrived three days late.
    Had forgotten shows something that came before a past moment (when she thought it). After a past verb like thought, have forgotten doesn't fit the past time frame.
  27. Our town's team ___ never won a championship, so tonight's final is a big deal for everyone.
    Has never + past participle describes experience up to now, and tonight's final puts the sentence in the present, so had doesn't fit. Is can't be followed by won here.
  28. When I opened the old box, I found letters that my grandmother ___ written to my grandfather during the war.
    Had written shows an action that came before another past action (finding the letters). Have written doesn't fit a story told in the past, and was can't come before written here.
  29. Our class ___ planned a picnic for Friday, but heavy rain was in the forecast, so we stayed inside.
    Had planned shows a plan made before other past events (the forecast and staying inside). Has planned doesn't fit a story that is all in the past.
  30. When we got to the museum, it was closed because the building ___ been damaged in a storm the night before.
    Had been damaged shows an event that happened before we got there. Was can't come before been, and has doesn't fit a finished past time like the night before.
  31. These days, finding an affordable apartment in our city ___ getting harder and harder.
    Is getting describes a change in progress now (these days). Was would put the change in the past, and has can't be followed by getting.
  32. Our soccer team ___ lost its best player to an injury, so the coach needs to change the lineup.
    Has lost connects a past event to a present result (the coach needs to change the lineup now). Had lost would need a later past event, and the sentence is about now.
  33. The concert tickets ___ just gone on sale, so let's buy ours before they sell out.
    Have just + past participle shows a very recent action with a result now (we can buy tickets now). Had just would need a later past event, and was can't come before gone here.
  34. Correct the words in square brackets: Since Nora joined the hiking club, she [is making] a lot of new friends.
    Accepted answers: has been making / 's been making / has made / 's made
    Since + a past time shows something that started then and continues up to now, so the present perfect (progressive) is needed, not the present progressive.
  35. Correct the words in square brackets: My cousin [move] to Denver just a few weeks ago, so she doesn't know many people yet.
    Answer: moved
    Ago marks a finished past time, so the simple past is needed: moved.
  36. Correct the words in square brackets: The power is out, and we [are] without heat since Tuesday morning.
    Accepted answers: have been / 've been
    Since Tuesday morning shows a situation from a past time up to now, so the present perfect (have been) is needed, not the simple present.
  37. Correct the words in square brackets: How long [you are working] at the hospital? You seem to know everyone.
    Accepted answers: have you been working / have you worked
    How long asks about a situation from the past up to now, so the present perfect (progressive) is needed, with have before the subject in a question.
  38. Correct the words in square brackets: [We been] married for almost twelve years, and we still laugh at the same jokes.
    Accepted answers: We have been / We've been
    The present perfect needs the helping verb have: we have been (we've been). Been can't be used alone after the subject.
  39. Correct the words in square brackets: Why [you no have answered] any of my messages this week?
    Accepted answers: haven't you answered / have you not answered / haven't you been answering / have you not been answering
    A negative present perfect question puts haven't (or have ... not) before the subject: Why haven't you answered ...? No can't be used to make a verb negative.
  40. Correct the words in square brackets: My dad [is volunteering] at the animal shelter for the past six months.
    Accepted answers: has been volunteering / 's been volunteering / has volunteered / 's volunteered
    For the past six months shows an activity from a past time up to now, so the present perfect progressive is needed, not the present progressive.
  41. Correct the words in square brackets: During college, Marta [had lived] in a tiny dorm room with two roommates.
    Accepted answers: lived / used to live
    This is a past situation with no earlier past event to compare it with, so the simple past is used. The past perfect is only for something that happened before another past time.
  42. Correct the words in square brackets: The old theater [has close] before I moved to this town, so I never saw a show there.
    Accepted answers: had closed / closed
    The theater closed before another past event (I moved), so the past perfect had closed fits. Has close is wrong: the present perfect needs a past participle and can't be used in a past story.
  43. Correct the words in square brackets: My grandparents left Ireland ten years before my mother [is born].
    Answer: was born
    Birth is a finished past event, so the simple past passive was born is used. Is born doesn't fit a past time.
  44. Correct the words in square brackets: [We're staying] at a cheap hostel since we got to Lisbon.
    Accepted answers: We've been staying / We have been staying / We've stayed / We have stayed
    Since we got to Lisbon shows an activity from a past time up to now, so the present perfect progressive is needed, not the present progressive.
  45. Correct the words in square brackets: Priya has been feeling homesick ever since she [has started] college in another state.
    Answer: started
    The since clause names a starting point at a finished past time, so it takes the simple past. The present perfect goes in the main clause, not after since.
  46. Correct the words in square brackets: My new studio is close to work, but [it very] dark.
    Accepted answers: it's very / it is very
    An adjective after the subject needs the verb be: it is very dark. The verb can't be left out.
  47. Correct the words in square brackets: [We are needing] a bigger car now that we have three kids.
    Answer: We need
    Need is a stative (non-action) verb, so it is not normally used in progressive forms. The simple present we need is correct.
  48. Correct the words in square brackets: I've been to five stores today, and so far [I don't find] a birthday gift for my sister.
    Accepted answers: I haven't found / I have not found
    So far means up to now, so the present perfect is needed: I haven't found.
  49. Correct the words in square brackets: In the 1990s, the Garcia family [had lived] in an apartment above their shop on Main Street.
    Accepted answers: lived / used to live
    This is a past situation at a stated time (in the 1990s) with no earlier past event to compare it with, so the simple past is used, not the past perfect.
  50. Correct the words in square brackets: In 2018, the company [had moved] its headquarters to Atlanta.
    Answer: moved
    A past event at a stated time (in 2018) takes the simple past. The past perfect is only needed to show an event before another past event.
  51. Correct the words in square brackets: The two scientists [had met and had married] while they were working in Antarctica in the 1980s.
    Accepted answers: met and married / met and got married
    Events at a stated past time take the simple past. Nothing here happened before another past event, so the past perfect is wrong.
  52. Correct the words in square brackets: The famous poet [had been born] in a tiny fishing village in 1921.
    Answer: was born
    Birth at a stated past time takes the simple past passive: was born. There is no later past event, so the past perfect is wrong.
  53. Correct the words in square brackets: I [am born] in a small town near the Canadian border, but I grew up in Boston.
    Answer: was born
    Being born is a finished past event, so the simple past passive was born is used, not the present.
