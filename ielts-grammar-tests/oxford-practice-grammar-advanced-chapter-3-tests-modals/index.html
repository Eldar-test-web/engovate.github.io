Oxford Practice Grammar Advanced Chapter 3 Tests – Modals

Choose or type the answer to each question, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. Which of these sentences contains a negative modal?
    Won't is the negative form of the modal will. Wasn't is a negative form of be, which is not a modal, and might is a modal but it is positive here.
  2. Which sentence contains three different modals?
    This sentence uses three different modals: can't, should and may. The guitar sentence uses the same modal (can) twice, and the sentence about resting has only two modals.
  3. Choose the best final sentence for this paragraph: There are lots of home remedies for a cold, such as hot lemon with honey, chicken soup or a spoonful of raw garlic. Trying all of them at the same time won't necessarily help you get better any faster, though. ___
    After saying that trying every remedy won't necessarily help, the paragraph ends with a prediction with will about how other people will react. The other endings refer to an idea spreading and to wet hair, which this paragraph doesn't mention.
  4. Choose the best final sentence for this paragraph: Many people believe that eating carrots helps you see in the dark. The origin of this belief is surprising. According to one popular story, it may have started during World War II, when the British government wanted to hide the fact that its pilots were using radar. ___
    The paragraph gives a possible origin with may have started, so the final sentence comments on what that origin shows. The other endings talk about a body position and about some animals, which don't appear in the paragraph.
  5. Choose the best final sentence for this paragraph: Some people say that you can stop hiccups by drinking water from the far side of a glass. You don't have to drink the whole glass, only a few sips while you bend forward over it. ___
    The paragraph describes bending forward over the glass, so the final sentence comments on that position, using may to say what is possible. The paragraph explains no origin and says nothing about hair.
  6. Choose the best final sentence for this paragraph: Older relatives often tell us that we shouldn't go outside with wet hair in winter because we will catch a cold. Nobody explains how wet hair could cause an illness, but few of us are going to test the idea. ___
    After 'few of us are going to test the idea', the paragraph ends by saying what we do instead: we simply follow the advice. The other endings refer to a body position and to some animals that the paragraph doesn't mention.
  7. Choose the best final sentence for this paragraph: My neighbor says that when cows lie down in a field, rain is on the way. Yesterday the cows on her farm were all lying down, yet the afternoon stayed dry and sunny. They must have had some other reason for resting. ___
    Must have had is a deduction about the past, and the final sentence suggests one possible reason with perhaps and the past perfect. The other endings are about people watching and wet hair, not about the cows.
  8. All the receptionists at this hotel are ___ to speak at least two languages.
    Be supposed to means 'be expected to', and only supposed can follow are here. Ought and should are modals, so they can't come after are, and 'are have' is not a possible verb form.
  9. Under the new policy, employees ___ to work from home two days a week.
    Be allowed to expresses permission and is followed by to + verb. Cannot, may and shall are modals, which are followed by the base form without to.
  10. Daniel ___ have fixed the leak himself if he'd really tried, but he called a plumber instead.
    Could have + past participle describes an ability that was not used in an unreal past situation (if he'd really tried). Must have would mean we are sure he did fix it, which contradicts calling a plumber, and may have and will have don't fit this unreal condition.
  11. Our team ___ be the smallest in the league, but we won more games than anyone else this season.
    May ... but is used to admit that something is true before a contrast. Could would suggest the speaker isn't sure about a known fact, and can and would don't express this meaning.
  12. ___ one of you please help me carry these boxes up to the third floor?
    Can is used to ask other people to do something. May asks for permission with I or we, not for help from others, and must and should don't make polite requests.
  13. Which expression must be changed to correct the sentence "Experts warn that food prices can be going to rise again this winter, which might hurt families on low incomes, so the government should act now"?
    Can is not used for a possible single event in the future, so it should be may (or might): may be going to rise. Might hurt expresses possibility correctly, and should gives advice.
  14. Which expression must be changed to correct the sentence "Mia ought not apologize so much: she has to work late tonight, and she was going to call to ask if we would mind starting dinner without her"?
    In standard English, ought is followed by to, also in the negative: ought not to apologize. The other expressions are correct: has to shows necessity and would mind makes a polite request.
  15. Which expression must be changed to correct the sentence "Omar's car may have been damaged in the flood, and he probably won't be able to drive it for weeks. When I heard this yesterday, I thought that I will lend him mine"?
    After a past verb of thinking (thought), will changes to would: I thought that I would lend him mine. May have been is a correct guess about the past, and won't be able to is a correct prediction.
  16. Which expression must be changed to correct the sentence "My grandmother would always answer 'Yes, you could' whenever we asked her 'Could we stay up late?', and she would even make us hot chocolate"?
    Could is a polite way to ask for permission, but the reply that gives permission uses can: Yes, you can. The two uses of would correctly describe what she did regularly in the past.
  17. Which expression must be changed to correct the sentence "Daycare costs so much that I wish I would have a job closer to home: then I could work part-time, I would be able to pick up the kids myself and I wouldn't have to pay a babysitter"?
    After wish, we use the past simple for a present situation we want to be different: I wish I had a job closer to home. Could, would be able to and have to are correct in the imagined result.
  18. Because you have only just moved here, you ___ with the local recycling rules, so we have attached a short guide.
    May not be familiar says it is possible that the person doesn't know the rules yet, which is why a guide is attached. The other combinations make no sense with 'with the local recycling rules'.
  19. This gift card ___ in any of our stores, but not on our website.
    Can be used says what is possible or permitted with the gift card. Must be paid and may not be familiar don't describe what a customer does with a gift card.
  20. The full fee for the course ___ before the first class, or your place will be given to someone else.
    Must be paid expresses an obligation, which matches the warning 'or your place will be given to someone else'. Won't be required would mean there is no need to pay, which contradicts the warning.
  21. If you book your ticket online, you ___ to show a printed copy at the entrance.
    Won't be required to means it will not be necessary to do something. Must be paid and may not be familiar can't be followed by to show and don't fit the meaning.
  22. If you return the rental car a day late, you ___ for the extra day.
    Will be charged predicts the certain result of returning the car late: you will have to pay. Won't be required and can be used make no sense with 'for the extra day' here.
  23. Visitors may not take photos inside the gallery. Same meaning: Visitors are ___ photos inside the gallery.
    Accepted answers: not allowed to take / not permitted to take / forbidden to take / prohibited from taking
    May not here means that permission is refused, so it becomes are not allowed to (or not permitted to) + base form.
  24. It's possible that the package was delivered to the wrong address. Same meaning: The package may ___ to the wrong address.
    Answer: have been delivered
    May have + past participle expresses a possibility about the past, and because the package receives the action, the passive have been delivered is needed.
  25. Our team had to work every other weekend last year, and nobody enjoyed it. Same meaning: Nobody on our team enjoyed ___ every other weekend last year.
    Answer: having to work
    Enjoy is followed by a gerund, so had to becomes having to: enjoyed having to work.
  26. It was impossible for the ancient builders to have moved these huge stones without some kind of machine. Same meaning: The ancient builders ___ these huge stones without some kind of machine.
    Accepted answers: couldn't have moved / could not have moved / can't have moved / cannot have moved
    Couldn't have (or can't have) + past participle says that something in the past was impossible.
  27. It was a bad idea to leave the cake in the oven for so long. Same meaning: The cake ___ in the oven for so long.
    Accepted answers: shouldn't have been left / should not have been left / ought not to have been left / oughtn't to have been left
    Shouldn't have + past participle criticizes a past action, and because the cake is what was left, the passive have been left is used.
  28. When the phone rang at 3 a.m., Leo was afraid it ___ be bad news about his grandfather.
    Might expresses a possibility that someone thinks of or worries about. Should doesn't express a feared possibility, was able to describes ability, and be able to needs a verb before it.
  29. The sign outside the restaurant had so many mistakes that it looked as if someone who ___ spell had written it.
    Couldn't expresses a lack of ability, here the ability to spell. Should, had to and was going to don't describe what someone is unable to do.
  30. When the fog finally lifted, the pilot ___ see the runway lights clearly.
    Was able to describes something someone managed to do on a particular occasion in the past. Be able to needs a verb before it, and should doesn't fit a past event.
  31. When I called the repair shop this morning, the mechanic told me that I ___ be able to pick up my car by five.
    Would is used after a past reporting verb (told) for what was the future at that time ('You'll be able to ...'). Couldn't and was able to can't come before be able to.
  32. My neighbor said she would ___ lend me her ladder on Saturday.
    After the modal would we need a base form, so be able to is correct. Was able to, couldn't and had to can't follow would.
  33. The festival organizers were hoping that local residents ___ be willing to volunteer at the information desk.
    Would after a past verb like were hoping expresses what someone hoped would happen. Couldn't gives the opposite of what they hoped, and was able to can't come before be willing to.
  34. Do you think your manager would ___ let you work from home on Fridays?
    After would we need a base form, and be willing to asks whether someone would agree to do something. Was going to, had to and should can't follow would.
  35. Halfway up the hill, Mr. Ortiz was so out of breath that he ___ stop and rest for a few minutes.
    Had to expresses a past necessity: he needed to stop because he was out of breath. Should doesn't fit this past story, and be willing to needs a verb before it.
  36. In my opinion, people who leave their trash on the beach ___ be fined.
    Should gives a strong opinion about what ought to happen, here followed by the passive be fined. Was going to and was able to don't agree with the plural subject, and be able to needs a verb before it.
  37. I ___ send the email when I noticed that I had attached the wrong file.
    Was going to describes something someone intended to do in the past but didn't do because something stopped it; just can also go inside it (I was just going to send). Should doesn't fit this past situation, and be able to needs a verb before it.
