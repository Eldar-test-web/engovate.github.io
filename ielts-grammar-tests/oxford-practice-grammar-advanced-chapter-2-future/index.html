Oxford Practice Grammar Advanced Chapter 2 – Future

Choose or type the answer to each question, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. Which sentence uses will to give or ask for information about the future?
    Will + base form can simply give information about the future, such as when a museum opens. The other two sentences use will for a threat and a request.
  2. Which sentence uses will to make a promise, request or threat?
    Will is used for promises, requests and threats, and "I promise" shows this is a promise. The other two sentences only give or ask for information about the future.
  3. Which sentence uses the future continuous for an action in progress at a particular time in the future?
    The future continuous (will be + -ing) describes an action in progress at a particular future time, here ten o'clock tomorrow. "We'll have landed" is the future perfect, and "leaves" is the present simple for a timetable.
  4. Which sentence uses the future continuous to talk about a plan or intention?
    The future continuous can express a plan or intention, as in we'll be moving in March. The other sentences use the future perfect and will for a decision made on the spot.
  5. Which sentence uses the future perfect to say that something will be completed by a particular time?
    The future perfect (will have + past participle) says something will be complete by a certain time, here December. "I'll have been working" is the future perfect continuous for an action that is still going on, not a completed one.
  6. Which sentence uses the future perfect continuous for an action lasting from an earlier point up to a future time?
    The future perfect continuous (will have been + -ing) looks back from a future time at an action that started earlier and is still going on. "Will have sold" is the future perfect for a completed action.
  7. Which sentence uses will for a prediction based on past experience or knowledge?
    Will is used for a prediction based on what we know from experience, here Kevin's habit of replying slowly. The other two sentences use be going to for present evidence and a decision.
  8. Which sentence uses will in a predictive conditional?
    A predictive conditional uses will in the main clause to say what will happen if something else happens. The other sentences use an imperative and the present simple for a timetable.
  9. Which sentence uses be going to for a prediction based on what we feel or think now?
    Be going to is used for a prediction based on what we can see or feel now, here a ladder starting to fall. The paint sentence is a decision already made, and "was going to" is a past prediction.
  10. Which sentence uses would or was/were going to for a past prediction about the future?
    Would can report a prediction made in the past about a later time, here what people thought in college. "I'd like" is a polite request, and "We're going to move" is a present plan.
  11. Which sentence uses be going to for a decision already made?
    Be going to shows a decision made before the moment of speaking, as "We've talked it over" makes clear. "You're going to spill" is a prediction from present evidence, and "I'll help" is a decision made on the spot.
  12. Which sentence uses will for a decision made at that moment?
    Will is used for a decision made at the moment of speaking, here as a reaction to hearing about the milk. "I'm going to apply" is a decision made earlier, and "starts" is a timetable.
  13. Which sentence uses the present simple for a future event in a schedule or timetable?
    The present simple is used for future events on a fixed schedule, such as tour times. "I'm having lunch" is a personal arrangement, and "will have started" is the future perfect.
  14. Which sentence uses the present simple for a future action in a clause after a subordinating conjunction?
    After conjunctions such as as soon as, when and before, the present simple refers to the future, not will. "Grandma's train gets in" is also present simple, but it is a timetable, not a clause after a conjunction.
  15. Which sentence uses the present continuous for a future action that has been planned or arranged?
    The present continuous is used for future actions we have planned or arranged, such as inviting friends for Saturday. The other sentences use will in a conditional and the future perfect.
  16. The first Saturday in June ___ the opening day of the city's new botanical garden.
    Will be gives information about a future date or event. Will alone needs another verb, and will have been would look back from a later time.
  17. By the time the new school building opens, the old one ___ in use for over a century.
    Will have been is used with for to measure a period up to a future point, here the opening of the new building. Will be does not show the time leading up to that point.
  18. After a ten-hour flight, most passengers ___ need a good night's sleep.
    Will + base form makes a prediction, and here the base form need follows it. Will be need and will have been need are not possible.
  19. ___ at home tomorrow evening, or should I leave the package with your neighbor?
    In a question about a future state, will comes before the subject and be comes after it: Will you be at home? Will you at home has no verb, and the perfect does not fit a single future moment.
  20. Throughout next week, the band ___ performing in small towns along the coast.
    Will be + -ing (the future continuous) describes actions in progress during a future period, here next week. Will performing is not possible, and will have been performing needs a later point to look back from.
  21. When the race ends on Sunday, the cyclists ___ riding for over twenty days.
    Will have been + -ing (the future perfect continuous) is used with for to look back from a future point, the end of the race, at an activity that started earlier.
  22. At midnight tonight, the sale ___ over, so place your order now.
    Over needs the verb be, so will be is used for this future state. Will have been over does not fit a single point in time like midnight.
  23. When the old satellite finally stops working, it ___ in orbit for over twenty years.
    Will have been is used with for to measure a period up to a future point, here the day the satellite stops working. Will be would not look back over the years before that point.
  24. The message inside my fortune cookie said, "You ___ (have) an unexpected visitor this week."
    Accepted answers: will have / 'll have
    Will is the usual form in written predictions such as fortune cookies and horoscopes, which are not based on present evidence.
  25. The taxi was speeding toward the intersection, and I could tell that the driver ___ (not/stop) at the red light.
    Accepted answers: wasn't going to stop / was not going to stop / wouldn't stop / would not stop
    A prediction made in the past about a later time uses was going to or would. Here the taxi's speed was the evidence at that moment.
  26. If we ___ (not/start) the meeting on time, we'll never get through the whole agenda.
    Accepted answers: don't start / do not start
    In an if-clause about the future, the present simple is used, not will, so the negative is don't start.
  27. Look at all this traffic! At this rate, we ___ (be) late for the wedding.
    Accepted answers: 'll be / will be / 're going to be / are going to be
    This is a prediction about the future, so will be or are going to be both work; the traffic is present evidence, which suits be going to.
  28. "I can't carry all these boxes up the stairs by myself." "Don't worry, I ___ (give) you a hand."
    Accepted answers: 'll give / will give
    Will is used for an offer or decision made at the moment of speaking, as a reaction to what someone has just said.
  29. Long walks in the fresh air always ___ (make) me sleepy.
    Answer: make
    The present simple is used for general truths and habits, not only for the future. The subject long walks is plural, so the form is make.
  30. Correct the words in square brackets: Scientists warn that the volcano [erupts] again sometime in the next fifty years.
    Accepted answers: will erupt / is going to erupt
    A prediction about a future event needs will or be going to. The present simple is only used for future events on a fixed schedule or timetable.
  31. Correct the words in square brackets: Give me back my charger by tonight, or [I tell] Mom you took it without asking.
    Accepted answers: I'll tell / I will tell / I'm going to tell / I am going to tell
    A threat about the future needs will (or be going to). The present simple I tell cannot express a threat.
  32. Correct the words in square brackets: Let's split the bill this time, [will we]?
    Answer: shall we
    After Let's, the question tag for a suggestion is shall we. Will we is not used with Let's.
  33. Correct the words in square brackets: Your bags look really heavy. Wait, [I carry] one of them for you.
    Accepted answers: I'll carry / I will carry
    An offer decided at the moment of speaking needs will. The present simple I carry does not express an offer.
  34. Correct the words in square brackets: By the time it reaches Jupiter, the space probe [will travel] more than 600 million kilometers.
    Accepted answers: will have traveled / will have travelled
    The future perfect (will have + past participle) gives a total that will be complete by a future time, here the moment the probe reaches Jupiter.
  35. Correct the words in square brackets: We [are going to] take the early train yesterday, but it was canceled because of the snow.
    Answer: were going to
    Was/were going to describes a past plan that did not happen. Yesterday puts the plan in the past, so are going to is wrong.
  36. Correct the words in square brackets: Let's not call the house; it's after eleven, so the kids [will fall] asleep by now.
    Answer: will have fallen
    Will have + past participle can express a confident guess that something has already happened by now. Will fall would refer to a later time.
  37. Correct the words in square brackets: Take an umbrella with you, because I think [it is raining] again before the game ends.
    Accepted answers: it'll rain / it will rain / it's going to rain / it is going to rain
    Predictions about the weather use will or be going to. The present continuous is for future actions people have arranged, and nobody can arrange rain.
  38. Correct the words in square brackets: I think the new phone [to be] in stores soon.
    Accepted answers: will be / is going to be / is about to be
    After I think, a future event needs a future form such as will be, is going to be or is about to be. Think + object + to be does not express the future.
  39. Correct the words in square brackets: Please don't call me between two and three tomorrow; [I will drive] the kids to their swimming lesson at that time.
    Accepted answers: I'll be driving / I will be driving
    The future continuous (will be + -ing) describes an action in progress at a particular future time, here between two and three tomorrow.
  40. Correct the words in square brackets: If the bus [will come] on time, we'll make it to the concert.
    Answer: comes
    After if, the present simple is used for a future condition, not will.
  41. Correct the words in square brackets: Don't rush if you're running late; [I wait] for you in the lobby, I promise.
    Accepted answers: I'll wait / I will wait
    A promise made at the moment of speaking needs will. The present simple I wait cannot express a promise.
  42. Correct the words in square brackets: [Will Ms. Patel to sign] the contract tomorrow, or does she need more time?
    Accepted answers: Is Ms. Patel to sign / Will Ms. Patel sign / Is Ms. Patel signing / Is Ms. Patel going to sign / Will Ms. Patel be signing
    Be to + base form asks about an arrangement or instruction: Is Ms. Patel to sign? Will is followed by the base form without to: Will Ms. Patel sign?
  43. Correct the words in square brackets: The plumber is here. Is he to wait for you, or [is to begin] work on the sink right away?
    Accepted answers: is he to begin / will he begin / is he beginning / is he going to begin
    A question needs a subject after is: is he to begin? Will he begin and is he beginning are also correct ways to ask about this future action.
  44. Correct the words in square brackets: Let's buy the tickets online tonight, before the prices [will go] up.
    Answer: go
    After before, the present simple is used for a future action, not will.
  45. Correct the words in square brackets: Hurry up and send the application, or it [doesn't reach] the office before the deadline.
    Accepted answers: won't reach / will not reach
    After or (meaning if not), the result is a prediction about the future, so it needs won't, not the present simple.
