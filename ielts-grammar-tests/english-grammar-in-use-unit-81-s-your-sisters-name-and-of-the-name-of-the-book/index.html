English Grammar in Use Unit 81 – -‘s (Your Sister’s Name) and Of… (The Name Of The Book)

Choose or type the answer to each question, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. We're painting the kitchen this weekend. Do you like ___?
    For things, we normally use of: the color of the walls. We use -'s mostly for people and animals, so the walls' color sounds wrong.
  2. ___ have just started kindergarten.
    For people, we normally use -'s: Priya's twins. The twins of Priya is not natural.
  3. Is this ___? Someone left it in the back seat of your car.
    Friend is a person, so we use -'s: your friend's jacket. The jacket of your friend is not natural.
  4. Please sign your name at ___.
    We say the top/bottom/middle/end of something with of, not -'s: the bottom of the form.
  5. We're having lunch with ___ on Sunday.
    For a person, we use -'s, also after a name ending in -s: Thomas's wife. The wife of Thomas is not natural.
  6. ___ keeps barking at night, and the whole street can hear it.
    With two people who share something, we put -'s after the second name only: Mark and Sofia's new puppy.
  7. The train has been stuck for an hour, and the engineers still haven't found ___.
    A delay is not a person or animal, so we normally use of: the cause of the delay.
  8. Do you like ___? The beans are from Colombia.
    Coffee is a thing, so we normally use of: the taste of this coffee. We use -'s mostly for people and animals.
  9. ___ are all over the living room floor again.
    Children is a plural that doesn't end in -s, so we add -'s: the children's toys.
  10. Have you seen ___? It's gone up again.
    A laptop is a thing, so we normally use of: the price of a new laptop.
  11. The Garcias next door have a dog. ___ barks every time a car goes by.
    With people we use -'s. Neighbors is a plural ending in -s, so we add only the apostrophe after the s: our neighbors' dog.
  12. ___ has gone completely gray.
    For a person, we use -'s: Uncle Leo's beard. The beard of Uncle Leo is not natural.
  13. Let's sit at ___. There are more empty seats there.
    We say the front/back/side of something with of: the back of the bus.
  14. I can't come to the barbecue on Saturday. I'm going to ___.
    Cousin is a person, so we use -'s: my cousin's wedding. The wedding of my cousin is not natural.
  15. Lena is dating ___.
    We don't use -'s after a long group of words, so we use of: the son of a woman I work with.
  16. We spent the weekend at ___ in the mountains.
    Both owners are people, so we use -'s twice: Nina's grandparents' cabin. Grandparents ends in -s, so it takes only an apostrophe.
  17. Do you know ___? There's a red circle with a bike in it.
    A sign is a thing, so we normally use of: the meaning of this sign.
  18. Many residents disagree with ___ to close the public pool.
    A city council is an organization (a group of people), so we can use of or -'s: the decision of the city council or the city council's decision.
  19. The vet says our ___ leg is broken, so she will need to rest for a few weeks.
    The leg belongs to one animal, so we use the singular with -'s: our horse's leg. Horses' would mean more than one horse.
  20. My ___ office is on the fifth floor of the hospital where she works as a surgeon.
    The office belongs to one person, so we use -'s: my mother's office. Mothers' would mean more than one mother.
  21. The ___ in our garden are finally turning red.
    Nothing belongs to the tomatoes here, so we need the ordinary plural with no apostrophe: tomatoes.
  22. The library has a big ___ section with picture books and comics.
    Children is a plural that doesn't end in -s, so we add -'s: a children's section (a section for children).
  23. Almost half of ___ population lives in or near Seoul.
    We can use -'s with places and countries: South Korea's population.
  24. We're having dinner at my ___ house tonight. They're cooking for the whole family.
    The house belongs to both parents, a plural ending in -s, so we add only an apostrophe: my parents' house.
  25. My phone is almost full because it has over ten thousand ___ on it.
    Nothing belongs to the photos here, so we need the ordinary plural with no apostrophe: photos.
  26. I didn't write this note. The handwriting is ___.
    The handwriting belongs to another person, so we add -'s to the whole phrase: someone else's. We can use -'s without a noun after it.
  27. In English class, we're reading one of ___ novels.
    The novels were written by one person, so we use -'s after the name: Mark Twain's novels.
  28. Rewrite "The forecast for tomorrow says it will snow." → ___ forecast says it will snow.
    Answer: Tomorrow's
    We use -'s with time words like tomorrow, yesterday and next week: tomorrow's forecast.
  29. Rewrite "Everyone is still talking about the game last week." → Everyone is still talking about ___ game.
    Answer: last week's
    We use -'s with time words, so the game last week becomes last week's game.
  30. Rewrite "Most of the restaurants in the town close early on Sundays." → Most of ___ restaurants close early on Sundays.
    Answer: the town's
    We can use -'s for places, so the restaurants in the town becomes the town's restaurants.
  31. Rewrite "The rail network in Britain is one of the oldest in the world." → ___ rail network is one of the oldest in the world.
    Answer: Britain's
    We can use -'s with the names of places and countries: Britain's rail network.
  32. Rewrite "Farmers in the region are worried about the lack of rain." → ___ farmers are worried about the lack of rain.
    Answer: The region's
    We can use -'s for places, so farmers in the region becomes the region's farmers.
  33. Our cabin is in the mountains. When we leave Denver at noon, we usually get there at three. So the cabin is about ___ (drive) from Denver.
    Accepted answers: three hours' drive / 3 hours' drive / a three-hour drive / a 3-hour drive
    We use -'s with periods of time, and only an apostrophe after a plural in -s: three hours' drive. A three-hour drive is also correct.
  34. Nadia leaves her office at 5:00 and walks to the gym. She gets there at 5:25. So the gym is ___ (walk) from her office.
    Accepted answers: twenty-five minutes' walk / 25 minutes' walk / a twenty-five-minute walk / a 25-minute walk
    With a plural period of time ending in -s, we add only an apostrophe: twenty-five minutes' walk. A twenty-five-minute walk is also correct.
  35. The office closes on December 20 and opens again on January 3. So the employees get ___ (vacation).
    Accepted answers: two weeks' vacation / 2 weeks' vacation / fourteen days' vacation / 14 days' vacation / a two-week vacation / two weeks of vacation
    We use -'s (or -s' with plurals) with periods of time: two weeks' vacation. A two-week vacation and two weeks of vacation are also correct.
  36. The baby cried all night. Sara finally fell asleep at 5 a.m., and her alarm went off at 6. So she only got ___ (sleep).
    Accepted answers: an hour's sleep / one hour's sleep / an hour of sleep / one hour of sleep
    With a singular period of time, we use -'s: an hour's sleep. An hour of sleep is also correct.
