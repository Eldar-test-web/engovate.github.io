Understanding and Using English Grammar Chart 11-8 – -ed/-ing Adjectives

Choose or type the answer to each question, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. Scene A: a big dog barks at a delivery driver, who drops a box and backs away. Scene B: a bear walks out of the woods, and the same dog runs into the house. Which scene does "The dog is frightened" describe?
    Frightened (-ed) describes someone who feels fear. In Scene B the bear makes the dog afraid, so the dog is frightened; in Scene A the dog causes the fear.
  2. Scene A: a big dog barks at a delivery driver, who drops a box and backs away. Scene B: a bear walks out of the woods, and the same dog runs into the house. Which scene does "The dog is frightening" describe?
    Frightening (-ing) describes the one that causes fear. In Scene A the dog scares the driver, so the dog is frightening; in Scene B the dog is the one who is scared.
  3. Scene A: a big dog barks at a delivery driver, who drops a box and backs away. Scene B: a bear walks out of the woods, and the same dog runs into the house. Which scene does "The driver is frightened" describe?
    The driver feels fear because of the dog in Scene A, so the -ed form fits: the driver is frightened. The driver is not in Scene B.
  4. Scene A: a big dog barks at a delivery driver, who drops a box and backs away. Scene B: a bear walks out of the woods, and the same dog runs into the house. Which scene does "The driver is frightening" describe?
    Frightening would mean the driver causes fear, but in Scene A the driver feels fear. The driver is frightened, not frightening, so the sentence fits neither scene.
  5. Scene A: a big dog barks at a delivery driver, who drops a box and backs away. Scene B: a bear walks out of the woods, and the same dog runs into the house. Which scene does "The bear is frightened" describe?
    Frightened would mean the bear feels fear, but in Scene B the bear causes the fear. The bear is frightening, not frightened, so the sentence fits neither scene.
  6. Scene A: a big dog barks at a delivery driver, who drops a box and backs away. Scene B: a bear walks out of the woods, and the same dog runs into the house. Which scene does "The bear is frightening" describe?
    In Scene B the bear makes the dog run away in fear, so the bear causes the feeling and is described with the -ing form: frightening.
  7. Half the audience fell asleep during the ___ (bore) presentation on tax law.
    Answer: boring
    The presentation causes the feeling of boredom, so it is described with the present participle (active meaning): a boring presentation.
  8. The ___ (bore) teenagers kept checking their phones during the long family dinner.
    Answer: bored
    The teenagers feel the boredom that the dinner causes, so they are described with the past participle (passive meaning): bored teenagers.
  9. Starting my first job in a new city was an ___ (excite) adventure.
    Answer: exciting
    The adventure causes excitement, so it is described with the -ing form: an exciting adventure.
  10. The ___ (excite) fans waited outside the stadium for hours to see the band arrive.
    Answer: excited
    The fans feel the excitement, so they are described with the -ed form: excited fans.
  11. The study found a ___ (surprise) link between sleep and memory.
    Answer: surprising
    The link causes surprise in the people who read about it, so the -ing form is used: a surprising link.
  12. The ___ (surprise) tourists stopped to watch a moose walk calmly down Main Street.
    Answer: surprised
    The tourists feel surprise because of the moose, so they are described with the -ed form: surprised tourists.
  13. During the thunderstorm, the ___ (frighten) puppy hid under my bed.
    Answer: frightened
    The storm frightens the puppy, so the puppy receives the action and is described with the past participle: the frightened puppy.
  14. Driving down the mountain in thick fog was a ___ (frighten) experience.
    Answer: frightening
    The experience causes fear, so it is described with the present participle: a frightening experience.
  15. After a twelve-hour shift in the emergency room, the nurse said it had been an ___ (exhaust) day.
    Answer: exhausting
    The day made the nurse very tired, so the day is described with the -ing form: an exhausting day.
  16. The ___ (exhaust) hikers set up their tent and went straight to sleep.
    Answer: exhausted
    The hikers feel very tired, so they are described with the -ed form: exhausted hikers.
  17. The view from the top of the volcano at sunrise was ___ (amaze).
    Answer: amazing
    The view causes the feeling of amazement, so it is described with the -ing form: amazing.
  18. We were ___ (amaze) that the tiny cafe could serve two hundred customers an hour.
    Answer: amazed
    We are the people who feel the amazement, so the -ed form is used: amazed.
  19. After losing his job and his apartment in the same month, Omar felt ___ (depress) for weeks.
    Answer: depressed
    Omar is the person who has the feeling, so the -ed form is used: felt depressed.
  20. The news about the factory closings was so ___ (depress) that I turned off the radio.
    Answer: depressing
    The news causes the sad feeling, so it is described with the -ing form: depressing.
  21. Moving furniture up five flights of stairs is ___ (tire) work.
    Answer: tiring
    The work causes tiredness, so it is described with the -ing form: tiring work.
  22. By Friday afternoon, most of the warehouse staff are too ___ (tire) to go out.
    Answer: tired
    The staff are the people who feel the tiredness, so the -ed form is used: too tired.
  23. The safety video on the flight was so ___ (bore) that nobody watched it.
    Answer: boring
    The video causes the boredom, so it is described with the -ing form: boring.
  24. The kids got ___ (bore) waiting in the long line at the passport office.
    Answer: bored
    The kids feel the boredom, so the -ed form is used: got bored.
  25. Dr. Patel gave an ___ (interest) talk about how bees communicate.
    Answer: interesting
    The talk causes interest in the listeners, so it is described with the -ing form: an interesting talk.
  26. Are you ___ (interest) in joining the hiking club this semester?
    Answer: interested
    The question is about the person who feels the interest, so the -ed form is used: interested in.
  27. Calling my new boss by the wrong name twice was really ___ (embarrass).
    Answer: embarrassing
    The mistake causes embarrassment, so it is described with the -ing form: embarrassing.
  28. Lina was too ___ (embarrass) to admit that she had forgotten her lines.
    Answer: embarrassed
    Lina is the person who feels the embarrassment, so the -ed form is used: embarrassed.
  29. The team's ___ (disappoint) results this season led to the hiring of a new coach.
    Answer: disappointing
    The results cause disappointment, so they are described with the -ing form: disappointing results.
  30. Many customers were ___ (disappoint) when the store stopped selling fresh bread.
    Answer: disappointed
    The customers feel the disappointment, so the -ed form is used: disappointed.
  31. My grandfather is a ___ (fascinate) storyteller who has lived on three continents.
    Answer: fascinating
    The grandfather causes fascination in his listeners, so he is described with the -ing form: a fascinating storyteller.
  32. The children were ___ (fascinate) by the octopus at the aquarium.
    Answer: fascinated
    The children feel the fascination that the octopus causes, so the -ed form is used: fascinated.
  33. Waiting forty minutes on hold with the internet company is ___ (frustrate).
    Answer: frustrating
    The waiting causes frustration, so it is described with the -ing form: frustrating.
  34. After three failed attempts to fix the printer, Ken was ___ (frustrate) and called a technician.
    Answer: frustrated
    Ken is the person who feels the frustration, so the -ed form is used: frustrated.
  35. The ___ (annoy) beep of the smoke alarm kept me awake all night.
    Answer: annoying
    The beep causes the annoyance, so it is described with the -ing form: the annoying beep.
  36. My neighbors were ___ (annoy) when I parked in front of their driveway.
    Answer: annoyed
    The neighbors feel the annoyance, so the -ed form is used: annoyed.
  37. The report revealed ___ (shock) levels of plastic in the ocean.
    Answer: shocking
    The levels cause shock in the people who read the report, so they are described with the -ing form: shocking levels.
  38. Everyone in the office was ___ (shock) to hear that the company was closing.
    Answer: shocked
    The people in the office feel the shock, so the -ed form is used: shocked.
  39. Riding a zip line through the rainforest was ___ (thrill).
    Answer: thrilling
    The ride causes excitement, so it is described with the -ing form: thrilling.
  40. My parents were ___ (thrill) when I got into nursing school.
    Answer: thrilled
    My parents feel the excitement, so the -ed form is used: thrilled.
  41. The museum keeps its rarest coins in a ___ (lock) glass case.
    Answer: locked
    Someone has locked the case, so it receives the action and the past participle is used: a locked case.
  42. Tripping on the stairs at my graduation ceremony was the most ___ (embarrass) moment of my life.
    Answer: embarrassing
    The moment causes embarrassment, so it is described with the present participle: an embarrassing moment.
  43. Two ___ (injure) players had to leave the field before halftime.
    Answer: injured
    The players were hurt; they received the action, so the past participle is used: injured players.
  44. Our coach designed a ___ (challenge) training program for the marathon team.
    Answer: challenging
    The program challenges the runners (active meaning), so the present participle is used: a challenging program.
  45. The package did not arrive on the ___ (expect) delivery date.
    Answer: expected
    People expect the date; the date does not expect anything. It receives the action, so the past participle is used: the expected date.
  46. Visitors to the museum can try an old ___ (print) press that still works.
    Answer: printing
    The press is a machine that prints; it performs the action, so the -ing form is used: a printing press.
  47. Only ___ (experience) climbers should try the north face of the mountain.
    Answer: experienced
    Experienced describes people who have gained skill through practice. It is the past participle form; experiencing is not used as an adjective for people.
  48. A ___ (grow) number of people now work from home at least one day a week.
    Answer: growing
    The number is getting bigger by itself; it performs the action, so the present participle is used: a growing number.
  49. The article gives a ___ (balance) view of both sides of the debate.
    Answer: balanced
    The writer has balanced the view, making it fair to both sides. The view receives the action, so the past participle is used: a balanced view.
  50. The ___ (spoil) little boy screamed until his parents bought him the toy.
    Answer: spoiled
    Someone has spoiled the boy by giving him too much, so he receives the action and the past participle is used: a spoiled child.
  51. Please keep your voices down. There's a ___ (sleep) baby in the next room.
    Answer: sleeping
    The baby is doing the action (sleeping), so the present participle is used: a sleeping baby.
  52. The movie ends with a ___ (thrill) car chase through the streets of Paris.
    Answer: thrilling
    The car chase causes excitement in the audience, so it is described with the -ing form: a thrilling car chase.
  53. Volunteers turned an ___ (abandon) factory into a community art center.
    Answer: abandoned
    Someone abandoned the factory; it received the action, so the past participle is used: an abandoned factory.
  54. Fish cannot survive in heavily ___ (pollute) rivers.
    Answer: polluted
    The rivers have been polluted by something; they receive the action, so the past participle is used: polluted rivers.
  55. Since we're only staying in Denver for six months, we rented a ___ (furnish) house.
    Answer: furnished
    Someone has put furniture in the house, so the house receives the action and the past participle is used: a furnished house.
  56. The two offices share a thin ___ (divide) wall, so we can hear every phone call next door.
    Answer: dividing
    The wall divides the two offices; it performs the action, so the present participle is used: a dividing wall.
  57. Citizens should be able to contact their ___ (elect) representatives easily.
    Answer: elected
    Voters elect the representatives, so the representatives receive the action and the past participle is used: elected representatives.
  58. The documentary shows some ___ (amaze) footage of whales feeding near the surface.
    Answer: amazing
    The footage causes amazement in the viewers, so it is described with the -ing form: amazing footage.
