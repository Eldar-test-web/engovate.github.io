English Grammar in Use Unit 58 – Verb + -ing or To… 3 (Like / Would Like etc.)

Choose or type the answer to each question, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. My grandparents moved into a small apartment near the beach after they retired, and they love it. They ___ (like/live) so close to the ocean.
    Answer: like living
    We use like + -ing for a situation that already exists. They live there now and enjoy it, so 'like living', not 'like to live'.
  2. Carlos has taught math at a local high school for ten years and still enjoys his job. He ___ (like/teach) teenagers.
    Answer: likes teaching
    Teaching is his job now, a situation that already exists, so we use like + -ing: 'likes teaching'.
  3. Whenever Mei Ling goes hiking, she stops every few minutes to photograph birds. She ___ (like/take) photos of wildlife.
    Accepted answers: likes taking / likes to take
    For something we do again and again, like can be followed by -ing or to + base form, so 'likes taking' and 'likes to take' are both correct.
  4. Last summer I had a job in a noisy warehouse, and I was glad when it ended. I ___ (not/like/work) there.
    Accepted answers: didn't like working / did not like working
    We use like + -ing for a situation that existed: I worked there and didn't enjoy it. The past form 'didn't like' fits last summer.
  5. Dev switched from engineering to art school last year, and he's much happier now. He ___ (like/study) design.
    Answer: likes studying
    He is an art student now, so this is a situation that already exists, and we use like + -ing: 'likes studying'.
  6. Since her video went viral, strangers stop Lena in the street, and it makes her uncomfortable. She ___ (not/like/be) so well known.
    Accepted answers: doesn't like being / does not like being
    Being well known is her situation now, and she doesn't enjoy it, so we use like + -ing: 'doesn't like being'.
  7. My brother never speeds or passes other cars on narrow roads. He ___ (not/like/take) chances when he's driving.
    Accepted answers: doesn't like taking / does not like taking / doesn't like to take / does not like to take
    This is about something he avoids again and again, so like can be followed by -ing or to + base form.
  8. Waiting for exam results makes Emma nervous. She ___ (like/know) her grades as soon as they're posted.
    Answer: likes to know
    'Like to do' means we choose to do something or think it's a good idea. Emma isn't talking about enjoyment; she wants her grades early, so 'likes to know'.
  9. Rosa has visited twelve countries this year alone. She really enjoys ___ (travel).
    Accepted answers: traveling / travelling
    Enjoy is always followed by -ing, not to. In US spelling, travel becomes traveling with one l.
  10. There's an empty seat next to me on the bus. Would you like ___ (sit) here?
    Answer: to sit
    Would like is followed by to + base form: 'Would you like to sit here?'
  11. Excuse me, would you mind ___ (turn) your phone off during the movie? The screen light is distracting.
    Answer: turning
    Would you mind is followed by -ing, not to: 'Would you mind turning ...?'
  12. My grandmother is 80, but she still likes ___ (do) her own gardening every morning.
    Accepted answers: doing / to do
    When like is about something we do again and again, both -ing and to + base form are correct: 'likes doing' or 'likes to do'.
  13. Doing homework on a Friday night isn't fun, but I like ___ (get) it done early so my weekend is free.
    Answer: to get
    'Like to do' means we choose to do something because we think it's a good idea, even if we don't enjoy it. Homework on Friday isn't fun, so 'like to get'.
  14. Our new neighbor is a retired engineer who enjoys ___ (be) useful, so he's always fixing things for people on our street.
    Answer: being
    Enjoy is followed by -ing, never to: 'enjoys being useful'.
  15. One day I would love ___ (go) on a trip around the world.
    Answer: to go
    Would love is followed by to + base form, just like would like: 'would love to go'.
  16. Our apartment is right next to a busy highway, and the noise never stops. We don't like ___ (live) here.
    Answer: living
    We use like + -ing for a situation that already exists. They live there now and don't enjoy it, so 'don't like living'.
  17. Before we start the meeting, I'd like ___ (talk) briefly about next month's budget.
    Answer: to talk
    I'd like (= I would like) is followed by to + base form: 'I'd like to talk'.
  18. When there's a problem at work, I like ___ (hear) about it right away, not at the end of the week.
    Answer: to hear
    'Like to do' means we choose to do something because we think it's better that way. Nobody enjoys hearing about problems, so 'like to hear', not 'like hearing'.
  19. The next bus is completely full. Would you prefer ___ (wait) for another one or take a taxi?
    Answer: to wait
    Would prefer is followed by to + base form: 'Would you prefer to wait ...?'
  20. My little cousin won't play board games with us anymore because she hates ___ (lose).
    Accepted answers: losing / to lose
    When we talk about things in general, hate can be followed by -ing or to + base form, so 'losing' and 'to lose' are both correct.
  21. My cousin had a barbecue last Saturday, but I had to work. I ___ (like/go).
    Accepted answers: would like to have gone / 'd like to have gone / would have liked to go / 'd have liked to go
    'Would like to have done' means we regret now that we didn't or couldn't do something. The speaker couldn't go because of work.
  22. Everyone at work is talking about last night's basketball game, but I missed it. I ___ (like/see) it.
    Accepted answers: would like to have seen / 'd like to have seen / would have liked to see / 'd have liked to see
    The speaker missed the game and is sorry now, so we use would like + to have + past participle: 'would like to have seen'.
  23. Luckily, my laptop was still on the train seat when I went back for it. I ___ (hate/lose) all my work.
    Accepted answers: would hate to have lost / 'd hate to have lost / would have hated to lose / 'd have hated to lose
    We use would hate + to have + past participle for something bad that could have happened in the past but didn't: 'would hate to have lost'.
  24. The author was signing books at the library yesterday, but I was out of town. I ___ (love/meet) her.
    Accepted answers: would love to have met / 'd love to have met / would have loved to meet / 'd have loved to meet
    The speaker couldn't meet the author and regrets it, so we use would love + to have + past participle: 'would love to have met'.
  25. The storm knocked out the power all night, so I'm glad my roommate was home with me. I ___ (not/like/be) by myself.
    Accepted answers: wouldn't like to have been / would not like to have been / wouldn't have liked to be / would not have liked to be
    We use wouldn't like + to have + past participle for a past situation we are glad we avoided: 'wouldn't like to have been'.
  26. The bus ride to the conference took six hours in heavy traffic. I ___ (prefer/travel) by plane.
    Accepted answers: would prefer to have traveled / 'd prefer to have traveled / would have preferred to travel / 'd have preferred to travel / would prefer to have travelled / 'd prefer to have travelled
    We use would prefer + to have + past participle for a past choice we now wish had been different. In US spelling, traveled has one l.
