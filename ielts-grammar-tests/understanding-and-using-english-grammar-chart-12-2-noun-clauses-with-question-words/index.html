Understanding and Using English Grammar Chart 12-2 – Noun Clauses With Question Words

Choose or type the answer to each question, then press Check to see if you are right.
  1. A: Where does your sister work these days? B: I'm not sure ___. She changed jobs last month.
    In a noun clause the subject comes before the verb, and does is not used: where she works. Where does she work is question word order.
  2. I need to return this book to Professor Novak. Could you tell me ___?
    After Could you tell me, the question becomes a noun clause, so the subject comes before the verb: where her office is. Where is her office is question word order.
  3. A: How old is your cousin Rachel? B: Hmm, I don't remember ___.
    Accepted answers: how old she is / how old Rachel is
    In a noun clause the subject comes before the verb: how old + she + is. Question word order (how old is she) is not used.
  4. A: Where does Mr. Okafor live? B: I'm not sure ___, but I think it's somewhere near the park.
    Accepted answers: where he lives / where Mr. Okafor lives
    Does is used only in the question. In the noun clause use subject + verb and put -s on the verb: where he lives.
  5. A: When does the farmers market open? B: Let me look online and find out ___.
    Accepted answers: when it opens / when the farmers market opens
    Drop does and put the subject before the verb, with -s on the verb: when it opens.
  6. A: What time are you and Kenji leaving for the airport? B: I'm not sure. I need to ask Kenji ___. He's driving.
    Accepted answers: what time we're leaving / what time we are leaving / what time we're leaving for the airport / what time we are leaving for the airport
    In the noun clause the subject comes before the helping verb: what time + we + are leaving. The pronoun changes to we because the speaker and Kenji are leaving together.
  7. A: Whose shoes are those by the front door? B: I don't know ___. They aren't mine.
    Accepted answers: whose shoes those are / whose shoes they are / whose they are / whose those are
    Those (or they) is the subject and are is the verb, so the subject comes first and be goes last: whose shoes those are.
  8. A: Who broke the printer? B: I have no idea ___. It was working fine this morning.
    Accepted answers: who broke it / who broke the printer
    Who is the subject of the question, so the word order stays the same in the noun clause: who broke it. Did is not added.
  9. A: Who are those men? They've been standing outside the bank all morning. B: I don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: who they are / who those men are
    Those men (they) is the subject of are, so in the noun clause the subject comes before be: who they are, not who are they.
  10. A: What happened? There's broken glass all over the floor! B: I'm not sure ___. I just got here.
    Accepted answers: what happened / what happened here
    What is the subject of happened, so the noun clause keeps the same word order as the question: what happened.
  11. A: Why did the band break up? B: Nobody knows. ___ is a mystery even to their fans.
    Accepted answers: Why the band broke up / Why they broke up
    Did is not used in a noun clause, so the past tense moves to the main verb: why the band broke up. The noun clause is the subject of is.
  12. A: Where did the summer go? School starts again on Monday! B: I know. I honestly don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: where it went / where the summer went
    Drop did and use the past tense of the verb after the subject: where they went.
  13. A: What are you building in the garage? B: It's a surprise, so I can't tell you ___.
    Accepted answers: what I'm building / what I am building / what I'm building in the garage / what I am building in the garage
    In the noun clause the subject comes before the helping verb: what + I + am building. The pronoun changes from you to I because the speaker is the builder.
  14. A: Do you remember what I told you yesterday? B: Sorry, ___ has completely slipped my mind.
    Accepted answers: what you told me / what you told me yesterday
    The noun clause is the subject of has, and it takes a singular verb. The pronouns change to fit the speaker: what you told me.
  15. How are the winners chosen? → Can you tell me ___?
    Answer: how the winners are chosen
    In the noun clause the subject (the winners) comes before the verb (are chosen).
  16. What does the abbreviation ASAP stand for? → Can you tell me ___?
    Accepted answers: what the abbreviation ASAP stands for / what ASAP stands for
    Does is not used in a noun clause. The subject comes before the verb, and the verb takes -s: what ASAP stands for.
  17. What was the final score? → Can you tell me ___?
    Answer: what the final score was
    The subject (the final score) comes before the verb, so was moves to the end of the noun clause.
  18. Who am I supposed to call if the heater breaks? → Can you tell me ___ if the heater breaks?
    Accepted answers: who I'm supposed to call / who I am supposed to call / whom I'm supposed to call / whom I am supposed to call
    In the noun clause the subject I comes before am: who I am supposed to call.
  19. When is your birthday? → Can you tell me ___?
    Answer: when your birthday is
    The subject (your birthday) comes before the verb is in the noun clause: when your birthday is.
  20. How much sugar do I need? → Can you tell me ___?
    Answer: how much sugar I need
    Do is used only in the question. In the noun clause use subject + verb: how much sugar I need.
  21. When do the stores close tonight? → Can you tell me ___ tonight?
    Answer: when the stores close
    Drop do and put the subject before the verb: when the stores close.
  22. Where is the bus going to stop? → Can you tell me ___?
    Answer: where the bus is going to stop
    The subject (the bus) comes before is going to in the noun clause: where the bus is going to stop.
  23. What time does the next train leave? → Can you tell me ___?
    Answer: what time the next train leaves
    Does is not used in the noun clause, so the verb takes -s after the singular subject: what time the next train leaves.
  24. Hana will start (on Monday). Question: ___?
    Accepted answers: When will Hana start / When will she start / What day will Hana start / What day will she start
    On Monday is a time, so ask with when. In a question the helping verb comes before the subject: When will Hana start?
  25. Hana will start (on Monday). Noun clause: Please tell me ___.
    Accepted answers: when Hana will start / when she will start / when she'll start / what day Hana will start / what day she will start / what day she'll start
    In a noun clause the subject comes before will: when Hana will start.
  26. She is moving here (because she wants to live near her parents). Question: ___?
    Answer: Why is she moving here
    A reason is asked about with why. In the question, is comes before the subject: Why is she moving here?
  27. She is moving here (because she wants to live near her parents). Noun clause: Please tell me ___.
    Accepted answers: why she is moving here / why she's moving here
    In the noun clause the subject comes before the helping verb: why she is moving here.
  28. She'll work in (Room 214, not Room 412). Question: ___?
    Accepted answers: Which room will she work in / What room will she work in / In which room will she work
    The answer picks one room from several, so ask with which (or what) + room. Will comes before the subject in the question.
  29. She'll work in (Room 214, not Room 412). Noun clause: Could you tell me ___?
    Accepted answers: which room she'll work in / which room she will work in / what room she'll work in / what room she will work in / in which room she will work / in which room she'll work
    In the noun clause the subject comes before will: which room she will work in.
  30. (Mr. Ruiz) is going to train her. Question: ___?
    Accepted answers: Who is going to train her / Who's going to train her
    The words in parentheses are the subject, so who replaces the subject and the word order does not change: Who is going to train her?
  31. (Mr. Ruiz) is going to train her. Noun clause: Do you know ___?
    Accepted answers: who is going to train her / who's going to train her
    Who is the subject, so the noun clause has the same word order as the question: who is going to train her.
  32. Mr. Ruiz is (the head of the design team). Question: ___?
    Accepted answers: Who is Mr. Ruiz / Who's Mr. Ruiz
    In a question with be, is comes before the subject: Who is Mr. Ruiz?
  33. Mr. Ruiz is (the head of the design team). Noun clause: Please tell me ___.
    Accepted answers: who Mr. Ruiz is / who he is
    Mr. Ruiz is the subject, so in the noun clause it comes before be: who Mr. Ruiz is.
  34. Hana lives (in an apartment above the bakery). Question: ___?
    Accepted answers: Where does Hana live / Where does she live
    A place is asked about with where. A simple present question needs does + subject + base verb: Where does Hana live?
  35. Hana lives (in an apartment above the bakery). Noun clause: I'd like to know ___.
    Accepted answers: where Hana lives / where she lives
    Does is not used in the noun clause; the verb takes -s after the subject: where Hana lives.
  36. Her old office is (in Boston). Question: ___?
    Accepted answers: Where is her old office / Where's her old office
    Ask about a place with where, and put is before the subject: Where is her old office?
  37. Her old office is (in Boston). Noun clause: Please tell me ___.
    Answer: where her old office is
    In the noun clause the subject (her old office) comes before is, so is goes at the end.
  38. She is nervous (because she doesn't know anyone here yet). Question: ___?
    Answer: Why is she nervous
    A reason is asked about with why, and is comes before the subject: Why is she nervous?
  39. She is nervous (because she doesn't know anyone here yet). Noun clause: Do you know ___?
    Accepted answers: why she is nervous / why she's nervous
    In the noun clause the subject comes before be: why she is nervous.
  40. Her husband works for (a solar energy company). Question: ___?
    Accepted answers: What company does her husband work for / Which company does her husband work for / Who does her husband work for / What company does he work for / Which company does he work for / Who does he work for
    The question needs does + subject + base verb, and the preposition for stays at the end: What company does her husband work for?
  41. Her husband works for (a solar energy company). Noun clause: Could you tell me ___?
    Accepted answers: what company her husband works for / which company her husband works for / who her husband works for / what company he works for / which company he works for / who he works for
    Does is dropped in the noun clause, so works takes -s after the subject: what company her husband works for.
  42. He has worked there (for six years). Question: ___?
    Answer: How long has he worked there
    A length of time is asked about with how long. In the present perfect question, has comes before the subject: How long has he worked there?
  43. He has worked there (for six years). Noun clause: Do you know ___?
    Accepted answers: how long he has worked there / how long he's worked there
    In the noun clause the subject comes before has: how long he has worked there.
  44. Where does Ms. Alvarez shop for groceries? → I don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: where Ms. Alvarez shops for groceries / where she shops for groceries
    Does is not used in a noun clause. The subject comes before the verb, and the verb takes -s: where she shops.
  45. What city is Ahmed from? → I don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: what city Ahmed is from / what city he is from / what city he's from
    In the noun clause the subject (Ahmed) comes before is, and from stays at the end: what city Ahmed is from.
  46. How long has Mrs. Chen been teaching here? → I don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: how long Mrs. Chen has been teaching here / how long she has been teaching here / how long she's been teaching here
    In the noun clause the subject comes before the helping verb has: how long Mrs. Chen has been teaching here.
  47. Where are you going to celebrate your birthday? → I don't know ___ yet.
    Accepted answers: where I'm going to celebrate it / where I am going to celebrate it / where I'm going to celebrate my birthday / where I am going to celebrate my birthday
    In the noun clause the subject comes before the verb, and you becomes I in the answer: where I am going to celebrate it.
  48. Where is the closest post office? → I don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: where the closest post office is / where the nearest post office is
    The subject (the closest post office) comes before is, so is goes at the end of the noun clause.
  49. What is Lucia's middle name? → I don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: what Lucia's middle name is / what her middle name is
    The subject (Lucia's middle name) comes before is in the noun clause: what Lucia's middle name is.
  50. What kind of car does your boss drive? → I don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: what kind of car my boss drives / what kind of car he drives / what kind of car she drives
    Does is dropped in the noun clause, so drive takes -s after the singular subject: what kind of car my boss drives.
  51. Whose jacket is this? → I wonder ___.
    Accepted answers: whose jacket this is / whose jacket it is / whose this is / whose it is
    In the noun clause the subject (this) comes before is: whose jacket this is, not whose jacket is this.
  52. How far is it to the next gas station? → I wonder ___ to the next gas station.
    Answer: how far it is
    In the noun clause the subject it comes before is: how far it is.
  53. How long has your uncle been retired? → I don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: how long my uncle has been retired / how long he has been retired / how long he's been retired
    In the noun clause the subject comes before has, and your becomes my: how long my uncle has been retired.
  54. Why are we learning this formula? → I wonder ___.
    Accepted answers: why we are learning this formula / why we're learning this formula / why we are learning it / why we're learning it
    In the noun clause the subject we comes before are: why we are learning this formula.
  55. Who is knocking on the door? → I wonder ___.
    Accepted answers: who is knocking on the door / who's knocking on the door / who is knocking / who's knocking
    Who is the subject of the question, so the word order does not change in the noun clause: who is knocking on the door.
  56. What is the Wi-Fi password? → I don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: what the Wi-Fi password is / what the password is
    The subject (the Wi-Fi password) comes before is, so is goes at the end of the noun clause.
  57. Where did Noah go after the game last night? → I don't know ___ after the game last night.
    Accepted answers: where Noah went / where he went
    Did is not used in a noun clause, so the verb itself is past: where Noah went.
  58. Why is the baby crying? → I don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: why the baby is crying / why the baby's crying
    In the noun clause the subject (the baby) comes before is: why the baby is crying.
  59. How often does Grace go swimming? → I don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: how often Grace goes swimming / how often she goes swimming
    Does is dropped in the noun clause, so go takes -s after the singular subject: how often Grace goes swimming.
  60. Why was the train late this morning? → I wonder ___ this morning.
    Accepted answers: why the train was late / why it was late
    In the noun clause the subject (the train) comes before was: why the train was late.
  61. How much did the concert tickets cost? → I don't know ___.
    Accepted answers: how much the concert tickets cost / how much they cost / how much the tickets cost
    Did is not used in a noun clause. Cost has the same past form, so the noun clause is how much the concert tickets cost.
  62. Which words are the noun clause in "What my grandparents remember about the war is worth recording."?
    The noun clause begins with what and is the subject of the sentence: What my grandparents remember about the war (is worth recording).
  63. Which words are the noun clause in "Good managers listen carefully to what their employees suggest."?
    What their employees suggest is a noun clause used as the object of the preposition to.
  64. Which words are the noun clause in "Where we park is a problem on game days."?
    The noun clause includes the question word: Where we park. It is the subject of is.
  65. Which words are the noun clause in "How you sleep affects your mood the next day."?
    How you sleep is a noun clause used as the subject of affects. The question word is part of the clause.
  66. Which words are the noun clause in "I believe how a company treats its workers matters more than its profits."?
    How a company treats its workers is a noun clause; it is the subject of matters.
  67. Which words are the noun clause in "My brother never trusts what he sees on social media."?
    What he sees on social media is a noun clause used as the object of trusts.
  68. A: Where ___ (Emma, go) on vacation last month? She came back with a great tan.
    Answer: did Emma go
    This is a direct question about the past, so it uses question word order with did: Where did Emma go?
  69. B: I'm not sure where ___ (Emma, go), but she mentioned a beach in Mexico.
    Answer: Emma went
    This is a noun clause after know, so it uses subject + verb with no did, and the verb is past: Emma went.
  70. A: Where ___ (my umbrella, be)? I left it by the door this morning.
    Answer: is my umbrella
    This is a direct question, so be comes before the subject: Where is my umbrella?
  71. B: Sorry, I have no idea where ___ (it, be). Did you check the car?
    Answer: it is
    Where it is is a noun clause, the object of have no idea, so the subject comes before be. The full form is needed at the end of a clause: it is, not it's.
  72. A: The windows are wide open, and it's raining! Why ___ (Omar, close, not) them before he left?
    Accepted answers: didn't Omar close / did Omar not close
    A negative past question uses didn't + subject + base verb: Why didn't Omar close them? Did Omar not close is also correct but very formal.
  73. B: We'll have to ask Omar why ___ (he, close, not) the windows. The carpet is soaked.
    Accepted answers: he didn't close / he did not close
    In a noun clause the subject comes first, followed by the negative verb: why he didn't close the windows.
  74. A: Your neighbor works at the hospital, right? How long ___ (she, be) a nurse?
    Answer: has she been
    She is still a nurse, so the present perfect is used with how long. In a question, has comes before the subject: has she been.
  75. B: I really don't know how long ___ (she, be) a nurse, but she trained my cousin twenty years ago.
    Accepted answers: she has been / she's been
    This is a noun clause after I don't know, so the subject comes before the helping verb: how long she has been a nurse.
  76. A: Which entrance ___ (we, be supposed) to use? The front door is locked.
    Answer: are we supposed
    This is a direct question, so be comes before the subject: Which entrance are we supposed to use?
  77. B: I have no idea which entrance ___ (we, be supposed) to use. Let's call the front desk.
    Accepted answers: we are supposed / we're supposed
    This is a noun clause after have no idea, so the subject comes before be: which entrance we are supposed to use.
