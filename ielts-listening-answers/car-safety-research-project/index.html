Questions

Questions 31–35 One Choice

Circle the correct letters A - C.

31 John was first interested in the subject because of something ________
  1. he had witnessed.
  2. he had read about.
  3. he had experienced.
32 The main research method was ________
  1. interviews.
  2. questionnaires.
  3. observation.
33 Which pie chart shows the proportion of men and women respondents?
  1. Pie chart A
  2. Pie chart B
  3. Pie chart C
34 How many respondents were there?
  1. 50 – 100
  2. 100 – 150
  3. 150 – 200
35 The most common type of road rage incident involved ________
  1. damage to property.
  2. personal violence.
  3. verbal abuse.

Questions 36–40 Matching

Which group gave the following advice?

Tick Column A if it was mainly women.

Tick Column B if it was mainly men.

Tick Column C if it was both men and women.

A. mainly women

B. mainly men

C. both men and women

36 Avoid eye contact with other drivers.
37 Inform someone of your likely arrival time.
38 Ensure car keys are ready when you return to the car.
39 Leave plenty of space when parking.
40 Keep all doors locked.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q31 B I had been interested and horrified by several newspaper reports on what people call 'road rage' Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript shows that John's interest came from reading news about "road rage" in the newspaper. This confirms he read about the subject.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means John started thinking about this topic after he read news stories in a newspaper.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because John says he felt interested after seeing stories in "newspaper reports." In English, when you find information in a newspaper, it means you "read about" it. The other choices (witnessing or experiencing) mean seeing it happen in real life or having it happen to you, but John specifically mentions reading the news.
Q32 A For our research we depended mainly on talking to individuals, asking them questions rather than using written questionnaires Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript explains that the researchers mostly used the method of speaking to people and asking for their answers instead of giving them papers to write on.
Answer Explanation:
The answer 'interviews' means that the researchers gathered information by talking directly to people and asking them questions face-to-face.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the speaker mentions that they got their information by talking to people at a garage and asking them questions about their experiences. They specifically avoided using paper-based questionnaires and instead used this verbal method, which is known as an interview. The key words here are 'talking to individuals' and 'asking them questions', which define what an interview is.
Q33 A Our respondents were both men and women, but the women were just slightly in the majority Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript says that the people who answered the survey were both men and women. There were more women than men, but only by a small amount.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that there were a few more women than men in the group of people who answered the questions.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is based on how the speaker describes the group of people he interviewed. He mentions that women were the "majority," which means there were more women than men. He also uses the word "slightly," which means the difference between the number of men and women was small. Therefore, the correct chart must show women taking up a little bit more than half of the circle.
Q34 B in the end we talked to a total of 135 drivers over those two days Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript shows that the researchers interviewed exactly 135 people who drive cars during the two-day study period.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the number of people who took part in the survey was between 100 and 150.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because John states the exact number of people his team interviewed during their two-day research period. He says the total was 135. Since 135 is greater than 100 and less than 150, choice B is the only category that fits this total.
Q35 C but the main type of incident was being shouted at – 79% had experienced that Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript shows that the most frequent event mentioned by the drivers was being yelled at, with nearly 80 percent of the people reporting this issue.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that yelling or using angry words was the most frequent problem people had on the road.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the speaker compares different types of road rage and states that the highest percentage of people experienced shouting. In the transcript, 79% of people were shouted at, which is 'verbal abuse'. This number is much larger than the 24% who had car damage or the 15% who faced physical violence. Therefore, it is the most common incident.
Q36 B Whereas it was men who said you should try to avoid looking directly at other drivers Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript shows that men were the ones who suggested that drivers should not look straight at other people on the road.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means that mostly men gave the advice to avoid looking at other drivers.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the speaker mentions that men specifically proposed the idea of not looking directly at other people driving cars to stay safe. In this context, "avoid looking directly" is a synonym for avoiding "eye contact."
Q37 A Both men and
oh sorry no
it was women who said you had to tell someone when you were due to get to a particular destination		 Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript shows the speaker correcting himself. He clarifies that it was the women in the study who advised letting others know when you are supposed to reach your destination.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that mostly women suggested telling someone when they expect to arrive at a place.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is A because the speaker mentions a specific piece of safety advice: telling someone your expected arrival time. In the transcript, the speaker starts to say both men and women gave this advice but quickly corrects himself to say it was just the women. The phrase 'tell someone when you were due to get to a particular destination' means the same thing as informing someone of your arrival time.
Q38 C Then, I had thought that it would be mainly men, but both sexes made the point that it's much safer to get keys out well in advance as you go towards your car Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript shows that while the researcher expected this answer from men, both men and women (both sexes) agreed that prepare your keys before you get to the car is important for safety.
Answer Explanation:
The answer C means that both men and women agreed that having your car keys ready before you reach your vehicle is a good safety tip.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the speaker, John, explicitly states that 'both sexes' made this point. Although he initially expected only men to give this advice, the results of his study showed that both men and women (both sexes) believe it is much safer to have your keys out early as you walk toward your car.
Q39 B Men were very aware that muggers or whatever might be concealed behind the car. They also made the point that you should leave plenty of room when you park your car so you can make a quick getaway if you need to Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript explains that men were worried about people hiding near their cars. Because of this, they suggested that drivers should leave a lot of space around their vehicle when they park so they can drive away fast if they are in danger.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is mainly men (Column B).
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the transcript specifically mentions that men were the ones who pointed out the importance of leaving extra space when parking. This group felt that having enough room would allow a driver to leave quickly (a 'quick getaway') if they felt unsafe. In the context of the talk, the word 'they' follows a sentence about 'men', showing that this specific advice came from the male drivers interviewed.
Q40 C Finally, locking doors at all times
men didn't think it was quite as important as women, but both gave it a high safety rating		 Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript shows that while men and women had slightly different levels of concern, both groups agreed that keeping doors locked all the time is a very good safety tip.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that both men and women suggested that keeping car doors locked is a good way to stay safe.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the text says that both men and women gave this advice a high safety rating. This means both groups agreed that locking doors is important for safety, even if women felt more strongly about it than men did. Synonyms to look for include "both" and "high safety rating," which show the agreement between the zwei groups.

Transcript

TUTOR Well, good afternoon. In today's session John Upton will be sharing some of the findings of his research project from last term. John...

JOHN Thanks. Well, first of all, a little bit about the background to the project. Our title, as you can see, is pretty straightforward: 'car safety'. But these days there's a lot more to it than the usual injunctions about drinking and driving or speeding. I had been interested and horrified by several newspaper reports on what people call 'road rage'. For example the famous incident of a man getting out of his car in a car park and hitting the driver of a van who had overtaken him earlier. It seemed to me that there were almost as many serious problems when cars were parked...i.e. were stationary...as when they were travelling at 90 miles an hour. So I decided to make this the focus of the project.

For our research we depended mainly on talking to individuals, asking them questions rather than using written questionnaires. We stopped people at a selected garage on the motorway over a two-day period, and asked them questions about what they'd observed or experienced themselves. Our respondents were both men and women, but the women were just slightly in the majority. We were pleased by the public's willingness to stop and chat to us...in the end we talked to a total of 135 drivers over those two days.

So what were our findings? Well, as you can see, 93% of respondents had had some kind of problem. A surprisingly large percentage—24% had had their car damaged in some way, but the main type of incident was being shouted at – 79% had experienced that. 15% had experienced violence on their own persons...they'd actually been hit by someone. The police tended only to be informed when there was physical violence involved.

So what strategies had people developed to ensure their own safety? Let's have a look at the figures here. Well, first of all, it was quite striking that there were often distinct answers from the men and women. It was mainly women, for example, who said one shouldn't ever stop to find out how to get somewhere. Whereas it was men who said you should try to avoid looking directly at other drivers. Both men and...oh sorry no...it was women who said you had to tell someone when you were due to get to a particular destination. Then, I had thought that it would be mainly men, but both sexes made the point that it's much safer to get keys out well in advance as you go towards your car. Men were very aware that muggers or whatever might be concealed behind the car. They also made the point that you should leave plenty of room when you park your car so you can make a quick getaway if you need to. Finally, locking doors at all times...men didn't think it was quite as important as women, but both gave it a high safety rating.
