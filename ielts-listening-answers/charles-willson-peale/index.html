Questions 37–40 Summary Completion

Complete the summary below.

Write ONE WORD ONLY for each answer.

Primarily, Peale was a painter but he was also a politician, scientist and 37 with many successful patents. He worked with Thomas Jefferson on the polygraph which was a desk that could be used for copying a 38. He wrote academic papers on different subjects and tried to make movies, spectacles and a velocipede (a kind of 39). Peale was also a naturalist and taxidermist and he helped unearth the skeleton of a mastodon which was the best 40 in his museum.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q31 1768 Whilst there he painted this portrait in 1768, see slide 1, Pitt as a Roman Senator Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript explains that Peale created the painting in slide 1, named "Pitt as a Roman Senator", in 1768.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is the year 1768.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 1768 because the speaker mentions that while Charles Willson Peale was in London, he made the painting shown in slide 1, titled "Pitt as a Roman Senator," in the year 1768.
Q32 first ever / first / first portrait He returned to America and in 1772 painted the first ever portrait of George Washington (see slide 2) Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript states that Charles Willson Peale went back to America and, in 1772, created the very first portrait of George Washington.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the 1772 painting was the earliest or very first portrait ever made of George Washington.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is supported by the speaker's mention of Slide 2. The speaker explains that after Peale returned to America in 1772, he painted the "first ever portrait" of George Washington.
Q33 warmth Just look at the slide—this painting is simply called The Peale Family and you can almost feel the exuberance of the family and their warmth towards one another Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript explains that when you look at the painting of *The Peale Family*, you can clearly sense the family's lively energy (exuberance) and their caring affection (warmth) for each other.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "warmth" means a feeling of friendliness, love, and affection between family members.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is confirmed by the speaker's description of Slide 3, which depicts the 1773 painting *The Peale Family*. The speaker mentions that looking at the painting allows you to feel both "the exuberance of the family and their warmth towards one another". The table prompt asks for what the painting shows alongside "exuberance &", making "warmth" the exact word needed.
Q34 Staircase Group This painting, the Staircase Group (1795), was exhibited in a doorway as a trompe l'oeil—and it is said that it did in fact 'fool the eye' of George Washington Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript explains that the 1795 painting made in the trick-the-eye style is called "the Staircase Group".
Answer Explanation:
The answer is the name of the artwork painted in 1795, which is "Staircase Group".
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "Staircase Group" because the speaker discusses a 1795 painting showing the artist's two sons, Raphaelle and Titian, painted in the "trompe l'oeil" style. The lecturer introduces this specific piece by saying, "This painting, the Staircase Group (1795), was exhibited in a doorway as a trompe l'oeil". Since the table already includes "The", the missing words are "Staircase Group".
Q35 daughter It's a rather macabre portrait of his first wife crying over the death of one of their children, their daughter, Margaret Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript explains that the painting shows Peale's first wife crying because one of their children, their daughter Margaret, had died.
Answer Explanation:
The answer refers to a female child of Charles Willson Peale and his wife.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is confirmed by the speaker's description of the 1772 painting 'Rachel Weeping'. The lecturer explains that it is a macabre portrait depicting the artist's first wife weeping over the death of their daughter, Margaret. Therefore, 'daughter' correctly completes the note describing the painting of his wife and deceased child.
Q36 1822 I'd like to show you one more slide to demonstrate his innovative approach—this is a portrait of his brother, James, sitting at his desk at night with only his face illuminated by a lamp. This was painted much later than the others—in 1822 Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript explains that the slide showing Peale's brother James sitting at night with his face lit by a lamp was created in the year 1822.
Answer Explanation:
The answer is the year 1822.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is 1822 because the table asks for the date of the painting described in slide 6 (a portrait of his brother James with his face illuminated in the dark). The speaker describes this exact slide of his brother James with his face illuminated by a lamp and specifies that it was painted in 1822.
Q37 inventor He was also an inventor who gained patents for a fireplace, porcelain false teeth, and a new kind of wooden bridge Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript explains that Peale created new inventions and received official patents for items like a fireplace, fake teeth made of porcelain, and a new style of wooden bridge.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means a person who creates or designs new things and devices.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "inventor" because the summary asks what Charles Willson Peale was in addition to being a painter, politician, and scientist who had many patents. The speaker explicitly mentions that Peale "was also an inventor who gained patents".
Q38 manuscript He collaborated with Thomas Jefferson on what was known as the polygraph—a kind of portable writing desk—but it wasn't any ordinary desk: this one could make several copies of a manuscript at once Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript states that Peale worked together with Thomas Jefferson to invent the polygraph, a special writing desk that could produce multiple copies of a manuscript simultaneously.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "manuscript" refers to a handwritten or typed piece of writing or document.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "manuscript" because the speaker explains that Peale and Thomas Jefferson worked together on an invention called the polygraph. This device was a special portable writing desk capable of making multiple copies of a "manuscript" at the same time. The summary statement matches this by asking what the desk was used to copy.
Q39 bicycle Oh, and he also tried his hand at inventing a fairly primitive but innovative motion picture technique; new types of eyeglasses; and a velocipede (which is a precursor to the bicycle) Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript explains that Charles Willson Peale worked on creating a velocipede, describing it as an early version of the bicycle.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "bicycle" refers to a two-wheeled vehicle that a person rides.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "bicycle" because the speaker explicitly defines a velocipede as an early form or "precursor to the bicycle". In the summary, "a kind of " corresponds directly to the transcript's explanation of what a velocipede is.
Q40 exhibit His most magnificent exhibit, however, was the complete skeleton of an extinct mammal known as a mastodon which he helped excavate; the event was memorialized in his extraordinary painting, The Exhuming of the Mastodon Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript states that the most impressive display item in Peale's museum was the mastodon skeleton that he helped dig up.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "exhibit" refers to an object or item that is displayed for the public to see in a museum.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "exhibit" because the speaker mentions that Peale established a scientific museum and that his "most magnificent exhibit" was the full skeleton of an extinct mastodon that he helped excavate. In the summary sentence, "the best exhibit" directly corresponds to the phrase "most magnificent exhibit" in the lecture.

Transcript

In this lecture today I'm going to introduce you to an American painter: Charles Willson Peale—you may be familiar with his portraits; but did you know that he never even saw a painting till he was a grown man?

He was born in Maryland in 1741. His father died when he was nine and the family struggled financially for the next few years and Charles became a saddle maker's apprentice. One day he went to Norfolk for supplies and there he saw paintings for the first time. He thought they were so bad that he felt sure he could do better so he decided to make painting his career. In 1766 he went to London to study painting with Benjamin West. Whilst there he painted this portrait in 1768, see slide 1, Pitt as a Roman Senator. Notice how elaborately symbolical this portrait is. The symbolism arises of course from Pitt's famous speech to the British Parliament where he draws an analogy between the ancient Roman Senate's view of a barbaric Britain and the prevailing European view of the time of a barbaric African continent fuelling the slavery trade. Perhaps you didn't know that the Romans used Britons as slaves? But I digress...back to Peale.

He returned to America and in 1772 painted the first ever portrait of George Washington (see slide 2). In 1773 he painted a group portrait of himself, his wife, mother, brothers, sister, his old nurse and an unidentified baby. Just look at the slide—this painting is simply called The Peale Family and you can almost feel the exuberance of the family and their warmth towards one another. He enjoyed great success as a portraitist prior to the Revolution and served with distinction in the Revolution. During this time he became friends with George Washington, Benjamin Franklin and Thomas Jefferson.

After the war he continued to paint and, when his wife died in the 1790s as a result of her eleventh pregnancy, he remarried. He had 17 children in all, naming the sons after famous painters or scientists. Although perhaps best known for his portraits of famous people, Peale liked novelty. Look at this slide of his two sons, Raphaelle and Titian, Life Size, climbing a narrow stairway. This painting, the Staircase Group (1795), was exhibited in a doorway as a trompe l'oeil—and it is said that it did in fact 'fool the eye' of George Washington. Even as far back as 1772, we can see his desire for difference in Rachel Weeping. It's a rather macabre portrait of his first wife crying over the death of one of their children, their daughter, Margaret. I'd like to show you one more slide to demonstrate his innovative approach—this is a portrait of his brother, James, sitting at his desk at night with only his face illuminated by a lamp. This was painted much later than the others—in 1822.

You know, Peale believed anyone could learn to paint and he taught painting to his brothers, sisters, sons, daughters, nephews, nieces, and other relatives. Four of his sons, Titian, Rubens, Rembrandt and Raphaelle, became painters as did his brother James.

Before I finish I'd like to tell you a bit more about Peale. He was active in politics for several years and throughout his life he maintained a lively interest in many branches of science. He was also an inventor who gained patents for a fireplace, porcelain false teeth, and a new kind of wooden bridge. He collaborated with Thomas Jefferson on what was known as the polygraph—a kind of portable writing desk—but it wasn't any ordinary desk: this one could make several copies of a manuscript at once. He also wrote papers on a wide variety of subjects from hygiene to engineering. Oh, and he also tried his hand at inventing a fairly primitive but innovative motion picture technique; new types of eyeglasses; and a velocipede (which is a precursor to the bicycle). Now some of the original velocipedes had pedals and some didn't—you sort of scooted along on them using your feet—unfortunately, I can't remember which type it was that Peale worked on.

He's also remembered for his work as a naturalist. He established the first scientific museum in America and he even invented his own system of taxidermy—for those of you who aren't sure what taxidermy is, it's the art of preparing, stuffing and presenting dead animals so that they appear lifelike. He was also well ahead of his time in that he placed his animals in a simulated natural environment. His most magnificent exhibit, however, was the complete skeleton of an extinct mammal known as a mastodon which he helped excavate; the event was memorialized in his extraordinary painting, The Exhuming of the Mastodon.

That is the end of section 4. You now have half a minute to check your answers.
