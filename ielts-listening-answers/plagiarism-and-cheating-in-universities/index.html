Questions

Questions 11–15 One Choice

Choose the correct letter, A, B or C.

11 The dean of a university had to resign for committing plagiarism
  1. when he was famous.
  2. more than 20 years ago.
  3. over a period of 20 years.
12 A survey of US school students showed that in 1989 copying occurred among
  1. 58% of them.
  2. 69% of them.
  3. 97% of them.
13 A recent survey showed that cheating is most common among
  1. high school students.
  2. undergraduate students.
  3. postgraduate students.
14 The main reason plagiarism has become more common is
  1. students can cut and paste from the Internet.
  2. the Internet contains a lot of information.
  3. teachers are too busy to check for plagiarism.
15 Using the computer program to detect plagiarism may cost a student
  1. 30c.
  2. 50c.
  3. 60c.

Questions 16–20 Sentence Completion

Complete the sentences below.

Write NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS for each answer.

  • The computer program to detect copying cannot tell whether students have copied from 16.
  • Every university in 17 is now using this computer program.
  • The most common penalty for plagiarism is to 18.
  • A method now being used to reduce the occurrence of plagiarism among undergraduates is 19.
  • Universities prefer to 20 about plagiarism rather than catch students who have plagiarized.

Answers & Explanations Summary

# Answer Evidence Explanation
Q11 B There was a famous case last year of a university Dean who had to resign when it came out that in a few books he'd written over 20 years ago there were some instances of plagiarism Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript states that a university leader had to quit his job because people found examples of copied writing in books he wrote more than twenty years ago.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the university dean copied other people's work more than twenty years in the past.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the woman mentions a university dean who was forced to leave his job after people discovered plagiarism in books he had written "over 20 years ago." The phrase "over 20 years ago" matches the meaning of the keyword phrase "more than 20 years ago." Therefore, he committed plagiarism more than 20 years before the event.
Q12 C One survey in the US found that back in 1969, 58% of school students let others copy their work, but this had risen to 97% in 1989 Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript states that a study of American students showed 58% allowed copying in 1969, but that number climbed up to 97% by 1989.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that 97% of US school students let others copy their work in the year 1989.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is C because the female speaker talks about a survey in the United States. She notes that in 1969, the percentage of students allowing copying was 58%, but by 1989, this number had increased to 97%. Pay attention to the keywords "risen to 97% in 1989", which directly points to 97% for the year 1989.
Q13 B A more recent survey in another country showed that more cheating happens now among undergraduate students than high school students. Male: That's disappointing. Female: Yeah. There are even instances of postgraduate students doing it, though it involves a much smaller proportion of them Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript states that undergraduate students cheat more than students in high school, and only a much smaller percentage of postgraduate students cheat. This shows undergraduate students cheat the most.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means undergraduate students at university cheat more often than the other groups of students.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the woman mentions a recent survey comparing the student groups. She explains that more cheating happens among undergraduate university students than high school students, while postgraduate students make up a much smaller percentage. Therefore, cheating is most common among undergraduate students.
Q14 B Anyway, what it really comes down to is that the Internet is so huge, there are just so many more sources than there used to be Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript says that the main reason behind this problem is that the Internet is enormous and offers far more places to get information than people had in the past.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means that the primary cause of increased cheating is the vast amount of material available online.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because the woman explains the core reason why cheating has increased. While she mentions cutting and pasting earlier, she emphasizes the main point by stating, "what it really comes down to is that the Internet is so huge, there are just so many more sources than there used to be." The phrase "what it really comes down to" signifies the main reason, and "so huge" with "so many more sources" directly corresponds to the idea that "the Internet contains a lot of information." She also explains that teachers cannot simply be blamed for being too busy, because the sheer size of the Internet makes it impossible for anyone to check everything.
Q15 B Male: Is it expensive? Female: No, it only comes to about 50c a student Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript shows the speaker stating that the software program is cheap, costing only about 50c (50 cents) per student.
Answer Explanation:
The answer B means that the cost for checking each student's work with the program is approximately 50 cents.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is B because when the man asks if the new software program is expensive, the woman clearly replies that it only costs around 50 cents per student. Other numbers mentioned nearby, such as 30% (the rate of plagiarism) and 60 (the number of countries using it), are distractors.
Q16 books Male: What about if the student's copied from books? Female: No, it only covers electronic material Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript shows the speaker asking if the software checks whether students copy text from books, and the response clarifies that the program only checks electronic content on computers.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means printed reading materials or paper books.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "books" because the man asks what happens if a student copies from books, and the woman answers "No, it only covers electronic material." This indicates that the software cannot check or detect plagiarism from printed books.
Q17 Britain It's in operation in all the universities in Britain, but only at some of the big ones in the United States Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript states that every single university in Britain uses this software, whereas in the United States, only some large universities use it.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "Britain" is the name of a country located in Europe (also known as Great Britain or the United Kingdom).
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "Britain" because the speaker mentions that the plagiarism detection software is actively running in every university in that country. The prompt asks which place has "Every university" using the program, which corresponds to the speaker saying it is "in operation in all the universities in Britain".
Q18 fail the paper An alternative is for the student to be failed for the whole course, but usually they just failed that paper Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript states that while a school could fail a student for the entire subject, most of the time the student simply gets a failing mark on that single written assignment.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means giving the student a failing grade on the specific essay or assignment they copied.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "fail the paper" because the question asks about the most common punishment for cheating. In the conversation, the woman discusses what happens when a student cheats: being expelled is rare, failing the entire course is an option, but "usually they just fail that paper." Here, "usually" matches "the most common penalty".
Q19 written exams Well, now some unis are putting less emphasis on papers done during term and going back to written exams where there's much less chance of cheating Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript states that some universities are giving fewer take-home essays and are instead using written tests again, because it is much harder for students to cheat during these exams.
Answer Explanation:
The answer "written exams" refers to paper-and-pencil tests taken in a classroom where students write their answers by hand.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "written exams" because the female speaker mentions that universities are trying to decrease cheating by returning to tests that students write in person. In the conversation, the male speaker asks what else can be done to reduce plagiarism ("reduce the amount of plagiarizing happening"), and she explains that some universities are "going back to written exams where there's much less chance of cheating".
Q20 explain to students Generally, unis take the attitude that prevention's better than cure, so they explain to students what plagiarism is and how to avoid it Excerpt/Transcript Explanation:
The transcript explains that universities think it is better to stop a problem before it starts, so they teach learners what plagiarism is and how they can avoid doing it.
Answer Explanation:
The answer means to talk clearly to learners to help them understand something.
Reason For Correctness:
The correct answer is "explain to students" because the speaker discusses how universities try to stop cheating before it happens. The female speaker notes that universities believe "prevention's better than cure," meaning they prefer preventing cheating rather than punishing it later. Therefore, universities "explain to students" what copying is and how to avoid it.

Transcript

Male: Hey, did you see that show on TV last night about people downloading music from the Internet?

Female: No, I was busy with my research into students cheating at university.

Male: What do you mean by cheating?

Female: Plagiarism.

Male: What's that?

Female: Basically, it's when you use someone else's ideas or work and pretend it's your own work. There's nothing wrong with using other people's ideas or research, but you have to say where you got the ideas or information.

Male: Uh-huh.

Female: There was a famous case last year of a university Dean who had to resign when it came out that in a few books he'd written over 20 years ago there were some instances of plagiarism.

Male: Oh yeah, I remember hearing about that. I thought they were being a bit tough on him - I mean, it was a long time ago.

Female: Well, it's a pretty serious thing to do in academic circles. It's intellectual theft. It's nothing new, but it seems to have become much more common recently.

Male: How can you tell?

Female: Oh, a few surveys have been done on it. One survey in the US found that back in 1969, 58% of school students let others copy their work, but this had risen to 97% in 1989.

Male: It's that bad, isn't it? Well, anyway, you know what school kids are like.

Female: Well, the trouble is, it doesn't stop there - they take their bad habits with them when they go on to uni. A more recent survey in another country showed that more cheating happens now among undergraduate students than high school students.

Male: That's disappointing.

Female: Yeah. There are even instances of postgraduate students doing it, though it involves a much smaller proportion of them.

Male: So why's more cheating happening now?

Female: Well, the Internet has made it easier. You can use a search engine to find what you need to know, then cut and paste. Before the Internet, you had to go to books and copy the information by hand, so it took a lot longer. Students often used to write their essays by hand, too.

Male: Yeah, I don't do much writing anymore, but I do plenty of typing.

Female: Well, your handwriting is pretty terrible, so that's probably not a bad thing. Anyway, what it really comes down to is that the Internet is so huge, there are just so many more sources than there used to be. In the older days if a teacher thought a student had cheated, they could look up the relevant books to check. The Internet is far too big for that. I know teachers are always complaining about being too busy, but it's beyond the capacity of anyone to have an overview of the Internet.

Male: Yeah, there are billions of pages out there. So does that mean it's impossible to stop it?

Female: Not exactly. There is this new software program being used now to check the work of almost 5 million students in about 60 countries. They find that there's a plagiarism rate of about 30%.

Male: Is it expensive?

Female: No, it only comes to about 50c a student.

Male: So, how does this computer program work?

Female: Well, when a student hands in a paper, they also have to submit it electronically. This is then compared to other essays and to a database of journals and of all the material that's on the Internet.

Male: What about if the student's copied from books?

Female: No, it only covers electronic material. The program then underlines any phrase, paragraph or page that's been copied from an electronic source, and this information is given to the teacher. It analyses around 10,000 term papers a day. It's in operation in all the universities in Britain, but only at some of the big ones in the United States.

Male: And what happens if they find that a student's cheated?

Female: Well, in extreme cases the student can be expelled from the uni. But expulsion can take a long time and give the uni bad publicity, so it doesn't happen much. An alternative is for the student to be failed for the whole course, but usually they just failed that paper.

Male: Yeah, I've never heard of anyone being expelled for cheating.

Female: Oh, it does happen, but you don't always hear about it. You know, the student involved's embarrassed, and the uni wants to protect its reputation.

Male: And what else can be done to reduce the amount of plagiarizing happening?

Female: Well, now some unis are putting less emphasis on papers done during term and going back to written exams where there's much less chance of cheating.

Male: Oh, like those tests we used to do in high school, all that tension and watching the clock?

Female: Yeah. They thought about introducing oral exams, but they'd be too time-consuming and expensive for undergraduate courses. Generally, unis take the attitude that prevention's better than cure, so they explain to students what plagiarism is and how to avoid it. That's the long-term solution. Anyway, it's not always easy to show for sure that someone's committed plagiarism in an essay.

Male: Even with this new software?

Female: That's right.
